# How the US Department of Energy Is Knitting Its Lab Supercomputers Together for AI

---

The US government is starting to knit its lab supercomputers together. The US government has recently conducted two AI training projects that illustrate how DOE is emerging as a significant actor in the AI infrastructure landscape. These projects leverage the extraordinary computational resources available across the national lab system — resources that collectively represent one of the largest concentrations of GPU compute in the world.

The first project focused on training large-scale scientific AI models for climate and materials science applications, using supercomputers at Oak Ridge, Argonne, and Lawrence Berkeley. The second explored federated learning approaches that allow models to be trained across multiple lab facilities without centralizing the training data.

These projects reflect a broader pattern of government investment in AI infrastructure that goes beyond the frontier model development being pursued by commercial labs. By building AI systems tailored to scientific discovery, DOE is creating capabilities that complement rather than compete with commercial AI research.

The implications for AI safety are significant: DOE's national labs have decades of experience handling classified and sensitive research, and the security practices developed in that context may offer lessons for how frontier AI development can be conducted responsibly at scale.
