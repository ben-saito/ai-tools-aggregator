# What's Behind the AI Industry's Latest Warnings of Doom?

The AI industry is having a reckoning moment. On the TechCrunch Equity podcast, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei have both signaled that it may be time to slow down the development of frontier AI systems. This marks a significant shift from the accelerationist stance that has dominated Silicon Valley for years.

The debate intensified after researcher Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic, citing concerns about AI safety. Both leaders have now publicly acknowledged that the risks posed by advanced AI systems warrant a more cautious approach to development.

---

## The "Pace the Frontier" Proposal

Amodei published a blog post outlining three broad strategies for decelerating AI development. He stated that Anthropic is "unilaterally committing" to one of these strategies, with Altman quickly adding that OpenAI would follow suit.

The core of the proposal centers on voluntary deceleration agreements between leading AI labs, combined with increased investment in alignment research and safety evaluations before each major model release.

Altman, speaking in a recent interview, acknowledged that "given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public" for OpenAI. The company has filed confidentially for an IPO but Altman indicated 2026 would be "ill-advised" for going public.

---

## Industry Implications

The alignment between OpenAI and Anthropic on this issue represents a notable departure from the competitive dynamics that have historically driven the AI industry. Both companies appear to be grappling with the reality that the capabilities of frontier models may be outpacing society's ability to ensure their safe deployment.

The voluntary deceleration approach faces skepticism from some quarters, with critics arguing that market competition makes binding agreements difficult to sustain. Others question whether slowing development in the US and Europe will simply shift frontier AI research to other regions with fewer safety constraints.
