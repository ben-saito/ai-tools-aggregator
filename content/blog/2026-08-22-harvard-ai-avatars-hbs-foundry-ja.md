# Harvard Business School Uses AI Avatars to Overcome Entrepreneur Resistance

Harvard Business School is expanding its reach through AI avatars to provide individualized feedback to entrepreneurs.

---

## AI Avatars Created by HeyGen

These avatars were created by a startup called HeyGen and are included in the eight-week, $699 HBS Foundry bootcamp for entrepreneurs. The program offers live sessions with instructors every week, but the AI avatars provide feedback during practice pitches and board meeting simulations.

New York Times reporter Sarah Kessler tested it herself by pitching to an AI-generated copy of Flybridge Capital co-founder Jeff Bussgang. Both the real Bussgang and his digital simulacrum were unimpressed by her plan to build "Uber for bananas," but Kessler noted the virtual version offered a noticeably frozen smile during her pitch.

---

## Student Reception

Project director Katharina Rings initially envisioned the AI component as more of a chatbot. However, after HBS released a trial version, students said they wanted a more guided experience.

While some college students have not been shy about expressing negative feelings toward AI, Foundry participants told Kessler they liked the avatars. Bussgang acknowledged his digital copy is a little "creepy," but said, "My students love it."

---

## The Future of AI-Assisted Education

This case demonstrates how AI use in education is advancing. HBS achieved individualized feedback through AI avatars at a scale impossible in traditional classroom settings.

Rather than replacing human instructors, AI serves as an available guide at any time. This experiment shows the potential for a new educational model where AI and human teachers coexist.
