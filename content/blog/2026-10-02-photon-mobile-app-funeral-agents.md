# Photon Holds Funeral for Mobile Apps, Raises $4.5M to Replace Them with AI Agents

TechCrunch held an unconventional event on September 17 in San Francisco: a funeral for mobile apps. The ceremony was organized by Photon, a startup that has now raised $4.5 million in seed funding to build AI agents that work over messaging platforms like iMessage, SMS/RCS, and email — replacing the need to download individual apps.

---

## The Thesis: Users Are Done Installing New Apps

Photon co-founder and CEO Daniel Tian laid out the core argument at the "app funeral": the era of downloading new apps is coming to an end. "Directionally, it's inevitable," Tian said, acknowledging the transition will take time.

The startup emerged from Tian and CTO Ryan Zhu's experience building consumer apps as students, frequently at hackathons. Every time they completed a new app, they hit the same barrier — users did not want to install yet another piece of software. Engagement dropped off a cliff after the initial download.

Instead, Photon is betting that users will increasingly prefer agents accessible through messaging apps they already use daily. Rather than opening a separate rideshare, food delivery, or e-commerce app, a user could message an agent that handles the request across multiple services through a single conversational interface.

---

## The Product: Building Agents for Messaging Platforms

Photon's platform lets developers build AI agents that operate over iMessage, SMS/RCS, email, and other messaging protocols. The agents can orchestrate actions across multiple back-end services while presenting a single chat interface to the user.

The company compares its approach to the early days of web development: just as websites eventually replaced desktop applications for many use cases, messaging-based agents may eventually replace traditional apps for a range of consumer tasks.

Meta's Muse, which has climbed to the top of app store charts, is cited as validation of the consumer appetite for conversational AI. But Photon sees its niche as serving developers who want to build agent experiences without the overhead of launching a full mobile app.

---

## The Market Context

The mobile app market has seen declining new installations over recent years, with user acquisition costs rising sharply. Simultaneously, large language models have made it technically feasible to build multi-step reasoning agents that can interact with external services via natural language instructions.

Investors in the seed round included representatives from Vercel, Stripe, and OpenAI, companies that participated in Photon's developer day panels alongside the app funeral event. The involvement of payments (Stripe) and deployment infrastructure (Vercel) reflects the broader platform approach Photon is taking — agents that can complete transactions, not just answer questions.

---

## Outlook

Photon is positioning itself at the intersection of the agentic AI wave and the ongoing shift in how consumers interact with digital services. Whether messaging platforms can fully replace app stores remains an open question, but the company has secured early funding to test the thesis at scale.

The startup plans to use the seed funding to expand its developer tooling and grow the range of services its agents can orchestrate. If the app funeral was a marketing stunt, the capital behind it suggests the bet is being taken seriously.
