# Intology Achieves 44.7% on PostTrainBench, Signaling AI R&D Automation Milestone

AI startup Intology, whose stated goal is to "automate R&D," has released a new version of Locus — software designed to transform LLMs into capable research agents — achieving a score of 44.7% on PostTrainBench, a benchmark measuring how well AI systems can improve open-weight models beyond their baseline performance.

---

## PostTrainBench: Measuring AI's Ability to Improve AI

PostTrainBench evaluates AI systems on their capacity to take an open-weight model and enhance its performance. The benchmark was introduced in March 2026, when the highest-scoring system achieved 23.2%. Intology's Locus running on Opus 5 now reaches 44.7%, outperforming all frontier-agent baselines and beating Fable 5 (41.8%).

The results were externally verified by the PostTrainBench authors and underwent contamination and cheating checks.

---

## Beyond Single-GPU Limits: PostTrainBench+

Intology also introduced a variant of PostTrainBench that removes the 10-hour wall-clock limit on a single GPU, allowing systems to use larger amounts of compute. With over 4,000 hours of H100 GPU time, Locus achieved 51.6% — surpassing the human baseline of 51.1%. The researchers predict this human baseline will be exceeded before the end of 2026.

---

## Real-World Applications

Locus has already been deployed in production: it "discovered and trained a language model end-to-end that now runs in production at ~2.8× lower error, ~5.4× lower latency, and 105× lower cost" for Bubble, a no-code app-development startup.

---

## Why This Matters

The PostTrainBench results add evidence that AI systems are capable of substantial AI R&D automation. Intology's jump of Opus 5 by 10 absolute percentage points with a better agent harness illustrates how the AI community may be under-eliciting current systems for their autonomous research potential. The trajectory suggests AI systems will play an increasingly direct role in building the next generation of AI.
