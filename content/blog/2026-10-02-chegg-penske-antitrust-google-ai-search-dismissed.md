# Judge Dismisses Chegg and Penske Antitrust Lawsuits Against Google AI Search

A federal judge has dismissed antitrust lawsuits filed by Chegg and Penske against Google over its AI-powered search features. The court acknowledged that AI search has consequences, but found it does not constitute an antitrust violation under current law.

---

## Background of the Cases

Chegg, the online textbook rental company, and Penske Media Corporation filed separate lawsuits alleging that Google's integration of AI summaries into search results unfairly harmed their businesses. Both companies argued that Google's dominant position in search allowed it to direct users away from their platforms.

The lawsuits claimed that Google's AI search features, which provide direct answers at the top of search results, constituted anticompetitive behavior. Chegg specifically argued that its textbook and tutoring business had been harmed by students using AI-powered search for homework help instead of subscribing to Chegg's services.

## The Court's Ruling

Judge [Name] dismissed both cases, ruling that the plaintiffs had not demonstrated that Google's behavior met the legal standard for antitrust violations. While acknowledging that AI search "comes with consequences" for traditional content businesses, the court found that providing relevant AI-generated answers does not inherently violate antitrust law.

The ruling is significant for the technology industry as it establishes that AI integration into search products is not automatically subject to antitrust scrutiny, provided the underlying search functionality remains accessible to competitors.

## Industry Implications

The dismissal represents a victory for Google and other search engines incorporating AI features. It suggests that courts may be reluctant to penalize AI-powered search enhancements unless there is clear evidence of anticompetitive exclusion of rivals.

The decision may encourage further AI integration into search products, as companies can now proceed with some confidence that such features will not automatically trigger antitrust liability. However, the ruling does not preclude future litigation if Google's AI search practices evolve in ways that more directly harm specific competitors.
