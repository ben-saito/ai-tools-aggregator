Amazon has announced it will stop using nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) when negotiating data center deals with local governments, following Microsoft's earlier decision to do the same. The move is part of a broader effort by major tech companies to address growing opposition to data center development in communities across the United States.

The announcement came on the TechCrunch Equity podcast, where Amazon executives discussed the company's changing approach to data center negotiations. The decision reflects increased scrutiny of the data center approval process and growing concerns about the environmental and community impact of large-scale AI infrastructure.

Microsoft previously made a similar commitment, and the two companies' decisions signal a potential industry shift toward greater transparency in data center siting negotiations. The change comes as cities and towns increasingly push back against data center proposals, citing concerns about energy consumption, water usage, and property values.

---

## Key Highlights

- **Company:** Amazon
- **Policy Change:** No NDAs in data center negotiations with local governments
- **Following:** Microsoft's similar commitment
- **Context:** Growing community opposition to data center development

---

## Industry Impact

The decision to drop NDAs represents a significant shift in how tech companies approach data center negotiations. Previously, confidentiality agreements were common in negotiations over incentives, environmental impacts, and operational details.

Community advocates have argued that NDAs prevent residents from understanding the true impact of data center proposals, making it difficult to engage in informed public discourse. Amazon's move addresses this concern directly.

The timing coincides with increased attention on AI infrastructure expansion and its associated costs—both financial incentives offered to tech companies and environmental impacts. Several municipalities have imposed moratoriums on data center development while they study the long-term implications.
