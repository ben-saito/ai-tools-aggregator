# AI Agents Are Coming to Rivian's Cars

---

Rivian recently rolled out an an AI assistant that lets drivers and passengers interact with the company's electric vehicles just by talking to them. But the carmaker's founder and CEO, RJ Scaringe, said at TechCrunch Disrupt this week that, in the future, Rivian's cars will be able to run AI agents made by other companies like Anthropic. "That's where it becomes magical," Scaringe said onstage. Scaringe didn't say when Rivian would implement this, and the company declined to offer more details.

## The Vision: An AI Agent Marketplace in Your Car

The announcement represents a shift from Rivian's current embedded assistant toward a more open platform. Rather than limiting owners to a single AI system, Rivian is exploring how third-party AI agents could operate within its vehicles — potentially allowing drivers to summon different AI capabilities depending on their needs.

"We're thinking about this as a platform," Scaringe explained at the conference. The idea borrows from the smartphone app store model: just as users can choose different apps for navigation, music, or messaging, future Rivian owners might select different AI agents for tasks like scheduling, real-time traffic reasoning, or vehicle diagnostics.

## Why Anthropic?

Anthropic's Claude models have gained traction in enterprise settings for their constitutional AI approach and stronger alignment properties. For a vehicle context — where AI decisions can have immediate physical consequences — the emphasis on safer, more controllable AI behavior makes Anthropic a natural partner candidate.

Rivian's interest in Anthropic agents also reflects a broader trend in the auto industry: traditional Tier 1 suppliers are being joined by AI companies as key technology partners. The distinction between a car company and an AI platform is increasingly blurred.

## Challenges and Safety Considerations

Running external AI agents in a vehicle raises obvious safety questions. An AI agent that can interact with your car's systems needs robust guardrails to prevent unauthorized access or unintended commands. Scaringe acknowledged that Rivian is thinking carefully about how to sandbox third-party agents so they can't compromise vehicle safety or driver privacy.

The timeline for actual implementation remains unclear. Rivian has not committed to a specific model year or software version for when external AI agents might appear in its vehicles.
