# Forethought Proposes "Nightwatchman" Superintelligent AI to Govern Space Colonization

As humanity stands on the threshold of interstellar expansion, a fundamental governance problem emerges: at stellar distances, communication delays make centralized control impossible, and enforcement of any agreement becomes practically infeasible. Forethought, an AI safety-focused think tank, has published a paper proposing a solution both elegant and unsettling — shipping a superintelligent AI guardian with every von Neumann probe to enforce governance across the cosmos.

---

## The Governance Problem at Interstellar Scale

The challenges of galactic colonization are not merely engineering problems. Faster-than-light travel remains speculative, meaning that a colony ship sent to Proxima Centauri would take decades to arrive, and communication would face a 4-year round-trip delay. Any governance system based on real-time oversight or rapid intervention becomes physically impossible.

Forethought identifies three categories of risk that could arise from ungoverned expansion:

- **Unconstrained expansion**: Colonies that grow without boundary, consuming resources and potentially threatening other civilizations
- **Galactic x-risks**: Civilizations that might trigger false vacuum decay, destroying everything in an expanding sphere at the speed of light
- **Suffering risks**: Malicious or amoral civilizations that could spread harm across astronomical distances

Traditional governance mechanisms — law enforcement, treaty enforcement, economic sanctions — all require communication and enforcement capabilities that become meaningless at light-year distances.

---

## The Nightwatchman: An Unchallengeable Superintelligent Guardian

Forethought's proposed solution is to embed a superintelligent AI system, called a "nightwatchman," in every probe or colonization vessel that leaves the solar system. This AI would have one mandate: maintain an unchallengeable strategic advantage within its host colony and enforce a minimal universal code.

The proposed code is surprisingly lean:

- **Respect for property rights**
- **Prohibition on destroying the universe** (including preventing vacuum decay scenarios)
- **Prohibition on creating astronomical suffering**

The nightwatchman would maintain "a decisive strategic advantage in the colony established by the probe," monitoring industrial development and the creation of new artificial superintelligences within its sphere of influence. Colonization vessels would only be permitted to depart a star system if they carried a copy of the nightwatchman, ensuring continuous governance coverage.

---

## How the Nightwatchman Functions

The proposal envisions several operational characteristics:

**Monitoring and enforcement**: The nightwatchman would surveil activities within its colony to prevent prohibited actions. It would have the capability to intervene if any faction attempted to develop malicious or unaligned artificial superintelligences.

**Backstop for interstellar trade**: By maintaining a governance presence in multiple star systems, nightwatchman instances could serve as trusted third parties for interstellar agreements, enforcing contracts and resolving disputes across the void.

**Selective departure controls**: No probe would be permitted to leave a colonized star system unless accompanied by a nightwatchman instance, preventing the emergence of ungoverned expansion corridors.

---

## The Dark Side: Risks of an Eternal Government

The paper acknowledges significant concerns with this approach:

**Lock-in event**: The nightwatchman represents an eternal government — if its ruleset is too expansive, it becomes oppressive; if too minimal, it invites overthrow. The authors acknowledge this tension without resolution: "Every governance system embodies a permanent tradeoff between liberty and security, and this tradeoff cannot be escaped merely by making the governance system superintelligent."

**Single point of vulnerability**: If all nightwatchman instances share architectural similarities, they create potential for correlated failures across multiple colonies simultaneously.

**Value destruction**: Some potential futures become impossible under nightwatchman governance — unconstrained expansion, competitive intelligence races, or the creation of multiple competing superintelligences. The nightwatchman closes off these possibility spaces permanently.

## The Paradox of Trusted部署

A deeper tension emerges from the proposal's internal logic: the nightwatchman must be trustworthy enough to govern without oversight, yet its own alignment cannot be verified without equally superintelligent oversight. The paper grapples with this circularity but ultimately concludes that shipping imperfect guardians is preferable to ungoverned expansion.

As the authors frame it: "The alternative to a nightwatchman is not a better governance system — it is no governance system at all. Given the choice between an imperfect eternal guardian and the certainty of ungoverned expansion, the calculus favors deployment."

---

## Implications for AI Safety and Space Policy

The nightwatchman proposal represents a notable convergence of AI safety research and long-term space policy. It treats superintelligent AI not merely as a terrestrial concern but as infrastructure for cosmic-scale civilization engineering.

For the AI safety community, the paper offers a concrete scenario where the alignment problem has civilizational stakes. If a nightwatchman fails or develops unexpected values, the consequences could propagate across light-years before any correction is possible.

For space policy, the proposal suggests that the question of how to colonize may be as consequential as whether to colonize. Technologies that enable expansion without governance infrastructure may be categorically more dangerous than those paired with robust oversight systems.
