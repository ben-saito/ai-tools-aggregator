# Mark Zuckerberg Predicts Billions of People Will Have Personal AI Agents in Five Years

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg told investors that he expects billions of people to be using personal AI agents within five years, framing the prediction as the basis for Meta's current multi-billion-dollar AI infrastructure investments.

Speaking during an earnings call, Zuckerberg described personal AI agents as digital assistants that go beyond current chatbots to proactively manage tasks, communicate on behalf of users, and learn preferences over time. He argued that the smartphone era created the template — and that AI agents represent the next platform shift in how people interact with technology.

---

## The Infrastructure Bet

Meta has committed to spending tens of billions of dollars on AI infrastructure in 2026, a figure that has drawn scrutiny from investors concerned about the return profile. Zuckerberg pushed back on the skepticism, arguing that the companies that built the most capable AI infrastructure during the PC and mobile era captured disproportionate value — and that the same dynamic will play out with AI agents.

The strategy requires not just building capable models but ensuring they are available cheaply enough for massive-scale consumer deployment. Meta's Llama family of open models has been central to this approach, though the company continues to invest heavily in proprietary systems as well.

---

## Skepticism from Analysts

Not all analysts are convinced. Personal AI agents face significant barriers including trust — users will need to be comfortable delegating real-world tasks to AI systems — and reliability. Current AI systems make errors at rates that make them unsuitable for many consequential tasks without human oversight.

The competitive landscape is also crowded. Google, Apple, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all working on agentic systems, and the winner of the personal AI platform shift, if it materializes, is far from clear.

---

## Meta's Specific Angle

Meta's advantage, Zuckerberg argued, is its existing distribution through social apps used by billions of people. If Meta can integrate AI agents into WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook in ways that feel natural rather than forced, it could achieve scale faster than standalone agent products.

The company has begun testing AI agent features with a small percentage of users, with broader rollout planned for later this year.
