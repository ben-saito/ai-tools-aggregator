# Obama Urges Democrats to Have a 'Clear Plan' for AI Safeguards

Former President Barack Obama has called on Democrats to make artificial intelligence a central part of their policy agenda, warning that the technology is moving too fast in private hands to be left unregulated.

---

## Obama: AI Must Become a 'Central Agenda' Item for Democrats

Obama made these comments on Thursday, September 10, at a Democratic fundraising event where he was interviewed by House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries. According to the New York Times, Obama's office provided a partial transcript of the conversation.

"When we talk about this stuff, my recommendation to Democrats is: let's make this one of our central agendas," Obama said. He argued that Democrats need to "have a very clear plan" to address concerns around AI's economic impact and ensure the technology is deployed safely.

"This is something that is moving very fast in private hands, and if we don't get on top of it, I think can be dangerous," Obama added. "If we do get on top of it, I do think it's beneficial. I genuinely believe that."

---

## Obama Has Spoken With Anthropic and OpenAI CEOs

The former president's comments come amidst growing scrutiny of frontier AI labs and their development timelines. According to reports, Obama has offered himself as a "sounding board" to AI executives and has spoken directly with both Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman.

House Minority Leader Jeffries responded to Obama's remarks by saying the former president "is correct that decisive action must be taken on artificial intelligence." Jeffries also criticized Republicans, stating that "Republicans have abdicated their responsibility" on AI policy.

---

## Political Divide on AI Governance

The remarks highlight a growing partisan split on how to approach AI regulation. While Democrats, including Obama, have emphasized the need for proactive governance and federal safeguards, Republican-led efforts have generally favored lighter-touch approaches that avoid constraining AI companies.

The broader debate mirrors an ongoing conversation in the AI industry itself. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman have both publicly discussed the need to "pace" frontier AI development, though they differ on specific strategies. Obama's intervention adds a political dimension to what has largely been a conversation within the tech industry.
