"Software is Over": Bold AI Developer Takes Aim at Adobe with Open Source Clones

A developer operating under the pseudonym Opus is building a suite of open-source Creative Cloud alternatives powered by AI. The project, still early-stage, aims to replicate Photoshop, Premiere, and other Adobe applications using local AI models and open-source tooling.

The core thesis: traditional software development is fundamentally limited by human-coded logic, whereas AI-native applications can learn and adapt in ways impossible for static software. "Software is over" refers to the idea that fixed-feature applications are giving way to AI systems that generate functionality on demand.

**What's been built so far:**

- Image editing capabilities approaching Photoshop for common tasks
- Video cutting and effects using open-source AI models
- Generative fill and content-aware operations running locally

**Limitations and challenges:**

- Performance varies significantly across hardware configurations
- Complex professional workflows remain difficult to automate
- Licensing of training data for open-source AI tools is legally murky

The project raises interesting questions about the future of software: if AI can replicate software functionality, what is the role of traditional application development? The answer likely involves a hybrid model where AI handles routine tasks and humans focus on creative direction.

For developers watching this space, the project demonstrates both the potential and the immaturity of AI-native application development. Ambitious claims require measured expectations.

---

## Developer Perspective

The Opus project illustrates a broader trend: AI is shifting the competitive advantage from feature completeness toward inference quality and user experience. Building a sustainable open-source creative tools ecosystem will require addressing both technical and legal dimensions.
