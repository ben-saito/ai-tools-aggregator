# Building the Materials Foundation for AI

MIT Technology Review published an article on September 16, 2026, examining how the AI boom is becoming a materials challenge as semiconductors and data centers approach their physical limits.

---

## AI Pushes Semiconductors to Their Limits

As AI computing demand accelerates, the infrastructure powering it is hitting physical walls that algorithms alone cannot overcome. Processor performance improvements are slowing, data center power consumption is surging, and thermal management has become a critical bottleneck.

According to Mike Finelli, Chief Technology and Innovation Officer at Syensqo: "AI is now, from a material standpoint, really pushing semiconductors and the data centers to their physical limits."

---

## Accumulating Requirements

AI workloads demand a remarkable combination of material properties:

- **High Temperature Durability**: Managing heat from increased compute density
- **Purity**: Trace impurities degrade device performance
- **Electrical Performance**: Supporting faster signal transmission
- **Chemical Resistance**: Long-term exposure to reactive environments
- **Plasma Resistance**: Endurance during manufacturing processes
- **Long-term Stability**: Reliable operation over decades

Finelli describes meeting all these requirements simultaneously as reaching "the top of the pyramid."

---

## AI Accelerates Materials Discovery

While AI strains materials, it also enables new approaches to materials science. AI systems can rapidly screen vast molecular universes to identify promising candidates for new semiconductor materials.

This creates a virtuous cycle: AI pushes materials to their limits, while AI-powered materials science accelerates discovery of solutions.

---

## Data Center Architecture Implications

Syensqo is developing materials for high-voltage data center architectures. As AI server power density increases, new requirements emerge for sealing materials, thermal management solutions, and advanced semiconductor packaging.

---

## Conclusion

Progress in AI compute capability now requires materials science advances at least as much as algorithmic improvements. The root cause of HBM shortages lies partly in the pace of new memory cell materials development. As this MIT article demonstrates, materials science is emerging as a key battleground in the AI race.
