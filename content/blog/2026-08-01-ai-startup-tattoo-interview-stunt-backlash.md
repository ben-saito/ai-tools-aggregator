# AI Startup LemonLime Faces Backlash Over Tattoo-for-Interview Stunt

Small AI startup LemonLime has apologized after a hiring stunt in which the company offered instant interviews to anyone who got permanently tattooed with the LemonLime logo. Seven people accepted the offer during a company party, prompting immediate backlash and criticism of the firm's workplace culture.

CEO Jordan Zietz acknowledged "poor judgement" in a LinkedIn post, stating he "messed up" and got "carried away" with the gimmick. The incident drew attention to LemonLime's aggressive hiring philosophy, which emphasizes cultural fit over traditional credentials.

"Our entire team shows up to the office 7 days a week," Zietz wrote in a recent post, noting that "over a dozen applicants thus far have withdrawn after stating that they do not work past 5 pm or on weekends." The company requires employees to push "critical product features to production while watching Pitch Perfect Sing-Along Edition at 1 a.m. on a Saturday," according to Zietz.

LemonLime, which describes itself as a company that "optimizes your existing data to work with AI," has attempted multiple unconventional recruitment tactics. In addition to the tattoo offer, the company created a game called "Is it a Lemon or a Lime?" and promised a perfect score as an alternative path to employment.

The controversy highlights ongoing debates about work-life balance in AI startups, where intense schedules and equity-based compensation are common despite market headwinds affecting the sector.
