# Researchers Use AI Models to Discover Zero-Day Vulnerabilities in Zoom — With Fewer Than 20 Prompts

---

## A Security Discovers Critical Screen Sharing Flaw Affecting All Zoom-Supported Platforms in Early June

Researchers at cybersecurity firm **A Security** used publicly available AI models to discover critical vulnerabilities in Zoom's screen sharing annotation protocol, the company reported in coordination with Wired. The researchers say the discovery took fewer than 20 prompts — work that previously would have required a team of five researchers approximately six months to accomplish.

Zoom issued a security advisory and began rolling out fixes across all supported platforms: Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android.

---

## AI Democratizing Zero-Day Research

A Security cofounder Omer Gull described the implications: "What is interesting for us and what we believe is dangerous is the democratization of these capabilities — the barrier to entry is dropping rapidly. Before it would have taken a team of five people maybe six months with a lot of refining and iteration to find this. Now people can reach the same results with under 20 prompts."

The researchers specifically targeted Zoom's real-time annotation component during screen sharing — a feature that involves complex, proprietary closed-source code. The AI models were trained to identify overlooked vulnerabilities in convoluted and obscure functions, a strategy paralleling how human bug hunters prioritize targets.

---

## Implications for Enterprise Security Posture

Zoom's position as a trusted communication platform for enterprises and governments makes it a high-value target. The AI-assisted discovery process demonstrates that vulnerability research — historically requiring deep expertise and significant time investment — is becoming accessible to actors with far fewer resources.

For security teams, the development underscores the dual-use nature of AI-powered security research: the same capabilities that help defenders find flaws can enable attackers to discover and exploit them at unprecedented speed.
