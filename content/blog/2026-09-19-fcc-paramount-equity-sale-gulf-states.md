# FCC Allows Paramount to Sell 49.5% Equity Stake to Gulf States

Ars Technica reported on September 18, 2026 that the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has approved Paramount Global's request to sell a 49.5% equity stake to investors from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar.

---

## The Deal Structure

Paramount, the parent company of CBS, MTV, Paramount Pictures, and other media properties, sought FCC approval to allow foreign investment from the sovereign wealth funds of three Gulf states. The transaction represents a significant shift in ownership structure for one of America's major media companies.

The FCC's decision allows the Gulf state investment vehicles to acquire nearly half of Paramount's equity, subject to various regulatory conditions and oversight mechanisms.

---

## Regulatory Concerns

The deal drew scrutiny from lawmakers and national security experts who raised concerns about foreign governments—particularly those with poor human rights records—gaining influence over American media outlets. Critics argued that such ownership could affect editorial decisions, news coverage, and the cultural influence of CBS News and other Paramount properties.

The FCC rejected these concerns, determining that the investment structure included sufficient safeguards to prevent foreign government interference in editorial operations. The commission reportedly imposed conditions requiring independent editorial oversight and restrictions on certain types of content decisions.

---

## Media Industry Context

The transaction occurs amid ongoing consolidation in the media industry as companies struggle with cord-cutting, streaming competition, and shifting advertising markets. Paramount has been exploring various strategic options, and the influx of capital from Gulf sovereign wealth funds provides liquidity and financial flexibility.

This deal represents one of the largest instances of foreign investment in a major American media company and may set precedent for how the FCC evaluates similar future applications from state-linked foreign investors.
