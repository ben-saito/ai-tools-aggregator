# Can Muse Overcome Meta's Trust Issues?

---

## TechCrunch Podcast Examines Meta's AI Strategy and Consumer Trust

On the latest episode of TechCrunch's Equity podcast, the hosts discussed how Meta's recent AI announcements managed to capture media attention during a particularly competitive week in the AI industry. With OpenAI and Anthropic both making significant model announcements, Meta managed to carve out its own narrative with consumer-focused AI products.

The discussion centered on Meta's fundamental challenge: the company whose core business is selling advertising based on user data is now asking consumers to trust it with increasingly personal AI interactions. As the podcast hosts noted, this creates an inherent tension that consumers are right to question.

## The Tamagotchi Approach to AI

Meta's strategy with Muse, its consumer AI agent, has taken a notably different direction from its competitors. Rather than focusing on coding assistants or enterprise tools — the bread and butter of OpenAI and Anthropic — Meta has leaned into consumer-facing, personal AI experiences. The device has been described as having a Tamagotchi-like quality: cute, personal, and designed for everyday interactions.

Sean O'Kane, who tried Muse for himself, found that the agent did deliver on some promised functionality, including finding unclaimed money. However, he characterized the feature set as more "a party-trick type thing" rather than a genuinely useful daily tool.

## The Trust Deficit Problem

The podcast hosts were direct about the core issue: Meta's business model depends on knowing as much about users as possible to serve targeted advertisements. When Meta then positions itself as a trusted personal AI companion — something that learns your habits, preferences, and personal information — the contrast is striking.

"Meta's business is to sell you ads," one host noted. "And yes, they'll make the argument that the more they know about you, the more accurate and interesting the ads will be — wake me up when we get to something that changes that equation."

## Competitive Landscape

Despite the trust challenges, Meta has managed to generate excitement around its AI products in a way that few consumer AI products have achieved. The company has been rapidly rolling out Muse features and expanding access. Whether the Tamagotchi approach to AI will translate into sustained engagement remains to be seen, but Meta's ability to capture mindshare in a crowded AI market is noteworthy.

The question of whether consumer trust can be built with a company whose incentives are fundamentally misaligned with user privacy remains open. Meta appears to be betting that the utility of its AI products will outweigh the trust deficit created by its advertising business model.
