# Jensen Huang Explains Why Nvidia Will Grow an Astounding 70% Next Year

Nvidia has its finger in every AI pie, and sees another year of plenty in its future, CEO Jensen Huang says. But he insists the company's deals are not circular.

---

## 'Most People Think Nvidia Builds a Chip'

"There's been endless hand-wringing over whether Nvidia's party will end as it faces increasing competition for GPUs and AI chips from all directions," TechCrunch reported. But Huang offered a different framing at a recent appearance.

"Most people think Nvidia builds a chip. I mean, you need airplanes to ship what we build," Huang said, adding that the company continues to battle perceptions that its dominance is fragile.

Huang pointed to the sheer physical scale of Nvidia's systems. Orders for just one product — a computer system that combines 36 Grace CPUs with 72 Blackwell GPUs — are currently experiencing 27% month-over-month growth.

---

## 70% Revenue Growth Guidance

Huang didn't limit his bullish view to current sales. He reiterated Nvidia's revenue outlook for next year, guidance that surprised analysts given the increasingly crowded AI chip market.

"I think we could grow 70% year over year. We're confident about that," Huang said. Analysts expect the company to end its current fiscal year with approximately $160 billion in revenue, and the 70% target would imply roughly $272 billion next year.

The confidence stems from what Huang describes as an "everyone else" dynamic — every AI lab, cloud provider, and enterprise is building out AI infrastructure, and Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem creates a gravitational pull toward its hardware.

---

## The Circular Deal Question

Critics have asked whether Nvidia's deals with AI labs are circular — meaning Nvidia profits both from selling chips and from the AI services those chips power. Huang rejected this characterization.

Despite increasing competition from custom chips built by Google (TPUs), Amazon (Trainium), Microsoft, and Meta, Nvidia's H100 and B100 series remain the de facto standard for training frontier models. The company's software moat, built over 15 years of CUDA optimization, remains the primary switching cost for AI developers.
