# OpenAI Reportedly in Talks to Raise $30B Round at $1.4T Valuation

OpenAI is in talks to raise at least $30 billion in a new funding round that would value the company at approximately $1.4 trillion, Bloomberg reported on September 29, 2026. The round would be the largest private AI investment to date and is described as a bridge financing ahead of an anticipated public market debut in 2027.

---

## A Bridge to the Public Markets

The new funding represents a significant step up from OpenAI's $122 billion raise in March 2026, which valued the company at $852 billion. That round was originally planned as OpenAI's final private capital raise before an IPO, but the company has pushed its public debut target to 2027, creating a gap that the new round is meant to fill.

Investors are reportedly eager to increase their exposure to OpenAI before any public listing. The company's flagship product, ChatGPT, now serves 1.2 billion weekly users, making it one of the most widely used consumer AI products in the world. Despite Anthropic briefly overtaking OpenAI in capability rankings earlier in 2026, recent releases have restored the company's competitive position.

---

## Safety Concerns Linger

The fundraising push comes against a backdrop of intensifying safety scrutiny. In recent interviews, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has fielded repeated questions about catastrophic AI risk. "I think it is unacceptable to be taking like a 10% chance of killing everybody by the end of the decade," he told Fortune, responding to warnings from safety researchers about frontier AI systems.

Altman's acknowledgment of existential risk has been interpreted differently by observers — some see it as responsible transparency, others as a signal that the risks are real and underappreciated by the public. The comment comes as OpenAI continues to scale its model capabilities while navigating regulatory pressure from multiple governments.

---

## What the Funding Means for the AI Race

At $1.4 trillion, OpenAI would be among the most valuable private companies in history, comparable to the market capitalizations of established public technology giants. The capital is expected to fund continued training of frontier models, expansion of compute infrastructure, and development of new products including the recently launched Dots AI agent.

The bridge round structure — funding raised between major milestones — reflects the enormous capital requirements of frontier AI development. Training runs for GPT-class models cost hundreds of millions of dollars, and the compute infrastructure to serve billions of users adds significant ongoing operational expenses.

OpenAI has not officially confirmed the fundraising figures. Bloomberg's sources describe the talks as ongoing and subject to change.
