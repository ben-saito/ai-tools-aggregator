# Micro1 AI Data Startup Reaches $500M Gross Run Rate Amid AI Training Boom

Micro1, an AI data startup, expanded its gross annual run rate from $100 million to $500 million in just eight months, according to a person familiar with the company. The rapid growth signals surging demand for high-quality AI training data as labs compete for scarce human-annotated datasets.

The company retains roughly 60-70% of its gross figure, putting its net annual run rate between $150 million and $200 million. While Micro1 still trails competitors like Mercor ($2 billion gross annualized revenue this summer) and Handshake ($1 billion), its growth trajectory demonstrates that demand for AI training data is far from saturated.

Researchers hypothesize that future AI spending on data could rival spending on compute -- a significant shift from the current paradigm where GPU infrastructure dominates capital expenditure.

---

## Data Quality and National Security Concerns

Micro1 employs domain experts including doctors, lawyers, and scientists on a contract basis to perform data labeling. The company sells the same datasets to multiple clients, a business model that has drawn criticism amid concerns about Chinese AI development.

Some critics argue that distributing off-the-shelf training data to Chinese model makers helps make their models as powerful as top U.S. models. Micro1 founder Ali Ansari addressed this directly on X last month, stating that unlike some competitors, Micro1 does not sell data to Chinese model makers.

"SOME HUMAN DATA COMPANIES WORK WITH FOREIGN ADVERSARIES," Ansari wrote. "AND THE RESULTS SHOW TODAY IN KIMI K3. WE BELIEVE IT IS SHAMEFUL TO CLAIM AMERICAN AI DOMINANCE DESIRES WHILE SELLING MILLIONS WORTH OF DATA TO COUNTRIES THAT WE ARE IN ADVERSARIAL COMPETITION WITH."

---

## From Recruiting to Data Labeling

Like competitor Mercor, Micro1 initially started as an AI recruiting startup. However, after noticing that data-labeling clients were using his AI platform to recruit engineers for annotation tasks, Ansari pivoted to the data-labeling business.
