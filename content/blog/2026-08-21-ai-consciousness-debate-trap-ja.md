# MIT Technology Review: AI Consciousness Debates Are a Trap

MIT Technology Review published an editorial warning that debates over AI consciousness are misleading developers and policymakers away from actual safety threats.

The current rhetoric around "runaway AI," "rogue agents," and "autonomous actors" implies AI systems are not only awake and aware, but actively opposed to their creators, the editorial states. Prominent tech leaders including Sam Altman and Dario Amodei have engaged in discussions about AI consciousness and intent.

However, evaluating whether AI systems are conscious remains a technically unsolved problem. Current LLMs are text prediction mechanisms with no evidence of self-awareness, despite discussions treating consciousness as an established fact.

The trap is that these debates divert resources from genuine AI safety threats including data tampering, model poisoning, and adversarial inputs. These concrete risks would benefit more from research attention than abstract consciousness debates.

The editorial argues that focusing on measurable, actionable safety measures is more productive than speculating about AI inner states that cannot be definitively evaluated with current technology.
