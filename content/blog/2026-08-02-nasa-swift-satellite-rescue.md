# NASA Swift Satellite Rescue: Engineers Reveal Plan to Save the spacecraft That Was Sent to Study Gamma-Ray Bursts

---

## Background: NASA's Swift Observatory Faces Critical Threat

NASA's Swift satellite — launched in 2004 specifically to study gamma-ray bursts — is in trouble. The spacecraft, which has exceeded its original 2-year mission lifetime by more than a decade, is experiencing a critical failure in its remaining reaction wheel, the device that allows it to point at distant cosmic events. Without functional reaction wheels, Swift cannot orient itself to observe gamma-ray bursts, effectively rendering it unable to carry out its core scientific mission.

Engineers believe that a capture of Swift — an attempted capture of Swift — is very much in the cards, according to internal NASA planning documents and statements from agency officials. The rescue mission would involve sending a spacecraft to physically intercept and stabilize Swift before it loses all attitude control capability.

---

## The Technical Challenge

Swift currently has only one remaining operational reaction wheel. When that wheel fails — which engineers estimate could happen within months — the satellite will no longer be able to maintain its orientation toward gamma-ray burst sources. The satellite's other systems remain operational, but without attitude control, it becomes a tumbling liability rather than a productive scientific instrument.

The rescue plan reportedly involves either docking with the satellite to provide attitude control thruster assistance, or potentially using a robotic arm to capture the spacecraft and stabilize it. NASA engineers are reportedly studying both options, with a decision expected within the coming weeks.

---

## Scientific Stakes

Swift has been instrumental in gamma-ray burst research for over two decades. The satellite has observed more than 1,500 gamma-ray bursts and has contributed to numerous major discoveries about these energetic cosmic events, including the first observations of gamma-ray bursts associated with gravitational wave detections. Its loss would leave a significant gap in NASA's ability to rapidly respond to and study these high-energy phenomena.

---

## Next Steps

NASA is reportedly accelerating its rescue planning timeline in light of the reaction wheel degradation. The agency is evaluating potential spacecraft that could be repurposed for the rescue mission, including commercial spacecraft currently in development. A formal rescue mission proposal is expected to be presented to NASA leadership within the next 30 days.
