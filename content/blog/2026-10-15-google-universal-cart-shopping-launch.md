Google on Thursday said "Universal Cart," a tool that lets users collect and monitor products they find across search and in the Gemini app, is now available to U.S. users. The company first previewed the tool at Google I/O in May. The idea is to make online shopping easier to manage. Find something you like, save it to your cart, and move on. When you return later, your items and shopping progress are still there, so you don't have to hunt down the same products or start over.

Universal Cart works across Google's ecosystem, pulling products from regular Google Search results and integrating them with the Gemini app's shopping capabilities. Users can add items from any website that Google indexes, not just participating retailers, making it more flexible than Amazon's wish lists or retailer-specific saved items.

The underlying technology relies on Google's product knowledge graph, which catalogs hundreds of millions of products and their attributes. When a user saves an item, Google extracts key product details — brand, model, price, specifications — and stores them in a standardized format that persists even if the original product page goes offline or changes its URL.

---

## AI-Powered Shopping Intelligence

What distinguishes Universal Cart from earlier Google shopping tools is its integration with Gemini's AI capabilities. The cart can intelligently group similar products, surface related recommendations based on browsing history, and alert users when prices drop on saved items. The AI also helps disambiguate products that share similar names but have different specifications.

For example, if a user saves a camera lens, Gemini can distinguish between different mount types, filter sizes, and focal lengths even when product titles use inconsistent terminology. This semantic understanding builds on Google's long-standing investment in product graph technology, enhanced with the language understanding capabilities of Gemini.

The cart also serves as a signal for Google's shopping graph. When users save items, Google learns which products attract interest but don't convert to purchases — valuable data for ranking search results and shaping product recommendations. This feedback loop helps Google refine its understanding of product relevance and price sensitivity across different categories.

## Competition and Market Context

Universal Cart positions Google more directly against Amazon's saved-item ecosystem and Pinterest's visual shopping features. Unlike Amazon's static wish lists, Universal Cart leverages Google's search breadth — users can save products from any indexed website, not just Amazon's marketplace. Against Pinterest, Google's cart benefits from integration with search intent, capturing users earlier in their shopping journey when they're still comparing options.

The timing reflects a broader push by Google to capture more of the e-commerce value chain. Google's advertising revenue depends heavily on product search queries, and better shopping tools keep users within Google's ecosystem longer. Universal Cart extends this by creating a persistent shopping context that survives across search sessions and surfaces products when users are ready to buy.

Retailers have mixed feelings about the launch. On one hand, Universal Cart lowers friction for purchasing by making any indexed product saveable. On the other hand, it reinforces Google's role as the intermediary between shoppers and retailers, potentially reducing direct brand relationships.
