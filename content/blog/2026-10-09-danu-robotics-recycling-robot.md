# Danu Robotics' fight to build a better recycling robot

---

For Danu Robotics founder Amy Ma, the eureka moment came after years working as a software developer at a London bank. Despite the high-touch office environment with all its perks, Ma was alarmed to realize that the building's recycling bins were being emptied into the trash. "Even though they have tons of money, there's all these different recycling bins that just end up in the same place," Ma told TechCrunch.

The more she investigated, the deeper the problem became. Recycling contamination — when non-recyclable materials mix with recyclables — is one of the leading causes of recycling facility failures worldwide. Material recovery facilities often report contamination rates of 20-30%, meaning significant portions of what communities carefully sort gets diverted to landfill.

Danu Robotics is building computer vision and robotic manipulation systems specifically designed for recycling sorting. Unlike industrial manufacturing robots that operate in controlled environments, recycling robots must handle highly variable inputs — different types of bottles, cans,纸张, packaging materials — often moving on conveyor belts at high speed.

The company's approach uses a combination of multi-spectral imaging (to identify material types beyond what visible light reveals), imitation learning (training from human sorters), and adaptive grasping (different grippers for different material types). Danu's robots are designed to work alongside human sorters rather than replace them.

The article notes that the recycling robotics space has seen increased investment as jurisdictions worldwide implement extended producer responsibility laws, making brands financially responsible for packaging end-of-life management. This creates direct financial incentives for improving recycling quality and yield.

Danu is part of a broader wave of AI and robotics startups targeting physical world automation tasks that have historically been resistant to automation due to their variability and complexity.
