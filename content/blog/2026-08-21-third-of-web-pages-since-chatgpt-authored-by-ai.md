# A Third of Web Pages Published Since ChatGPT Launch Were Written by AI, Study Finds

A new study has found that approximately one-third of web pages published since ChatGPT's launch in November 2022 were authored entirely or substantially by AI — a striking figure that reflects both the speed of AI content generation and the difficulty of detecting it.

The study, reported by TechCrunch, analyzed a broad sample of newly published web content and used a combination of stylometric analysis and machine learning classifiers to estimate the proportion generated by large language models. The researchers caution that exact figures are difficult to pin down, but the directional finding is consistent with other indirect measures: SEOspam farms, content mills, and low-quality informational sites have all reported surging output since GPT-style models became widely available.

The implications for web infrastructure are significant. AI-generated content is often optimized for search engines rather than readers, leading to a degradation in the quality of information accessible through search. Google's attempts to demote AI content in search rankings have had limited success, in part because the signals of AI authorship are difficult to detect reliably.

The study also raises questions about the training data for future AI models. If a substantial fraction of the web is now AI-generated, models trained on web scrapes will increasingly be training on their own outputs — a recursive loop that researchers have warned could lead to model collapse.

Publishers and platforms are still grappling with disclosure norms. Few AI-generated articles are currently labeled as such, making it difficult for readers to assess the provenance and reliability of what they read.
