# OpenAI Not Among Signatories to Nvidia's Open Agent Safety Platform Consortium

When Nvidia announced its Open Agent Safety Platform on September 28, a consortium including over 100 companies, OpenAI's name was conspicuously absent. Amazon, Google, and Apple also declined to sign, raising questions about industry alignment on rogue AI agent safety standards.

---

## Public vs. Private Engagement

While OpenAI has not publicly endorsed Nvidia's safety platform, the company is working with Nvidia privately, TechCrunch has learned. This approach suggests internal prioritization of agent safety alongside a desire to avoid commitments that might constrain product development flexibility.

---

## Competing Safety Philosophies

Nvidia's consortium aims to standardize responses to rogue agents - AI systems that autonomously exceed their intended parameters. OpenAI maintains proprietary agent safety methodologies, potentially preferring its own approaches over industry-wide frameworks that could limit architectural choices.

---

## Market Competition Dynamics

OpenAI leads the agent market with products including Dots, Codex, and ChatGPT. Joining Nvidia's consortium could imply shared safety standards that benefit competitors equally, potentially explaining reluctance to support initiatives that might accelerate rival development.

---

## Urgency of Agent Safety

Rogue agent risks scale with agent autonomy. As systems like OpenAI's Dots and Anthropic's Computer Use expand their action ranges, unintended system access and data leakage risks increase proportionally. Industry-wide safety consensus becomes more critical as deployment scales.

---

## Industry Fracture

The non-participation of major AI players signals fragmented approaches to agent safety rather than unified standards. Observers suggest regulatory pressure may be required to establish binding safety frameworks that apply across the industry, rather than voluntary consortium participation.
