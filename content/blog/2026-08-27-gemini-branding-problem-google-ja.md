# Google's Gemini Has a Branding Problem, and So Does the Rest of AI

Google's recent Gemini announcements highlight a broader challenge facing AI companies: the proliferation of product brands that confuse consumers rather than clarify them. In the Gemini app, users can switch between chat, Spark, and Daily Brief - three separate features, each with its own icon and place in navigation.

The issue Google highlights is that users "shouldn't have to guess whether a task requires Spark, a Daily Brief, or a quick inbox search." Yet by giving each feature distinct branding, Google has created exactly that situation. Daily Brief offers "proactive, personalized updates" using data from Gmail and Calendar, but the actual capability falls short of the promise.

This branding fragmentation is not unique to Google. Across the AI industry, companies have launched multiple products with overlapping functionality, creating a maze of names and capabilities that users must navigate. The result is cognitive overhead that undermines the convenience AI is supposed to provide.

---

## The Killer App Problem

The proliferation of brands may also signal that AI companies are still searching for a "killer app" - a single, compelling use case that drives mass adoption. When companies are not certain which feature will resonate, they launch multiple products and let the market decide. This scatter-shot approach creates user experience challenges.

Google's situation is particularly complex because its various AI features have evolved organically from different product teams. Consolidating them under a unified brand requires organizational alignment that may be difficult to achieve.

---

## Implications for AI Adoption

Consumer AI adoption depends partly on ease of use. When users must understand the differences between multiple products to use them effectively, adoption slows. Google's recognition of this problem suggests the industry may be entering a consolidation phase, where overlapping products are rationalized and user experience improves.
