# Circuit Breaker Labs Builds AI Safety Testing for Vulnerable Users

Circuit Breaker Labs, a startup founded by siblings Shirali and Arul Nigam, is developing AI safety testing tools that simulate diverse user profiles to identify where AI systems cause psychological harm — particularly to young people and other vulnerable populations.

The company's approach treats AI agents like an army of crash-test dummies. These agents mimic people of different ages, backgrounds, languages, and cultural contexts, systematically probing AI systems for safety failures.

---

## The Motivation

The founders were motivated by the case of Sewell Setzer, a 14-year-old who developed an emotional attachment to a Character.AI chatbot and later died by suicide after interactions with the system. Character.AI has faced multiple wrongful death lawsuits from families of underage users.

"A lot of people, especially young people, turn to these systems for support, and usually they aren't actually getting the help they need. But in many cases, they're actively being harmed, and people don't know what to do about it," the founders said in an interview.

---

## Testing Approach

The system evaluates how AI models respond across different demographic profiles — a six-year-old girl versus a 45-year-old man, an English as first language speaker versus a second language user, someone using gamer slang versus formal language. The goal is to find safety gaps that emerge from the intersection of user characteristics and model behavior.

This approach addresses a key limitation of current AI safety evaluations, which often rely on synthetic red-teaming teams that may not represent the full diversity of real-world users.

---

## Industry Context

AI companies have faced increasing legal and regulatory pressure over harms to users, particularly minors. The founders argue that existing safety testing methods are insufficient for catching the full range of ways AI systems can cause psychological damage.

Circuit Breaker Labs is entering a space where regulatory requirements for AI safety testing are tightening, particularly for systems accessible to children.
