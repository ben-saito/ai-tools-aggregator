# Google Buys 100 Million Spirit Airlines Employee Emails for AI Training, Flight Attendants Object

Google has won an auction to purchase approximately 100 million employee emails and workplace records from bankrupt Spirit Airlines for $10 million. The data includes decades of employee communications, HR information, payroll data, and productivity measurements. Google says it will use the data to improve its AI models after de-identification by a third-party service.

---

## The Deal

The data sale comes after Spirit Airlines went bankrupt and auctioned off a massive dataset. Google placed the opening bid at $5 million, covering the cost of third-party scrubbing from the start. Competing bidder Mercor Corporation tried to avoid third-party de-identification terms, offering to scrub the data itself—Google's bid of $10 million plus third-party scrubbing won the auction.

The dataset includes Spirit computer programs, applications, code, and worker data spanning decades. Google's privacy commitments cover consumers but not former Spirit workers, who face potential re-identification risks even after de-identification.

---

## Privacy Concerns

The Association of Flight Attendants (AFA) filed an objection, arguing that the sale's privacy protections are "consumer-facing" while the "payload is disproportionately employee-facing." The AFA wrote: "Nearly every consumer-facing category is designated 'Not Included.' Nearly every category under 'Team Member'—time card information, employee data, corporate and crew training records, payroll records, employee documents—is designated 'Included.'"

Electronic Frontier Foundation attorney Adam Schwartz told Ars: "EFF opposes using a person's data for a new purpose without first getting their consent, which does not happen when a bankrupt company sells its employees' emails to become AI training data."

---

## Google's Position

A Google spokesperson said: "We acquired part of an enterprise dataset from Spirit Airlines, which can be helpful in improving our products and AI models. We will not receive any personal information from this dataset. Any data we receive will be rigorously scrubbed of any personally identifiable information by a third party before receipt."

The court is expected to rule on the sale in September. Flight attendants have asked the court to require Google to extend the same privacy protections to workers that it provides to consumers.
