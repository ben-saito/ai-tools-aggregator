# Norway Moves to Temporarily Ban AI Glasses That Can Record Bystanders Without Consent

Norway's center-left government announced on Monday, October 5, 2026, that it will introduce legislation to parliament for a temporary ban on AI-powered smart glasses in public spaces where bystanders can be photographed, filmed, or recorded without their knowledge. The ban targets devices capable of capturing audio and video of people who have not consented to being recorded.

---

## A Growing List of Concerns

Norway's Minister of Digital Affairs said the government is "concerned about the introduction of powerful new technology where people risk being photographed, filmed, or recorded without their knowledge." The legislation aims to create a framework for permanent rules after studying the impact of these devices on privacy and public safety.

The list of institutions already taking action is growing. Schools in Oslo and Norway's largest company, the state-owned oil giant Equinor, have already implemented their own bans on smart glasses in certain settings. Australia's government is considering restrictions in specific public spaces, and courtrooms in several countries have issued bans as the use of wearable AI devices has increased.

---

## Meta Ray-Bans and Copycat Devices in the Regulatory Spotlight

The law, if passed, would primarily affect devices like Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses and cheaper copycat products that can capture video, audio, and in some cases perform real-time AI analysis of the wearer's surroundings. These devices have become increasingly popular, but regulators worldwide are struggling to keep pace with the rapid advancement of AI-powered wearable technology.

The Norwegian government said it does not want to propose a total ban on smart glasses, but instead aims to restrict use in situations where there is a risk of filming people without consent. The temporary ban would remain in effect while the government develops permanent regulations.

---

## Global Regulatory Landscape

Norway is not alone in grappling with how to regulate AI glasses. Governments around the world are trying to figure out how to balance the benefits of AI-powered wearable technology with the privacy rights of ordinary citizens who may be recorded without knowing it.

Courtrooms in several countries have already banned the use of smart glasses, and a growing number of private businesses are implementing their own restrictions. The challenge for regulators is that the technology is evolving faster than the legal frameworks designed to govern it.

---

## What Comes Next

The Norwegian government's proposed law is expected to move quickly through parliament. If passed, it would represent one of the most comprehensive national-level responses to AI smart glasses to date, potentially setting a precedent for other countries considering similar measures.

The temporary ban would give regulators time to study the impact of these devices and develop permanent rules that balance innovation with privacy protection.
