# Trump Administration Using AI to Deny Medical Care for Seniors in Disastrous Experiment

---

## Healthcare AI System Demonstrates Structural Incentives to Reject Claims; Vulnerable Population Bears the Cost

The Trump administration has deployed artificial intelligence systems in the evaluation of healthcare claims for seniors, with evidence emerging that the technology systematically denies care to elderly patients in what critics are describing as a disastrous experiment in automated governance. Vendors implementing the AI systems have, according to analysts, "an incentive to deny as many claims as possible" — creating a structural misalignment between the technology's optimization targets and patient wellbeing.

The deployment represents a significant expansion of algorithmic decision-making into sensitive government services affecting some of America's most vulnerable citizens. Unlike private-sector AI deployments that can be evaluated through market mechanisms, healthcare decisions made by government AI systems carry direct life-and-death consequences that are difficult to appeal or overturn.

Healthcare advocacy groups have raised alarms about the lack of transparency in how these AI systems evaluate claims, what training data they use, and how they handle edge cases involving patients with complex medical histories. Unlike human reviewers, AI systems cannot explain their reasoning in clinically meaningful terms, making it nearly impossible for patients or their advocates to understand why a claim was denied or what evidence might support an appeal.

---

## Structural Problems With AI in Government Healthcare Decisions

The deployment highlights broader questions about the appropriateness of AI systems for high-stakes government decisions affecting individual rights and wellbeing. Unlike AI applications in commercial settings, where errors can be corrected through market competition or regulatory intervention, government AI decisions often lack robust appeal mechanisms and operate at scales that make individual review impractical.

The structural incentive problem — where vendors profit more from denying claims than approving them — represents a fundamental misalignment that technical safeguards alone cannot resolve. Policy experts have called for stronger oversight mechanisms, mandatory transparency in AI decision-making, and explicit requirements that human reviewers examine any AI-recommended denials before they take effect.
