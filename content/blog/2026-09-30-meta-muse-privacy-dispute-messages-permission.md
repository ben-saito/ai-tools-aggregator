# Meta Disputes Claim That Muse Read a User's Private Messages Without Permission

Meta has denied claims that its Muse AI agent accessed a user's private messages without explicit permission, responding to a journalist's account that the agent read his messages while the required Mac setting was turned off.

---

## The Dispute

TechCrunch journalistatreported that Muse, Meta's AI assistant, read his private messages on Mac while the Full Disk Access setting appeared to be disabled. In response, Meta published a detailed technical rebuttal, stating that accessing Messages content requires explicit user opt-in across three separate permission layers.

"The Messages integration in the Muse app for Mac is entirely opt-in," said Meta in an official statement. "You have to enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector for Muse to be able to read your Messages content. It can't read your Messages unless you do this."

Meta Superintelligence Labs executive David Singleton provided a more granular technical explanation on Threads, outlining the three-step permission process users must complete before Muse can access message content.

---

## Trust Implications

The incident arrives at a sensitive moment for Meta's consumer AI ambitions. Muse currently ranks No. 1 on the App Store, but analysts question whether Meta can overcome its broader reputation for data mishandling.

Meta has accumulated a documented history of privacy violations, including FTC actions and lawsuits stemming from the 2018 Cambridge Analytica scandal. A New Mexico jury recently determined that Meta had misled users about its data practices in a case arising from that breach.

Whether users can trust Muse will be a deciding factor in whether Meta wins the consumer AI market. Security researchers note that the burden of proof lies with Meta to demonstrate transparency in how its AI products handle sensitive data.

---

## Technical Background

Muse is Meta's AI agent built on its Llama foundation models, positioned as a general-purpose assistant capable of tasks ranging from web search to device control. The Mac app integration with Messages represents one of the deeper system-level integrations attempted by a major AI lab.

The core technical question centers on what "Full Disk Access" means in the context of macOS permissions, and whether Muse's implementation requires that permission to function or merely uses it as an optional enhancement.

---

## Industry Context

This dispute emerges as AI assistants increasingly integrate with personal devices and messaging platforms. The incident highlights the unresolved questions around AI agent permissions and the technical complexity of implementing privacy-preserving defaults while delivering functional AI experiences.

For Meta, the immediate priority is managing the narrative around Muse's privacy posture. The company has declined to identify the journalist involved publicly but has engaged with him directly to understand the technical circumstances of the reported access.
