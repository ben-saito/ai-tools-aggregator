# Amazon Drops NDAs with Government Agencies as Data Center Backlash Intensifies

Amazon Web Services CEO Garman has responded to growing community opposition to data center construction across the United States, announcing that Amazon no longer uses nondisclosure agreements with government agencies working on data center projects. The announcement comes as more than 100 data center moratoriums are under consideration nationwide, with New York already implementing a one-year permit freeze.

---

## The NDA Admission

Garman's statement, buried in a longer blog post defending data centers, marks a notable shift in Amazon's approach to community opposition. "We no longer use nondisclosure agreements with the government agencies we work with on our projects," Garman wrote, a departure from the opacity that environmental activists have long criticized.

Environmental activist Erin Brockovich recently testified that the top complaint she hears about data center projects is lack of transparency, with developments frequently announced only after permits have already been approved.

---

## The Moratorium Wave

The backlash has produced concrete regulatory results. New York state enacted a one-year moratorium on permits for large data centers, and Garman estimates more than 100 similar moratoriums are currently being considered across the country. His message to regulators was blunt: "If these measures are enacted, the U.S. could be writing its own losing ticket to this race, and the consequences would last generations."

The AI industry has made similar arguments at the highest levels. At a recent White House summit, nearly every major tech CEO signed an AI safety pledge that included commitments on data center development practices.

---

## Garman's Four Myths

The AWS CEO devoted most of his blog post to refuting what he called four common objections to data centers: water consumption, electricity rate increases, pollution, and lack of community benefit.

On water, Garman cited Amazon's own report showing that direct data center water consumption accounts for only 0.5% of U.S. industrial water usage — "orders of magnitude less than golf courses, almond farming, and many other industries." He noted that Nvidia recently announced a new cooling system that eliminates nearly all water usage inside the data center itself.

On electricity, Garman acknowledged that rates have risen in some states with heavy data center concentration, but argued that aging grid infrastructure is the primary cause rather than the facilities themselves. This contrasts with findings from an independent watchdog that identified data centers as the main driver of a 76% year-over-year price increase on America's largest electrical grid.

Regarding pollution, Garman pushed back on critics who focus on maximum permitted emission levels. He noted that data center backup generators "almost never run" — approximately 10 hours per year for required maintenance testing — and that the industry has been targeted in an NAACP lawsuit over SpaceX and xAI's facility emissions.

---

## A Crisis of Trust

Garman's defensiveness reflects a broader challenge facing the AI industry. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei recently characterized the data center opposition as "fundamentally a crisis of trust," arguing that communities assume governments and tech companies are "always cooking up some new way to screw them over." Whether Amazon's NDA abandonment and $1 billion in community contributions will be enough to rebuild that trust remains to be seen.

Amazon's next test will be whether the company's words translate into changed outcomes on the ground — or whether communities remain skeptical of promises made by an industry whose physical footprint is rapidly expanding across the American landscape.
