# Keenable: Ex-Yandex Search Lead Builds Web Index for AI Agents

Andrey Styskin, who previously led search, AI, and cloud at Russian search giant Yandex, and German AI researcher Matthias Petri are tackling a problem that AI developers face daily: finding relevant information at web scale to ground AI agent responses. Their new startup, Keenable, emerged from stealth on August 25, 2026 with a $26 million seed round led by Accel.

---

## The Search Gap for AI Agents

Current AI chatbots perform significantly better when they can ground their responses in source documents. But the search infrastructure that powers this -- largely built for human users -- is not optimized for AI agent workflows. Google and Microsoft, the dominant search providers, are both building their own AI systems and have shown limited interest in opening search APIs to competitors.

"This actually creates a new flywheel that is different from what Google learned," Styskin said of the opportunity. At Amazon, he worked on search infrastructure for Alexa and saw data from Cloudflare showing AI traffic to websites had surged dramatically.

---

## 100 Billion Document Index for AI

Keenable says it has been building a web search index of more than 100 billion documents. Its API is already in production use at several AI labs and inference providers, serving both training and inference-time grounding needs. The startup is also building proprietary retrieval capabilities, including an upcoming reranking model to improve result quality.

According to Accel partner Zhenya Loginov, "AI players have very few options when it comes to web-scale search infrastructure." Other players in the space include Brave's Leo browser and Exa, but Styskin believes the market is large enough to support multiple entrants.

---

## Cost and Competition

The biggest challenge is cost. "Don't ask -- it is painfully expensive," Styskin said of building a web-scale index. But with a $26 million seed round, Keenable is pacing itself for the long haul. The company plans to open its API to general availability later this year.

The long-term goal is becoming "the next Google for AI agents," though Styskin acknowledges the path there requires proving the product works at scale before challenging the incumbent.
