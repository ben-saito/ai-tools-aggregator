# People Really Hate AI, So Why Can't They Get Enough?

A new analysis from MIT Technology Review published on October 5, 2026, explores one of the central paradoxes of the current AI era: public sentiment toward AI is souring fast, yet usage of AI products and services continues to skyrocket. The contradiction is becoming impossible to ignore.

---

## The Love-Hate Relationship

According to the Pew Research Center, more adults in the United States now think AI will have a negative impact on them personally and on society in general than think it will be positive. More than half of people worldwide say AI products and services make them nervous, according to a Stanford University report. In a May Gallup poll, 71% of US adults said they would oppose certain applications of AI in daily life.

And yet the numbers tell a different story. ChatGPT reached a billion monthly users in May 2026, according to market analysis firm Sensor Tower. Google DeepMind's Gemini is close behind in usage metrics. According to Pew, half of US adults now say they use a chatbot -- more than twice the number who said so in 2023. One in four say they use one every day. The adoption curves look like those of the most successful consumer products in history.

---

## The Self-Loathing AI User

The CEO of Springboards, a startup building an LLM designed to produce a wider variety of responses than mainstream rivals, put it simply in a recent interview: "We often say that we're a self-loathing AI." The phrase captures something real about the current moment. Users simultaneously recognize the problems with AI -- the hype, the manipulation, the privacy concerns -- and continue to use it anyway.

Researchers studying public attitudes toward AI have found that the love-hate dynamic may be more accurately understood as a single population rather than two distinct groups. The same person who worries about AI's societal impact may still use it for work, research, and communication several times a day.

---

## What the Data Actually Shows

The Stanford AI Index and multiple independent surveys paint a consistent picture. Global AI usage continues to climb even as trust in AI companies and products erodes. The people most enthusiastic about AI adoption are often the same people most vocal about its risks.

The gap between stated attitudes and actual behavior may reflect several things: the genuine utility of AI tools, the lack of viable alternatives, social and professional pressure to adopt new technology, and a form of resignation that the technology is going to spread regardless of personal preferences.

---

## What Comes Next

The contradiction shows no signs of resolving. AI companies continue to ship new features and expand capabilities, while regulators and researchers call for slower deployment and more careful consideration of societal impacts. For now, the data suggests that the world will keep using AI even as it keeps complaining about it.
