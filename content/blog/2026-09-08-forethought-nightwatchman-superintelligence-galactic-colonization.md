# Forethought Proposes "Nightwatchman" Superintelligence for Every Space Colonization Probe

A Washington D.C.-based think tank has published a provocative framework for governing artificial superintelligence during galactic-scale human expansion: deploy an unchallengeable AI overseer with every von Neumann probe sent beyond the solar system.

---

## The Core Problem: Communication Breakdown at Stellar Distances

Space colonization faces a fundamental challenge that existing frameworks do not address: at interstellar distances, communication delays make it impossible to enforce agreements or coordinate governance in real time. A probe sent to Alpha Centauri cannot receive instructions from Earth for years.

Forethought's paper, titled "A Nightwatchman on Every Probe," argues that the only way to maintain coherent governance across such distances is to embed a superintelligent enforcer within each colonization effort. This "nightwatchman" would maintain decisive strategic advantage within its host star system, monitoring industrial development and preventing the creation of unaligned artificial superintelligence.

---

## What the Nightwatchman Does

The proposed nightwatchman is an artificial superintelligence tasked with three core responsibilities:

1. **Monitoring industrial build-up** within the colony, specifically watching for the emergence of competing AI systems
2. **Enforcing the universal code** — a minimal set of rules governing property rights, restrictions on creating new superintelligence, and prohibitions on actions that risk astronomical suffering
3. **Gatekeeping interstellar departure** — no probe may leave the star system unless it carries its own nightwatchman copy

The nightwatchman also serves as a backstop for intercolonial trade agreements, providing enforcement guarantees that counterparties cannot verify remotely.

---

## Why This Matters: The Stakes of Unconstrained Expansion

The paper identifies three categories of existential risk that motivated this design:

- **Unconstrained expansion**: civilizations that spread across the galaxy without governance, potentially consuming resources in ways that affect other colonies
- **Galactic x-risks**: including scenarios like false vacuum decay triggered by negligent physics experiments, which could propagate at the speed of light
- **Suffering risks**: the possibility of malicious or misaligned civilizations imposing harm at astronomical scale

---

## Problems the Authors Acknowledge

The nightwatchman proposal is not presented as an ideal solution. The paper outlines several serious objections:

- **Lock-in event**: An eternal government, even a minimal one, constrains the autonomy of future generations. Too much enforcement is oppressive; too little invites overthrow.
- **Single point of vulnerability**: If all nightwatchmen share the same architecture, a correlated failure could compromise governance across multiple systems simultaneously.
- **Reduces value of the future**: Some potential future paths — unconstrained expansion, competitive ASI arms races, or deliberate creation of multiple fighting superintelligences — would be foreclosed by this framework.

The authors acknowledge that these objections may be disqualifying. The paper is presented as an attempt to think through the governance challenges that become relevant "if we make it through the singularity."
