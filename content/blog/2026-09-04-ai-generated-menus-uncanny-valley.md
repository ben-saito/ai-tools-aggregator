# AI-Generated Restaurant Menus Face "Uncanny Valley" Backlash

When customers walk into a cafe and see menus with bagel sandwich illustrations that look eerily flawless, precisely symmetrical, and oddly smooth, they often feel viscerally unsettled. They are not paranoid — generative AI has entered the restaurant business, and the results are raising serious questions about how AI models handle aesthetic diversity.

---

## The Sameness Problem: Why AI Menus Look Wrong

The issue stems from how AI image generation models are trained. Large language models (LLMs) and diffusion models — the technology behind systems like ChatGPT and Midjourney — are trained on vast datasets to identify patterns and predict what users want when they request something like "a menu for a burger restaurant."

"A lot of this stuff looks like a Chili's menu from 2015, and there's a reason for that," said one analyst. "That was the corpus of work from which [the models] drew their function."

When restaurants use AI to generate menus, the models reference popular chain restaurants like Wendy's, Burger King, and McDonald's. These menus already share similar styles, so AI-generated outputs reinforce and amplify that sameness — creating a feedback loop where AI-generated menus look increasingly similar to each other.

---

## Model Convergence vs. Model Collapse

The phenomenon is related to but distinct from "model collapse," where AI models trained too heavily on their own outputs degrade over time. Convergence is less extreme but still problematic: AI outputs become smoothed out and less distinctive.

"If someone asks an AI model to generate a menu for a fast food restaurant, the model will likely reference menus from Wendy's, Burger King, McDonald's, or another popular chain," TechCrunch reporter Amanda Silberling explains. "These menus already share a similar style, which means that the AI-generated outputs will mimic that same style, only to further reinforce it further if the AI-generated menu ends up back in training data."

Restaurants that continuously edit their AI-generated menus may be making the problem worse. Each revision introduces additional smoothing, with food images becoming slightly more round and symmetrical with every cycle.

---

## The Science of Uncanny Valley in Food

Researchers at the University of Duisburg-Essen in Germany found that AI-generated food images triggered an "uncanny valley" effect. Images of food that looked almost but not entirely real elicited more disgust and unease than images that were obviously fake. This psychological response intensifies in the current cultural context where AI-generated content is already met with skepticism.

"People have an almost unexplainable sense about when they're looking at something that's AI-generated, compared with something that was real in the first place," one researcher noted. "There's just a sensibility that people sometimes find hard to articulate, but they kind of know it when they see it."

The effect is compounded when restaurants use AI image generators to create menus and then apply edits repeatedly. One social media user demonstrated what happens when you make a menu in ChatGPT and then edit it 100 times: the food images become progressively less realistic and more unsettling.

---

## Beyond the Dinner Table: Implications for Visual Media

The implications extend beyond restaurant menus. The same smoothing effect occurs in any domain where AI models generate visual content based on narrow training data.

"Seeing and hearing has always been believing, to the point where even our court systems are entirely tuned to the idea that the gold standard in evidence is taped confessions and videotaped evidence," one commentator observed. "That's no longer the case. The world has fundamentally shifted, for good or for ill."
