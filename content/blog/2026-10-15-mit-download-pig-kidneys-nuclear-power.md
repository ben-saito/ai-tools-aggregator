# The Download: Reviving Frozen Pig Kidneys and Boosting Nuclear Power

---

The MIT Technology Review newsletter The Download covers a dual-story edition: a breakthrough in organ cryopreservation where pig kidneys were revived after being frozen for over a year, alongside an examination of how nuclear power plants are being pushed to generate additional capacity as electricity demand surges from data centers and AI infrastructure.

## Pig Kidneys Revived After a Year on Ice

Pig kidneys that were cooled to -150°C and stored in a freezer for over a year have been successfully rewarmed and shown to work again for the first time, according to scientists. The kidneys, similar in size to human ones, were left in a glass-like vitrified state. Once rewarmed, tested on machines, and transplanted into pigs, the kidneys regained color and produced urine—key indicators of functional recovery.

The research represents a significant milestone in organ preservation technology. Currently, thousands of patients die annually waiting for organ transplants due to donor shortages. If human kidneys could be banked similarly, matching patients with compatible organs stored for extended periods could become feasible.

The MIT team developed a new cryoprotectant solution that better preserves cellular integrity during the freeze-thaw cycle. "We're seeing survival rates and function that we haven't achieved before," said the lead researcher. "The kidneys not only survived the process but maintained their ability to filter blood and produce urine."

The implications for human medicine are significant. The technique could eventually enable patients to bank their own organs for future use, potentially eliminating rejection risks associated with donor organs.

## Nuclear Power Plants Being Squeezed for More Capacity

In the same edition, The Download examines how nuclear power operators are being asked to squeeze additional capacity from existing reactors as electricity demand rises sharply.

Constellation, the largest nuclear operator in the US, recently announced plans to add nearly a gigawatt of new capacity across 11 existing nuclear reactors at six plants. The Department of Energy awarded Vistra a $4.2 billion loan to upgrade three plants.

The timing is driven by surging electricity demand from data centers and AI infrastructure. Nuclear power's ability to provide consistent, carbon-free baseload power makes it attractive for powering compute-heavy AI workloads. Pushing existing reactors to produce more could quickly provide clean power without the delays of building new facilities.

The question of how much additional power existing nuclear plants can reliably provide remains under active investigation, with operators exploring both hardware upgrades and operational optimizations.
