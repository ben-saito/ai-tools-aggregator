# Researchers Publish Framework for Deliberate AI Pacing

A multidisciplinary team has published a research agenda outlining how the world might deliberately pace AI development, moving beyond improvised reactions toward systematic, targeted interventions.

---

## The Case for Pacing Research

The paper, authored by researchers from ACS Research, University of Toronto, Wharton School, Harvard, Cambridge, and others, argues that recent enthusiasm for AI slowdowns has outpaced rigorous research into how pacing might actually work.

"The world will pace progress one way or another," the authors write. "Absent better tools, it might do so haphazardly, through improvised reactions overfit to prior expectations, in ways that fail to actually address risks."

The practical challenge is significant: governments face competing incentives around security and citizen welfare, while infrastructure providers -- semiconductor and cloud companies -- have "the greatest incentives to push ahead and the most to lose by pacing."

---

## What Can Be Paced

The researchers identify multiple intervention targets across the AI development pipeline:

**Inputs and infrastructure:** Compute, data, power, capital, researchers.

**Capability development:** Algorithmic R&D, pre-training, post-training.

**Model weights and algorithms:** Non-rival goods that are "extraordinarily difficult to control."

**Use and distribution:** Internal use, deployment, access, and diffusion.

Some targets like compute and researcher time are "rival goods" -- resources whose use by one actor restricts availability for others. These can be monitored or redirected. Model weights, however, are non-rival and essentially impossible to control once released.

---

## Questions for Every Phase

The framework outlines decision points for each stage of pacing:

**Pre-pacing:** What signals of emerging risk are tracked? Who watches and interprets them?

**At trigger:** Who decides a trigger condition has been met? What sequence of actions does triggering set in motion?

**During intervention:** How is compliance observed, verified, and enforced?

**At exit:** How do decision-makers distinguish an intervention that has served its purpose from one that has failed?

---

## Arguments For and Against

**For pacing:** Gives more time to address AI-driven threats in cyber and biosecurity; unpredictable progress means scary developments can emerge suddenly; prevents lose-lose scenarios like governments relinquishing military decisions to AI systems.

**Against pacing:** Delays benefits in healthcare and science; increases concentration of power; may create false security and capability overhangs; pacing early can be less efficient than pacing late when AI assists with surgical interventions.
