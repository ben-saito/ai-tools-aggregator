# Toby Ord Models the Limits of Recursive Self-Improvement

Philosopher and AI researcher Toby Ord has published an analysis of how recursive self-improvement (RSI) might unfold in practice, arguing that resource and time constraints will ultimately limit the pace of an intelligence explosion.

---

## The Four Phases of an Intelligence Explosion

Ord outlines four distinct phases of RSI-driven growth:

**Phase 0:** Initial exponential growth driven by human-only research.

**Phase 1:** RSI drives generation time down toward machine speeds. Growth becomes super-exponential as AI systems begin improving themselves.

**Phase 2:** Fully automated RSI continues at the faster exponential rate but begins to saturate as limits are approached.

**Phase 3:** The system approaches an inflection point, revealing a logistic rather than exponential trajectory as absolute limits constrain further improvement.

"The generation time for training the next generation of models would bottom out at some unyielding finite limit," Ord writes. "This provides an important kind of barrier to singular growth."

---

## Five Categories of Limits

Ord identifies multiple hard limits on RSI:

- **Limits of intelligence itself:** "Even an optimal reasoner would be neither omniscient nor omnipotent."
- **Limits of intelligence per unit resource:** Even optimal hardware and algorithms face physical constraints within our solar system.
- **Limits of hardware:** Even an optimal silicon chip remains far below theoretical physical compute limits.
- **Limits of algorithms:** Even an optimal neural network falls short of maximum achievable intelligence per unit of compute.
- **Limits of training data:** Training data lacks information in many domains, especially non-verbalizable and contextual knowledge.

---

## RSI Weak Links

RSI consists of many distinct components improved across multiple abstractions and timescales, from decades (designing EUV lithography successors) to months (improving pretraining) to seconds (designing better scaffolds). Each presents a potential bottleneck.

"If the human-only trajectory were A(t) and RSI sped this up to A(10t), we would be getting a decade of human-only progress each year," Ord notes, "introducing many of the dangers -- even without any change in the fundamental shape of the curve."
