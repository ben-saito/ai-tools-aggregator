# Meta Launches 'Contributor Pricing' for Muse Spark -- 95% Discount in Exchange for Sharing Prompts and Outputs

Meta on September 3 unveiled an unusual pricing model for its new Muse Spark model: a roughly 95% discount for users who contribute their prompts and model outputs to Meta's training data pool.

Under standard pricing, one million input tokens cost $1.25; under the contributor tier, the same volume costs just $0.10. Output tokens drop from $4.25 per million to $0.20.

---

## A Direct Payment for Training Data

Meta has struggled to gather training data through conventional means. An initiative launched earlier this year to monitor employees' PC usage attracted wide internal criticism and was paused in June. The company declined to comment to TechCrunch on the new pricing model.

For model builders, real-world workflow data is increasingly vital for making agentic tools work better. "The reason we saw a big jump in [coding agent] capabilities between April 2025 and October 2025 was that Claude Code, by default, would store all your coding agent sessions and use them for reinforcement learning training," developer Mario Zechner told TechCrunch.

Princeton professor Arvind Narayanan noted that large companies have shown strong resistance to having their data used for model training. "They stick with token-billed Enterprise plans even though the subscription-based consumer plans like Claude Max and ChatGPT Pro are discounted by 10x-20x or even more," he wrote. "The main difference between the plans is data retention + enterprise IT governance."

Meta's pricing guide frames the contributor tier as lowering barriers for "prototyping, testing integrations, and scaling experiments where training on your data is acceptable."

---

## Competing on Price in the Frontier AI Market

The strategy arrives as Anthropic's newest Fable and Mythos models launched with lowered costs for cached token processing, while OpenAI cut prices on its latest models at the end of July. Meta is essentially paying users -- rather than charging them -- for access to the data that makes agentic AI products more effective.
