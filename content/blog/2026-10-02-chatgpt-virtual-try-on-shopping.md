# ChatGPT Can Now Virtually Try On Clothes

OpenAI is rolling out new shopping features for ChatGPT that let users virtually try on clothing and accessories using their own photos, save products to a Favorites library, and receive personalized recommendations. The feature represents OpenAI's latest push into consumer AI commerce, building on the success of ChatGPT Images 2.5.

---

## Shopping Features Built on ChatGPT Images 2.5

The new shopping features leverage the ChatGPT Images 2.5 model, which OpenAI claims produces more natural lighting and richer textures than previous versions, with improved ability to follow editing instructions. Users can upload photos of themselves and see how garments look on their body type, rather than relying on generic model images.

The Favorites library lets users save products they discover during conversations, creating a persistent shopping list across sessions. The feature integrates with retailer product catalogs through ChatGPT's existing browsing capabilities.

OpenAI has previously explored e-commerce integration, including an earlier experiment with instant checkout that the company ultimately pivoted away from. The virtual try-on approach represents a different strategy focused on discovery and visualization rather than transaction completion.

---

## AI Assistants Compete in Shopping Use Cases

The launch positions ChatGPT as a competitor in the AI shopping assistant space against Amazon's Rufus, Apple's forthcoming AI shopping features, and standalone virtual try-on services. Industry observers note that AI assistants are increasingly being evaluated on practical consumer applications beyond general conversation.

The timing coincides with broader retail interest in AI-powered fit visualization, as return rates from online clothing purchases remain a significant cost driver for retailers. Virtual try-on technology aims to reduce uncertainty before purchase, potentially lowering return frequency.

---

## Technical Implementation

The feature uses multi-modal capabilities combining image generation, body pose estimation, and garment simulation. Unlike static model images that represent a narrow range of body types, the virtual try-on creates personalized visualizations based on user-provided photographs.

OpenAI has implemented content safety measures to prevent the feature from being used to visualize garments on images of people without their consent. The system includes safeguards against misuse for non-shopping purposes.
