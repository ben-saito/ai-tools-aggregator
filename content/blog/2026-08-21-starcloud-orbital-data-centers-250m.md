# Starcloud Raises $250M for Orbital Data Centers as Launch Options Dry Up

There's about to be a big fight to secure access to space — and not just for satellites, but for data centers.

Starcloud, a startup building orbital AI computing infrastructure, has raised $250 million in a funding round to accelerate deployment of its space-based data centers. The company is positioning itself at the intersection of AI compute demand and Earth-based data center constraints like land availability, power grid limitations, and cooling water shortages.

The concept of orbital data centers is not new — companies like Axiom Space and Lockheed Martin have explored space-based computing — but Starcloud appears to be among the first to pursue it specifically for AI inference workloads. The economics remain challenging: launching heavy compute hardware into orbit costs thousands of dollars per kilogram, and servicing or upgrading space-based systems is far more difficult than ground-based infrastructure.

Still, the funding round signals investor appetite for alternative compute sources as AI companies face mounting pressure to secure power and land for large-scale deployments. Several hyperscalers have already committed to nuclear and renewable energy deals to address these constraints; Starcloud is betting that orbit offers a complementary solution for certain workloads.

The timing is notable: SpaceX's launch cadence has made orbital deployment more economically feasible than in previous decades, and advances in radiation-hardened compute hardware have improved the viability of space-based servers. Whether the market for orbital AI compute proves large enough to justify the capital investment remains an open question — but with $250 million in fresh funding, Starcloud is positioned to find out.
