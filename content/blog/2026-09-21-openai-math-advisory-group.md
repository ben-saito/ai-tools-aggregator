# OpenAI Forms Math Advisory Group as AI Resolves 100+ Open Problems

---

## A New Kind of Research Steering

OpenAI has established a dedicated mathematics advisory group as its AI systems increasingly solve open mathematical problems that have stumped human researchers for decades. The group comprises mathematicians and researchers who will provide external perspective on the company's mathematical research direction.

According to OpenAI, the advisory group will not have authority to slow down or redirect research priorities. Instead, their role is purely consultative—offering insights into the implications and applications of breakthrough results as they emerge.

---

## AI's Growing Mathematical Capabilities

The formation of this group coincides with OpenAI's AI systems successfully resolving more than 100 open mathematical problems. These range from longstanding conjectures in number theory to complex problems in algebraic geometry. The company has begun publishing these results in academic venues, with several papers currently under review at major mathematics journals.

The scale of mathematical problem-solving represents a significant milestone in AI reasoning capabilities. Unlike pattern recognition tasks, solving open mathematical problems requires multi-step logical deduction and creative proof construction—capabilities typically considered hallmarks of human mathematical intuition.

---

## Advisory Scope and Limitations

The advisory group's limited authority reflects OpenAI's broader approach to external input on research priorities. Unlike some AI labs that have established external review boards with stop-button authority, OpenAI's mathematics group functions as a consulting body rather than a governance mechanism.

The company has stated that the group will have access to research findings before publication and will provide feedback on mathematical rigor and potential implications. However, final decisions on research direction remain with OpenAI's internal teams.

This structure has drawn criticism from some AI safety researchers who argue that advisory-only models provide insufficient checks on potentially transformative research directions. Proponents counter that the group represents a meaningful step toward incorporating external domain expertise into frontier AI development.
