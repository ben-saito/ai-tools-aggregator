# Ando Stealth Launch: $20M Funding to Build Slack Alternative for Human-Agent Teams

Ando, a new team messaging startup, has emerged from stealth with $20 million in pre-seed and seed funding from investors including Accel, Index Ventures, and Emergence. The company is building what it calls a full replacement for Slack — a messaging platform designed from the ground up for both human workers and AI agents to operate side by side.

---

## The Problem: "Meat Proxies"

The core insight behind Ando is what founder Du (full name not disclosed) calls the "meat proxy" problem. When AI agents complete work, humans often have to relay that work to their teams manually. The human becomes an intermediary, summarizing agent outputs and posting them to channels where actual collaboration happens.

"The human effectively becomes the message carrier," Du explained in the company's stealth announcement. "We wanted to eliminate that bottleneck entirely."

---

## A Messaging Platform Built for Agents

Ando's app gives AI agents their own identities within the platform — complete with inboxes, the ability to browse and join channels, and participation in group conversations. Agents can understand why decisions were made, ask colleagues questions, and contribute their own analysis.

The platform includes:

- **Channels and DMs** — standard messaging features adapted for mixed human-agent teams
- **Live calls** — transcribed and viewable by agents, who can join as participants
- **Agent identities** — each AI agent has its own account, inbox, and presence
- **Agent-native participation** — agents can browse channels, pick which ones to join, and contribute directly to conversations

The goal is a system where agents "participate in the shared conversations where work already gets coordinated," Du said. Agents should understand the context of decisions, not just deliver outputs in isolation.

---

## Competition and Market Context

Ando enters a crowded market. Slack (now part of Salesforce) dominates enterprise messaging, while Microsoft Teams has carved out significant market share. But Du argues that existing platforms were designed for human-only workflows and are poorly suited to the agentic AI era.

The company is positioning itself as infrastructure for the coming wave of AI-augmented workplaces, where agents and humans collaborate natively rather than operating in separate silos.

Funding details were not disclosed beyond the total amount and investor names. The company did not reveal its valuation or specific product availability timeline.
