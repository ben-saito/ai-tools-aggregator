# Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 Brings On-Device 30B MoE AI to Mobile

Qualcomm has unveiled its latest flagship mobile processor, the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6, at its annual Snapdragon Summit. The new chip represents a significant step forward in mobile AI capabilities, featuring dedicated silicon for running large language models directly on-device.

---

## Running a 30-Billion Parameter MoE Model on Your Phone

The flagship feature of the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 is its ability to run a 30-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts (MoE) model locally on the device. This is a substantial leap from the 20-billion-parameter MoE model Apple announced at WWDC in June for its third-generation foundation models.

The Extreme version of the new Snapdragon chip includes a dedicated accelerator element specifically designed to run AI workloads more efficiently. This hardware-level optimization allows the device to handle complex AI tasks without relying on cloud connectivity, addressing both latency and privacy concerns.

Qualcomm says the new chips can aid in improved personalization for AI agents. The sensing hubs integrated into the silicon can run small models of up to 200 million parameters continuously in the background, enabling always-on AI features without significant battery drain.

---

## Camera and Audio Improvements

Beyond AI, the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 delivers notable improvements to traditional mobile computing workloads. The new CPU provides pixel-level control for cameras, facilitating more professional-level photography experiences along with improved stabilization and motion understanding.

For audio, both chips leverage AI to boost vocal clarity and reduce background noise. Qualcomm's new voice bubble technology isolates the user's voice during calls, ensuring clearer communication even in noisy environments.

---

## On-Device AI Acceleration Trend

The announcement reflects a broader industry shift toward running AI models directly on mobile hardware. By processing AI tasks locally, manufacturers can offer faster response times, reduced privacy risks associated with sending data to external servers, and functionality that works without internet connectivity.

Qualcomm's implementation of dedicated AI accelerators marks a competitive response to Apple's neural engine architecture and Google's Tensor chips. The company says the new platform will begin appearing in consumer devices starting in early 2027.
