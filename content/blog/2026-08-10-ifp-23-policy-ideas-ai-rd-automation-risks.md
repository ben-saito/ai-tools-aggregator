# IFP Publishes 23 Policy Ideas to Address Risks of Automated AI R&D

As AI systems grow more capable of automating AI research itself, policymakers face a growing challenge: how do you govern technology that is progressively automating its own development? The Institute for Progress (IFP), a policy think tank, has published a set of 23 actionable recommendations across 7 categories designed to help the United States prepare for increasingly automated AI R&D.

---

## The Core Problem: AI Driving Its Own Development

The IFP report, titled "How Should the US Prepare for Increasingly Automated AI R&D?", identifies recursive self-improvement (RSI) as a structural risk: as AI systems become better at automating research, the pace of AI development accelerates beyond the ability of governments and societies to respond. The report frames the current moment as "driving a car with only an accelerator and no brakes."

The 23 recommendations fall into 7 categories:

- **Transparency** into automated AI R&D
- **State capacity** to understand and respond to automated AI R&D
- **Risk management** that accelerates defensive and commercial AI uses
- **Verification technology** development
- **AI resilience** investment
- **Extending the US AI lead** to buy time for risk management
- **International cooperation** options for managing automated AI R&D risks

---

## Why This Matters: Fewer Options Means Worse Outcomes

The IFP report argues that the fewer options available for dealing with RSI, the worse the long-term outcomes. The current landscape lacks what the report calls "brake pedals" and "telemetry" for AI development: mechanisms to slow down or monitor the trajectory of increasingly autonomous AI research systems.

The recommendations emphasize that accelerating defensive AI uses — systems designed to counter malicious applications — should go hand-in-hand with safety research, ensuring that the US maintains both competitive advantage and societal resilience.
