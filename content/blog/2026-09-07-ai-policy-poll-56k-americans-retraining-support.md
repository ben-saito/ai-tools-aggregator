# Poll of 56,000 Americans Reveals Broad Support for AI Worker Retraining Programs

A comprehensive survey of 56,000 Americans on AI-related policy preferences reveals strong public support for workforce retraining programs and social safety net expansion, while ideas like a US sovereign wealth fund and universal basic income face significant opposition. The polling from the Center for Shared AI Prosperity (CSAIP) provides a map of where the political terrain lies as policymakers grapple with AI's economic disruption.

---

## What Americans Want

The CSAIP polled participants on 79 distinct AI-related policy ideas. The results show Americans broadly supportive of economic policies addressing AI disruption, with job retraining and worker compensation leading in popularity.

**Top policies by support:**
- Expand apprenticeships: +66 net favorability
- Require severance for automated-away jobs: +63 net favorability
- Sector-based job training programs: +60 net favorability

**Bottom policies by support:**
- US sovereign wealth fund: -51 net favorability
- Tax on distributed profits: -33 net favorability
- Universal basic income: -33 net favorability

The pattern suggests Americans favor targeted, workplace-specific interventions over broad wealth redistribution mechanisms when it comes to AI disruption policy.

## The Political Reality

The polling helps identify where organizing efforts will need to focus. "A popular policy is not the same as an effective policy," the CSAIP noted. "This polling helps us understand where the public is already open to a policy idea, and where bold policy ideas will need strong organizing to gain traction."

This distinction matters for AI governance: policies with strong surface appeal may face implementation challenges, while less popular-sounding mechanisms like sectoral collective bargaining might prove more durable.

## Implications for AI Companies

For AI companies navigating an increasingly regulated sector, these results suggest that workforce transition programs will face fewer political obstacles than attempts to reshape broader social insurance systems. Companies exploring AI deployment in vulnerable sectors may find more regulatory runway by supporting retraining initiatives than by advocating for fundamental income reforms.
