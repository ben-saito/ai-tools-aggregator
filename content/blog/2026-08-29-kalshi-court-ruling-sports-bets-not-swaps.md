# Kalshi Ruling: Sports Bets Are Gambling, Not “Swaps” — Appeals Court Confirms

A federal appeals court has upheld a lower court ruling against Kalshi, the derivatives exchange that sought to offer event-based contracts on political outcomes. The three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit ruled that Kalshi's political event contracts are effectively gambling under Nevada law, regardless of how they are labeled.

Kalshi had argued that its contracts were “swaps” — a derivatives classification that would exempt them from Nevada gambling regulations. The company obtained regulatory approval from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) to list these contracts, arguing they served a legitimate hedging purpose for commercial parties.

The court disagreed, finding that the fundamental nature of the contracts was to speculate on political outcomes, not to hedge commercial risk. The ruling noted that Kalshi's primary customers were not commercial hedgers but retail traders seeking to profit from political events.

The decision creates a significant precedent for how financial contracts tied to political outcomes are classified. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission had approved Kalshi's contracts under the argument that they provided economic utility, but the appeals court found this insufficient to overcome Nevada's gambling laws.

Kalshi is expected to appeal further. The company has built its business around the premise that political prediction markets can operate as regulated derivatives rather than gambling products.
