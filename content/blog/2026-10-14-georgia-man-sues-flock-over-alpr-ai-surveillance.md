In September 2024, Murkerson sued the department , accusing them of excessive force. The case was only recently settled. While that lawsuit was pending, Murkerson says his car’s license plate was searched multiple times in the Flock system. Additionally, he alleges that deputies followed or monitored him as he drove to and from his home. His attorneys claim that, when searching for Murkerson’s vehicle, sheriff’s deputies entered nonsense rationales. This has been a widely reported weakness of the Flock system. “Throughout the relevant period Flock’s platform required only that some text be entered in a free-form field, and it accepted whatever was typed—‘investigation’ and, on twenty separate occasions at one department, ‘hehehe,’” the October 8 complaint states. “The only justifications entered for the searches of Mr. Murkerson’s vehicle were ‘info’ and ‘37.’ Neither corresponds to any investigation, case, incident, or call for service. A search recorded that way documents no investigation. It documents only that someone typed something. Flock has since conceded, by its own conduct, that its platform should have required a verifiable law enforcement justification before executing a search.” In August 2026, Flock announced it would change its software and require “case codes for all law enforcement searches by the end of the year” as a way to mitigate these abuses.
