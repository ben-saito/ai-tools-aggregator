# Databricks Raises $5B at $190B Valuation After $15B Investor Demand

Databricks planned to raise $1B. Investors wanted $15B. The data and AI company ended up raising $5B at a $190B valuation.

---

## The Trigger: A News Report and a Flood of Calls

Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi told TechCrunch that the turning point was The Information publishing an article in June about the company's plans. Ghodsi said he was focused on a company conference at the time.

"As soon as that article went out, there was a long line of investors that started calling. My phone blew up. It was like the worst timing for us because we were busy with our conference," he said.

The interest was extraordinary: from a select group of investors the company evaluated, there was $15B in demand.

---

## More Shares, Not More Money

With that much demand, saying no to long-term backers risked alienating them. Databricks chose to issue additional shares rather than turn investors away. In July, the company announced it closed a new round at an $188B valuation (without disclosing the amount raised).

On August 13, Databricks confirmed the final figures: $5B raised, valuation pushed to a rounded $190B. The round was led by Coatue, with participation from Blackstone, MGX, various T. Rowe Price family office accounts, and new investor Sixth Street Growth. Approximately two dozen VCs participated.

---

## Financials: $7B ARR, 80% Growth, Cash Flow Positive

Ghodsi shared key metrics:

- Annualized run rate revenue: $7B (growing 80% year-over-year)
- Core cloud data warehouse product: $1.5B ARR (100% YoY growth)
- Lakehouse (database for AI agents): $100M ARR (launched June 2025)
- Genie (AI analytics chatbot): "insanely popular," he said

The company is already cash-flow positive. So why raise additional capital after raising $20B over the past 20 months? Ghodsi did not fully explain, but the magnitude of investor demand made the round difficult to refuse.

---

## Why This Matters for AI Infrastructure

Databricks positions itself as the data backbone for AI agents and generative AI applications. Its Lakehouse platform serves as the authoritative data store that AI agents query and update. With the AI agent market heating up across enterprise software, investors priced Databricks as infrastructure worth owning regardless of cost.
