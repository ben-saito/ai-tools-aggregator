# Microsoft streamlines Copilot, kills AI podcasts and popular features

Microsoft is simplifying its Copilot AI assistant by merging consumer and business apps and discontinuing several underperforming AI features including AI-generated podcasts, Group Chats, Deep Research, and its Mico character.

---

## Consolidation after AI app space shift

The move acknowledges that personal and professional uses of AI often overlap, and that Microsoft's prior strategy was too complicated to make Copilot competitive with ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

Two years ago, Microsoft described AI as a "generational shift" in technology it wanted to lead. Today, the consolidation follows a broader trend in the AI app space that has seen Claude merging Cowork into Chat, OpenAI merging Operator into ChatGPT, and Google adding specialized capabilities to Gemini.

---

## Features being discontinued

According to Microsoft, consumers will lose access to Group Chats, AI-generated podcasts in Copilot, Copilot Labs experiments, and the Mico character. The company will combine the functionality of its consumer-facing Copilot app with the business-oriented Microsoft 365 Copilot app.

Microsoft's support documentation details the changes, which represent a significant shift in the company's AI strategy after years of rapid experimentation with various Copilot features.

---

## Competitive pressure mounts

The consolidation comes as competition in the AI assistant market intensifies. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have all been expanding their AI offerings, while startups continue to capture market share with specialized solutions.

Microsoft's decision to streamline Copilot suggests the company is shifting from broad experimentation to focused product development as the AI market matures.
