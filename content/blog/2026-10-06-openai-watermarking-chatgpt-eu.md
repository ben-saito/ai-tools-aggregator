# OpenAI Begins Watermarking ChatGPT Text in the EU

OpenAI has announced it will begin watermarking ChatGPT and Codex text generated in the European Union to comply with the AI Act's transparency requirements. The watermarks will be invisible markers embedded in AI-generated content that can be detected with specialized tools.

---

## EU AI Act Transparency Requirements

The European Union's AI Act requires operators of general-purpose AI systems to implement measures to identify AI-generated output, including watermarking for text generated by large language models. OpenAI's move represents one of the most significant implementations of this requirement by a major AI provider.

The watermarking system will apply to text generated by ChatGPT and Codex for users within the EU, with the technical implementation designed to be resistant to minor editing but potentially circumvented by more aggressive modifications.

---

## Editing Can Degrade Watermark Detectability

OpenAI noted that editing AI-generated text can make the invisible watermarks harder to detect. Light editing — rewording sentences, changing word order — may preserve enough of the watermark pattern for detection. However, more substantial modifications like translation or substantial rewriting can break the watermark entirely.

This limitation means organizations that need to verify content provenance will need to assess whether edited content still carries detectable watermarks, or implement additional verification methods for highly modified material.

---

## Implications for AI Content Identification

The watermarking initiative represents a shift from voluntary AI transparency measures to mandated technical implementation. As the AI Act's provisions take effect, more AI providers will need to implement similar systems, creating a technical baseline for identifying AI-generated text across the industry.
