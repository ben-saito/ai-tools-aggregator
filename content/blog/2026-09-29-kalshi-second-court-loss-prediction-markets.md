# Kalshi Loses Again: Second Court Rules Prediction Markets Are Gambling

Kalshi, the CFTC-regulated derivatives exchange that lets Americans trade contracts on real-world outcomes, has lost its second court battle over whether prediction markets constitute illegal gambling. A federal appeals court panel ruled that Kalshi's sports wagers do not qualify as "swaps" under commodities law and that states may regulate them as gambling.

---

## The Legal Saga

Kalshi launched its sports contracts in 2023, allowing users to trade on outcomes like which NFL team would win a given game. State gambling authorities immediately moved to shut them down. Kalshi argued the contracts were legally permissible "swaps" under the Commodity Exchange Act — and that the CFTC, not individual states, had exclusive jurisdiction.

The first ruling came from a lower court, which agreed with state gambling regulators. Now the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals has affirmed that ruling, finding that Kalshi's contracts fail the legal test for swaps and that states have authority to enforce their gambling laws.

---

## What This Means for Prediction Markets

The ruling is a significant blow to the prediction market industry. Kalshi has been one of the most prominent platforms offering political and event-based contracts, and the company has argued that these markets provide valuable forecasting information that traditional polls cannot match.

The company may appeal to the Supreme Court. Legal experts say the case presents an important question about the boundaries of CFTC authority and the distinction between speculation and gambling. If Kalshi takes it further, the case could fundamentally reshape the regulatory landscape for event-based contracts.

---

## Broader Implications for AI Forecasting

This ruling comes as AI companies increasingly use prediction markets as data inputs for forecasting models. Some AI systems have begun incorporating Kalshi-style contract prices as signals about real-world outcomes. A shutdown or contraction of prediction markets could force AI developers to find alternative data sources.
