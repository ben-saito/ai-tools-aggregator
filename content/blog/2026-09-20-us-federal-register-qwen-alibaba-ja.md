# US Federal Register Website Briefly Deployed Alibaba's Qwen AI Model

The US government's Federal Register website briefly offered visitors the option to search public comments using Alibaba's Qwen AI model — an open source Chinese AI system the FBI had recently designated as a national security concern. The contradiction was discovered and publicized in mid-September, prompting removal of the service.

---

## What Happened

The National Archives, which operates the Federal Register website, integrated an Alibaba Qwen model into its public comment search functionality. Users could select the AI-powered search option when browsing proposed federal regulations and their associated public comments. The service was live for at least a day before social media users identified the discrepancy on September 15.

An archived version of the site's source code confirmed that the Qwen integration was removed on September 17. Neither the National Archives, the White House, nor the FBI has commented publicly on the incident.

---

## The FBI's Recent Designation

This incident is particularly striking given the FBI's recent statements about Chinese AI companies. Earlier in September, the FBI named Alibaba among six leading Chinese firms allegedly conducting "industrial-scale distillation" — a process the agency claims helps China cut costs and development time in the global AI race by replicating the capabilities of US frontier models.

The juxtaposition of the FBI's aggressive stance toward Chinese AI companies with a US government agency's direct use of one of those same companies' technology generated significant criticism. Daniel Castro, president of the Information Technology and Innovation Foundation, called the situation an "insane disconnect."

---

## Technical Details of the Integration

The Qwen model was deployed as a search enhancement for the Federal Register's public comment system. Users searching for information about proposed regulations would have been offered the option to receive AI-generated summaries or answers derived from the public comment corpus. The National Archives apparently selected an open source Qwen variant rather than a US-based model for this purpose.

Open source AI models from Chinese laboratories have gained significant traction globally due to their accessibility and competitive performance. However, their use in sensitive government contexts raises questions about data handling, model provenance, and regulatory compliance.

---

## Policy Implications

The incident surfaces several policy concerns:

**AI supply chain security**: Government agencies deploying AI systems must consider the origin of models and the implications of using technology from strategic competitors

**Open source governance**: Unlike proprietary models, open source AI cannot be restricted by export controls once deployed — any actor can download and run Chinese open source models outside of official channels

**Interagency coordination gaps**: The disconnect between the FBI's public statements and the National Archives' technology choices suggests insufficient coordination on AI procurement policy

**Federal AI procurement guidelines**: The episode may accelerate development of explicit federal guidelines governing AI system sourcing, particularly for national security-adjacent applications

---

## Broader Context: US-China AI Competition

The Federal Register incident reflects the intensifying US-China AI competition. The FBI's industrial espionage allegations against Chinese AI companies, combined with evidence of actual US government use of those same companies' technology, illustrate how complex the technological relationship between the two nations has become.

Open source AI models operate outside many traditional export control frameworks, creating practical challenges for governments seeking to limit technology transfer. The Qwen model's availability as open source means any federal agency could theoretically deploy it independently of official procurement channels.
