# Meta AI Agent Muse Reaches No. 2 on US App Store — But the Numbers Tell a More Complex Story

Meta's personal AI agent Muse has climbed to the second position on the US iOS App Store Top Charts, according to new data from Sensor Tower. But compared to Meta's previous app launches, the debut tells a story of measured adoption rather than explosive growth.

---

## The Numbers: 83,000 Downloads and Counting

Muse has been downloaded more than 83,000 times in the United States since launch. For a personal AI agent focused on daily life management, that represents a credible starting point. The app is currently US-only, a notable constraint compared to Meta's typical global-first rollout.

The launch comparison to other Meta products is stark: Threads recorded over 4.3 million US downloads on its first day. Meta AI app hit 108,000 downloads on debut. Muse's 83,000 cumulative total sits well below both figures.

---

## What Makes Muse Different: The Scope of Access Requested

The more interesting differentiator may be what Muse asks from users. According to TechCrunch's reporting by Sarah Perez, Muse requests broad access to users' emails, calendars, payment information, health services, and more. This goes far beyond a chatbot that answers questions.

Where Meta AI and Threads serve as communication and discovery interfaces, Muse is positioned as a persistent personal agent that understands the full context of a user's life. That ambition may be precisely why adoption is slower: users are being asked to hand over significantly more than they would for a simple app download.

---

## Competitive Position in the AI Agent Market

Meta is pitting Muse against not just ChatGPT and Claude but against its own family of products. The strategic logic is clear: if an AI agent knows your schedule, contacts, and preferences, it becomes the hub through which all other Meta services are accessed.

The 83,000 figure may represent the leading edge of a wave rather than a ceiling. Meta is expected to expand Muse internationally and broaden the access permissions over the coming months. Whether users follow will depend heavily on trust dynamics that remain in flux following years of scrutiny over Meta's data practices.
