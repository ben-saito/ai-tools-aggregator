# Google Launches Universal Cart, a Gemini-Powered Shopping Hub

---

Google has launched **Universal Cart**, a tool that lets users collect and monitor products they discover across Google Search and within the Gemini app. The feature is now available to U.S. users and represents Google's latest step toward embedding AI assistance directly into the shopping experience.

The company first previewed Universal Cart at Google I/O in May. The core idea is straightforward: instead of managing separate shopping carts across retailer websites, users save items to a centralized Google-hosted cart that persists across sessions and retailers. When users return, their selections and shopping progress are intact.

---

## How Universal Cart Works

Universal Cart aggregates items from multiple retailers into a single interface. Users can add products from any website that implements Google's shopping metadata standards, then manage everything through Search or the Gemini app. The AI layer — powered by **Gemini** — can surface related products, track price changes, and provide purchase recommendations based on cart contents.

Unlike traditional retailer-specific carts, Universal Cart maintains state across devices and sessions. A user can browse on desktop, add items on mobile, and complete purchases on either platform without losing selections.

The feature also integrates with Google's existing price comparison and deal surfaces, allowing Gemini to proactively flag price drops on cart items or suggest alternatives from different retailers.

---

## AI as Shopping Assistant

Universal Cart exemplifies the shift from search-as-shopping to AI-as-shopping-assistant. Rather than users actively seeking products, Gemini anticipates needs based on browsing history, cart contents, and stated preferences. The AI maintains context across interactions, learning from each cart modification to improve future recommendations.

This represents a meaningful departure from traditional e-commerce UX, where every session starts from scratch. Universal Cart instead treats shopping as a continuous, AI-mediated activity rather than a series of discrete transactions.

---

## Broader Implications

By owning the cart layer, Google positions itself between consumers and retailer websites — capturing shopping intent data and intermediating the purchase decision. This mirrors Amazon's strategy with its first-party cart but applies it across the open web rather than a single marketplace.

For retailers, Universal Cart represents both an opportunity (access to Google's AI recommendation infrastructure) and a threat (potential loss of direct customer relationships). The rollout will likely depend heavily on how Google structures revenue sharing and data access terms.
