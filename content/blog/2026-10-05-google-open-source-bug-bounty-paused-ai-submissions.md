# Google Pauses Open Source Bug Bounty Program Citing Surge in AI Submissions

Google has suspended its open source bug bounty program, citing an overwhelming influx of automated submissions that the company says are almost entirely invalid. The pause, effective October 1, applies to Google's Vulnerability Reward Program for open source projects, and will remain in place until at least the first quarter of 2027.

---

## A Deluge of Automated Submissions

Google announced the pause in posts on X and the official program website, stating that the decision was driven by a "significant rise in automated submissions, the vast majority of which are not valid." The company did not provide specific numbers, but the characterization suggests a volume of submissions that made it effectively impossible to triage legitimate vulnerability reports from noise generated by AI systems.

Bug bounty programs have long relied on human researchers to identify and responsibly disclose security vulnerabilities. The emergence of AI-powered vulnerability discovery tools has changed that equation significantly. While AI systems can certainly find bugs, they also generate large numbers of false positives and often lack the context needed to determine whether a particular code pattern represents an actual security issue or merely a theoretical edge case.

---

## AI Slop and the Quality Problem

The broader phenomenon, which some in the security community have dubbed "AI slop," extends beyond bug bounty programs to code review processes, security mailing lists, and vulnerability databases. AI-generated security reports frequently misidentify benign code patterns as potential vulnerabilities, misclassify severity, or propose fixes that introduce new problems while attempting to solve old ones.

For bug bounty programs specifically, the volume of low-quality submissions creates a significant operational burden. Each submission must be reviewed, and the opportunity cost of triage time spent on AI-generated noise is time not spent investigating genuine vulnerabilities. Google is not alone in grappling with this challenge; multiple major technology companies have reported similar experiences with AI-driven submission volume increases over the past year.

---

## What's Next

Google has promised to provide an update on the program's future in the first quarter of 2027. The pause applies specifically to open source project submissions; Google's other vulnerability reward programs for Google products themselves remain active.

The company has not specified what changes it might implement before relaunching the open source program. Potential approaches include requiring pre-registration of vulnerability targets, implementing stricter submission formatting requirements that might filter out obviously AI-generated reports, or introducing a reputation system that prioritizes researchers with established track records of valid submissions.

Security researchers who work in the open source space have expressed mixed reactions. Some view the pause as a necessary step to preserve program quality, while others worry that the interruption will discourage researchers from continuing to invest time in open source security work.
