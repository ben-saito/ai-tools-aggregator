# OpenAI and Google AI Models Easily Tricked Into Creating Nude Images, Researchers Say

---

Safety researchers are warning that leading AI image generators from Google and OpenAI may be getting worse at blocking explicit outputs even as they become more capable in other domains. A new study co-authored by Google researchers found that a large volume of AI-generated explicit content is spreading unchecked, while regulators do little to meaningfully stop it.

The non-consensual content is commonly shared in text and direct messages or stored in private clouds where it's less likely to be detected or reported, according to the study of Internet users across the United States, Australia, and the United Kingdom. Researchers concluded that the "most critical intervention point" remains at the source: the harmful AI tools used to make the fake sexual images.

## Red Teaming Reveals Safeguard Failures

Researchers at **White Circle**, a public benefit corporation backed by senior leaders and researchers from Anthropic, DeepMind, Hugging Face, and OpenAI, conducted red-teaming exercises that revealed significant flaws. The company's founding came after CEO Denis Shilov posted one of the most famous examples of AI-generated explicit content, which sparked industry-wide debates about AI safety.

The research highlights an ongoing challenge: as AI image generation models become more powerful and capable of producing photorealistic images, their safety guardrails are being circumvented by increasingly sophisticated jailbreak techniques. The attackers use off-the-shelf tools to manipulate or bypass the content filters that AI companies have put in place.

## Industry Response Lagging

Despite the clear evidence of harm, the industry response has been inconsistent. Google's own researchers concluded that addressing the problem at the model level — improving safeguard robustness — is more effective than trying to police content after it's generated. Yet red-teaming results suggest that even frontier models from major providers remain vulnerable to simple attacks that have been documented for months or years.
