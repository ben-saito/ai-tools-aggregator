# Google SynthID Watermark: 100 Billion AI Images Tagged, But Disinformation Remains Unsolved

Google's SynthID digital watermark has been applied to more than 100 billion AI-generated images and videos, according to a report from Ars Technica on July 29, 2026. The technology aims to identify AI-generated content, but researchers caution that it does not solve the broader problem of AI disinformation.

SynthID works by embedding an invisible signal into AI-generated media that can be detected even after common image modifications. The scale of deployment is impressive — 100 billion tagged images in a relatively short timeframe — but the technology has limitations.

Starling Lab estimates that AI generated approximately 1.5 billion images in just 18 months, a pace that outpaced photography's first 149 years of existence. This explosion of AI content has made it increasingly difficult to distinguish real from fake, especially in contexts where images are shared out of original context.

## SynthID Scale

- **Images/videos watermarked**: 100+ billion
- **Timeframe**: Relatively short since launch
- **Detection capability**: Works through common image modifications

---

## The Disinformation Challenge

While watermarking technology can help identify AI-generated content, it does not address the core challenge: even when we can detect that an image is AI-generated, we cannot automatically determine whether it is being used deceptively. A watermarked image could be shared in a legitimate context, while an unmodified real photograph could be miscaptioned to spread false information.

The effectiveness of watermarking ultimately depends on ecosystem-wide adoption and integration into content verification workflows.
