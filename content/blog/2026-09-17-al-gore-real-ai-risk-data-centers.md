# Al Gore Says the Real AI Risk Isn't Data Centers — It's Where the Technology Is Heading

Former US Vice President Al Gore says he isn't losing sleep over AI data center emissions. In an interview with TechCrunch, Gore suggested he is far more concerned about the AI industry's own warnings about where the technology is headed — a notable shift from the mainstream debate that has focused heavily on the environmental impact of compute-intensive AI training.

---

## Environmental Critics Miss the Point, Gore Suggests

The backlash against AI data centers — their water consumption, electricity draw, and carbon footprint — has dominated much of the public discourse around AI's societal costs. Gore acknowledged these concerns are real but argued they represent the wrong target.

"Those are legitimate concerns," Gore reportedly told TechCrunch. "But they are not the concerns that keep me up at night."

The implications of AI systems themselves — their trajectory toward autonomy, the concentration of power among a handful of labs, and the difficulty of maintaining meaningful human control — are the issues Gore characterized as the more consequential long-term risks.

---

## A Cautious Stance From a Climate Pioneer

Gore's measured stance on AI contrasts with his decades of advocacy on climate change, where he has been an outspoken critic of the fossil fuel industry. That measured tone reflects, in part, the complexity of the current moment: AI is simultaneously a tool with enormous potential to accelerate scientific discovery and a technology whose long-term behavior remains uncertain.

Industry leaders including OpenAI's Sam Altman and Anthropic's Dario Amodei have both publicly acknowledged that advanced AI systems could pose existential risks to humanity. Those admissions have given regulators and watchdog organizations ammunition to push for earlier intervention — but they have also created a rhetorical opening that some critics say the industry uses to distract from nearer-term harms.

---

## The Data Center Debate Continues

While Gore's remarks suggest a broader perspective on AI risk, the environmental debate shows no signs of cooling. Data center construction has become a flashpoint in communities across the United States, with local governments grappling with zoning requests, water rights, and electricity grid strain.

Microsoft, Google, and Amazon have all announced major data center expansion plans in the past year, with spending on AI infrastructure reaching into the hundreds of billions of dollars annually across the industry.
