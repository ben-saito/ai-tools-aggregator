# Ukraine's Kamikaze Drones Get AI Upgrade: 50,000 Shrike Drones to Receive Autonomous Targeting

Ukraine is rapidly scaling up its use of AI-powered autonomous drones on the battlefield. In mid-July, the Ukrainian military began receiving Shrike drones made by Ukrainian company SkyFall, equipped with AI-powered autonomy hardware and software developed by US company Auterion. The two companies plan to deliver 50,000 drones equipped with Auterion's Skynode S strike kits in the coming months, representing a $100 million deal.

---

## From FPV to Fire-and-Forget: How the AI System Works

The system allows human operators to manually fly Shrike first-person view (FPV) drones into a battlefield area, designate a target up to half a mile away, and then "flip the switch to turn it into fire-and-forget terminal guidance mode," according to Lorenz Meier, co-founder and CEO of Auterion. The operator can abort a strike or redirect to a different target as long as they maintain a radio signal connection with the drone.

However, if the connection is lost due to buildings, terrain blocking the signal, or enemy jamming on the same radio frequencies, the drone can still automatically track and fly into targets without any human guidance. Auterion's software relies solely on visual information from the Shrike drone's main onboard camera, enabling the drones to track and strike moving targets without GPS — a critical advantage as GPS jamming has become commonplace on modern battlefields.

---

## Targeting the Frontlines: FPV Drones Become More Lethal

Until now, the Ukrainian military has mainly relied on human operators to manually guide drones toward their targets. The exception has been longer-range strike drones such as the $5,000 AI-powered "Hornet" drones developed by California-based Perennial Autonomy, founded by former Google CEO Eric Schmidt. Those drones often fly far beyond the frontlines to target Russian oil refineries, energy infrastructure, factories, and ships.

Auterion focused on creating AI-powered capabilities for cheaper, shorter-range FPV drones that have become the most common weapons responsible for killing or wounding soldiers on the frontlines. The company developed a tightly integrated flight control and terminal guidance system optimized to run on a Western-made Arm system-on-a-chip costing just $18.

"What we are doing is replacing the existing flight controller — which has no computer, no AI — with avionics that has a microprocessor unit, has AI on board, and can track into the target fully autonomously," Meier told Ars Technica.

---

## Already Proving Effective: Javelin of the Sky

Shrike drones have already struck many vehicle-size targets since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in 2022, including armored tanks, electronic warfare systems, artillery guns, and rocket launchers. The drones have reportedly taken down two Russian Mi-28 helicopters, each worth as much as $19 million, according to the Kyiv Independent.

---

## The Strategic Shift: AI on the Cheap

The deployment of 50,000 AI-equipped drones represents a significant shift in the economics of modern warfare. By combining cheap FPV airframes with affordable AI hardware, Ukraine is building an autonomous strike capability at a fraction of the cost of traditional weapons systems. The $18 Arm chip stands in contrast to the expensive precision-guided munitions these drones replace, potentially making AI-equipped drones the "Javelin" of the next phase of the conflict.
