# FCC Proposes Robocall Scorecard to Rate Phone Companies on Spam Blocking

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has proposed a new "robocall scorecard" system that would grade phone companies on their effectiveness at blocking unwanted spam and scam calls, moving beyond simple compliance checklists to measure actual outcomes.

---

## From Checklist to Outcomes

The FCC's proposed scorecard system represents a shift from measuring whether phone companies filed the correct paperwork to evaluating their concrete results in protecting consumers from illegal robocalls. Under the proposal, carriers would be rated on a combination of operational practices and measurable outcomes, including how often legitimate calls are incorrectly blocked.

"The Scorecard will empower consumers and encourage providers to continue to combat illegal robocalls by providing the public with an assessment of the effectiveness of voice service providers' efforts," the FCC's Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau said in a public notice.

---

## How the Rating System Would Work

The scorecards could include several components:

- **Call-blocking statistics**: Data on how many robocalls were blocked versus legitimate calls mistakenly filtered
- **Customer complaint records**: Tracking consumer reports about unwanted calls and blocking issues  
- **Enforcement action history**: Records of FCC enforcement against the provider for robocall violations
- **Risk classification**: Providers could be classified as low, medium, or high risk, or receive letter grades on a numerical scale

The system would apply to domestic voice service providers with retail customers, including wireless carriers, wireline phone companies, and VoIP services. Wholesale-only providers would be excluded from the requirements.

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr framed the initiative as the commission's top consumer protection priority, noting that while tools to combat robocalls exist, the focus is shifting toward results rather than compliance procedures.

---

## Consumer and Industry Reactions

Consumer advocates have responded cautiously to the announcement. Teresa Murray of the Public Interest Research Group (PIRG) said the FCC's past efforts to combat robocalls have been "disappointing for years," predating even the 2019 robocall crackdown law. She expressed hope that the scorecard approach could be more effective if it includes the right information.

The phone industry is expected to push back on aspects of the proposal that could result in poor ratings, particularly around the methodology for measuring legitimate call blocking. Companies that score poorly could face reputational damage and potential regulatory consequences.

---

## Public Comment Period

The FCC is seeking public comment on what data sources should be used for the scorecard, how scores should be calculated, and how results should be presented to consumers. The proceeding is open at the FCC's website, and stakeholders have until the comment deadline to submit feedback.

Commissioner Anna Gomez, the commission's only Democrat, said the scorecard "could increase consumer awareness of how providers fight illegal robocalls" if designed with appropriate metrics.

---

## Context: Broader FCC Consumer Protection Efforts

The robocall scorecard is not the FCC's first initiative to provide consumers with useful data about telecom providers. The commission previously required internet providers to display pricing information on broadband labels, though recent votes have rolled back requirements to list all discretionary fees on those labels.
