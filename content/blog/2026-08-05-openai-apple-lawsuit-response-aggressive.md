# OpenAI Calls Apple Trade Secrets Lawsuit "Aggressive and Oddly Personal"

OpenAI has responded to Apple's lawsuit, calling the allegations "aggressive and oddly personal" and denying any possession or desire for Apple's trade secrets, marking a significant escalation in the dispute between the two companies.

---

## OpenAI's Response

"We do not have, nor want, any of their trade secrets," OpenAI wrote in a public statement, accusing Apple of "making vague accusations" and "trying to change their narrative."

The company detailed what it called procedural irregularities in Apple's approach, noting that Apple's lawyers had "emailed the wrong person after confusing two Asian last names" and "claimed they had a discussion with our General Counsel, which they now concede never happened."

OpenAI stated that Apple "never raised the specific allegations in this lawsuit at that time" and "in fact told us that they were 'resolving any issues.' We then heard nothing for five months until they sued."

---

## Background: The Dispute

The lawsuit, filed last month, claimed OpenAI had stolen hardware designs as it prepared to launch its own AI-focused consumer devices. The dispute has intensified as both companies increasingly enter each other's territory.

Apple has partnered with Google to revamp its Siri voice assistant, while OpenAI acquired io, the design studio founded by former Apple design chief Sir Jony Ive, and hired multiple former Apple employees.

Apple alleged that Chang Liu, a former employee who worked on "the most sensitive product development programs," accessed Apple's trade secrets after leaving the company in January. Apple also named OpenAI's chief hardware officer Tang Tan, accusing him of seeking proprietary information when interviewing Apple employees.

"Tang has always been clear with the team that we do not want, and must not use, any confidential information from other companies," OpenAI stated.

---

## Latest Motions

Apple on Monday sought a preliminary injunction to prevent former employees and OpenAI from accessing, using, or disclosing its trade secrets. The company also filed a second motion as the legal battle continues to unfold.
