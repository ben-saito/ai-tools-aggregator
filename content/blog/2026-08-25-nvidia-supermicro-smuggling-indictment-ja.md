# Nvidia Senior Manager Indicted for Smuggling AI Servers to China via Supermicro

On August 24, 2026, Ars Technica reported that a senior Nvidia manager was indicted for allegedly smuggling AI servers to China in coordination with Supermicro. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had previously publicly criticized Supermicro's CEO Roger Liang for the same conduct.

---

## The Indictment

According to U.S. prosecutors, a Nvidia senior manager conspired with Supermicro to circumvent export controls on AI chips destined for China. Supermicro is a major server manufacturer that relies heavily on Nvidia GPUs for AI-focused systems.

China has been barred from acquiring Nvidia's most advanced AI chips (Hopper and Blackwell architectures) under U.S. export control regulations enacted in 2022. Despite these restrictions, Nvidia developed lower-spec versions (such as H800) for the Chinese market, but smuggling routes remained a concern for authorities.

---

## Jensen Huang's Public Criticism

Weeks before the indictment became public, Jensen Huang openly criticized Supermicro CEO Roger Liang. "Using Nvidia's IP while violating export rules," Huang said at a public event, in remarks that now appear pointed given the allegations.

The relationship between the two companies had been close for years through joint product development, but this incident has severely strained that partnership.

---

## Geopolitics of AI Hardware

AI servers have become strategic assets in the U.S.-China tech competition. Washington maintains strict export controls on advanced AI chips, while Beijing accelerates domestic chip development. Nvidia walks a fine line between business interests and geopolitical pressure.
