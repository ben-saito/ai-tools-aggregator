# Sequoia-Backed Empirik Launches with $21M to Predict IT Outages Before They Happen

Empirik, a startup incubated by Sequoia Capital, has emerged from stealth with $21 million in funding to bring predictive intelligence to IT infrastructure management. The company's platform aims to do for IT operations what Cursor did for software engineering — automating the detection and prevention of system failures before they impact end users.

---

## The Problem: Reactive IT Costs Billions Annually

Enterprise IT teams spend billions of dollars annually on reactive firefighting — diagnosing and resolving outages after they occur. The average cost of IT downtime ranges from $140,000 to $540,000 per hour, depending on the industry. For financial services, healthcare, and e-commerce platforms, even brief outages can translate into millions in lost revenue and damaged customer trust.

Traditional monitoring tools alert operators after a problem has already manifested — CPU utilization spiking, disk space exhausted, or a service failing health checks. By that point, users are already experiencing degraded performance or complete service disruption.

Empirik's approach flips this model. Rather than reacting to symptoms, the platform analyzes patterns across infrastructure telemetry to identify precursor signals that typically precede an outage by hours or even days.

---

## How Empirik's Prediction Engine Works

According to Sequoia's investment thesis, Empirik has built a machine learning system trained on historical incident data from thousands of enterprise infrastructure deployments. The model identifies correlations between subtle environmental changes — temperature fluctuations in data center racks, gradual memory leaks, irregular network latency patterns, and batch job scheduling anomalies — and subsequent system failures.

The platform integrates with existing infrastructure including AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, and on-premises Kubernetes clusters. It provides a unified dashboard showing predicted failure windows ranked by severity and estimated blast radius.

Unlike generic anomaly detection, Empirik's system is said to understand the specific dependency graph of each customer's architecture. This allows it to distinguish between benign anomalies in non-critical paths and genuine precursors to cascading failures.

---

## Enterprise Context and Competitive Landscape

The IT operations artificial intelligence market (AIOps) has grown significantly, with incumbents like Splunk, Datadog, and New Relic all incorporating machine learning features. However, most existing tools focus on faster detection and diagnosis rather than true prediction.

Empirik's differentiation lies in its predictive focus and its separation from the observability platform wars. The company positions itself as a complementary layer that can sit atop existing monitoring infrastructure.

Sequoia's backing signals confidence in the team's pedigree. The firm has a strong track record in infrastructure software, having previously backed companies like Snowflake, HashiCorp, and GitHub.

---

## Outlook

With $21 million in seed and Series A funding, Empirik will use the capital to expand its engineering team and build out integrations with additional cloud providers and enterprise tooling. The company is currently in early customer engagements with several Fortune 500 companies.

The broader trend toward autonomous IT operations suggests that predictive maintenance — common in manufacturing and aerospace — is finally arriving for software infrastructure. If Empirik's predictions prove reliable, enterprises could shift from costly reactive incident response to proactive remediation, potentially saving millions in downtime-related losses annually.
