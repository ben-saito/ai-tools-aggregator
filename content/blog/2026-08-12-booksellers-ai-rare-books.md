# Booksellers Suspect AI Firms Are Buying and Then Destroying Rare Books

---

## Literary Community Alarmed by Bulk Purchases of Rare Collections

Rare book dealers and collectors are raising alarms about what they describe as a disturbing pattern: AI companies purchasing rare books in bulk, potentially to digitize and destroy the originals. The practice, if confirmed, would represent a significant threat to irreplaceable literary heritage.

Reports from multiple independent booksellers describe similar experiences: orders for rare first editions, manuscripts, and historical documents placed by buyers associated with AI companies, followed by cancellation of orders and requests for bulk purchases of specific titles that are then absorbed into corporate collections.

---

## Digitization or Destruction?

The concern centers on whether AI companies are using these acquisitions to create training datasets for AI models, with some booksellers reporting that their sold items have disappeared without explanation. The literary community suspects that some firms may be acquiring books solely to extract content, then disposing of physical copies in ways that make recovery impossible.

Copyright implications complicate the situation, as many of these books remain under copyright protection. The practice of bulk acquisition for AI training purposes has drawn legal challenges from authors and publishers, but the specific question of what happens to physical books after acquisition remains largely unaddressed.

---

## Bookseller Coalition Forms Response

In response, rare book dealers have begun forming information-sharing networks to identify buyers associated with AI companies and to track the movement of rare materials. Some dealers have implemented policies requiring disclosure of end-use intentions for bulk purchases.

The incident reflects broader tensions between AI companies seeking training data and cultural institutions protecting physical archives. Unlike digital archives, rare books represent unique physical artifacts that cannot be replicated if destroyed.

---

## Regulatory Questions

Advocates are calling on regulators to investigate the flow of rare book acquisitions and to consider whether export controls or documentation requirements should apply to bulk purchases of historical materials by AI companies. The issue intersects with questions of cultural heritage protection, copyright enforcement, and the ethical sourcing of AI training data.

---

*Reports based on Ars Technica reporting as of August 12, 2026.*
