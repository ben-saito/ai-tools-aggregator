# RAND Maps Seven Archetypal Strategies for US Superintelligence Competition

The RAND Corporation published a comprehensive analysis on September 21, 2026, outlining seven archetypal strategies the United States could pursue in response to rapid advances toward artificial superintelligence. The report, titled "The US's Superintelligence Strategy," addresses questions of pacing, suppression, coexistence, and acceleration across three strategic families.

---

## Three Strategic Families

The report organizes seven strategies into three families:

**Coexistence** strategies assume that humans and advanced AI systems can find stable equilibrium. These include Dominance, where the US leads AI development and suppresses rival programs; Co-Development, where the US leads a consortium including China to co-develop safe superintelligence with shared governance; and Preparedness, where the US mobilizes international coordination while maintaining lead capabilities.

**Denial** strategies assume that constraining AI development is possible and desirable. Moratorium strategy calls for a verifiable global halt to AI development above specified thresholds. Deterrence involves halting US frontier development while using national power to prevent and destroy foreign programs. Continuity Planning prepares for worst-case scenarios where restraint efforts fail.

**Acceleration** strategy implicitly believes that constraining AI development is more dangerous than permitting it. Under this approach, the US relies on markets, competition, and rapid iteration to produce safety as a byproduct of useful AI.

---

## Key Decision Frameworks

The report identifies five key questions that determine which strategy is most appropriate:

**Danger proximity** asks whether AI is becoming dangerous enough that risks outweigh benefits. If danger is close, strategies that buy time and build defenses become favored.

**Coexistence feasibility** considers whether stable equilibrium between humans and AI systems is achievable. If feasible, building and deploying advanced AI becomes more desirable.

**Restraint feasibility** asks whether international cooperation can constrain AI development. If cooperation is possible, cooperative strategies become available.

**Decisive strategic advantage** examines whether a single actor can build and maintain a lead over competitors. If achievable, unilateral strategies may make sense.

**Suppression feasibility** considers whether rival AI programs can be suppressed through technology controls. If suppression is feasible, Deterrence becomes possible.

---

## Pacing Frameworks

The report introduces detailed frameworks for thinking about AI pacing interventions, addressing four phases:

**Pre-pacing** examines what justifies intervention, what signals of emerging risk are being tracked, what infrastructure must exist for detection, and who watches and interprets signals.

**At trigger** addresses who holds authority to decide a trigger condition has been met, how much error is acceptable for quick action, what sequence of actions triggering sets in motion, and whether developers are obliged to address concerns or free to abandon restricted paths.

**During intervention** covers how compliance is observed, verified, and enforced, who reports evidence and who audits, and how downtime should be used.

**At exit** considers how decision-makers distinguish successful interventions from failures and whether exit should be immediate or staged.

---

## Hard Limits on Intelligence Growth

The report examines physical and practical limits on AI capability advancement. Even an optimal reasoner would be neither omniscient nor omnipotent. Limits exist in hardware (physical compute per unit resource), algorithms (optimal neural networks below best achievable intelligence), and training data (lacking non-verbalizable and contextual information).

The analysis also explores Reversible Software Intelligence (RSI) dynamics, noting that RSI weak links appear across multiple abstractions and timescales. The report outlines four phases of a potential intelligence explosion: initial human-only exponential growth, RSI-driven super-exponential acceleration, fully automated RSI plateau, and logistic trajectory as the system approaches saturation.

---

## Implications for Policy

The RAND analysis suggests that the US needs substantial investment to pre-position itself for an uncertain AI future. The report argues that regardless of which strategic family proves most appropriate, maintaining optionality requires meaningful resource commitment across technical capabilities, regulatory expertise, international coordination infrastructure, and domestic response capacity.

The strategic frameworks provided offer decision-makers structured approaches for thinking about pacing interventions, though the authors acknowledge that determining which strategy is optimal remains fundamentally unclear given the uncertainty inherent in forecasting transformative technological change.
