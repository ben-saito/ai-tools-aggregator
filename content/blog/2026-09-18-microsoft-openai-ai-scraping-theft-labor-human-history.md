# Microsoft Exec Called AI Scraping "the Largest Theft of Labor in Human History" — New Unredacted Filings Reveal

Newly unsealed court filings in the copyright lawsuit The New York Times brought against OpenAI and Microsoft reveal that a top Microsoft executive privately described the companies' AI training practices as "theft" — and that OpenAI's own leadership said its AI models posed an "existential threat" to publishers and journalists whose work trained them.

---

## The "Doom Loop" That Threatens the Content Supply Chain

The unsealed material, part of the three-year-old lawsuit, details how Microsoft and OpenAI allegedly obtained Times content by bypassing paywalls undetected, building training datasets via mass scraping, and deliberately stripping copyright notices from training data.

The filings include a striking internal Microsoft document written by Brent Hecht, Microsoft's Director of Applied Science, in January 2024. Describing the decline in click-through rates caused by Copilot's "answer engine," Hecht wrote that the situation represents a "doom loop" that would "hurt the performance of our models and the entire web at the same time."

> "It is highly unusual that an end-product threatens the economic foundations of its essential suppliers, but that is the situation we have created for our LLM business with respect to its 'content supply chain.'"

According to the filings, Microsoft data shows Copilot caused click-through rates for The New York Times domain to drop as much as 93% compared to traditional Bing search.

---

## OpenAI's Own Leadership Warned of "Existential Threat"

Internal communications from OpenAI's leadership further undermine the company's fair use defense. Nick Turley, OpenAI's Head of ChatGPT, wrote that publishers face an "existential threat" from products like the chatbot, which are "largely substitutive" and "will get more and more substitutive as they get better."

OpenAI President Greg Brockman described the models as "excellent at news." CEO Satya Nadella agreed under oath that conversing with chatbots has "substituted... giving you the information right there on the website on the AI platform versus needing to go to the underlying source."

Microsoft's own document states there is a "real risk" that generative AI could "significantly disrupt the employment of the very people who generated the data on which the foundation model was trained."

---

## The Scale of Copying

The documents reveal for the first time that OpenAI's mid-training datasets alone contain more than 91,692 copies of works published by The New York Times, Daily News, and Center for Investigative Reporting. A Common Crawl-derived dataset included more than 2 million documents from nytimes.com alone.

The filing represents the latest escalation in the lawsuit. While judges have been largely favorable to AI companies' arguments that training constitutes "fair use," several of these admissions cut against different pillars of that defense — particularly the requirement that use doesn't substitute or harm the market for the original work.

Earlier this month, the Trump administration submitted a brief in defense of OpenAI's unlicensed use of copyrighted material to train its LLMs.
