# Nvidia AI Server Smuggling Scandal: Senior Manager Linked to $2.5B Scheme

---

## The Scheme: How Restricted AI Servers Reached China

A senior manager at Nvidia has been linked to an alleged smuggling operation that funneled $2.5 billion worth of restricted AI servers to China through Taiwan, according to Reuters reporting and confirmed by Ars Technica coverage. The case has escalated since Supermicro co-founder Wally Liaw was arrested in March for allegedly orchestrating the scheme.

Since 2022, the US has required export licenses for semiconductors shipped to China, aimed at preventing China from gaining access to advanced AI compute infrastructure. The restriction was designed to maintain US leadership in the AI race. But the indictment alleges that Supermicro — one of the world's largest server manufacturers — became a conduit for routing Nvidia-powered servers to Chinese customers through systematic document falsification.

---

## Taiwan Investigation Uncovers 130 B300 Servers

Taiwanese prosecutors have charged suspects with breach of trust and document forgery in connection with the case. The investigation found that co-conspirators attempted to falsify customs documents to make it appear that 130 B300 servers were installed and operational at a Taiwan facility. In reality, 74 of those servers were ultimately exported and delivered to Chinese customers, while 56 were intercepted by Taiwanese customs officials who detected irregularities.

The scale of the operation is staggering: $2.5 billion in restricted AI server hardware allegedly circumventing US export controls through a combination of falsified documentation and offshore routing.

---

## Nvidia's Exposure and Industry Implications

Nvidia has maintained that it complies fully with all export regulations and has cooperated with investigations. The company has been vocal about the importance of the US export control framework in maintaining AI competitiveness. However, the involvement of a senior manager highlights the downstream risks that chip manufacturers face when their products enter complex supply chains.

Supermicro, in a statement to PC Mag, said it is cooperating with Taiwanese investigators and emphasized its commitment to protecting advanced technologies and intellectual property.

---

## National Security and AI Compute Competition

The case underscores the tension between US export controls designed to limit China's AI development and the global nature of semiconductor supply chains. Nvidia's B300 servers, based on the Blackwell architecture, represent some of the most advanced AI training infrastructure available. The ability to channel these systems to Chinese entities through intermediary markets directly undermines US policy objectives.

The investigation is ongoing, with prosecutors in Keelung pursuing charges against multiple suspects connected to the scheme.
