# UP.Labs Rebrands to Vantora, Raises $100M to Build Physical AI Startups

UP.Labs, the startup that builds other startups for industrial corporations, has rebranded to Vantora and raised $100 million in fresh funding from Silversmith Capital Partners. The company, which creates startups exclusively for its corporate partners, is now focused entirely on physical AI -- AI systems that interact with the physical world rather than operating purely in software.

---

## From UP.Labs to Vantora

The company launched in 2022 with Porsche as its first corporate partner, building startups designed to address specific strategic needs of major industrial corporations. The model was distinctive: instead of selling software services, Vantora creates entirely new startup companies that are wholly owned or deeply integrated with their corporate sponsors.

Now operating under the name Vantora, the company has announced a $100 million Series A from Silversmith Capital Partners -- its first outside investment. The funding will accelerate the company's focus on building AI systems for physical industrial applications.

"We were missing on the biggest value problems, which had the biggest upside because of that," said Sam Kuolt, CEO of Vantora, in a recent interview. "Imagine you're a Fortune 100 industrial company and you need to retrofit all of your hardware and machines for autonomy. You need to own that, it needs to be sovereign, and you can't rely on a third party to go do that for you. You need to own that intelligence layer. They're never going to let us go sell that to their competitors."

---

## Physical AI: The New Frontier

Physical AI refers to AI systems that operate in and interact with the physical world -- robotics, autonomous vehicles, industrial automation, and smart infrastructure. Unlike software AI that processes text, images, or data, physical AI must understand and respond to real-world constraints, physics, and environments.

Vantora's focus on physical AI reflects a broader industry trend. As language model capabilities have matured, leading AI companies and investors are increasingly looking toward physical AI as the next major frontier. The challenge of making AI systems that can reliably perceive, reason about, and act in physical environments has proven more difficult than software AI, but the commercial applications are substantial.

Vantora's approach addresses a specific pain point in physical AI adoption: large industrial corporations want to integrate AI capabilities into their operations, but are reluctant to depend on third-party vendors for core intellectual property. By creating a startup that is effectively an extension of the corporation's own R&D efforts, Vantora allows partners to own the AI systems that transform their operations.

---

## Corporate Partnerships and Track Record

Vantora has built startups for several major corporate partners, including Porsche, Alaska Airlines, J.B. Hunt, Wabash, and TDG (parent of Ashley Furniture). The company is also working with new partners in industrial manufacturing and oil and gas, though it has declined to name specific companies.

The company still shares office space with venture firm Up.Partners, but is now a fully independent entity. The $100 million from Silversmith represents its first external funding, signaling a transition from self-funded operations to institutional growth financing.
