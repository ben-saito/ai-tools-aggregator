# Brian Chesky: AI agents need their own operating system

Brian Chesky, co-founder and CEO of Airbnb, is arguing that the next major platform shift in artificial intelligence will require a fundamentally different kind of software infrastructure -- one where AI agents need their own dedicated operating system.

---

## The Argument for an Agent-Native OS

In a recent interview, Chesky described the current state of consumer AI as "the app era of the smartphone before iOS" -- a collection of individual applications that do not communicate with each other or share context. He believes the breakthrough that will unlock the full potential of AI agents is a unified operating layer that can coordinate across apps, devices, and services on behalf of users.

The core problem Chesky identifies is that existing operating systems and app platforms were designed around human interaction patterns. AI agents, which can act autonomously across multiple services, need a different paradigm -- one where permissions, context, and actions are managed at the OS level rather than within individual apps.

---

## What This Means for Airbnb

Chesky has already begun making Airbnb's infrastructure agent-friendly. The company has updated its APIs and developer platform to support agentic workflows, allowing AI systems to browse, book, and manage travel arrangements on behalf of users. Chesky described this as preparing Airbnb for a future where the primary interface is not a screen but an agent that acts on a user's behalf.

The company is also exploring how its platform could become a destination for agentic travel planning -- where an AI assistant could negotiate, book, and manage multi-leg travel without human intervention at each step.

---

## Broader Industry Trend

Chesky's argument reflects a growing view among tech executives that the current app-centric computing model is ill-suited for agentic AI. Several companies, including Apple with its Intelligence platform and Microsoft with its Copilot runtime, are moving toward OS-level AI integration.

The debate centers on whether existing mobile operating systems can be extended to support agents or whether a new abstraction layer is needed. Chesky's position is that the latter is true -- that truly agent-native computing requires starting from different first principles.
