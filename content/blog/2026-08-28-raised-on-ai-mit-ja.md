# Raised on AI: Children of the Agent Age

MIT Technology Review examines how children born today will grow up in a world fundamentally shaped by AI agents and chatbots. The article explores the long-term implications for a generation that will never know a time without these technologies.

---

## Digital Footprints from Birth

The article describes parents creating digital identities for their children before they can walk—setting up email accounts, plastering photos across platforms, and building digital footprints that will persist for decades. Children today are growing up with AI assistants that know their preferences, habits, and routines.

For this generation, interacting with AI is not a novelty but a baseline assumption about how information and services work.

---

## The Agent-Native Generation

Unlike older adults who adapted to AI later in life, children today will develop cognitive patterns shaped by conversational AI from infancy. The article explores research on how this might affect attention spans, expectations of responsiveness, and assumptions about intelligence and assistance.

The piece also raises questions about dependency: when every homework question can be answered instantly, what becomes of the struggle that often drives learning?

---

## What This Means for Developers

For AI developers, this generation represents both an opportunity and a responsibility. Products designed today will shape how this generation thinks about intelligence, agency, and the relationship between humans and machines.

The article suggests developers should consider not just what their AI can do, but what mental models children will form when interacting with these systems from early childhood.
