# Microsoft is openly competing with OpenAI, Anthropic more than ever

Microsoft pitched its own homegrown AI models, harnesses, and even a Mythos competitor to Wall Street on Wednesday, marking a significant shift in the company's public positioning around its AI strategy. Where Microsoft once positioned itself primarily as a cloud infrastructure partner for AI labs, it is now clearly presenting itself as a direct competitor to OpenAI and Anthropic in the foundation model market.

---

## The pitch to investors

In a presentation to investors, Microsoft executives outlined plans for continued growth built on proprietary AI capabilities. The company showcased its in-house model family, which includes reasoning models and multimodal systems designed to compete directly with offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic.

The presentation included what analysts interpreted as a direct challenge to Anthropic's enterprise positioning — Microsoft emphasized its ability to offer integrated AI solutions across the enterprise software stack, from Teams to Azure, with tighter integration than standalone model providers can achieve.

---

## A changed relationship

The presentation marks a clear departure from the narrative Microsoft maintained during its early partnership with OpenAI. Previously, Microsoft positioned itself as the preferred deployment platform for OpenAI's models, offering Azure-exclusive integration and co-marketing. The relationship was framed as complementary rather than competitive.

The shift reflects the maturation of the AI market. As foundation models have become commodity infrastructure, cloud providers that once relied on third-party AI models are investing in proprietary alternatives that preserve margin and reduce dependency.

---

## Implications for the AI ecosystem

Microsoft's competitive positioning has significant implications for AI startups. Companies that built businesses on the assumption of Microsoft's neutrality — using Azure as a neutral platform while deploying models from multiple providers — may need to reconsider as Microsoft pushes its own model family.

The Mythos reference in particular suggests Microsoft is building out a full stack from hardware through applications, similar to the vertical integration strategy pioneered by Google's TPU program. For AI developers, this raises questions about lock-in and the long-term viability of platform-agnostic development approaches.
