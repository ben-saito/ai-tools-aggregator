# TechCrunch Publishes Guide to Essential AI Terms: From AGI to Neural Networks

TechCrunch has released a comprehensive glossary of fundamental AI terminology, addressing the growing confusion around industry jargon as AI adoption accelerates across sectors. The guide covers essential concepts including artificial general intelligence (AGI), AI agents, chain-of-thought reasoning, and model distillation — terms that have become standard in industry discourse but often lack clear public explanations.

---

## Why a Glossary Now?

The rapid emergence of AI applications has outpaced public understanding of basic terminology. Concepts that once lived exclusively in research papers now appear in mainstream product announcements, regulatory discussions, and workplace conversations. The TechCrunch guide aims to bridge this knowledge gap with concise, accessible definitions written for a technically curious but non-specialist audience.

The timing reflects broader industry concerns about AI literacy. As AI systems become embedded in hiring, healthcare, finance, and creative industries, understanding terminology has shifted from optional knowledge to practical necessity.

---

## Key Terms Explained

**AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)** refers to AI systems that match or exceed human capability across most cognitive tasks. The term remains contested — OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has described it as "the equivalent of a median human that you could hire as a co-worker," though no system currently meets this bar. The ambiguity has regulatory implications, as different definitions of AGI trigger different compliance obligations.

**AI Agents** describe programs that use AI to perform multi-step tasks autonomously — beyond simple chatbot responses. Examples include systems that file expenses, book travel, or maintain codebases without continuous human oversight. The TechCrunch guide distinguishes agents from standard AI assistants by their ability to take actions across multiple steps and external systems.

**Chain-of-Thought Reasoning** involves breaking complex problems into intermediate steps, improving output quality at the cost of processing time. This technique has become standard in frontier model deployments, particularly for logic and coding tasks.

**Distillation** is a technique for transferring knowledge from large models to smaller, more efficient ones. A "teacher" model generates outputs that train a "student" model, preserving performance while reducing computational requirements. The guide notes that GPT-4 Turbo likely emerged from distillation of the larger GPT-4, and that unauthorized distillation from competitor models typically violates terms of service.

**Mixture of Experts (MoE)** architectures split neural networks into specialized sub-networks ("experts"), activating only relevant pathways for each task. This approach reduces computational overhead while maintaining model capacity — a design choice increasingly visible in frontier systems.

**Model Context Protocol (MCP)** is an open standard developed by Anthropic that enables AI models to connect to external tools and data sources through a unified interface. The guide compares MCP to a USB-C port for AI — a standardized connection point replacing proprietary integrations.

## The Hallucination Problem

The glossary dedicates substantial attention to hallucination — the phenomenon where AI systems generate confident but incorrect information. The guide attributes this to training data gaps and highlights ongoing work on specialized (vertical) models as one mitigation approach. Hallucination remains a critical reliability concern for high-stakes AI applications in healthcare, legal, and financial contexts.

---

## Neural Networks and Deep Learning Fundamentals

The guide covers foundational architecture concepts, explaining how deep learning enables models to identify important characteristics in data without explicit human feature engineering. Neural networks — multi-layered algorithmic structures — learn patterns from errors through iterative adjustment, a process underlying all modern generative AI systems.

Diffusion models, which slowly destroy data structure by adding noise then reversing the process, power most contemporary image, music, and text generation. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which use competing generator and discriminator networks, remain important for realistic data synthesis despite newer architectures.
