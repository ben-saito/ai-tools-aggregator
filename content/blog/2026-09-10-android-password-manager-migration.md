# Android Password Manager Migration: Google Enables Secure Login Transfers

Google has announced that Android can now securely migrate your logins between password managers. This new capability addresses a long-standing pain point for users who want to switch password managers without losing their saved credentials.

---

## The Problem: Locked-In Users

Previously, switching password managers on Android meant manually exporting and re-entering hundreds of login credentials. Users who wanted to move from one service to another had no standardized way to transfer their data securely. This created friction that discouraged competition and innovation in the password manager space.

---

## How the Migration Works

The new Android feature uses a standardized, encrypted transfer protocol. When you initiate a migration:

1. Both password managers must support the new API
2. You authorize the transfer on your device
3. Credentials are encrypted and transferred directly between apps
4. No data leaves your device unencrypted

Google says the encryption keys never leave your device, making this approach more secure than cloud-based export/import workflows.

---

## Current Support and Limitations

App support is slim right now, but Google says more password managers are coming. The initial rollout targets major providers, with smaller services expected to add support in the coming months.

The feature is built into Android's credential manager framework, which Google has been expanding as part of its broader security strategy. This follows similar moves by Apple, which introduced password migration tools in iOS.

---

## Why This Matters for Security

Password managers remain one of the most effective tools against phishing and credential stuffing attacks. By making it easier to switch services, this update could:

- Increase competition in the password manager market
- Encourage more users to adopt password managers
- Reduce the use of weak, repeated passwords
- Lower the risk of credential-related breaches
