# Runlayer Sues Rippling Over Alleged Theft of MCP Gateway Product Idea

MCP startup Runlayer has filed a lawsuit against HR and IT platform provider Rippling, alleging that Rippling evaluated Runlayer's MCP gateway product and then proceeded to build a competing version itself, according to TechCrunch.

---

## The Model Context Protocol and Gateway Products

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) has emerged as a key integration standard for connecting AI agents with external tools, data sources, and enterprise systems. MCP gateways serve as intermediaries that manage how AI agents authenticate, request data, and execute actions across multiple connected services.

Runlayer built a gateway product designed to help enterprises deploy MCP-based integrations securely. The lawsuit alleges that during due diligence — including technical evaluation sessions — Rippling gained access to Runlayer's product architecture and go-to-market approach, then used that information to build a competing product.

---

## Implications for Enterprise AI Procurement

The dispute highlights IP protection challenges in the AI tooling space. As MCP adoption grows, the boundary between legitimate evaluation and IP theft becomes legally and commercially significant. For enterprises, this case may prompt more careful scrutiny of vendor evaluation processes and clearer contractual boundaries around product confidentiality.

The lawsuit also underscores the tension between established platform companies and specialized AI tooling startups. Rippling, as a major HR and IT platform provider, has both the distribution reach to quickly commoditize specialized tools and the customer relationships to gain access to startup products under the guise of potential partnership.

---

## What Comes Next

The outcome of this case could establish precedents for how AI tooling IP is protected in enterprise sales processes. If Runlayer prevails, it could lead to stronger contractual protections and more cautious evaluation practices. If Rippling wins, it may encourage larger platforms to engage more aggressively with specialized AI tooling startups.
