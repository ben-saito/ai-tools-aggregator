# Apple Expands Trade Secrets Lawsuit Against OpenAI — Seeks Preliminary Injunction to Block Device Development

Apple is escalating its legal battle with OpenAI. In a new court filing made public on August 4, 2026, Apple requested a preliminary injunction to prevent OpenAI from developing AI-powered hardware products based on Apple's proprietary technology. The company also revealed that additional former employees may have been involved in taking confidential data.

---

## Expanded Scope: More Ex-Employees Implicated

The original lawsuit, filed in July, named two former Apple employees — senior systems engineer Chang Liu and Chief Hardware Officer Tang Yew Tan — as defendants. Apple's new filing now suggests the scope of potential trade secrets theft may be wider than initially alleged.

According to the filing, Apple is requesting expedited discovery from the accused employees, OpenAI and its foundation, and **io**, the device startup co-founded by Apple's former lead designer Jony Ive. io is developing a new AI hardware device, and Apple suspects the company may be using Apple's confidential technical information in its development work.

---

## The Core Allegation: Systematic Exfiltration

At the heart of the case is the allegation that OpenAI, through its Apple-born executives, orchestrated a systematic effort to recruit Apple engineers and extract sensitive technical information. Apple's complaint alleges that OpenAI's hardware leadership specifically asked job candidates during Apple interviews to reveal details about Apple's chip architecture and unreleased product roadmaps.

The preliminary injunction Apple is seeking would halt OpenAI's device development while the lawsuit proceeds — a significant escalation that could impact OpenAI's rumored hardware ambitions, including the companion device being developed in partnership with Jony Ive's io.

---

## OpenAI's Position

OpenAI has denied Apple's allegations and is contesting the lawsuit. The company has not publicly commented on the specific claims in the new filing. The case is proceeding in California federal court.

---

## Why It Matters for Developers

This lawsuit highlights the intensifying competition over AI hardware and the sensitivity of proprietary chip designs. For developers working in AI infrastructure, the case underscores the importance of clean hiring practices when recruiting talent from competitor organizations. If Apple succeeds in obtaining an injunction, it could delay OpenAI's hardware ambitions and set a precedent for how trade secrets are protected in AI partnerships.
