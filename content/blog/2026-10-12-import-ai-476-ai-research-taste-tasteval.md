# AI Systems Are Developing Good Research Taste, New Benchmark Shows

---

AI systems are developing more of the creative intuitions that human researchers describe as "good scientific taste," according to a new benchmark called TasteVal from P-Zero Research. The benchmark measures how well AI systems can conduct eight tasks representative of frontier AI development, spanning pretraining data curation, model fine-tuning, preference modeling, and robustness to adversarial prompts.

## What TasteVal Tests

TasteVal defines research taste as the ability to pick interesting problems to solve, design experiments, and interpret experimental results. The benchmark evaluates how well a model iteratively designs experiments and draws conclusions from their outcomes, checking results against a human baseline from 24 experts at OpenAI, Google DeepMind, NVIDIA, and Microsoft Research.

## Key Results

**Research taste is doubling every 3 months**: The experimental research taste of frontier models has doubled every 3.0 months since December 2025 (95% CI: 1.7–5.0 months).

**Opus 5.5 exceeds human baseline**: The best model, Opus 5.5, significantly exceeds the human baseline with a compute multiplier of 2.30x, at roughly 1/30 of the expert baseliners' average cost per attempt.

**The slope changed around late 2025**: Research taste appeared to grow more slowly in prior years, with the rate of improvement accelerating at the end of 2025.

## Why RSI Matters

In analysis of recursive self-improvement (RSI), systems with better taste can more effectively identify promising research directions. The better their taste, the higher the chance of productive RSI. This benchmark quantifies a capability that has been assumed but never formally measured.
