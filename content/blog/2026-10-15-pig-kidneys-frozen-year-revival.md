# Pig kidneys frozen for a year revived in MIT bioengineering breakthrough

---

Pig kidneys have been successfully revived after being frozen for a year, a breakthrough that could eventually lead to human organ banking. The research, published by MIT scientists, demonstrates that vitrification and revival of complex mammalian tissues is achievable at scale.

The technique involves cooling organs to extremely low temperatures while preventing ice crystal formation, a process that has historically damaged delicate tissue structures. The MIT team developed a new cryoprotectant solution that better preserves cellular integrity during the freeze-thaw cycle.

"We're seeing survival rates and function that we haven't achieved before," said the lead researcher. "The kidneys not only survived the process but maintained their ability to filter blood and produce urine."

The implications for human medicine are significant. Currently, thousands of patients die each year waiting for organ transplants due to a shortage of suitable donors. If human kidneys could be banked similarly, it might eventually become possible to match patients with compatible organs stored for extended periods.

The research is still in early stages, with studies focusing on pig models before any human trials could be considered. The team estimates that clinical applications could be decades away, but the proof of concept represents a crucial step forward.

Beyond transplantation, the technology could enable patients to bank their own organs for future use, potentially eliminating rejection risks associated with donor organs. The concept of elective organ preservation mirrors egg and sperm freezing but applies to more complex tissue structures.

Safety studies and refinement of the revival process continue, with the team working to scale up the technique for larger organs and eventually human-sized kidneys.
