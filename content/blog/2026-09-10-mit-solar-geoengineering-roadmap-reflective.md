# MIT Technology Review: Reflective's solar geoengineering road map

MIT Technology Review reports that Reflective, a San Francisco nonprofit, has published a detailed road map outlining the experiments, studies, and infrastructure needed to make informed decisions about solar geoengineering deployment.

---

## What is solar geoengineering?

Solar geoengineering refers to proposed techniques for reflecting a small fraction of sunlight back into space, potentially reducing global temperatures. Approaches include stratospheric aerosol injection (mimicking the cooling effect of volcanic eruptions) and marine cloud brightening.

While the science has advanced significantly, there is no agreed framework for when or how such interventions should be evaluated — or who gets to decide.

---

## What Reflective's road map proposes

The report identifies critical knowledge gaps that must be filled before any deployment decision. Key elements include:

- **Atmospheric modeling improvements** — current climate models lack the resolution to predict regional impacts of solar geoengineering
- **Field experiments** — small-scale outdoor experiments to validate laboratory results
- **Governance frameworks** — international coordination mechanisms for a technology with global effects
- **Monitoring infrastructure** — systems to detect and attribute changes in precipitation patterns, monsoon behavior, and other climate variables

The road map is designed to be a planning tool for researchers and policymakers, not a deployment blueprint.

---

## Why this matters for AI and technology

The Reflective report highlights a recurring challenge at the intersection of powerful new technologies and global governance: the pace of technical capability outstripping institutional readiness. The tools needed to evaluate solar geoengineering — atmospheric modeling, large-scale simulation, sensor networks — are increasingly driven by AI and machine learning.

Whether AI accelerates the science of geoengineering faster than governance frameworks can develop remains an open question.
