# New Survey Maps Which AI Policies Have Cross-Party Appeal — and Which Do Not

---

## Research identifies the AI regulations voters want regardless of political affiliation

A new survey conducted ahead of the 2026 midterm elections has mapped which artificial intelligence policies attract support across the political spectrum and which remain deeply partisan. The research focused on identifying AI governance approaches that could form the basis for bipartisan legislation, an increasingly rare commodity in a polarized environment.

The top-performing policies in terms of cross-party appeal centered on AI transparency and accountability measures. Voters across party lines expressed strong support for requiring AI systems to disclose when they are interacting with humans versus other AI agents. Mandatory bias auditing for AI systems used in hiring, lending, and housing decisions also polled strongly across demographic groups.

---

## Policies with Broadest Support

The survey found that AI disclosure requirements consistently outperformed direct regulation of AI capabilities. Requiring companies to label AI-generated content received support from approximately 80% of respondents across all parties. Similarly, rules requiring human review of AI decisions in high-stakes contexts like insurance underwriting and medical diagnosis scored high marks across the aisle.

Worker displacement assistance for communities affected by AI-driven job automation also attracted majority support from independents and members of both major parties — though the specific policy design (retraining programs versus universal basic income supplements) remained contested. The finding suggests that framing AI policy as labor policy may be a viable path to bipartisan coalitions.

---

## Policies with Lowest Support

Unsurprisingly, direct government intervention in AI model development — such as mandated compute caps or capability restrictions — faced significant partisan opposition. However, the survey revealed an unexpected finding: even policies framed as safety measures received pushback when respondents were informed they could slow down AI development. Voters who identified as economic nationalists opposed capability restrictions primarily on competitive grounds, while voters who identified as progressive expressed concern about unintended consequences for innovation.

The deepest divisions centered on immigration-linked AI policy. Proposals to restrict visa programs for highly skilled AI researchers were supported by self-described nationalist-leaning respondents but strongly opposed by the majority across other voter segments.

---

## The Political Path Forward

Researchers noted that the polling data suggests a potential bipartisan AI governance agenda exists, but it requires careful framing. Policies that emphasize transparency, accountability, and worker protection are easier to sell across party lines than those framed as limiting AI capabilities or restricting the technology sector. The data implies that AI safety advocates may have more legislative success positioning their priorities as consumer and worker protection rather than as technology restrictions.

The challenge is that many of the most consequential AI governance questions — international arms control for AI systems, compute governance frameworks, and frontier model oversight — are precisely the areas where partisan consensus is hardest to achieve.
