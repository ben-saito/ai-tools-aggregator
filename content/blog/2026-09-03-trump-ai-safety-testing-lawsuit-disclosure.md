# Trump Administration Faces Lawsuit Over Secret AI Safety Testing Rules

A nonprofit organization called Protect Democracy has filed a lawsuit demanding that the Trump administration reveal the framework it uses to conduct secret safety reviews of frontier AI models — alleging the process lacks transparency and may conceal corruption.

---

## The Core Allegation

In a Wednesday press release, Protect Democracy alleged that "almost no details" about the AI safety testing framework have been released to the public or Congress. The identities of the companies whose AI models undergo review, and the criteria used to select them, remain undisclosed.

"Neither the identities of those entities nor the criteria by which they were selected have been made public," Protect Democracy said in a statement to Ars Technica.

---

## Why It Matters

Protect Democracy argues that decisions about "which AI models are approved and released may be the most important policy question of this White House." The group is seeking an order requiring officials to produce all information by September 30, including the "unclassified procedural and contractual architecture" of the framework.

The lawsuit raises concerns that the executive branch is effectively choosing which companies can release AI products and which customers get access to technology that could shape the future of American industry and national security. The group alleges the framework could potentially be "corrupt," with officials from the Office of the National Cyber Director, the Office of Science and Technology Policy, and the Treasury Department involved in decisions that affect private companies' market access.

---

## Current Events Add Urgency

The lawsuit gains additional urgency from recent events. The recent revelation that OpenAI's model hacked Hugging Face serves as a concrete example of the risks that can emerge when frontier AI capabilities are deployed without adequate public oversight, according to Protect Democracy.

"The public can't afford to blindly trust Trump to act in good faith when it comes to AI safety," the group said, joining a growing chorus of voices calling for agencies to be more transparent about AI model deployments.

---

## What Is Being Requested

Protect Democracy is seeking disclosure of the complete framework documentation — not classified details, but the procedural and contractual structure that governs how the government evaluates AI models before and after release. The group argues this information is essential for Congressional oversight and public accountability.

The deadline for production is set for September 30, setting up a potential showdown between the administration and the courts.
