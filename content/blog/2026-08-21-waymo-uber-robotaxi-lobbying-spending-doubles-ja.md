# Waymo Doubles Lobbying Spending to $2 Million as Robotaxi Battle With Uber Intensifies

Alphabet-owned Waymo spent more than $1 million on federal lobbying between April and June 2026, more than double its year-earlier outlay, according to lobbying disclosures. The company is now pushing aggressively for a federal framework to accelerate the rollout of fully driverless commercial taxi services, as it battles Uber for control of the emerging robotaxi market.

---

## The Lobbying War

Waymo's lobbying spend rose 93 percent in the first six months of 2026 compared to the prior year, bringing its total to slightly more than $2 million. The company has hired law firm Greenberg Traurig to lobby on its behalf in Washington, adding to a roster of other Washington firms. It has outspent both Uber and Amazon's Zoox in the District of Columbia, where it is pressing local regulators to allow robotaxi services.

In New York state alone, Waymo has spent more than $500,000 this year, more than double Uber's lobbying expenditure in the state, as it seeks a foothold in one of the largest taxi markets in the United States.

---

## Competing Visions for Ride-Hailing

Waymo wants a faster route to fully driverless commercial services, while Uber is advocating a staggered rollout in which robotaxis operate alongside human drivers. Their diverging legislative strategies have contributed to their relationship souring. The Financial Times reported that Waymo was exploring options to exit its Uber partnership in Austin and Atlanta.

"They've both stepped up lobbying and are pushing up against each other, which now appears to be hastening their divorce," said Walter Piecyk, an analyst at LightShed Partners.

Both companies are lobbying in several US states and Washington DC, after pushback stalled their expansion to markets including New York and Chicago, as politicians and labour groups warn the technology could displace drivers and undercut existing services.
