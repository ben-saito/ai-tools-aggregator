# Restate Raises $20M Series A to Bring Durable Infrastructure to AI Agents

Berlin-based Restate, a startup building durable execution infrastructure for multi-step workflows, has closed a $20 million Series A led by Singular, with participation from Redpoint. The funding comes as AI agent adoption drives surging demand for infrastructure that can handle long-running, fault-tolerant workflows.

Restate was founded by veterans of Apache Flink, the open-source stream processing framework. Co-creator Ewan, who previously served as CTO of Data Artisans (acquired by Alibaba in 2019 and rebranded as Ververica), built Restate's durable execution engine from the ground up rather than layering on top of an external database.

The core problem Restate addresses is workflow durability. Traditional software crashes are recoverable, but AI agent workflows — which run longer and take unpredictable paths — require infrastructure that can track exactly what happened and resume from failure points without losing state. "You need to make sure you track exactly what you do to make it reproducible and have a consistent outcome," Ewan told TechCrunch.

Unlike competitors that rely on external databases for durability, Restate built its own storage, replication, and redundancy layers. The company claims this architecture makes it significantly faster and more lightweight than alternatives.

The most direct competitor is Temporal, which announced a $550 million Series E at a $12.55 billion valuation earlier this month. Despite being much smaller, Restate has already attracted high-profile customers including vibe coding platform Replit, along with Fortune 500 companies in the financial sector.

The funding will be used to expand the go-to-market team, hire additional engineers, and open a San Francisco Bay Area office to be closer to current and prospective customers.

Ewan sees durable infrastructure becoming as fundamental as databases. "If we fast-forward a couple of years, it's one of those tools, like a database, that I think pretty much any company will have a use case for, no matter their shape and size," he said.
