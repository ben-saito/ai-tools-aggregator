# TerraPower Moves to Position Nuclear Reactors as AI Data Center Power Solution

As AI data centers consume ever-larger amounts of electricity, nuclear power startups are positioning themselves as a key solution. Bill Gates-founded TerraPower is reportedly planning to announce its first data center project in 2026, according to Bloomberg.

---

## The Nuclear-Data Center Match

The conversation around data centers and power has intensified as AI workloads drive unprecedented electricity demand. Nuclear power offers a significant advantage over solar and wind — it provides dispatchable, carbon-free power that can run continuously regardless of weather conditions.

However, nuclear reactors operate most efficiently at steady, full-tilt output. This creates a challenge for data centers whose loads fluctuate significantly during AI training runs and inference bursts.

---

## Small Modular Reactors and the Nuclear Advantage

To address this mismatch, small modular reactors (SMRs) are gaining attention. SMRs offer faster ramping capabilities — able to adjust output at about 10% of rated capacity per minute, according to Naval Research Laboratory data. This makes them more flexible than traditional large-scale nuclear plants for matching variable data center loads.

Tech companies are already committing to nuclear power procurement. Meta has agreed to purchase eight of TerraPower's Natrium reactors — a sign that AI data center operators see nuclear as a viable long-term power strategy.

---

## The Economics of Nuclear for AI

The case for nuclear power at data centers is fundamentally economic. Nuclear plants have the highest capital costs of any generation technology, but the lowest operating costs once built. For hyperscalers planning 10-20 year infrastructure investments, nuclear offers price stability uncoupled from fossil fuel markets.

The nuclear industry argues that the combination of high capital costs and the need for steady output makes data center load actually well-suited to nuclear — unlike intermittent renewables that struggle to match variable demand without extensive storage.
