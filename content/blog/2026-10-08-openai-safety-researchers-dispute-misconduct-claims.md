# Fired OpenAI safety researchers dispute misconduct claims, warn of chilling effect

---

Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak, and Mikita Balesni — the three safety researchers that OpenAI fired last week — published an open letter denying the company's claims that they mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures. They warned that their dismissal signals a chilling effect that will have ripple effects across the company's culture.

In the open letter, the researchers stated: "We have become concerned that internal and external communications around our firing have made our former colleagues afraid to speak openly about safety concerns."

The dispute centers on whether the researchers improperly shared sensitive information externally, or whether their termination reflects a broader shift in OpenAI's approach to safety research and internal dissent.

The incident has drawn attention to the tension between commercial pressures and safety research within frontier AI labs. OpenAI has faced increasing scrutiny over its commitment to safety as it moves toward more commercial products and faces competitive pressure from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and other players.

The dismissed researchers — all with established reputations in AI safety — argued that the company's narrative around their termination was itself having a chilling effect on internal discussion. They noted that the communications around the firing created "a signal to everyone else that speaking up has consequences."

The TechCrunch article provides context on the three researchers' backgrounds, their contributions to OpenAI's safety work, and the broader implications for the AI safety community as the industry navigates questions about research independence and corporate governance.
