# Fired OpenAI Safety Researchers Dispute Misconduct Claims, Warn of Chilling Effect

---

Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak, and Mikita Balesni — the three safety researchers OpenAI fired last week — have published an open letter denying the company's claims that they mishandled sensitive information. The researchers allege their dismissal will have ripple effects across OpenAI's culture and beyond.

---

## The Core Dispute

The three researchers were dismissed after allegedly sharing confidential company information with a third-party AI safety organization. OpenAI stated they violated company policy by "accessing and handling sensitive company information." However, the researchers' open letter directly contradicts this narrative.

"We have become concerned that internal and external communications around our firing have made our former colleagues afraid to speak and operate in ways that, until last week, were an integral part of working at OpenAI," the researchers wrote in their letter to OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee, Safety Advisory Group, and Mission Advisory Council.

---

## Implications for AI Safety Culture

The dispute arrives at a sensitive moment for OpenAI, which has faced ongoing scrutiny about its approach to AI safety. The researchers' letter suggests a climate of fear has taken hold, potentially chilling the kinds of internal debate essential for identifying and addressing AI risks.

The letter calls into question whether OpenAI's internal safety processes can function effectively when employees face career-ending consequences for raising concerns. If experienced safety researchers believe they cannot operate freely, the company's ability to self-correct before deploying dangerous capabilities may be compromised.

---

## Broader Industry Context

This incident reflects broader tensions within the AI safety community. As AI labs push toward increasingly powerful systems, the line between legitimate safety research and inappropriate information sharing remains contested. The researchers' move to go public with their version of events underscores how internal grievance processes often fail to resolve disputes transparently.

The OpenAI Safety and Security Committee — now the recipient of the researchers' letter — faces pressure to respond publicly. Whether their response will satisfy external observers remains to be seen.
