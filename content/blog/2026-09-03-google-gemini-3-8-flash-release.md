# Google Releases Gemini 3.8 Flash, Its Third Flash Model in Six Weeks

Google has released Gemini 3.8 Flash, the third Flash-tier model the company has shipped in just six weeks, continuing its rapid iteration pace at the Flash performance level.

---

## Two Flavors of Flash

Gemini 3.8 Flash comes in two variations. The standard Flash model is described by Google as a "workhorse" suitable for agentic tasks, software development, and general-purpose applications. The second variant, Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, replaces the 3.5 version and targets cybersecurity-specific workloads — a niche but increasingly important segment as AI systems become more deeply integrated into security-sensitive infrastructure.

For developers, Google is offering API access at an "introductory rate" through the end of the year: $0.00015 per million tokens for input, with the rate increasing after the promotional period ends.

---

## Benchmark Performance

Google's own benchmark numbers show Gemini 3.8 Flash competing with or outperforming larger and more expensive models on several key metrics. The model now sits at the top of the DeepSWE leaderboard, which measures a model's ability to solve complex software engineering problems — a meaningful benchmark for developer-facing AI tools.

However, the picture is more mixed on agentic computer use. While Gemini 3.8 Flash improves over its 3.7 Flash predecessor on the OSWorld-2.0 test of agentic computer use, it remains significantly behind the market leader, Claude 3.5, in this category. Computer use — the ability for an AI model to interact with desktop interfaces, navigate systems, and execute multi-step tasks — has been a persistent challenge for Google's Gemini family.

---

## The Flash Cadence Strategy

The rapid release of three Flash models in six weeks represents an unusually compressed update cycle. Google appears to be using the Flash tier as a testing ground for incremental improvements that may eventually inform the Pro and Ultra lines. Meanwhile, Pro model updates have reportedly paused, suggesting Google is concentrating its resources on the Flash pipeline for now.

The $0.00015 per million tokens introductory pricing is notably competitive and suggests Google is aggressive about capturing developer workloads in the current crowded AI API market.
