# Omilia Raises $67M Series B to Scale Its Multi-Tool Customer Support Platform

Omilia, an Athens-based company that has been automating voice calls and customer support interactions since 2002, has raised a $67 million Series B growth round led by Expedition Growth Capital. The company disclosed on August 6, 2026 that it has grown its annual recurring revenue 10x since its last fundraise in 2020, reaching $60 million ARR.

---

## The Case Against One-Size-Fits-All AI

The customer support automation space has seen an influx of generative AI-native startups — Sierra, Decagon, and others — that lead with large language models for every interaction. Omilia CEO Dimitris Vassos argues this approach is inefficient for the majority of inbound queries that involve basic, repetitive information retrieval.

「We will use any available weapon to win the battle for customer service. Companies like Sierra, Decagon, etc. identify as generative AI companies. Their sole purpose is to deploy generative AI and limit themselves. You may have a bazooka, but if your enemy is near you, you need a knife. This is the reality of the contact center, where you need multiple tools,」 he said.

Omilia's platform instead deploys a mix of technologies — ranging from simple decision trees to LLMs — depending on the complexity of the query. Basic balance inquiries or password resets don't require a model; nuanced troubleshooting or emotional escalations do.

---

## Self-Learning Agents Across Contact Channels

Omilia has expanded beyond its voice call roots to build self-learning agents that operate across text, chat, and phone channels. The agents adapt based on interaction outcomes, optimizing resolution paths over time without manual rule updates.

The company's key differentiator, according to Vassos, is unit economics: both for Omilia and its customers. The company claims its customers see measurable ROI improvements without the high per-query costs associated with LLM-heavy alternatives.

---

## Funding Context

This is Omilia's second major fundraise after a $20 million round from Grafton Capital in 2020. The company has been profitable in recent years, Vassos said, which reduced the urgency for large infusions while competitors burned capital on compute and model costs.

Expedition Growth Capital, the lead investor, focuses on growth-stage B2B software companies in Europe and the US. Terms of the round were not disclosed.
