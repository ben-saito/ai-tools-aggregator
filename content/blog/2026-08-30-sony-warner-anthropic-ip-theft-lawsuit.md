# Sony Music and Warner Sue Anthropic Over Alleged "Brazan Campaign" of Intellectual Property Theft

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and numerous other music publishers have filed a lawsuit against Anthropic and its co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann, accusing the AI lab of conducting an organized campaign to illegally torrent, scrape, and download copyrighted works to train its AI model Claude.

---

## The Allegations

The lawsuit, filed in late August 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, claims Anthropic engaged in "blatant theft" by using thousands of copyrighted works to train Claude. The case is notable for its breadth — it accuses Anthropic of "flagrant piracy" through illegal torrenting to obtain millions of copies of books, including works containing lyrics and musical compositions.

This is not the first intellectual property lawsuit Anthropic has faced. The same legal team behind this case also represents Concord Music Group and Universal Music Group in a lawsuit filed earlier in 2026, and previously led the Bartz v. Anthropic case, in which a group of authors accused Anthropic of using copyrighted text without permission.

---

## Key Differences from Previous Cases

While the new lawsuit makes similar arguments to earlier cases, there are notable differences. The current lawsuit is particularly broad and builds on existing cases by focusing on the method of acquisition — specifically alleging that Anthropic used torrenting infrastructure to obtain copyrighted materials at scale. The publishers argue this goes beyond simple web scraping and constitutes active copyright infringement through illegal downloading.

---

## Anthropic's Response

Anthropic has denied the allegations. "We disagree with the publishers' claims and we intend to defend ourselves robustly in court," said a company spokesperson in a statement to TechCrunch.

---

## Industry Context

The lawsuit reflects growing tension between AI companies and intellectual property holders over training data. Music publishers and authors have increasingly turned to legal action as AI labs incorporate large-scale copyrighted material into model training pipelines. The outcome of cases like this could establish precedent for how AI companies source training data and whether scale-based copying constitutes fair use or infringement.
