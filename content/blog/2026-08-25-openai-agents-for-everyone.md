# OpenAI Is Building AI Agents for Everyone — The Mass Market Challenge

OpenAI is pushing to bring AI agents from software engineers to everyday consumers, but a significant adoption gap reveals how difficult that transition may be.

---

## From Coders to Everyone Else

OpenAI is building AI agents for everyone. Will everyone use them? That is the central question facing the company as it attempts to expand ChatGPT Work beyond its developer-focused user base.

The challenge is stark: an OpenAI-backed study found that in June, 98% of OpenAI employees were using Codex, the company's agentic coding tool. But just 17% of organizational subscribers and less than 1% of individual subscribers were using it. This near-total internal adoption versus negligible external adoption represents both the opportunity and the obstacle for OpenAI.

OpenAI's ChatGPT Work product was released last month at $20 per month — the company's lowest subscription tier. The product is designed to allow white-collar workers to deploy AI agents that hook LLMs up to the digital workflows used by accountants, investors, doctors, and other professionals whose work is dominated by computers. It is a modified version of Codex, the company's coding agent, retooled for non-engineers.

---

## The Adoption Problem

The disconnect between internal and external usage highlights a fundamental challenge in moving AI agents from developers to mainstream users. For software engineers, a command-line interface was sufficient to change how software is built and deployed. But most people do not use command-line interfaces — there is a reason Windows replaced DOS.

OpenAI's non-engineering workforce initially found Codex "actively hostile" when asked about code and technical readouts meant for software changes. The company spent months between February and now making the tool more general purpose for non-developers.

Industry observers see this as a key challenge for the broader AI industry. Christian Catalini of a16z wrote on the "It's Time to Build" blog that if AI labs cannot rapidly acquire the complementary assets needed to scale AI in the market, value will accrue elsewhere.

---

## The Commercial Calculus

The economic incentive is clear: agents that work for longer stretches burn through more tokens, making them more lucrative per user. If coding has proven lucrative territory for AI labs, reaching new professions is crucial — not just for OpenAI but for the industry at large — to justify the massive investment in training and computation.

Vertical-specific competitors like Harvey (for law) and Clay (for sales) have been targeting these customers with a model-agnostic approach, plugging in whichever AI works best at the time. OpenAI's bet is that its general-purpose agent approach can compete across multiple verticals simultaneously.
