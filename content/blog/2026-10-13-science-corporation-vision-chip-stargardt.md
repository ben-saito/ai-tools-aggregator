# Science Corporation Implants Vision-Restoring Chip in First Stargardt Disease Patient

---

Science Corporation CEO Max Hodak announced that the company has implanted its vision-restoring chip, PRIMA, into its first patient with Stargardt disease, a genetic disorder that causes progressive vision loss typically beginning in childhood. The successful implantation marks a significant milestone in the development of bionic vision restoration for patients who would otherwise face complete blindness.

PRIMA works by replacing the function of the retina, the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye that is damaged in Stargardt disease. The chip is implanted directly onto the retina and works in conjunction with a pair of glasses equipped with a camera. The camera captures visual information and transmits it to the chip, which stimulates the remaining healthy retinal cells to send signals to the optic nerve.

---

## How PRIMA Differs From Previous Bionic Vision Systems

Science Corporation's approach builds on and extends prior work in retinal prosthetics, most notably the Argus II system developed by Second Sight. Unlike earlier systems that provided only crude visual resolution — allowing patients to detect light and dark but not to recognize faces or read — PRIMA is designed to enable much higher acuity vision through a combination of improved electrode design and advanced AI-powered image processing.

The glasses worn by PRIMA patients do not simply relay camera footage to the chip. Instead, an onboard AI processes the visual scene in real time, identifying faces, text, and objects of importance and enhancing those elements before transmitting the signal to the implant. This preprocessing step allows the chip to deliver more useful visual information within its resolution constraints.

---

## Clinical Significance and Path Forward

The Stargardt disease trial follows an earlier trial in patients with dry age-related macular degeneration, the most common cause of blindness in developed countries. Data from the macular degeneration trial showed that patients with PRIMA implants could read letters on an eye chart that they could not read without the device, and could recognize faces with significantly greater accuracy.

For Stargardt disease patients, who typically lose vision earlier in life, the implications are particularly significant. The company plans to enroll additional patients in the trial and is working with regulators to define the pathway toward commercial approval.
