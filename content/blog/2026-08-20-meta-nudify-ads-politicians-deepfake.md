# Meta Ran Ads for an App That Could Nudify Female Politicians

Meta platforms ran advertisements for a mobile application called Kromix that promised to generate non-consensual intimate images of real women, including female US politicians, according to a report by WIRED. The ads, which were removed after WIRED inquired about them, targeted male users and explicitly promised "no restrictions" and that "all the characters are real people."

---

## The Ads and What They Contained

Kromix bills itself as an "AI image styler." One advertisement featured a pornographic video with a deepfake closely resembling a prominent female US politician, shown in front of US flags before transitioning to explicit content. Another ad posed the question "What if she moved?" above a video of the same politician lookalike.

The ads ran for between five and 46 hours and received mostly fewer than 10 impressions. According to Meta's ad library, the campaign consisted of 32 individual advertisements. The Tech Transparency Project, which discovered the ads, noted that they were exclusively targeted to male users.

---

## Platform Enforcement Failures

Apple has struggled to keep similar tools — which violate its policies against nonconsensual intimate imagery and sexually explicit content — off the iOS App Store. San Francisco's city attorney sent Apple a cease-and-desist letter earlier in 2026 demanding the removal of 13 specific apps and that the company stop "aiding and abetting" the sale of explicit deepfake images.

Meta's ad review process approved the Kromix advertisements despite policies prohibiting content that promotes nonconsensual intimate imagery. After WIRED contacted Meta for comment, the ads were removed.

---

## Policy Implications

The incident highlights the challenge that major technology platforms face in moderating AI-generated non-consensual intimate content, even when explicit policies prohibit such material. The ads' use of political figures' likenesses adds a dimension of election interference concern, coming amid reports of the National Republican Senatorial Committee warning AI companies that data centers could affect election outcomes.

Katie Paul, director of the Tech Transparency Project, said the ads represent a broader pattern: "All of the ads that we've seen so far broadly on the nudify issue are exclusively sexualizing and nudifying and putting women in danger."

---

## Context: The Minnesota xAI Ban

This incident follows a separate but related legal development: a federal judge denied xAI's attempt to block Minnesota's first-in-the-nation law banning applications that create non-consensual sexualized images. That law, which targets the "nudify" app category broadly, is set to take effect. The two cases — Meta's ad platform hosting nudify promotions and xAI's challenge to the underlying technology — represent different angles of the same regulatory and ethical problem.
