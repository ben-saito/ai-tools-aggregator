# Meta Acknowledges Muse AI Was "Heavily Inspired" by OpenClaw

Meta has confirmed that its AI assistant Muse is directly inspired by OpenClaw, an open-source AI agent project, after social media users noted striking similarities between the two systems. The acknowledgment came from Friedman, Meta's Chief AI Officer who joined from GitHub alongside former GitHub CEO, in a statement on X.

---

## From Inspiration to Implementation

Friedman stated that the Meta team had "fallen in love with OpenClaw" and wanted Muse to be a similar tool for a broader audience. He clarified that while Muse draws heavily from OpenClaw's design philosophy, "Muse itself was built from scratch."

The comparison went beyond aesthetics. Users noted that Muse's own system files contained filenames and content nearly identical to OpenClaw's workspace files, leading one AI app co-founder to characterize Muse as "LITERALLY OpenClaw for normies" in a viral post on X.

---

## OpenClaw's Rise and Acquisition

OpenClaw gained significant traction in the AI agent community before being acquired by OpenAI earlier this year. Its open-source nature meant that Meta's development team could freely study its architecture and design patterns. The episode illustrates the rapid pace at which AI agent capabilities and design patterns spread through the open-source community.

---

## Implications for AI Development Culture

The Muse/OpenClaw situation highlights a broader question in AI development: how much inspiration from existing systems constitutes original work versus replication. OpenClaw's creator declined to comment on whether any legal or intellectual property concerns exist, but the incident underscores the blurred lines in AI agent design where similar approaches to task automation, workspace management, and tool use naturally converge.
