# Discord Launches Revised Age Verification System After User Backlash

Discord is rolling out a revised age verification system today that replaces its original plan requiring ID uploads or face scans. The new approach gives users more privacy-friendly options after significant backlash to the February proposal.

---

## From ID Scans to Automatic Estimation

Discord's original plan, announced in February, would have required users seeking to access age-restricted servers and channels to either upload government IDs or submit to face scans. The privacy concerns were heightened by a 2025 incident in which hackers stole government IDs from 70,000 Discord users.

The revised system, detailed by Discord CTO and co-founder Stanislav Vishnevskiy, takes a different approach. Instead of requiring verification documents upfront, Discord's system will automatically estimate user ages and place each user into one of three categories: adult (18+), teen (13-17), or "unconfirmed" for cases where there isn't enough data to make a determination.

---

## How the New System Works

For users placed in the teen group, Discord will apply additional protections. Message requests from non-friends will go to a separate inbox for review, and users will receive alerts about friend requests from people they don't share mutual friends or a server with. Sensitive images will also be automatically blurred for teen accounts.

Adults who want to access age-restricted content but are incorrectly placed in the 13-17 age group or marked as "unconfirmed" will have several options to confirm their age without uploading ID documents.

---

## Privacy-Friendly Options

"In February, the only methods we'd planned for to confirm your age were facial age estimation or an ID scan. Based on your feedback, we committed to giving you more options that meet our high privacy standards," Vishnevskiy wrote in the announcement.

Discord estimates that more than 90 percent of users will not be asked to confirm their age at all, as the automatic estimation system should correctly categorize the vast majority of users.

---

## Industry Context

The revised approach reflects broader concerns about data collection practices in social platforms, particularly when it comes to minors. Regulators in multiple jurisdictions have pushed for stronger age verification mechanisms, but the methods remain controversial due to privacy implications.

Discord's shift away from mandatory ID uploads represents a balance between compliance with regulatory expectations and addressing user privacy concerns—a tension that continues to shape how platforms handle age-restricted content.
