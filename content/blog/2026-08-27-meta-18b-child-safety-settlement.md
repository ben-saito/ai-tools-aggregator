# Meta Settles Child Safety Claims for $18B; Florida Rejects Deal as "Peanuts"

Meta has agreed to a landmark settlement totaling nearly $18 billion to resolve claims by US states that its platforms harmed children through addictive features and inadequate safety measures.

---

## The Settlement Breakdown

The multistate settlement provides up to $16.7 billion for 47 states and the District of Columbia, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands, and Puerto Rico. Texas simultaneously announced a separate $1 billion settlement of its own lawsuit against Meta, bringing the total potential payment to states to nearly $18 billion.

A clause in the settlement would reduce Meta's payment by $5 billion if other major social media companies do not agree to similar terms.

---

## New Restrictions on minors

Under the settlement, Meta agreed to impose a default two-hour daily time limit for users under 18 across Facebook and Instagram. This limit is cumulative across both apps, and time spent scrolling counts even if someone has multiple accounts.

Minors will receive prompts after every 15 minutes of continuous screen time. Meta will also block notifications during school hours and restrict app usage during critical overnight hours.

---

## Florida Calls the Deal "Peanuts"

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier rejected the multistate settlement, posting on X that "the payouts are peanuts compared to the profound harm" Meta's platforms have caused children. New Mexico, also not part of the settlement, recently won judgments requiring Meta to pay $375 million in civil penalties and $567 million for a public nuisance abatement fund.

The advocacy group Public Citizen criticized the settlement as insufficient, noting that "billions of dollars sounds enormous to ordinary Americans, but for Meta, spread over a decade, it is hardly the kind of penalty that will force one of the world's wealthiest Big Tech companies to rethink how it does business."

---

## Legal Context

The states alleged that Meta designed its products to foster compulsive use by children and failed to warn users of addiction and mental health risks. Meta currently uses ID checks and face analysis to verify user ages.

The states and Meta urged a judge to approve the 10-year settlement in a filing in US District Court for the Northern District of California.
