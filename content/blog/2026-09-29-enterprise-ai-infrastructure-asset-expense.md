# Making AI an Asset, Not an Expense: The Enterprise AI Infrastructure Shift

As AI moves from isolated experiments to production portfolios, enterprises are confronting a fundamental question: should AI infrastructure be treated as a capital investment or a consumption expense? The answer is reshaping how companies budget for, deploy, and think about artificial intelligence.

---

## From Experimentation to Production

For the past several years, most enterprise AI spending looked the same: token-by-token payments to cloud API providers, with no long-term commitments and maximum flexibility. This model made sense during experimentation. Teams were still figuring out which use cases worked, which models performed best, and how to integrate AI into existing systems.

But as AI moves from pilots into production — IT assistants, customer-service agents, research tools, and business-process automation — the economics change. Usage becomes steady, predictable, and recurring across multiple models and data sources. At that point, the question shifts from "which model is cheapest" to "how do we run AI economically and predictably at scale."

---

## The Consumption Pricing Problem

Deloitte's 2026 State of AI in the Enterprise report captures the transition underway. Worker access to AI rose 5% in 2025, and the share of companies with at least 40% of their AI projects in production is expected to double within six months. When AI becomes a portfolio of always-on workloads rather than a collection of experiments, the economics change fundamentally.

Consumption pricing gives teams flexibility and limits commitment. But when usage is steady, large enough to keep capacity productive, and business-critical, leaders need to ask a different question: at what point does it make more sense to own the infrastructure than to rent it by the token?

---

## The Asset Mindset

The shift is not abstract. It mirrors the cloud-versus-on-premises debate — but with a twist specific to AI: inference costs do not decline with scale the way traditional software hosting costs do. A service that serves 10 million queries per month costs proportionally more to run than one serving 1 million, with no natural economies of scale.

This reality is driving a new generation of infrastructure choices. Enterprises are evaluating dedicated GPU clusters, fine-tuned smaller models that run cheaper at inference time, and hybrid approaches that use frontier models only for tasks that genuinely require them.

The next 12 to 18 months will determine which model wins: consumption-based AI or owned AI infrastructure. For companies with predictable, high-volume AI workloads, the math increasingly favors ownership.
