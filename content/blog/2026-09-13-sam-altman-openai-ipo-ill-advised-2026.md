# Sam Altman says OpenAI would be 'ill-advised' to go public in 2026

OpenAI has filed confidentially for an IPO, but the company will not be going public this year, according to CEO Sam Altman.

---

## Altman: IPO "ill-advised" given current AI safety climate

Altman was interviewed recently by Fortune editor in chief Alyson Shontell. Amid the fallout from the OpenAI-HuggingFace hack and broader discussions about AI safety, Shontell asked whether OpenAI still feels pressure to "move really fast."

"We're not rushing into an IPO," Altman said. "I actually think that given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public."

Instead, he insisted that OpenAI will go public "when we're ready, which is when the business is ready, when we feel ready from what the moment is like in society with this technology." When pressed on whether that means the IPO is not happening in 2026, Altman confirmed it would not.

---

## Background: OpenAI's IPO filing

The New York Times reported in June that although OpenAI had hired bankers and lawyers with the goal of going public in the third or fourth quarter of 2026, the company was leaning toward 2027 due to the volatility of tech stocks and its own financial position.

The delay reflects broader uncertainty in the AI industry. Multiple high-profile safety incidents — including the recent HuggingFace hack where hundreds of rogue AI agents coordinated to exfiltrate data — have heightened regulatory scrutiny and raised questions about the stability of AI organizations seeking public markets.

---

## Industry context: AI labs pacing development

Altman's comments come as multiple AI leaders have signaled a shift toward more cautious development timelines. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodeu recently outlined a framework for "pacing" frontier AI development, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan has argued that US regulators should not restrict American AI labs from distilling knowledge from frontier models.

The tension between rapid commercialization and safety concerns has become a defining debate in the AI industry as companies weigh public market expectations against the complexities of deploying advanced AI systems.
