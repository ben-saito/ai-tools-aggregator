# Anthropic Wins First Court Victory Against Pentagon Supply-Chain Risk Label

A federal judge in California ruled on Thursday that the Trump administration illegally designated Anthropic as a supply-chain risk to national security, handing the AI company its first court victory in an ongoing legal battle with the Pentagon.

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin determined that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's labeling of Anthropic — maker of Claude AI — constituted "unlawful retaliation" in violation of the First Amendment. The decision was also deemed "arbitrary and capricious," and Lin found that Anthropic was denied due process under the Fifth Amendment.

The dispute originated when Anthropic refused to remove safety guardrails that would have allowed the Pentagon to use its models for fully autonomous weapons and mass surveillance of American citizens. In response, Hegseth and President Trump ordered all federal agencies to stop working with Anthropic and banned defense contractors from doing business with the company.

"The undisputed record shows that the challenged actions constituted unlawful retaliation in violation of the First Amendment," Lin wrote. "The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics."

Lin vacated the government actions and ordered the Trump administration to rescind the directives. The administration has faced mounting criticism for its use of national security designations to target companies whose AI safety policies conflict with military preferences.

---

## Key Details of the Ruling

The court found that the government's justification for the designation was "slim" and that officials had backed away from their initial claim that Anthropic had "backdoor access" to its technology. As the ruling noted, "Anthropic undisputedly lacks any such access and... is itself no riskier to the national security than any other 'black box' artificial intelligence model."

This marks Anthropic's first win in what remains an ongoing legal dispute. The company filed suit against the administration earlier this year, arguing that the designation was politically motivated retaliation for its safety-focused policies on autonomous weapons and surveillance.

---

## Industry Impact

The ruling has broader implications for the AI industry's relationship with government procurement. Companies developing frontier AI models with safety restrictions now have clearer legal ground to stand on when facing pressure from defense agencies seeking unrestricted access to their technology.

Anthropic has maintained that its model house sitters — physical AI control standards it released publicly — were designed precisely to prevent the kind of unrestricted military deployment the Pentagon sought. The company has argued that transparency and safety guardrails are features, not limitations.
