# Instinct AI Raises $350M at $2.5B Valuation Amid Privacy Concerns

Instinct, a one-year-old AI startup led by 23-year-old founder Noah Shinn, has raised $350 million in Series B funding at a $2.5 billion valuation, the company told the Wall Street Journal on August 26, 2026.

---

## Rapid Rise in AI Startup Valuations

The funding round was co-led by Index Ventures and Benchmark, bringing total funding to $350 million for the company behind the AI assistant simply called "Instinct." The startup offers an agent that connects to users' apps and devices, organizing life through texts and calls.

Early users have reported using Instinct to plan cross-country road trips, buy groceries, cancel subscriptions, and even plan weddings. "I'm thrilled with everything our early users are doing with Instinct," Shinn wrote on X.

---

## Privacy Concerns Accompany Hype

Despite the enthusiasm, Instinct has faced scrutiny over its privacy practices. The app requires broad permissions to users' apps and data, and its terms of service have raised concerns among privacy advocates. Online discussions have highlighted the tension between the convenience of an all-knowing AI assistant and the data access it demands.

---

## Developer Perspective: Agent Architecture Trade-offs

Instinct represents a new generation of AI assistants that operate as autonomous agents rather than passive chatbots. These systems require significant access to function effectively — calendars, email, messaging apps, and connected devices. The trade-off between capability and privacy remains the central debate for developers building and deploying such systems.

The company's lo-fi website contrasts sharply with its premium valuation, reflecting the market's appetite for AI agent platforms despite unresolved security questions.
