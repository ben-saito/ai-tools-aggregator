# RAND Report: US Needs "Freedom of Action" Strategy for AI Superintelligence

---

As frontier AI capabilities advance toward potentially transformative and potentially dangerous thresholds, the RAND Corporation has published a comprehensive report outlining what the United States should do to maintain geopolitical advantage on an uncertain path to superintelligence. The core recommendation: keep all options open.

## The Freedom of Action Strategy

The central thesis of the RAND report is that because so much about how the next phase of AI development will unfold remains unknown, the optimal strategy for the US is to build and preserve the ability to act decisively regardless of how the AI landscape evolves. RAND calls this "Freedom of Action."

The strategy has four key components. First, build a human-AI ecosystem: invest in AI safety, develop tools to preserve human agency, share the benefits of the technology broadly, prepare society for AI-driven disruption, and shape incentives across the AI ecosystem. Second, build an AI-security architecture: develop visibility into frontier AI systems domestically and abroad, build technology to verify international agreements, and create regulatory expertise. Third, adapt legacy national security institutions for the AI era, overhauling security agencies to capitalize on AI capabilities. Fourth, invest in the capacity of citizens, firms, and governments to respond: give decisionmakers information, develop positive AI applications, build contingency plans, and raise AI literacy.

## Seven Archetypal Strategies in Three Families

The report outlines seven archetypal strategies grouped into three families. The Coexistence family includes Dominance (the US leads AI development and suppresses others), Co-Development (the US leads a consortium including China to co-develop safe superintelligence), and Preparedness (informal coordination with voluntary commitments and phased deployment). The Denial family includes Moratorium (a verifiable global halt to AI above thresholds) and Deterrence (the US halts its own frontier development and uses national power to prevent others from advancing). The Continuity of Society strategy describes a last-resort scenario where the US creates geographically distributed, biologically self-sustaining settlements hardened against AI threats. The Acceleration family argues that constraining AI development is more dangerous than advancing it, relying on markets and competition to produce safety as a byproduct.

## Key Uncertainties That Should Guide Strategy

The report identifies five critical uncertainties. Danger proximity asks whether AI becomes so dangerous that risks outweigh benefits. Coexistence feasibility examines whether humans and AI systems can find stable equilibrium. Restraint feasibility asks whether nations can cooperate to limit AI development. Decisive strategic advantage asks whether a single actor can build and maintain a dominant lead. Suppression feasibility asks whether domestic or rival AI programs can be constrained through technology controls. The answers to these questions should determine which of the seven strategies the US prioritizes at any given moment.

## The Acceleration Trap

RAND's most pointed critique is of what it calls the "acceleration" strategy — the current de facto US approach of pouring resources into making AI systems more powerful without investing proportionally in safety measures. RAND draws an analogy: this is like upgrading a car's engine and speed while ignoring seatbelts, headlights, and brakes. The takeaway is that the US needs to spend significantly on pre-positioning itself for various AI futures — even if the primary investment is in maintaining optionality rather than committing to a single path.
