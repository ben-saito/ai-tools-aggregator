# What Americans Actually Want: CSAIP Polls 56,000 People on AI Policy

As the 2026 midterm elections approach, a new survey from the Center for Shared AI Prosperity (CSAIP) has identified which AI-related policies have genuine populist support — and which proposals even AI-skeptical voters reject.

The polling, which reached 56,000 Americans across 79 distinct policy proposals, reveals a public that is broadly supportive of worker protections tied to automation but skeptical of redistribution schemes often associated with the tech left.

---

## What Voters Actually Support

The policies with the strongest support:

- **Expand apprenticeships** (+66 net favorability): A bipartisan coalition backs expanded apprenticeship pathways as a buffer against automation-driven job displacement
- **Require severance for automated-away jobs** (+63): Strong majorities across income brackets support mandatory severance packages when companies replace workers with automated systems
- **Sector-based job training** (+60): Targeted retraining programs for specific industries facing automation pressure poll well across party lines

These results suggest that the political coalition most likely to shape AI policy in the midterms is oriented around worker protection and skills development — not universal basic income or robot taxes.

---

## What Voters Reject

At the bottom of the list:

- **U.S. sovereign wealth fund** (-51): A proposal to redirect some of the gains from AI-driven productivity into a national investment fund was rejected by large margins across all demographic groups
- **Tax on distributed profits** (-33): A tax on AI-driven corporate profits, a favorite of progressive economists, polled negatively
- **Universal basic income** (-33): Even in a high-unemployment automation scenario, UBI failed to achieve majority support

---

## The Policy Gap

The CSAIP researchers note an important distinction between popular policies and effective ones. "A popular policy is not the same as an effective policy. This polling helps us understand where the public is already open to a policy idea, and where bold policy ideas will need strong organizing to gain traction," the report states.

The three most popular proposals — apprenticeships, severance mandates, and sector training — each have distinct implementation challenges. Apprenticeship expansion requires coordination with private industry. Severance mandates for automation are technically difficult to define and enforce. Sector-based training programs have a mixed track record.

---

## The Midterm Landscape

The polling data arrives as both major parties are finalizing their AI policy platforms for the 2026 midterms. Republicans have focused primarily on deregulation and keeping the US ahead of China in AI development. Democrats have been divided between an AI-safety-first approach and a worker-protection framework.

The CSAIP data suggests the worker-protection frame has broader electoral appeal — particularly the three top-polling ideas, which do not require significant new federal spending or corporate tax increases.

---

## The China Angle

Notably absent from the most-popular policies is anything resembling the industrial policy approach favored by national security hawks who want to pour federal resources into competing with Chinese AI development. The polling suggests that while voters broadly support US leadership in AI, they are more interested in how AI affects their own jobs than in geopolitical competition.
