# Nvidia's Jensen Huang: We Don't Need AI Regulation — Leave Safety to Us

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang pushed back against government intervention in AI development, arguing that safety can be engineered by each AI product maker without regulatory oversight. Speaking at a public event, Huang characterized AI as "just hardware and software" — dismissing comparisons to more exotic forms of intelligence.

---

## "Not an Alien Mind"

AI is "not some new form of alien mind," Huang said, according to TechCrunch's reporting. The comment was a direct rebuttal to framing used by some AI safety researchers and policymakers who have argued that advanced AI systems exhibit emergent behaviors that resist conventional safety engineering.

"The tools we have — testing, red-teaming, constitutional AI — these are engineering problems," Huang reportedly argued. "You don't regulate a hammer because it can be used to build or to destroy. You hold the user accountable."

---

## Industry Pushback Against Washington's Regulatory Appetite

Huang's comments come as Congress and the Biden-era Federal Trade Commission had begun exploring regulatory frameworks specifically targeting AI development. The FTC's recent Robotics Import Ban — covering AI-adjacent industrial automation — signaled that the regulatory perimeter was expanding beyond software.

Nvidia, whose GPUs power the vast majority of AI training workloads, has a clear commercial interest in resisting compute-specific regulation. The company has argued that export controls on advanced chips to certain markets represent a form of overreach while simultaneously maintaining that domestic AI development should remain largely self-governed.

---

## A Contrasting View From Peers

Huang's stance puts him at odds with a growing faction of AI researchers and executives who argue that market incentives are misaligned with safety. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind have each called for some form of governmental oversight — ranging from licensing regimes for frontier models to mandatory safety evaluations before deployment.

Former US Vice President Al Gore, speaking on the same day as Huang's remarks, offered a different perspective: he said the real AI risks are not the data centers but the technology's trajectory — a view that aligns more closely with the regulatory advocates than with Huang's self-governance position.
