# New Mexico Court Orders Meta to Pay Additional $567M in Child Safety Case

A New Mexico court judge on August 6, 2026 ordered Meta to pay an additional $567 million fine in a case regarding social media harms and addiction, bringing the company's total liability in this case to $942 million. The ruling adds to a $375 million penalty levied in March 2026 in Los Angeles.

---

## The Ruling: Behavioral Changes Required

Beyond financial penalties, the court ordered Meta to make significant operational changes to its platforms within New Mexico. The company must remove Like counts from its platforms, restricting engagement metrics to users over 18 with parental approval for minors. Push notifications to users under 18 in the state must be paused between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., and monthly usage must be capped at 90 hours — roughly three hours per day.

The court characterized Meta's platforms as creating a "public nuisance" in New Mexico, requiring the company to abate the harm.

---

## The State's Case

"Significant numbers of people in New Mexico experience harm from Meta's products due to risks of sexual exploitation, interference with education, and adverse mental health outcomes," the order stated. The judge acknowledged that Meta is not the only platform contributing to youth mental health crises, but maintained that Meta's platforms play a significant part.

State Attorney General Raul Torrez said: "For years, Meta knew its platforms were harming New Mexico's kids, from feeding a youth mental health crisis to connecting predators with children, and it chose engagement and profit over their safety. Today, Meta is paying for that choice."

Meta disputed the characterization. A company spokesperson said: "We work hard to keep people safe on our platforms and have been transparent about the challenges of identifying and removing bad actors and harmful content. We remain confident in our record of protecting teens online and will continue to defend ourselves against claims that misrepresent the facts."

---

## Broader Legal Exposure

The New Mexico ruling is not an isolated case. Meta faces multiple major lawsuits across the United States. A joint lawsuit by 33 states has been consolidated in an Oakland, California federal court. Additional states, including Tennessee, have filed their own cases against the company. The cumulative legal exposure spans antitrust, child safety, and platform addiction claims.

---

## Context for AI Safety

While not a direct AI model case, the ruling reflects growing regulatory focus on digital platform effects — particularly attention, engagement, and addiction mechanics — that many AI safety researchers have cited as a key concern with AI-driven content ranking and recommendation systems. The behavioral modification remedies ordered by the court parallel broader AI governance discussions about platform accountability for algorithmic harm.
