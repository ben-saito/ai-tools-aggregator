# Jensen Huang Tells Trump 'We're Not Going to Let an AI Slowdown Happen'

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang publicly disagreed with the growing calls from other AI industry leaders to slow the development of artificial intelligence, telling President Donald Trump during a live conference appearance that he would not support efforts to decelerate the industry. The exchange, which featured Trump joining via phone call, highlighted a deepening rift within the tech community over AI safety and development pace.

---

## Huang Joins Trump in Rejecting AI Deceleration

The confrontation unfolded at the All-In Summit, a technology investment conference featuring prominent venture capitalists Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, and David Sacks — all close Trump advisors. Huang was on stage discussing the AI industry's direction when Trump called in unexpectedly.

"I can't let this happen," Trump said, referring to calls from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and others to pause frontier AI development. "That could be political people. It could also be China. And we're not going to let that happen. It's a hoax."

Huang immediately aligned with the president. "You're right. We're not going to let that happen, sir," Huang replied as the audience broke into applause. Trump had put Huang on speaker so conference attendees could hear the exchange directly.

---

## The Industry Split Over AI Pace

The public disagreement exposes a fundamental fracture in how AI leaders view the technology's trajectory. Amodei has argued for slowing capability improvements to allow safety measures to catch up, a position Musk and Altman have also endorsed. Huang's Nvidia, which supplies the GPU infrastructure underlying much of the industry's AI training, has a clear financial interest in continued rapid expansion.

Trump and his allies have gone further, suggesting that opposition to data center construction represents an organized effort to undermine American economic competitiveness. "We have to be a little bit careful," Trump conceded to Huang. "We have to do things and we have to do them prudently, but that doesn't mean we're going to stop an industry."

---

## Political Dimensions of AI Governance

The episode illustrates how AI governance debates are becoming increasingly politicized. What began as technical safety discussions within research labs have evolved into broader questions about national competitiveness, with political leaders positioning themselves on opposite sides of industry infighting.

Huang's willingness to publicly align Nvidia with Trump's position signals a strategic choice to embed the chipmaker's interests within the administration's economic agenda, even as regulatory scrutiny of Nvidia's market dominance intensifies.
