# Can the US battery market untangle from China?

MIT Technology Review reports that the United States is hitting records for rapid growth in its energy storage market, but the expansion is happening largely with Chinese-made battery cells. A new analysis examines the challenge of building a domestic supply chain.

---

## The scale of US battery deployment

According to the report, US battery installations reached a new quarterly record in mid-2026, with 20.2 gigawatt-hours of new capacity added in a single quarter. Energy storage is increasingly central to grid stability as renewable energy from wind and solar grows.

The problem: the vast majority of battery cells currently installed in the US come from Chinese manufacturers. Building a domestic supply chain at sufficient scale to meet demand — while simultaneously deploying that supply chain — is a multi-year challenge that cannot be solved by policy alone in the short term.

---

## Why China dominates battery manufacturing

China's dominance in lithium-ion battery manufacturing is the result of over a decade of sustained industrial policy, cheap electricity for refinement and cell production, and vertical integration from raw material processing to pack assembly. The rest of the world, including the US, largely exited the battery manufacturing chain during the 2000s and 2010s.

Reshoring involves not just building factories, but rebuilding the entire upstream supply chain: cathode material processing, electrolyte production, cell formatting, and the specialized manufacturing equipment for each step.

---

## The AI connection

Battery technology is increasingly relevant to AI infrastructure. Data center backup power, GPU cluster cooling systems, and edge AI devices all depend on battery technology. As AI compute demand grows, so does the energy infrastructure needed to sustain it.

The US government's incentives for domestic battery manufacturing are competing with Chinese cost advantages that reflect both labor costs and decades of process optimization. Whether US-built batteries can achieve cost parity without ongoing policy support is an open question.
