# OpenAI Safety Employee Resigns, Citing Broken Culture

David Robinson, a safety employee at OpenAI, has resigned from the company, publicly stating that the organization's "culture is broken." Robinson, who described himself as "something of a cliché" for being an AI safety employee leaving a leading AI company, cited deep-rooted cultural problems as his primary reason for departing.

---

## A Recurring Pattern in AI Safety

Robinson's resignation adds to a growing list of high-profile departures from OpenAI's safety team. The resignation comes at a time when the company is facing increased scrutiny over its approach to AI development and safety protocols. Robinson's departure highlights the ongoing tension between aggressive deployment schedules and robust safety measures within the organization.

"I am something of a cliché," Robinson admitted in his public statement, acknowledging the irony of a safety researcher leaving a company at the center of AI safety concerns. The resignation has sparked renewed debate about whether major AI labs are genuinely prioritizing safety or merely using it as performative rhetoric.

---

## Industry Implications

The resignation arrives amid broader discussions about AI governance and the responsibilities of leading AI companies. OpenAI, which positioned itself as a safety-first organization, has faced criticism for seemingly contradictory statements about development timelines and safety measures.

Robinson's move underscores the challenges faced by internal safety teams at commercial AI labs, where pressure to ship products quickly can conflict with thorough safety evaluations. The incident may further fuel calls for independent oversight of AI development at major labs.
