# Anthropic CEO: AI Backlash Is "Fundamentally a Crisis of Trust"

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has directly countered criticism that his warnings about AI dangers have fueled backlash against AI in the United States, arguing the problem is "fundamentally a crisis of trust."

---

## Investor Criticism: "Be a More Positive Advocate"

The criticism came from investor Gavin Baker, who argued on the All-In podcast and on X that Amodei's cautionary stance on AI has contributed to government crackdowns on data centers and increased regulatory pressure.

Baker wrote that Amodei has "lost the argument" on AI regulation, and suggested that as "someone who is about to be the CEO of one of the most important companies in the world," he should "make an effort to be a more positive advocate for his own industry."

---

## Amodei's Response: "Risk and Benefit Written Equally"

Amodei pushed back with a series of posts. He disagreed that his messaging has been "disproportionately negative," asserting his writing has been "about equally balanced between risks and benefits."

He explained that he wrote the essay "Machines of Loving Grace" because he "didn't feel the AI industry was painting an inspiring enough picture of how the technology could radically transform the world for the better."

---

## "The Public Has a Negative View of AI" — But Not Because of Warning Messages

Amodei acknowledged "the public has a negative view of AI" and agreed "this is a big problem." However, he disagreed that his own warning messages are the cause.

As Amodei frames it, distrust of AI is not simply a product of his cautionary statements. Rather, it stems from a combination of factors: lack of information, vague fears about what could go wrong, and a broader trust deficit toward industry leaders.

---

## Industry-Wide Challenge

The exchange highlights a broader challenge facing the AI industry: how to communicate both the risks and benefits of AI in a way that maintains public trust while still allowing the technology to advance.

Anthropic has been an active participant in AI safety discussions, supporting California's transparency requirements for large AI companies. How the industry navigates this trust gap will be critical as AI policy continues to develop in the United States.
