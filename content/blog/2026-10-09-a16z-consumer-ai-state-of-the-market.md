# a16z's Olivia Moore on the State of Consumer AI: Still Early, Still Unexplored

A new report from Andreessen Horowitz partner Olivia Moore maps the top 100 consumer AI applications and finds ChatGPT dominates by a wide margin — but half a dozen consumer categories remain almost entirely untouched by AI products. Moore argues this represents a massive opportunity, not a crisis.

The report arrives at a moment of heightened pessimism about consumer AI economics. OpenAI has visibly pivoted back toward enterprise customers, and most AI revenue continues to flow through subscriptions and API usage concentrated on business users. But Moore sees reasons for optimism in categories where AI adoption remains nascent.

---

## What the Top 100 Consumer AI Apps Look Like

ChatGPT maintains its position as the dominant consumer AI product by user base and engagement. However, the report identifies surprising staying power from smaller players: Suno (AI music generation) and ElevenLabs (AI voice synthesis) have retained meaningful user bases months after initial launches — suggesting certain creative tooling niches have genuine product-market fit beyond early adopters.

More interesting than what's working, perhaps, is what's not. Moore's analysis identifies half a dozen consumer categories where AI penetration appears to be essentially zero — segments where the technology has failed to produce compelling consumer products despite demonstrated enterprise value.

---

## The Economics Problem

The revenue picture for consumer AI remains challenging. Moore notes that only 2.2% of U.S. households currently pay for AI products — a number that suggests either enormous growth potential or limited genuine consumer demand outside a narrow segment of power users.

The current business model — subscriptions plus per-token charges — works well for enterprises but creates friction for individual consumers. Moore is more interested in alternative monetization approaches: advertising-supported AI products, transaction-based models, or hardware-integrated AI services that don't require explicit subscription decisions.

"I don't necessarily want to see that user number increase," Moore said in an interview. "I'm more interested in getting back to a world where the consumer is monetized in a way that isn't subscription dollars out of their own pocket."

---

## Why OpenAI's Enterprise Pivot Isn't the Whole Story

Despite OpenAI's movement toward enterprise, Moore sees room for consumer AI to grow — just not through the same products that work for businesses. The key challenge is that current AI products are predominantly useful for tasks (writing, coding, research) that have historically been enterprise-dominated use cases.

The unexplored consumer categories likely require different product thinking: AI that fits into existing consumer routines rather than asking users to adopt new workflows centered on AI tools.
