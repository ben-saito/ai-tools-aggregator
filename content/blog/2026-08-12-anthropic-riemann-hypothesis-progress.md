# Anthropic Unreleased Model Makes Progress on Riemann Hypothesis

Anthropic announced on August 11, 2026, that an unreleased model made significant progress on one of mathematics' oldest unsolved problems: the Riemann hypothesis. While the model did not solve it outright, it substantially raised the lower bound of solutions for which the hypothesis holds true.

---

## How the Model Made Progress

Anthropic researchers prompted the model to attempt a proof of the hypothesis, then left it running for approximately 36 hours. The model coordinated 60 sub-agents that tested 650 different approaches to the problem, ultimately increasing the verified solution range by a significant margin.

Two of the 60 sub-agents focused on developing core mathematical ideas, while 13 others contributed supporting concepts. Thirty sub-agents attempted but were unable to generate new ideas, 13 acted as validators checking the correctness of arguments, and two wrote the final paper. The total compute cost came to approximately $31 million.

The result was confirmed by two of Anthropic's in-house mathematicians before publication.

---

## The Riemann Hypothesis: A $1 Million Problem

The Riemann hypothesis, first proposed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, concerns the distribution of prime numbers. It carries a $1 million bounty from the Clay Mathematics Institute, and the prize remains unclaimed more than 150 years later.

The hypothesis states that the real part of all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function equals 1/2. Computational verification had previously confirmed the hypothesis holds for the first 10 trillion non-trivial zeros. The Anthropic model pushed this boundary further, though a general proof remains elusive.

---

## What This Means for AI in Mathematics

The finding reignites questions about AI's ability to contribute to open problems in mathematics. Traditionally, AI has excelled at pattern recognition and optimization tasks, but the Riemann hypothesis requires novel theoretical reasoning that has stumped human mathematicians for generations.

Anthropic noted that the staff member who prompted the model had no significant mathematical training, raising questions about whether the model, rather than the human, was the source of the breakthrough.
