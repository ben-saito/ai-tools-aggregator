# Operation Bluebird Launches New Twitter Alternative as Legal Battle Over Trademark Continues

A startup called Operation Bluebird has launched a new social media platform using the Twitter name, even as Elon Musk's X Corp. continues legal fighting over the trademark.

---

## The Trademark Dispute

Operation Bluebird argues that when Elon Musk bought Twitter in 2022 and renamed it X, the company abandoned the Twitter intellectual property. Stephen Coates, one of Operation Bluebird's cofounders, told Ars Technica: "We are a small company, we have investors, and we have a product. And we have waited months and months to launch, and we are not going to wait anymore."

X Corp. quickly sued Operation Bluebird late last year and asked a federal judge in Delaware to issue a preliminary injunction to prevent a new version of Twitter from launching.

---

## Court's Preliminary Ruling

In an April 2026 hearing, US District Court Judge Colm Connolly gave a tentative ruling from the bench suggesting X seemed to have given up intellectual property claims over the word "tweet" and the Twitter bird logo, and possibly even the word "Twitter" itself. The judge has not yet issued a final ruling on X's preliminary injunction request.

Despite the ongoing litigation, Operation Bluebird has proceeded with its launch.

---

## Industry Context

The case highlights the complexities of trademark law when a company rebrands. If X abandoned its Twitter trademarks, others may be free to use them - creating an unusual situation where the most valuable brand identity in social media could become available for others to claim.
