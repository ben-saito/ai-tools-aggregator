# Meta Open Sources Muse Hardware Development Kit for Consumer Gadgets

Meta is making it easier for developers and hobbyists to build hardware devices powered by its Muse speech-to-speech AI model, releasing an open source toolkit that includes firmware, a Linux software development kit, and reference designs for several types of consumer devices. The move positions Muse as a platform rather than a product, with Meta hoping that third-party hardware makers will extend its reach into TVs, appliances, and other connected devices.

---

## Muse Gadgets: Building on the Muse Model

On Friday, Meta introduced Muse Gadgets, an open source project that provides everything needed to build hardware that connects to Muse. The toolkit includes low-level firmware for device controllers, a Linux SDK that handles communication with Meta's cloud-based Muse API, and several reference implementations demonstrating practical applications.

The reference designs Meta has published include ideas for connecting Muse to a television, building a smart display device, and creating a voice-controlled interface for existing electronics. Each reference design includes bill of materials, PCB layout files, and step-by-step assembly instructions.

---

## Why Meta Is Going Open Source

Meta's decision to open source the hardware development toolkit reflects a strategic calculation about the limits of its own hardware engineering capacity. Rather than designing and manufacturing every possible Muse-powered device itself, Meta is effectively inviting the broader hardware development community to create new form factors and use cases.

This approach mirrors Meta's strategy with its open source Llama language models, where releasing the model weights freely allowed third parties to build products and services that Meta itself had not envisioned. The company appears to be applying the same logic to hardware: if the underlying AI capability is compelling enough, external developers will find applications that Meta's own product team would never have considered.

For consumer electronics manufacturers, the Muse Gadgets toolkit offers a path to adding voice AI capabilities to their products without the licensing costs or development complexity of building a speech AI system from scratch. Meta is essentially positioning itself as the AI engine supplier for a ecosystem of hardware makers.

---

## The Reference Designs

Among the reference designs Meta has published is a television interface that uses Muse for hands-free control and content discovery. Rather than navigating menus with a remote, users can speak natural language commands to find content, adjust settings, or get recommendations. A second reference design demonstrates a tabletop smart display that can answer questions, manage smart home devices, and provide visual information alongside voice responses.

A third design shows how Muse could be integrated into a voice-controlled interface for existing consumer electronics, essentially providing a retrofittable upgrade path for devices that were not originally designed for voice interaction.

---

## Business Model Considerations

Meta has not disclosed the specifics of how it plans to monetize the Muse Gadgets ecosystem. The Muse model itself is accessed via a cloud API, which means that volume usage by hardware makers could generate ongoing API revenue for Meta even though the development toolkit itself is free. This structure is similar to how Amazon profits from Alexa not through device sales but through the services that Alexa-enabled devices consume.

The open source approach also carries risks. Once the firmware and SDK are publicly available, it becomes difficult to control who uses them or what they are used for. Meta has not indicated whether it plans to implement any usage policies or restrictions on commercial use of the toolkit.
