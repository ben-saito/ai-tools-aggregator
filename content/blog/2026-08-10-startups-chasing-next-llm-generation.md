# Startups Chase Next-Generation LLMs as Transformer Era Shows Limits

MIT Technology Review explored how a new wave of startups is working to surpass the limitations of transformer-based large language models. Published August 10, the report examines architectural innovations that may define the next era of AI development.

---

## Transformers Power Every Major LLM, But Are Showing Limits

In 2017, Google researchers published the landmark paper "Attention Is All You Need," introducing the transformer architecture that now powers every major large language model. Nine years later, the AI industry built on transformers is facing fundamental scaling challenges.

Dense attention — the core mechanism that lets transformers encode meaning by comparing every token with every other token — becomes computationally expensive as text grows. A 10,000-word document can require 50 million multiplications. OpenAI is set to spend 50 billion dollars on computing this year, and data center electricity consumption is projected to double by 2030.

---

## The Problem: Reasoning Models Expose Transformer Weaknesses

The latest advances in LLMs — including reasoning models and expanded context windows — are not neat extensions of transformer technology but rather workarounds that expose its underlying flaws. Transformers struggle to handle large amounts of information simultaneously, yet reasoning models require processing chain-of-thought scratch pads that add to data volume.

The industry recognizes this ceiling: "The entire AI industry is built on transformers," said Justin Dangel, cofounder and CEO of startup Subquadratic. "They are one of the most important innovations in the history of computer science, and they have changed the world. But they are starting to show their age."

---

## The Opportunity: LLMs+ and Next-Generation Architectures

MIT Technology Review dubbed this emerging category of models "LLMs+" — the next generation that moves beyond the transformer paradigm. A wave of startups is now competing to build what comes next, betting that the companies currently leading the market will need to adopt new architectures to continue advancing.

The new approaches being explored include architectures that handle long contexts more efficiently, methods that reduce the quadratic scaling of attention, and hybrid designs that combine transformer strengths with new innovations.
