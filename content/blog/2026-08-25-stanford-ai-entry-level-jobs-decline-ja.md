# Stanford Study: AI Impacts Entry-Level Jobs Hardest, Youth Employment Down 19%

On August 24, 2026, Ars Technica reported on a Stanford University study finding that AI is hitting entry-level jobs the hardest. Youth employment in AI-impacted fields fell 19% compared to more AI-resistant occupations.

---

## Key Findings

The Stanford Internet Observatory study analyzed AI's effect on the labor market in detail. Since 2023, AI-powered business automation has accelerated, but new job creation has failed to keep pace.

The most affected job categories include:
- Data entry and processing
- Basic customer service
- Standard report generation
- Repetitive analytical tasks

These roles share common characteristics: structured, repetitive tasks with clear rules that are highly automatable by AI systems.

---

## disproportionate Impact on Young Workers

Young workers in high-AI-displacement-risk occupations experienced a 19% employment decline during the study period, compared to workers in more AI-resistant roles.

Economists note that while AI was expected to automate routine tasks and create new jobs, the transition period has disproportionately burdened young workers entering the job market for the first time.

---

## Balancing AI Adoption with Labor Market Stability

The study highlights both the promise and challenge of AI adoption. Policymakers face mounting pressure to ensure that AI's productivity gains do not come at the cost of employment stability for vulnerable worker populations.
