# The Sameness Problem Behind Unappetizing AI-Generated Menus

A wave of AI-generated restaurant menus has swept through the food service industry, offering restaurant owners a seemingly effortless way to refresh their offerings. But customers can viscerally sense that something is wrong with the food — even before they take a bite.

The core issue is what researchers call "the sameness problem." AI image generation models tend to produce images that look strikingly similar, sharing a flat, generic aesthetic that betrays their synthetic origin.

---

## Why AI Menus Look Wrong

Amanda Silberling reports for TechCrunch that AI-generated menu illustrations often share a specific, unsettling look. A burrito with cheese so bubbly and perfectly melted that it resembles avant-garde art rather than food. Shrimp that are impossibly uniform. Ice cream scoops that are geometrically perfect spheres.

"It's almost like an alien trying to make a pizza without understanding its core principles," Reality Defender CTO Alex Lisle told TechCrunch. Reality Defender is a AI content detection company.

The problem stems from how these models are trained. Large language models and diffusion models — the AI systems behind chatbots like ChatGPT and image generators like Midjourney — are trained on vast datasets of existing images and text. When it comes to food photography, the training data skews heavily toward stock photography and generic restaurant menus.

"A lot of this stuff looks like a Chili's menu from 2015, and there's a reason for that," Lisle said. "That was the corpus of work from which [the models] drew their function."

---

## The Training Data Problem

New training data is invaluable to companies building AI models. Amazon has reportedly sourced rare books to scan and add to its training data, only to destroy those books once they've been digitized. The same dynamic applies to food imagery — the AI models trained on existing restaurant photography reproduce the biases and limitations of that data.

The result is a feedback loop: AI-generated menus look generic because they're trained on generic images, which reinforces the generic aesthetic, which makes future AI-generated menus look even more alike.

This is distinct from the question of whether AI-generated food photography is appetizing. Many restaurants use AI-generated images precisely because they're cheap and fast — but the visual quality suffers in ways that may undermine customer trust.

---

## Detection and the Broader AI Content Problem

Reality Defender and similar companies have built detection tools to identify AI-generated content. But the arms race between generation and detection is ongoing, with each side improving over time.

The menu problem illustrates a broader challenge with AI content generation: when the goal is to produce something "good enough" at scale, the sameness problem becomes a liability. For restaurants trying to establish a unique identity, generic AI imagery may ultimately hurt brand perception more than it helps.
