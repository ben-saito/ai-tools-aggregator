# GSK and Relation Therapeutics Expand AI Drug Discovery Collaboration Worth Up to $110 Million

---

GSK has entered into a research collaboration with British biotechnology company Relation Therapeutics worth up to $110 million, expanding the companies' existing work in AI-assisted drug discovery. The agreement places biological data generation alongside AI model development as core pillars of the partnership.

Under the agreement, Relation will generate large-scale datasets measuring how human cells respond to genetic changes and drug interventions. The data will be used to train AI models designed to identify potential drug targets, including models within Relation's MORGAN platform.

## Lab-in-the-Loop Approach Links Experiments and Computation

Relation describes its Lab-in-the-Loop approach as a combination of laboratory experimentation and computational analysis. The work includes tissue profiling, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, sequencing, and target validation, while machine learning is used for target identification, prioritization, validation, and experimental design.

The company also conducts perturbation experiments that measure how genetic changes affect cellular characteristics associated with disease. Those results are then analyzed alongside genetic and patient-derived biological data.

The collaboration builds on earlier agreements between GSK and Relation focused on fibrotic diseases and osteoarthritis. Those projects involved observational studies designed to create two functional disease datasets using Relation's Lab-in-the-Loop platform. The earlier work combined human genetics, single-cell multi-omics generated from human tissue, functional assays, and machine learning to identify and validate potential disease targets.

## Biological Data as Foundation Model Training Material

Public repositories remain an important source of training material for biological foundation models, although combining information produced across different studies can introduce technical challenges. A 2025 review in Experimental & Molecular Medicine noted that repositories hold vast amounts of data but face interoperability issues when integrated for AI training purposes.

Relation's approach addresses this by generating proprietary datasets specifically designed for AI model training rather than relying solely on public repositories. This allows the company to control data quality and relevance directly.
