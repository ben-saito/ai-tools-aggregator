# TechCrunch Founder Summit 2026: AI Strategy Takes Center Stage in Boston

TechCrunch has released the full agenda for its 2026 Founder Summit, scheduled for November 4 in Boston. The event brings together investors, founders, and operators for sessions covering fundraising, hiring, team building, and a dedicated track on building AI-native companies — reflecting the degree to which artificial intelligence has moved from a feature to a foundational strategy for early-stage startups.

---

## The Case for AI-Native Over AI-Enhanced

One of the summit's most anticipated sessions moves beyond the question of how to add AI features to an existing product. Lior Div, co-founder and CEO of 7AI, will lead a session titled "Building an AI-Native Company," examining what changes — from team structure to product architecture to customer engagement — when AI is the foundation rather than an enhancement.

The distinction matters because building AI features on top of an existing company often produces awkward compromises: legacy data models, human-in-the-loop workflows designed for a pre-AI world, and organizational structures that replicate old silos in new wrappers. An AI-native approach starts from different assumptions about what is automatable and what human judgment is actually necessary.

---

## Investor Perspectives on Fundraising in an AI-Dominated Market

Brian Devaney, a partner at Underscore, will break down what investors are looking for in 2026 — a year in which AI startups have continued to attract disproportionate capital but where investors have grown more discerning about differentiation. The session will cover where founders lose leverage in fundraising conversations and how to stand out in a market where every pitch deck mentions large language models.

---

## Early Hiring as Cultural Foundation

Early hiring decisions shape a startup's trajectory far beyond the obvious operational impact. Melissa Taunton, a partner at NEA, will share lessons from working with founders navigating rapid growth phases, including the hiring mistakes that are hardest to undo and the frameworks for evaluating whether a candidate fits the culture you're trying to build — not just the culture you have today.

Tina, a speaker whose session focuses on evaluating startup readiness, will present frameworks for assessing when timing, talent, and technology align for a given market opportunity. The session draws on case studies from both fission and fusion energy startups to illustrate how analogous evaluation frameworks apply across sectors.

---

## Beyond Silicon Valley: Building from Boston

Chase Garbarino, co-founder and CEO of HqO, has spent over a decade building category-defining companies from Boston without relocating to Silicon Valley. HqO has raised $200 million and scaled to more than 30 countries. His session at the summit addresses how founders can build durable companies outside the traditional startup hub ecosystem — and why doing so has become more viable as distributed work, remote collaboration tools, and AI-powered operations reduce the geographic premium on talent access.

---

## Key Dates and Registration

TechCrunch Founder Summit 2026 takes place November 4 in Boston. Early-bird pricing — offering savings of up to $200 and 50% off a second ticket — runs through September 25. The event features six industry stages with sessions spanning fundraising, product development, hiring, and AI strategy.
