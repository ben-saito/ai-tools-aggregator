# Ex-Meta Scientists Launch Perceptron to Bring Visual AI to Factory Floors

Perceptron, a startup founded by two former Meta research scientists, launched Isaac 0.5 this week — a frontier vision model designed to help machines perceive, reason, and act in industrial settings. The company aims to solve what it calls the "false choice" in physical AI: generalist foundation models that require multiple dedicated cloud GPUs, or narrow models that handle perception or control but never both.

The company was co-founded by Armen Aghajanyan and Akshat Shrivastava, both veterans of Meta's Fundamental AI Research (FAIR) division. Founded in November 2024, Perceptron has been developing vision models specifically for industrial deployment scenarios.

## Isaac 0.5 Capabilities

Isaac 0.5 is designed for vision-guided robots navigating complex environments like warehouses and manufacturing facilities. The model is being released as open-weight, meaning its parameters and training materials can be inspected by anyone — a notable choice for an industrial AI product given concerns about competitive secrecy in manufacturing.

Perceptron's approach focuses on enabling machines to perform dexterous manipulation tasks that have traditionally challenged robotics systems. The company positions its technology as the missing piece between general-purpose AI models and deployable industrial automation.

## The Physical AI Landscape

Perceptron enters a market where robot hardware has advanced significantly, but AI brains remain limited. The company argues that current physical AI forces an inefficient trade-off: generalist models are computationally expensive to deploy per instance, while specialized models lack the flexibility to handle varied tasks.

The startup's open-weight strategy aligns with broader trends in the AI industry where transparency about training and capabilities has become a competitive differentiator, particularly for safety-critical industrial applications.
