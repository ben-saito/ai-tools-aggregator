# Researchers Outline Research Agenda for "Pacing" AI Development

---

With all the recent talk of AI pacing and AI slowdowns, there has been a lot of enthusiasm for the general idea, but the actual research field for systematically evaluating and implementing development pacing remains underdeveloped. A new research agenda seeks to change that.

The concept of "pacing" AI development refers to intentional mechanisms to slow, accelerate, or modulate the rate of AI progress — not to halt progress entirely, but to ensure that societal adaptation can keep pace with capability advances. This contrasts with both unrestricted development and calls for outright pauses.

## What a Pacing Research Agenda Looks Like

The proposed research agenda identifies several key areas needing development:

**Measurement frameworks** — Before we can pace anything, we need robust ways to measure what we're pacing. Current benchmarks often lag behind deployed capabilities, and there is no consensus on what metrics best capture "progress" in a meaningful sense.

**Governance mechanisms** — What institutional structures could implement pacing? This includes both formal mechanisms (regulatory frameworks, international treaties) and informal ones (industry norms, best practice guidelines).

**Economic modeling** — How do different pacing strategies affect innovation incentives, market structure, and competitiveness? A research agenda must account for the economic realities that drive development decisions.

**Safety integration** — Pacing mechanisms need to be compatible with — and potentially reinforce — safety research. The goal is not slower development regardless of consequences, but development that maintains alignment with human values.

## Challenges and Open Questions

The agenda acknowledges several hard problems. One is the difficulty of coordinating across jurisdictions when AI development is global. Another is the inherent unpredictability of research progress — we cannot always know in advance which breakthroughs will be transformative.

A third challenge is the relationship between pacing and safety: some safety improvements require more capable AI to verify, which creates tension with slowing overall progress.

## Connection to AI Strategy Debates

The research agenda connects to ongoing debates about national AI strategies. The RAND Corporation has published analyses suggesting the US should maintain flexibility in its approach to AI development, keeping multiple options open rather than committing to a single trajectory.

This suggests that pacing research is not merely technical but deeply political — involving questions of international relations, economic competition, and values.
