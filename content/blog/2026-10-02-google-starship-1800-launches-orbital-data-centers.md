# Google Says Starship Needs 1,800 Launches Before Space Data Centers Can Scale

Google has concluded that SpaceX's Starship rocket needs to complete approximately 1,800 launches before the company can realistically deploy orbital data centers at scale, according to Project Suncatcher, Google's initiative to place compute infrastructure in space.

---

## Project Suncatcher: Google's Orbital Computing Push

Google launched its first advanced Tensor Processing Unit into orbit aboard a Planet Labs satellite to test whether custom AI chips can function in the space environment. The experiment, managed by Google executive Travis Beals, represents the company's first step toward establishing orbital data center capacity.

"We've done testing on the ground, but there's no test that's completely as good as the real thing," Beals said, explaining why Google chose to proceed with an actual orbital test rather than relying solely on Earth-based simulation.

The chip launched aboard the satellite must demonstrate it can operate reliably in orbit, withstand radiation exposure, and maintain stable power and cooling systems in the vacuum of space before Google commits to larger orbital deployments.

---

## The Scale Challenge: 1,800 Launches

Project Suncatcher's analysis indicates that deploying meaningful orbital compute capacity would require approximately 1,800 Starship launches, each carrying multiple satellites. This calculation accounts for the number of satellites needed to provide continuous coverage and sufficient compute density, combined with Starship's projected payload capacity.

SpaceX is currently in the process of proving Starship's reliability, having completed multiple test flights with varying degrees of success. The rocket represents SpaceX's next-generation heavy lift vehicle, designed to eventually replace the Falcon 9 program which is scheduled to end in 2028.

---

## Competing for Launch Capacity

The orbital data center vision faces significant practical constraints beyond rocket reliability. Both Starcloud and Google are competing for limited launch capacity as SpaceX works through its manifest of customer commitments. Starcloud has already reserved substantial launch capacity for its own orbital computing constellation.

Google's approach differs from Starcloud's in that Google is developing custom TPU hardware optimized for space operation, while Starcloud's architecture uses more conventional compute hardware adapted for orbital deployment. Both approaches face the same fundamental challenge: the high cost and limited availability of launch capacity.

The analysis highlights the infrastructure challenges facing AI companies seeking alternatives to traditional Earth-based data centers, particularly as land availability, power grid capacity, and cooling constraints increasingly limit expansion options in key markets.
