# Sony Suit Cites Anthropic Staff Chats Praising Z-Library Piracy

Sony Music and Warner's federal lawsuit against Anthropic has introduced new evidence: internal company chats in which Anthropic staff discussed and appeared to endorse the use of Z-Library, a notorious piracy platform, for obtaining copyrighted books. The chats were cited in the latest court filing as evidence of willful copyright infringement.

---

## Internal Communications Surface in Court

The lawsuit, which accuses Anthropic of systematic piracy in training its Claude AI models, has taken a new turn with the introduction of internal Slack messages and emails. In one exchange, Anthropic employees discussed Z-Library as a resource, with some messages appearing to express enthusiasm for the platform's vast library of pirated academic and commercial texts.

The music industry's legal team presented these communications as evidence that Anthropic staff were aware of and actively utilized illicit data sources. This contrasts with Anthropic's public position that training on publicly available data constitutes fair use.

---

## The Z-Library Context

Z-Library operates as one of the largest online libraries of pirated content, hosting millions of academic papers, books, and other copyrighted materials without authorization. The platform has been the subject of numerous shutdown attempts by rights holders and law enforcement, though it has proven resilient through domain changes and mirror sites.

For AI training purposes, Z-Library represents a rich source of high-quality text that would otherwise require licensing agreements or subscriptions to access legitimately. The lawsuit alleges that Anthropic systematically scraped such sources rather than pursuing authorized data partnerships.

---

## Legal Significance

The internal chat evidence could prove significant for damages calculations. Willful copyright infringement can trigger enhanced statutory damages, and evidence of employee awareness may strengthen the music labels' case for punitive damages beyond simple compensation for copying.

Anthropic has not yet responded publicly to the specific chat evidence. The company's previous statements have emphasized that Claude's training followed industry-standard practices for data sourcing, though the company has acknowledged using web-scraped data in model development.

---

## Industry-Wide Implications

The case highlights ongoing tensions between AI developers and content creators over training data practices. The music industry lawsuit is one of several major intellectual property cases currently working through courts, with publishers, artists, and media companies all pursuing claims against AI companies.

The outcome could establish important precedents for how AI companies source training data and what constitutes acceptable data collection practices in the future.
