# Laplace Intelligence Red-Teams Robots and Self Driving Cars Before They Hit the Real World

---

If there's one lesson the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident taught us, it's that AI agents can be unpredictable. "Imagine if something like that happened in the physical world," Howie Sun, CEO and co-founder of Laplace Intelligence, told TechCrunch. "AI agents are moving from computers into the real world, and we don't really want physical AI to fail in the real world."

Ann Arbor-based Laplace is building automated red-teaming — essentially, stress-testing a system by deliberately trying to make it fail — for robotics and autonomous vehicles. The startup has raised a $10.5 million Series A to scale its platform.

## How Laplace's Red-Teaming Works

Laplace's platform automatically generates adversarial scenarios for robotic systems and autonomous vehicles. Rather than manually crafting test cases, the system uses AI to identify edge cases and failure modes that human testers might miss. The platform can simulate thousands of scenarios in parallel, generating perturbations to sensor inputs, environmental conditions, and decision pathways.

"We're essentially giving these systems a comprehensive stress test before they ever touch the real world," Sun said. The platform covers perception systems, planning systems, and control systems.

## The Physical AI Safety Challenge

The broader industry is grappling with how to safely deploy AI in physical environments. Traditional software testing doesn't map well to AI systems that can behave in unexpected ways when faced with novel situations. Laplace argues that automated red-teaming is essential because the combinatorial space of real-world scenarios is too large for manual testing alone.
