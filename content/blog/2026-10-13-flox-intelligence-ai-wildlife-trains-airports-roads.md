# Flox Intelligence is building AI to keep wildlife away from trains, airports, and roads

---

When I was eight, I remember our train to Mumbai ground to a halt with a great noise. When we started moving again, a good eight hours later, someone told me we'd hit a buffalo. That memory stayed with Vineeth Kammara founding Flox Intelligence — a company using AI to prevent wildlife collisions with human infrastructure.

Flox's system uses a combination of acoustic sensors, thermal cameras, and edge AI to detect animals near railways, runways, and highways before a collision becomes inevitable. When an animal is detected, the system triggers deterrents — from ultrasonic warnings to drone-based harassment — to redirect wildlife before trains or aircraft arrive.

The AI model is trained on thousands of hours of wildlife behavior data across different species, geographies, and environmental conditions. It can distinguish between a deer and a dog, a peacock and a vulture, and predict movement trajectories based on animal behavior patterns.

Wildlife strikes cost the aviation industry over $1 billion annually and cause dozens of fatalities. Rail collisions with large animals derail trains and injure passengers. Highway collisions with deer alone cause over $1 billion in vehicle damage each year in the US. Flox is targeting all three verticals with a modular sensor platform that can be deployed along infrastructure corridors.

The company has contracts with several European railways and is in discussions with US Class 1 freight railroads and a major US airport authority.
