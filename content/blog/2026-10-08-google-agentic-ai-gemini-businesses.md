# Google Brings Agentic AI to Gemini, Starting with Businesses

---

Google is embedding autonomous AI agents directly into its Gemini platform, targeting enterprise users who need AI that can plan, execute tasks, and coordinate across multiple business applications without constant human oversight.

The new Gemini agent system can delegate work to subagents, call upon multiple AI models simultaneously, and is even assigned its own workplace identity — including an email address — making it a first-class participant in corporate workflows.

## Why This Matters for Developers

This represents a shift from **AI as a chatbot** to **AI as a coworker**. Traditional LLM interfaces require constant prompting and human decision-making at every step. Agentic systems like this one can receive a high-level goal — "prepare the Q4 product roadmap for the board meeting" — and autonomously navigate email, documents, calendars, and third-party APIs to produce a result.

For developers building on Google's ecosystem, the implications are significant:

- **Multi-model orchestration** becomes a first-class feature, not a custom implementation
- **Agent-to-agent communication** is built into the platform infrastructure  
- **Enterprise identity and access management** (IAM) now extends to AI agents

Google is positioning this as the next evolution of its AI strategy, moving beyond the single-prompt interactions that characterized earlier Gemini features. The company is targeting businesses first, with consumer rollout expected to follow.

## The Competitive Landscape

Google's push into agentic AI puts it in direct competition with Microsoft's Copilot agents, OpenAI's agent frameworks, and emerging startups like Anthropic's Claude agents. Enterprise customers increasingly want AI that can operate autonomously within defined guardrails — and Google is betting that deep integration with Workspace, Drive, and Meet will give it a structural advantage.
