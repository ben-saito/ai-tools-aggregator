# Robot Carnival in Shanghai Showcases Chinas Humanoid Robotics Push - MIT Technology Review

---

## Childrens Excitement and Government Strategy Converge at Shanghai RD Hub

At a robot carnival held at an R&D center on the outskirts of Shanghai, children and parents explored the latest in robotics technology. An 11-year-old boy excitedly watched his new mini robotic dog attempt a handstand, while parents and children queued to try various robotic demonstrations.

MIT Technology Review correspondent attended the event, which showcased the growing interest in humanoid robots in China. The country has positioned embodied AI - embedding AI into physical systems - as a key pillar of its latest five-year plan.

---

## Over 100 Companies Focus on Robotics in Shanghai Hub

The busy R&D hub houses more than 100 companies researching, developing, manufacturing, and marketing robotic technologies. These firms produce specialized bots capable of tasks ranging from transporting heavy loads to inspecting sewage pipes.

At the carnival, a tense battle broke out between two remote-controlled robots firing projectiles, while nearby, young attendees learned how to make a quadrupedal robot walk up stairs. Yang Duan, a product manager at DexForce, said humanoid robots can free people from physical labor and mundane chores.

---

## Challenges Facing Humanoid Robot Commercialization

Despite the excitement, significant barriers remain to humanoid robot adoption. Safety concerns, the challenge of balancing on two legs, high prices, and short battery life have limited appeal. Many roboticists remain skeptical that the human form factor is optimal for robots.

The event demonstrated that public exposure matters. Elsewhere, humanoid progress has been slow due to these challenges, but bringing robots directly to consumers through events like this helps build market understanding and acceptance.

---

## Government Strategy and Private Sector Momentum

The Chinese government has positioned embodied AI as a priority in its national strategy, with both public funding and policy support driving the sector forward. The carnival format serves dual purposes: cultivating future consumers and building public understanding of robotics technology.

The event featured major Chinese robotics companies showcasing their latest humanoid and non-humanoid designs, demonstrating the breadth of the countrys robotics ambitions.
