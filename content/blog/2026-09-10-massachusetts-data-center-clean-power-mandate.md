# Massachusetts Becomes Third State to Restrict Data Centers Over Clean Power Concerns

Massachusetts has become the third state in as many months to impose new restrictions on data center development, requiring large facilities to secure clean power or contribute to a ratepayer protection fund.

---

## A New Mandate: Clean Power or Payment

Governor Maura Healey's executive order targets data centers larger than 25 megawatts of peak demand. Under the new rules, developers must bring their own power generation that meets Massachusetts clean energy standards, or alternatively fund new clean energy construction nearby, or pay into a ratepayer protection fund.

The mandate is notably stricter than the state's existing clean energy standard. While the standard requires industries to source a portion of their electricity from approved renewable sources like wind, solar, and hydro, the governor's office clarified that data centers under this order must meet 100% of their electricity demand with clean energy generation.

---

## A National Trend: States Push Back Against Data Centers

Massachusetts is the third state to act in quick succession. In August, Texas Governor Greg Abbott announced that all new data centers in the state would need to submit to audits by the public utility commission and ERCOT, the Texas grid operator. In July, New York's governor halted construction of new data centers sized at 50 megawatts or larger.

The shift represents a dramatic reversal from just a few years ago, when tech companies and data center developers were showered with tax incentives to locate facilities in states competing for the jobs and economic activity they brought.

Now, politicians across multiple states are responding to growing public opposition. Concerns include visual impact, noise, strain on power grids, and the environmental footprint of facilities that consume enormous amounts of electricity to power and cool rows of servers.

---

## Secrecy Prohibited and Tax Exemptions Paused

Massachusetts is also directing communities to avoid signing non-disclosure agreements with data center developers, aiming to increase transparency around deals that have sometimes been negotiated behind closed doors.

The governor is simultaneously pausing applications for a data center sales tax exemption that went into effect last month, giving regulators time to implement the new restrictions.

---

## Industry Pushback: AI PAC Enters the Political Arena

As public sentiment has shifted, the technology industry has begun pushing back politically. Leading the Future, a pro-AI super PAC funded by Marc Andreessen, Ben Horowitz, and Greg Brockman, is purchasing advertising in battleground states ahead of upcoming elections. The group argues that restricting data center development will hamper American AI competitiveness.

---

## Why This Matters

Data centers are the physical infrastructure underpinning the AI boom. As large language models and AI services require massive compute resources, the demand for data center capacity has surged. States that once vied to attract these facilities now face pressure from residents and local officials questioning the tradeoffs.

The Massachusetts order sets a high bar: 100% clean energy for facilities above 25 megawatts. How the state defines and enforces "clean power" will be watched closely by other jurisdictions considering similar measures.
