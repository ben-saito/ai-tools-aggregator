# AI Models Fail Standard Intelligence Tests: Can Humans Do Better?

MIT Technology Review explores a growing body of research showing that AI models consistently fail certain types of intelligence tests that humans handle readily. The article invites readers to try the same tests themselves.

---

## The Testing Problem

Puzzles and games have been central to AI development since the field's inception. Developers have long used game-playing performance as a benchmark for progress. But recent research reveals systematic blind spots in how AI systems approach novel problems.

The tests in question involve reasoning patterns that humans find intuitive but that trip up current models. These are not impossible challenges—they are straightforward puzzles that reveal something about how AI systems actually process information.

---

## What the Failures Reveal

The article argues that these failures illuminate important gaps in current AI architectures. Models that excel at pattern matching and statistical inference can still struggle with tasks requiring genuine analogical reasoning or spatial intuition.

Understanding these limitations helps researchers identify what new capabilities are needed—and what the current generation of models fundamentally cannot do despite impressive benchmark performance.

---

## Try It Yourself

The piece includes seven tests that readers can attempt to see how they compare to AI systems. The challenge illustrates the difference between tasks where AI excels and tasks where human intuition still holds advantages.

For developers, the takeaway is that benchmark performance does not equal general intelligence—and that some categories of problems remain distinctly human.
