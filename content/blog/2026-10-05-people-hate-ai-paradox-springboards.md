# People Really Hate AI, So Why Can't They Get Enough?

A new MIT Technology Review piece explores a striking paradox in how the public relates to artificial intelligence: people simultaneously express deep skepticism and distrust toward AI systems while continuing to adopt them at an accelerating rate.

The observation comes from a conversation with the CEO of Springboards, a startup building an LLM designed to generate a wider variety of responses than its mainstream rivals. At the start of the conversation, the CEO offered a candid framing: "We often say that we're a self-loathing AI."

That phrase captures a tension that has become increasingly visible in the AI industry. Users voice frustration with AI for perpetuating biases, destroying jobs, spreading misinformation, and concentrating power -- yet adoption continues to climb. AI assistants are now embedded in daily workflows across industries, from drafting emails to writing code to supporting research tasks.

The disconnect between stated attitudes and actual behavior presents both a challenge and an opportunity for AI builders. Products that acknowledge this complexity -- rather than pretending it doesn't exist -- may be better positioned to earn genuine user trust.
