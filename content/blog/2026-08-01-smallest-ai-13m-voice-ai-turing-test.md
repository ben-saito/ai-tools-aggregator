# Smallest.ai Raises $13M for Voice AI That Passes the Turing Test

Smallest.ai, a seed-stage startup developing ultra-fast voice AI models, has raised $13 million in a funding round led by venture capital firms focused on enterprise automation. The company's core technology is a voice synthesis system designed to generate human-like speech with latency low enough to enable natural real-time conversations. According to the founding team, the primary target use case is AI-powered phone agents that can sustain extended calls without detection as automated systems.

The pitch centers on a specific technical threshold: the startup claims its models can hold conversations that pass informal Turing test conditions in phone call contexts, where victims are not actively trying to distinguish machine from human. This is a narrower claim than passing a formal Turing test but reflects the practical constraints of deployed voice AI in customer service and support applications. The company cites internal evaluations showing that callers failed to correctly identify the AI in the majority of extended interactions.

The $13M round will be used to expand model training infrastructure and scale to a larger number of concurrent call channels. Smallest.ai is positioning itself against larger voice AI providers by emphasizing latency and naturalness metrics rather than broad platform coverage. The company has not disclosed its model architecture or training data composition.

The voice AI market has seen significant investment activity, with established players including ElevenLabs, Microsoft, and OpenAI all competing for enterprise voice agent deployments. Smallest.ai's approach focuses specifically on the phone call segment, where latency constraints are most stringent and caller expectations differ from those of voice assistant or content generation applications.
