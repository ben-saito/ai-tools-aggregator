# Oracle and OpenAI Promise 2GW of Renewables for Stargate Data Center as Opposition Grows

Oracle has announced a plan to invest in 2 gigawatts of renewable energy projects in New Mexico as part of the $165 billion Project Jupiter data center that the company is building for OpenAI. The announcement comes as local opposition threatens to delay construction of the facility, which is part of the broader Stargate AI infrastructure initiative announced by the Trump administration in 2025.

---

## From Gas Turbines to Fuel Cells

Project Jupiter, located in Santa Teresa, New Mexico, was originally planned to run on 2.2 gigawatts of natural gas generation that would have produced approximately 14 million tons of CO2 emissions per year, according to Michael Thomas, CEO of the Cleanview data platform. Thomas noted that this would have effectively "wiped out 20 years of carbon emissions reductions in the state of New Mexico."

In response to local protests and court battles, Oracle and OpenAI have pivoted from gas turbines to fuel-cell technology produced by Bloom Energy. The fuel cells generate electricity through an electrochemical process without combustion, reducing nitrogen oxide emissions by more than 90 percent compared to the original gas turbine plan.

"The deployment of 2.45 gigawatts of fuel cells could substantially reduce the amount of other air pollutants, like carbon monoxide and sulfur dioxide," according to Julia Robin, Vice President of Network Strategy at Bloom Energy.

---

## Renewables Pledge Draws Skepticism

Oracle's announcement included a commitment to invest in 2 gigawatts of new renewable energy projects in New Mexico and a $1 million pledge for carbon capture research. However, experts point out significant limitations.

"Like all matching programs, this would be synthetic in the sense that 2 GW of renewables wouldn't directly power the data center," Thomas wrote in a LinkedIn post.

The fuel cells powering Project Jupiter will still run on natural gas because it remains "available and reliable, especially in this region of the country," Robin acknowledged. The companies have set a target of "100 percent carbon-free energy matching by 2031," but this refers to renewable energy credits rather than direct powering of the facility.

"Even with the new plan, the data center could be the largest source of emissions in the state," Thomas noted.

---

## Local Opposition Continues

New Mexico's Land Office has twice blocked requests to run a natural gas pipeline to the data center site in Doña Ana County. State residents and environmental groups have organized protests and filed lawsuits against the project.

The opposition has already forced one significant change: the switch from gas turbines to fuel cells. However, critics argue that the fundamental environmental impact remains substantial as long as the facility relies on natural gas combustion, even with advanced fuel-cell technology.

Project Jupiter is part of the Stargate AI infrastructure program, a national initiative to build massive data center campuses to support the computational demands of frontier AI models. Oracle's data center will serve as a key computational hub for OpenAI's AI services.
