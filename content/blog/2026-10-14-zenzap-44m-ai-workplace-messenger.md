Zenzap, a startup building an AI-powered private messenger aimed at businesses in emerging markets, has raised $44 million in Series A funding to expand its footprint beyond WhatsApp, Telegram, and traditional enterprise communication tools.

The platform offers a single encrypted messaging app with built-in AI agents that can draft documents, summarize conversations, schedule meetings, and pull data from connected business tools like CRM systems and ERP platforms — all without the data-sharing risks associated with consumer apps.

Zenzap's target customers are companies in regions like Southeast Asia, Africa, and Latin America, where workers frequently use consumer chat apps for work purposes, creating compliance and data governance challenges. The startup positions itself as a drop-in replacement for WhatsApp with enterprise-grade security and AI capabilities baked in.

The messaging app supports end-to-end encryption, offline message delivery, and integration with legacy systems like SAP and Salesforce. Its AI layer sits on top of the chat interface as an always-available assistant that can answer employee questions, generate reports, and automate routine workflows.

Founder Guy Weiss said the funding will be used to expand Zenzap's presence in Southeast Asia and the Middle East, and to deepen integrations with regional productivity tools. The company currently operates in 14 countries and plans to launch in six more by the end of the year.
