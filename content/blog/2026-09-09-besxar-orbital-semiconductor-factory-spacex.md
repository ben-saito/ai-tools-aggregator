# Besxar Wants to Build Chip Factories in Orbit — Using SpaceX Falcon 9 Rockets

A startup founded by a former OpenAI employee is taking semiconductor manufacturing to new heights — literally. Besxar, founded by former OpenAI staffer Ashley Pilipiszyn, is developing orbital fabrication facilities for producing advanced semiconductor precursors, leveraging the vacuum of space to achieve manufacturing conditions impossible on Earth.

---

## Why Build Chips in Space?

The core proposition of orbital semiconductor manufacturing rests on physics: the vacuum of space eliminates contamination sources that plague Earth-based chip fabs. On Earth, semiconductor fabrication requires enormous energy expenditure to create and maintain ultra-clean environments. In orbit, the natural vacuum is already orders of magnitude better than any artificial cleanroom.

"We're at a time where it's actually more cost-effective to go where the physics already works," Pilipiszyn told TechCrunch. "Don't do it on Earth where you're fighting physics."

Semiconductor fabrication requires precisely controlled environments where even trace amounts of particulate matter can destroy chips. The vacuum of space naturally eliminates airborne contamination, while microgravity enables manufacturing of materials with novel properties impossible to create under Earth's gravitational pull.

---

## SpaceX Falcon 9 as the Workhorse

The company's near-term strategy relies on SpaceX's Falcon 9 rocket — the most frequently flown orbital vehicle in history, with 163 round trips in the last year alone. This established launch infrastructure gives Besxar a reliable path to orbit without waiting for next-generation vehicles.

The company's first two "fabships" — small canisters designed to carry and test semiconductor materials in orbit — flew on a July Starlink mission. The goal was to prove the canister design could survive launch and operate in the space environment.

"The flown samples were the cleanest and had the least amount of particulate matter compared to nonflown terrestrial wafers, which is fantastic for us as we scale up," Pilipiszyn said.

---

## Scaling Up to Starship

Over the next two years, Besxar plans to iterate on its orbital fabrication technology, with the ultimate goal of flying larger fabrication facilities onboard SpaceX's Starship — the next-generation rocket still in development. Starship's massive payload capacity would enable significantly larger orbital factories than current vehicles can support.

Once Besxar produces qualification samples, the company aims to supply leading chipmakers with space-manufactured semiconductor materials. The company name itself is drawn from **beskar**, the metal used in Star Wars Legends comic books to make Mandalorian armor — chosen, Pilipiszyn noted, because the material properties in space manufacturing mirror the fictional strength of the alloy.

The venture represents an unusual intersection of frontier AI infrastructure demand — which is driving unprecedented chip demand — and the emerging commercial space industry. As AI training requires increasingly specialized silicon, the limiting factor on advanced chip production may shift from design capability to manufacturing environment quality.
