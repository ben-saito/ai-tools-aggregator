# AI Models Crack World War II Enigma Messages Using LLM Cryptanalysis

A pair of cryptanalysts report having solved two long-unsolved Enigma-encoded messages from World War II using large language models developed by OpenAI and Anthropic. The achievement represents a novel application of frontier AI capabilities to historical cryptographic challenges and demonstrates the evolving nature of machine performance on codebreaking tasks.

---

## Turing's Other Test

Alan Turing is best known for the Turing Test, which measures whether a machine can exhibit intelligent behavior indistinguishable from a human. However, during his lifetime Turing's most consequential work was breaking the Enigma cipher used by Nazi Germany—a feat credited with shortening the war by years.

The messages cracked by the research team were among those remaining unsolved from World War II communications intercepted by Allied intelligence. The Enigma machine's encryption was considered virtually unbreakable before British codebreakers at Bletchley Park, including Turing, developed techniques that ultimately revealed German military communications.

---

## How LLMs Approach Cryptanalysis

The research team used LLMs from OpenAI and Anthropic as part of their cryptanalysis workflow. Rather than treating the language models as direct decryption oracles, the researchers leveraged the models' ability to identify patterns, make probabilistic inferences about language structure, and reason about encoding schemes—all capabilities relevant to breaking historical ciphers.

Modern LLMs have demonstrated surprising performance on tasks requiring formal reasoning about structured data. The Enigma challenge represents an interesting test case for whether these capabilities transfer to formal cryptographic analysis, where the goal is recovering plaintext from encoded signals without access to keys.

---

## Implications for AI Cryptanalysis Capabilities

The achievement adds to growing evidence that frontier language models possess emergent capabilities relevant to security applications. While the Enigma messages represent historical rather than contemporary encryption, the underlying techniques—pattern recognition, probabilistic reasoning, systematic search across candidate solutions—have direct relevance to modern cryptographic analysis.

Security researchers have begun exploring LLM-assisted approaches to vulnerability discovery, protocol analysis, and cryptographic implementation auditing. The Enigma result suggests these approaches may have genuine analytical power beyond simple pattern matching.
