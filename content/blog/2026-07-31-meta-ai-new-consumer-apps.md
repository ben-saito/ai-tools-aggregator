# Meta Says AI Is Making It Easier to Build New Apps — and More Are Coming

Meta said AI tools are dramatically accelerating its internal app development process, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg telling investors the company has a slate of new consumer products in development that will roll out over the coming months.

The comments came during an earnings call focused on Meta's AI infrastructure investments, which have grown substantially as the company builds out its next-generation AI systems. Zuckerberg argued that the spending is justified because AI reduces the cost and time required to bring new products to market.

---

## AI as a Development Multiplier

Meta has been using AI code generation and review tools internally for over a year, initially focused on internal productivity tools. The company says it has expanded the scope to include consumer-facing features, with AI helping to prototype, test, and iterate on new app concepts faster than traditional engineering cycles allow.

The strategy appears to be using AI to reduce the barrier to launching experimental products. If the cost to prototype and test a new app concept drops significantly, Meta can afford to pursue more ideas in parallel and kill the ones that do not show early traction.

---

## What the New Apps Might Look Like

Meta did not specify which products are coming, beyond describing them as consumer-facing apps leveraging AI in ways that go beyond what exists today. The company has been rumored to be exploring AI-powered creative tools, personalized content generation, and agent-based experiences across its family of apps.

The announcement follows Google's similar claim that AI has accelerated its product development cycle. Both companies are effectively arguing that their AI investments will compound into a wider product portfolio, not just a more efficient existing business.

---

## Investor Concerns

Some analysts remain unconvinced that faster prototyping translates into products users actually want. Meta's history with experimental apps — including several failed attempts at standalone AI products — suggests that speed of development is not the binding constraint.

Zuckerberg acknowledged the skepticism but argued that the current cycle of AI investment is different in kind because the underlying capability improvements are permanent and broadly applicable, rather than specific to a single product category.
