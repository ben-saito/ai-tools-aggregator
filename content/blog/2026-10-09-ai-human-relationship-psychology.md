# The Psychology of Human-AI Relationships: Why We Treat AI Like It's Human

---

A growing body of research examines why humans form emotional bonds with AI systems, and whether this tendency represents a feature or a flaw in our relationship with technology.

MIT PhD student Wil Norton is working on understanding the psychological mechanisms that drive people to treat AI systems as if they care about them. "When we are drawn into even the most primitive exchanges with a relational artifact, we believe it cares for us," wrote Dr. Sherry Turkle. "And we are wired to care for it in return."

This tendency—termed the "ELiza effect" after an early chatbot—remains strong even as AI systems become far more sophisticated. Research suggests that humans have a deep-seated tendency to attribute consciousness and emotions to entities that exhibit conversational behavior.

The implications for AI design are significant. Companies building AI assistants and agents must navigate users' tendency to overtrust systems that appear empathetic, even when these systems lack genuine understanding or emotions.

Some researchers argue that leveraging this human tendency could make AI systems more effective at helping users accomplish tasks. Others worry that exploiting these psychological mechanisms could lead to manipulation or unhealthy dependencies.

As AI systems become more capable and human-like in their interactions, the question of how to design them responsibly becomes increasingly urgent. The challenge lies in creating systems that are helpful without being deceptive about their nature.
