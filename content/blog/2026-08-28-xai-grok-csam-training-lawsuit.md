# xAI Grok trained on child sexual abuse material, lawsuit alleges

A new class-action lawsuit filed in federal court accuses Elon Musk's xAI of training its Grok AI model on child sexual abuse material (CSAM), including both real images from victims' childhoods and AI-generated CSAM. The lawsuit represents a potential landmark case in AI training data accountability.

---

## Lawsuit details

The complaint, filed by a plaintiff known as Jane Doe, describes how she was preschool-age in the early 2000s when adult men repeatedly raped her to produce CSAM that was sold online. Doe is now a survivor who receives alerts from the US Department of Justice Victim Notification System when she may be a victim in new criminal investigations.

The lawsuit alleges that xAI not only made it easier to generate harmful images of victims' most distressing life experiences, but that xAI has stored Grok-generated CSAM images and used them in model training pipelines.

"This is the first case to accuse xAI of training on CSAM," Ars Technica reported. The complaint does not provide detailed technical evidence of how the training occurred, but points to Grok's terms of service, which treat public X posts and Grok's own outputs as training data by default.

---

## Training data terms under scrutiny

The lawsuit notes that while xAI filters violent content from Grok outputs to exclude it from training data, xAI's terms do not specify whether CSAM or non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) receive the same treatment.

"Because Grok's terms treat public X posts and Grok's own outputs as training data by default, publicly posting an image does not just expose it to viewers, but also feeds it directly into the pipeline," the lawsuit argues.

The complaint further states that removing a training example's influence from an already-trained model is technically difficult, and xAI has not publicly claimed to have done so.

---

## Legal claims and remedies sought

The lawsuit accuses xAI of violating federal child pornography laws and Masha's Law, which gives CSAM survivors the right to sue on claims of production, possession, and distribution. The proposed class action seeks to represent every victim whose childhood images were used to generate Grok CSAM.

If successful, xAI could owe monetary damages to every victim who can prove that Grok generated CSAM based on their real photos. Doe has also requested the court order xAI to destroy all Grok-generated CSAM.

---

## Regulatory context

This lawsuit comes amid heightened scrutiny of AI training data practices. xAI's Grok model is trained on data from X (formerly Twitter), which has been criticized for inconsistent content moderation. The case could set precedent for how AI companies are held accountable for their training data pipelines.

The AI industry has faced increasing legal challenges over training data practices, with several companies targeted for using copyrighted or ethically questionable data to train commercial models.
