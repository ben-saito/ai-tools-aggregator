# The Ecologist Revealing Invisible Networks That Revive Rainforests

---

Daisy Dent, an ecologist at MIT Technology Review, studies the underground fungal networks that connect trees in tropical rainforests. Her research reveals how these mycorrhizal networks facilitate nutrient sharing between trees, effectively creating a "wood wide web" that supports forest regeneration after disturbance.

## Fungal Networks in Rainforests

Rainforest trees are connected by mycorrhizal fungi that exchange nutrients across species boundaries. A tree with excess carbon can send it through fungal networks to a neighboring tree in need. This communication system has implications for forest resilience and for understanding how rainforests might respond to climate change.

## Data and AI in Ecology

Dent's work involves large-scale sensor networks to monitor forest conditions in real time. The data collected from these sensors is analyzed using AI models to understand patterns in forest health and to predict regeneration outcomes.

## Implications for Conservation

Understanding these networks changes how conservationists think about forest restoration. Rather than planting individual trees, effective restoration may require reconstituting entire fungal communities that support forest ecosystems.
