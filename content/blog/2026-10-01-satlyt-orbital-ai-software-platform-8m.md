# Satlyt Raises $8M to Build Open AI Software Platform for Satellites

Satlyt, a startup founded by a former Google and SpaceX product manager, has raised $8 million in seed funding to develop open software for running AI workloads on satellites. The company is positioning itself as the "Android of orbital computing" — an open ecosystem that works across multiple providers' spacecraft, in contrast to SpaceX's closed, all-in-one approach.

---

## From Google Cloud to SpaceX to Orbital AI

The company's founder, a Kenyan American engineer, previously worked in Google's cloud computing business before joining SpaceX's Starlink communications network in 2024. That experience convinced him there was untapped potential for computing power in orbit.

"The folks like SpaceX who are doing orbital data centers—if they are the iPhone, we'll build Android as a horizontally integrated, open ecosystem," said the founder.

---

## Launching Alongside Google's Project Suncatcher

The timing aligns with a wave of investment in orbital infrastructure. SpaceX has gone all-in on orbital data centers, and Satlyt's software is set to launch on a SpaceX rocket this week alongside the first prototype for Google's own space data center effort, Project Suncatcher.

Unlike SpaceX, Google, or competitors like Starcloud and Cowboy Space Company, Satlyt is not building its own spacecraft. Instead, the team is building the software layer to operate AI models on satellites — drawing comparisons to VMware and Snowflake, companies that let users run complex software on distributed infrastructure.

---

## Gemma AI Model Already Deployed On Orbit

Satlyt has already flown its software on two demonstration missions. Earlier this year, the company deployed Google DeepMind's Gemma AI model onboard a spacecraft operated by Momentus. The model cut the size of a transmission about onboard software errors by more than 60% — the kind of efficiency that can save hundreds of thousands of dollars per satellite per year.

This week's launch will put Satlyt's software to work on a spacecraft built by TakeMe2Space, an Indian startup that manufactures computing hardware for satellites. The mission has three customers: NASA is paying to test protocols for cloud computing in space; Stellerian is another commercial customer; and a third party is also participating.

---

## A Shared Computing Cloud Across Satellites

Currently, few high-powered GPUs are in orbit. The near-term opportunity lies in operational efficiency workloads like the ones Satlyt has already demonstrated. The company's next project is to create a shared computing system — a cloud — spanning two different satellites, a capability it expects to attempt next year.

"The way to think about it is that we turn your satellite into a revenue-generating managed service," the founder said.

As orbital computing shifts from science experiment to infrastructure, Satlyt is betting that openness and interoperability will be the key differentiator against vertically integrated competitors building closed satellite ecosystems.
