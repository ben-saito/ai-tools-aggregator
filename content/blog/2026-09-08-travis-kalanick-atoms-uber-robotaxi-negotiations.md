# Travis Kalanick's Atoms Reportedly Negotiating with Uber for Robotaxi Technology Integration

Uber founder Travis Kalanick's AI and robotics startup Atoms is reportedly in talks with Uber about integrating its robotaxi technology into the ride-hailing giant's platform, according to a Financial Times report. The discussions represent the latest step in Kalanick's pursuit of what he has called "unfinished business" in autonomous vehicles.

---

## Atoms' Rapid Expansion

Earlier this summer, Atoms announced a $1.7 billion funding round led by Andreessen Horowitz. Despite the mega-round, Kalanick remained reticent about the specific targets of the startup's technology. The FT report now provides the clearest picture yet: Atoms is preparing for a hiring spree and acquisitions aimed at establishing itself as a major player in the autonomous vehicle industry.

The startup has already made a strategic acquisition in this space: Pronto, an autonomous mining startup led by Anthony Levandowski, who previously served as Uber's self-driving chief. Levandowski was convicted of stealing trade secrets and sentenced to 18 months in prison, later pardoned by President Donald Trump.

---

## Uber's Existing AV Partnerships

Uber has already established partnerships with multiple autonomous vehicle companies, creating a potential distribution channel for Atoms' technology. The company has invested $100 million in Atoms — a figure previously confirmed by TechCrunch — signaling its strategic interest in the startup's capabilities.

The potential partnership with Atoms would add another dimension to Uber's expanding AV network, which already includes agreements with companies across the autonomous vehicle landscape.

---

## "Unfinished Business"

Kalanick's push into autonomous vehicles carries a sense of déjà vu. During his tenure as Uber's CEO, he championed the company's autonomous vehicle ambitions before his departure in 2017. The acquisition of Pronto and the reported negotiations with Uber suggest Kalanick is picking up where he left off.

Sources emphasized that robotaxis do not represent the entirety of Atoms' plans, but the direction aligns with Kalanick's description of the recent funding round as an opportunity to pursue "unfinished business" in the autonomous vehicle sector.
