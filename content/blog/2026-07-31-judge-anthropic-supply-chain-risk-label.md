# Judge Says Trump Admin Still Lacks Evidence for Anthropic Supply Chain Risk Label

A federal judge said the Trump administration has not presented enough evidence to justify labeling Anthropic a supply chain risk, casting doubt on the government's ban on its AI technology.

The ruling represents a significant setback for the administration's policy targeting advanced AI companies. Officials had argued that Anthropic's models posed national security risks, but the court found the evidence presented fell short of the legal standard required to sustain such a designation.

---

## Background of the Case

The Trump administration moved to restrict Anthropic's operations in the United States earlier this year, citing concerns about the company's ties to foreign infrastructure and the potential for data access by hostile actors. Anthropic pushed back, arguing that the designation was politically motivated and lacked factual basis.

The judge's decision centered on the burden of proof required under existing trade and national security law. Agencies seeking to restrict commercial technology on supply chain grounds must present concrete evidence, not speculation, the court noted.

---

## Industry and Policy Implications

The case is being watched closely by AI companies and investors, as it could set a precedent for how future administrations attempt to regulate advanced AI systems. If the ruling stands, it may constrain the government's ability to use executive action to restrict AI providers without clear statutory authority.

Anthropic has continued to operate normally while the legal proceedings unfold. The company declined to comment on the ruling directly but issued a statement emphasizing its commitment to working with policymakers on evidence-based AI governance.

---

## What's Next

The government could appeal the decision to a higher court, though legal experts say the ruling's reasoning is tightly tied to the specific factual record in this case. Broader legislative efforts to establish clearer criteria for AI supply chain restrictions remain under discussion in Congress.
