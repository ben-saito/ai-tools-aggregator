# Google Tests Orbital Data Center with 4 TPUs — Suncatcher Satellite Launches October 1

Google is testing an experimental satellite data center called "Suncatcher" (codenamed MVP) that will process AI workloads in orbit. The satellite is scheduled to launch on October 1, 2026 aboard SpaceX's Falcon 9 rocket as part of the Transporter-18 rideshare mission.

---

## Refrigerator-Sized Satellite Carries 4 Google TPUs — 15-Minute Test Window

The MVP satellite is approximately the size of a refrigerator and houses four of Google's custom TPU AI accelerators — the same chips used in Earth-based data centers for AI model training and inference. On Earth, data centers house thousands of these chips consuming massive amounts of power.

For this orbital test, the solar panels supply only about one kilowatt of power — enough to run a microwave or a hair dryer. Google did not build the satellite from scratch; while the AI hardware inside is Google's, the spacecraft bus comes from a satellite imagery provider.

---

## Testing TPU Performance in Space Conditions

Google has conducted ground testing, but launching the satellite will provide a fuller picture of current challenges. The team needs to evaluate how Tensor Chips handle extreme temperature swings and space radiation. In many space missions, hardware is designed specifically for these conditions from the outset.

The test window is intentionally limited — the satellite will run for approximately 15 minutes at a time. Eventually, Google envisions networks of orbiting satellites communicating over high-speed laser connections, likely requiring expensive dedicated launches.

---

## Orbital AI Infrastructure: Competing Visions

Google's approach differs from Elon Musk's SpaceX/Starlink vision for Mars-orbital data centers and SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son's skepticism about the practicality of space-based AI infrastructure. Google's strategy leverages existing space infrastructure while starting with small-scale tests to verify technical feasibility.

The broader industry debate centers on whether space-based AI compute can deliver on its promises of low latency and global coverage while overcoming challenges of cost, maintainability, and physical constraints. Google's Suncatcher test results will be a key data point for the future of orbital AI computing.
