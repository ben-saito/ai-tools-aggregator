# Toby Ord Models Dynamics of RSI-Driven Intelligence Explosion

---

Researcher Toby Ord has tried to think through what recursive self-improvement (RSI) could look like in practice. His take, outlined in recent analysis, is that the dynamics of RSI are ultimately difficult to predict but potentially extremely rapid.

Ord, known for his work on existential risk and the long-term future of civilization, has been thinking about RSI as a theoretical scenario in which an AI system becomes capable of improving its own capabilities, leading to a feedback loop of accelerating intelligence.

## Key Features of the RSI Dynamic

Ord identifies several characteristics that make RSI-driven development distinct from ordinary capability advances:

**Compression of development cycles** — Normal research and development involves human researchers whose productivity is relatively constant. An RSI system could improve the efficiency of the improvement process itself, leading to exponentially decreasing cycle times.

**Unpredictable capability jumps** — Because RSI involves the system improving at improving, the rate of progress can shift dramatically without warning. Ordinary development curves are relatively smooth; RSI curves can have sharp inflection points.

**Measurement difficulty** — It becomes harder to assess the capabilities of a system that is actively changing its own architecture in ways that may not be transparent to external observers.

## What This Means for Governance

If RSI is even theoretically possible, it has profound implications for AI governance. Traditional regulatory approaches assume relatively predictable development timelines and identifiable points of intervention. RSI could bypass these assumptions entirely.

Ord's analysis suggests we should be thinking about governance mechanisms that can respond to extremely rapid capability changes — possibly involving real-time monitoring of AI research progress, international coordination on advanced AI development, and new frameworks for evaluating risk from systems that may be self-modifying.

## The Broader Pacing Context

This analysis connects to the broader "pacing AI" research agenda, which seeks to understand how society might maintain control over AI development trajectories even in scenarios involving rapid, self-reinforcing capability advances.

The fundamental question is whether RSI is a realistic near-term scenario or a more speculative concern. Ord's work does not claim RSI is likely or imminent, but rather seeks to understand what it would look like if it occurred.
