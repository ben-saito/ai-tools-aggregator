# Apple CEO John Ternus: The Best AI Device Is Still the iPhone

At Apple's "Surprise and Shine" event on September 9, 2026 — the company's first major public appearance since John Ternus officially took the reins from Tim Cook — the new CEO laid out a clear vision for Apple's AI strategy: the iPhone is the "intelligent personal hub" that Apple will build its AI future around.

---

## Ternus Defines the 'Intelligent Personal Hub'

In his keynote, Ternus described the ideal AI device as one that has a broad understanding of a user's personal context, is always with the owner, and has a powerful processor capable of running AI models on-device. The device must also have a large display, high-quality cameras and microphones, and long battery life.

"You'd also want it to be super easy to use without a big learning curve," Ternus added. "And finally, you'd want it to work seamlessly with your other devices, apps, and services, so it feels like one integrated experience."

"In other words," he concluded, "there's no product in the world better designed to be your intelligent personal hub than iPhone."

---

## On-Device AI as Privacy Moat

Ternus drew a sharp distinction between Apple's approach and that of competitors: "Others see [users' personal] data as something to collect and store." Apple, he said, runs Apple Intelligence on device "whenever it can" — a deliberate positioning of on-device processing not just as a technical choice but as a philosophical statement about user trust.

"Honestly, trust only goes so far when your data is no longer yours to control," Ternus said, in what reads as a pointed reference to cloud-dependent AI competitors.

---

## The Transition from Cook: Continuity and New Direction

Ternus took over as CEO from Tim Cook, who moves to the role of executive chairman. The transition marks the end of an era that saw Apple grow into the world's most valuable company, but Ternus is betting that the AI era will reward the same principles that made Apple successful: integrated hardware and software, privacy-first processing, and a deep understanding of personal context.

The company's strategy stands in contrast to approaches from OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft, all of which are building AI products that rely heavily on cloud infrastructure. Apple is wagering that consumers who care about privacy will pay a premium for an AI experience that stays on their person rather than in the cloud.
