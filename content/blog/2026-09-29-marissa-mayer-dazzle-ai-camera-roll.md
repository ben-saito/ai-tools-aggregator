# Marissa Mayer's Dazzle: AI Assistant Built Entirely on Your Camera Roll

Former Yahoo CEO Marissa Mayer is unveiling Dazzle, a new AI personal assistant that eschews the typical approach of building user context from email, calendars, and messaging apps. Instead, Dazzle derives all its understanding of users from a single source: their photo library.

---

## A Different Approach to AI Assistants

Mayer, who previously founded Sunshine (which launched the AI-powered photo tool Shine in 2024), argues that photos represent an underappreciated source of personal information. "You think that photos are an underappreciated source of information," Mayer said. "You'll be surprised what we can learn about you and how good a job we can do with your photos."

Dazzle analyzes all photos stored on a user's phone to understand their hobbies, interests, food and style preferences, how they spend their time, and their social connections. The startup raised an $8 million seed round in December 2025.

---

## Two Core Experiences

Dazzle offers two primary modes of interaction. For immediate tasks, users can scan recent photos to pull specific details — for example, populating a calendar from an event flyer or finding a repair person after photographing a broken appliance. For longer-term planning, the assistant mines the photo library to generate personalized recommendations for vacations, gifts, and activities.

In testing, vacation recommendations picked up on past travel patterns — suggesting Mediterranean destinations based on trips to Spain and Greece. However, limitations exist: the assistant failed to remember a user's daughter already knows how to roller skate when asked about buying skates for an upcoming birthday.

---

## Privacy and Competition

Mayer emphasizes that Dazzle prioritizes privacy and discards any personal information the AI flags as sensitive. This positioning contrasts with competitors like Meta's Muse and Instinct, which have faced security concerns and require access to more sensitive data like emails and messages.

The AI assistant market has grown crowded, with Meta, OpenAI, Google, and Apple all launching personal AI tools. Mayer argues that given security concerns surrounding other products, users may prefer an assistant that limits its data collection to photo libraries rather than granting broad access to communications.

Dazzle is available through its app or via text interaction.
