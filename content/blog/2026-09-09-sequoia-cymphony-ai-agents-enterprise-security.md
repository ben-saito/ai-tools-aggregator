# Sequoia doubles down on Cymphony as AI agents create new enterprise security risks

Enterprise security teams are facing a new challenge: AI agents that operate at machine speed with access to the same sensitive corporate data as human employees, but without the same access controls and identity tracking. Sequoia Capital is betting big on解决这个问题 by backing Cymphony, a startup that has emerged with $30 million in funding to help companies manage their growing AI workforce.

The funding includes a $25 million Series A co-led by Sequoia and SMBC Fin Atlas Beyond Fund, valuing the New York- and Tel Aviv-based startup at more than $100 million after investment. The round follows a previously undisclosed seed investment from Sequoia.

---

## The problem: AI agents lack identity controls

Unlike employees, AI agents do not necessarily go through standard access and identity controls. They can access multiple systems and handle large amounts of corporate data, making it difficult for enterprises to track who -- or what -- has access to what. As more enterprises deploy AI agents to automate workflows, this gap in visibility creates significant security risks.

Cymphony is building a platform that gives security teams a single view of employees, AI agents, and other non-human identities, including the systems and sensitive data they can access. At the core of its platform, the two-year-old startup has built identity and access management capabilities specifically designed for the AI agent era.

---

## Enterprise AI workforce management becomes a new category

The launch of Cymphony reflects a broader trend: as AI agents proliferate in enterprise environments, companies need new tools to govern and monitor these digital workers. Traditional IAM (Identity and Access Management) systems were designed for human employees, not for autonomous or semi-autonomous AI agents that can make decisions and take actions across multiple systems.

Cymphony's approach centers on providing comprehensive visibility into all non-human identities operating within an enterprise, enabling security teams to enforce least-privilege access policies and detect anomalous behavior from AI agents in real time.

---

## Funding landscape for AI security startups

The $30 million raise for Cymphony comes as venture capital investment in AI security startups continues to grow. With the rise of agentic AI systems -- AI that can autonomously plan and execute multi-step tasks -- the attack surface for enterprises has expanded significantly. Investors see opportunities in startups that can provide security tooling specifically for this new paradigm.
