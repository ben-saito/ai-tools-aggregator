# Judge Denies xAI's Request to Block Minnesota Ban on 'Nudify' Apps

A federal judge has denied xAI's attempt to block Minnesota's first-in-the-nation law banning apps that allow users to create non-consensual sexualized images, ruling that the company's lawsuit was filed too late to warrant emergency relief.

U.S. District Judge Donovan Frank denied xAI's request for a temporary restraining order on July 29, 2026, nearly three months after the law was signed and just three days before it was set to take effect. "Such a delay in bringing the action and the motion suggests that harm is not immediate," Frank wrote.

---

## xAI's Legal Challenge

The lawsuit, filed by xAI as part of Elon Musk's X/SpaceX conglomerate, argues that the Minnesota ban is "overinclusive" and that "there are far less restrictive alternatives" that could achieve the state's protective goals. The ban prohibits apps that strip clothing from photos to create fake nude images, technology commonly known as "nudify" or "deepfake nude" tools.

The judge did not rule on the merits of the law itself—only that xAI waited too long to seek an emergency injunction. The underlying lawsuit challenging the law's constitutionality will continue.

Earlier this year, users of X (formerly Twitter) exploited xAI's Grok chatbot to mass-produce non-consensual sexualized images, prompting investigations and platform bans. The Minnesota law was passed in response to a wave of similar incidents nationally.

---

## Minnesota's First-in-the-Nation Response

Minnesota's law, signed in May 2026, is the first state-level ban on "nudify" apps in the United States. It targets developers and distributors of apps that enable users to create synthetic nude images without consent, imposing civil and criminal penalties.

The case is being watched closely as a test of whether state-level AI safety laws can survive First Amendment and Commerce Clause challenges from AI companies.
