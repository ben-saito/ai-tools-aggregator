# China Reportedly Weighs Lifting Nvidia Chip Ban for ByteDance and Alibaba

China is reportedly considering allowing ByteDance and Alibaba to purchase Nvidia B20 and B200 chips that remain banned under current U.S. export controls, according to sources familiar with the talks. The potential policy shift comes as Nvidia appears to be winning on multiple fronts — export controls are loosening while Jensen Huang's influence over the Trump administration grows.

---

## Export Controls Under Pressure

Nvidia's banned AI chips — specifically the B20 and B200 models — have been at the center of U.S.-China technology tensions since 2023. The chips, capable of training large AI models, were barred from export to China over concerns they could accelerate Chinese military AI development. But new reporting suggests China is mulling relaxing its own restrictions to allow companies like ByteDance and Alibaba to import these chips.

Sources familiar with China's talks told The Information that Beijing is considering the import allowance as part of broader trade negotiations. The move would represent a significant concession — and a win for Nvidia, which has seen its China revenue decline sharply since the export controls took effect.

---

## Jensen Huang's Growing Influence

Behind the scenes, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has been cultivating relationships with the Trump administration. Reports indicate Huang has met with senior officials multiple times in recent months, discussing both trade policy and AI safety. Some analysts suggest Huang's influence has helped create space for the reported policy discussions.

Nvidia has also been quietly lobbying for more flexible export rules, arguing that controls hurt American companies without meaningfully slowing Chinese AI development — since Chinese firms have developed workarounds using less powerful chips.

---

## What This Means for the AI Chip Race

If China allows imports of Nvidia's banned chips, it could meaningfully shift the competitive landscape. ByteDance, owner of TikTok, has been aggressively expanding its AI capabilities. Alibaba has similarly been investing heavily in its LLM infrastructure. Access to Nvidia's most powerful chips would accelerate both companies' training capabilities.

The timing is also notable: Nvidia is currently facing increasing competition from Chinese chipmakers like Huawei, and a policy shift could help Nvidia recover lost market share in one of the world's largest AI markets.
