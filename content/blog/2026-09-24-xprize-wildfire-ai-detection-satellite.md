# XPRIZE Wildfire Winners Detected Fires in 10 Minutes But Could Not Stop Them

The $11 million XPRIZE Wildfire competition concluded with teams successfully detecting wildfires within 10 minutes using AI and satellite imagery, but no team managed to autonomously suppress a fire within the required timeframe. The competition highlighted both the progress and the remaining challenges in applying AI to wildfire response.

---

## The Competition Structure

XPRIZE Wildfire was divided into two tracks. The space-based detection track challenged teams to accurately detect all fires across a large swath of Australia within 10 minutes of ignition. The autonomous response track required teams to autonomously detect and extinguish a high-risk fire in rural Alaska while ignoring decoy fires, also within 10 minutes.

The competition attracted over 300 teams globally, with 45 qualified technical submissions in the space-based detection track alone.

---

## AI at the Core of Detection

The winning approaches relied heavily on AI systems processing satellite data in near real-time. Teams built AI and software systems that analyzed imagery from existing satellite constellations, including data from weather monitoring satellites not originally designed for wildfire detection.

The UK-based SIRIUS Wildfire Alliance took first place and $500,000, offering a system that identifies and models potential wildfire spread using both optical and radar satellite data. Second place and $250,000 went to Team Snuffed of Arizona, recognized for transparent and uncertainty-aware wildfire detection especially user-friendly for firefighters.

Special recognition of $100,000 each went to MyRadar, DeepFire, and Mayday.AI, which adapted a volcanic ash detection algorithm to identify wildfire smoke through cloud cover.

---

## The Detection-to-Suppression Gap

While detection technology has matured significantly, autonomous fire suppression remains elusive. Early proposals included using sound to extinguish fires, lasers, and robotic dogs. But practical constraints around time and geography eliminated most of these approaches.

"We want to be able to have technologies that can detect a fire before it is detected by anyone else," said Andrea Santy, program director of XPRIZE Wildfire. "A 911 call is still the most typical way that we find out about wildfires, and on average, that is going to be at least 15 minutes."

The real-world test took place at the New South Wales Rural Fire Service in Australia in April 2026. No team fully met the autonomous response challenge requirements to win the $3.5 million grand prize from the Minderoo Foundation.

---

## New Satellite Constellations Coming Online

The competitive landscape for wildfire detection is changing with new dedicated satellite constellations. The German company OroraTech launched a wildfire-specific satellite constellation in March 2025. The Google-backed FireSat program launched its first microsatellites in July 2026, representing a new generation of purpose-built wildfire detection platforms.

---

## AI Policy Dimension

The competition comes at a time when AI-enabled autonomous weapons and dual-use systems are under scrutiny. The autonomous response track raised questions about the deployment of AI systems in emergency response scenarios, an area where regulatory frameworks are still catching up with the technology.
