# YouTube Introduces Gemini-Powered Custom Algorithm Builder

YouTube is rolling out a new feature that lets users describe the type of content they want to see in their own words, then uses Google's Gemini AI to construct a personalized feed around that description. The feature represents a fundamental shift in how users can shape their viewing experience on the platform.

---

## How the Custom Feed Builder Works

To create a custom feed, users type a natural language description of what they want the feed to contain into a prompt box on YouTube. The prompt can describe a particular interest, theme, or any other specification for the content they wish to discover.

Google's Gemini AI model processes the description and constructs a personalized feed matching those parameters. The resulting feed is then pinned to the top of the YouTube home page in its own dedicated tab, making it easy to access regularly.

The feature is rolling out initially to a subset of users in the United States, with plans to expand to additional markets in the coming months.

---

## Competing in the Personalized Discovery Space

YouTube's move follows similar experiments by other social platforms. Decentralized social network Bluesky popularized the concept of user-defined feeds as a differentiator from competitors like X. This year, Bluesky introduced an AI tool called Attie that assists users in building custom feeds through natural language.

YouTube believes its scale as the world's largest video platform gives it unique leverage to apply this approach. The company says custom feeds will help users better explore the vast catalog of videos now hosted on the service, addressing the challenge of discoverability that grows with platform size.

---

## Implications for Content Creators

The feature could significantly alter how content gets discovered on YouTube. Traditional algorithmic curation relies on watch history, engagement metrics, and subscriber relationships. Custom feeds add a new pathway where content matching a user's stated interests can surface regardless of prior interaction patterns.

For creators, this means understanding the descriptive language users employ to find content in their niche may become increasingly important for visibility.
