# Groq Raises $350M at $3.5B Valuation as It Pivots from AI Chips to Neocloud

Groq, the former AI chipmaker that once competed directly with Nvidia on inference hardware, has raised $350 million in a new funding round led by investment firm Disruptive, with planned participation from Nvidia itself. The round values the company at $3.5 billion — down significantly from the $6.9 billion valuation Groq commanded just eleven months ago, though the company insists this represents a new baseline rather than a down round.

The funding marks the next step in Groq's deliberate pivot away from chip manufacturing toward becoming a "neocloud" — a provider of GPU-powered AI inference infrastructure. In June, Groq raised $650 million to begin this transition, and the company now operates 13 data centers across North America, Europe, the Middle East, and Asia Pacific, serving more than 6 million developers, enterprises, and AI-native companies.

---

## From Chip Maker to Cloud Provider

Groq originally built custom LPUs (language processing units) designed to outperform Nvidia GPUs on inference workloads. After losing its competitive edge in the AI accelerator market, the company shifted strategy, acquiring GPU clusters from Nvidia and repositioning itself as an inference-first cloud service.

The new capital will fund expansion from Groq's current 54 megawatts of compute capacity to more than 200 megawatts by 2027 — a nearly 4x increase in installed infrastructure.

"We are building Groq into the world's leading AI inference cloud," said Alex Davis, Groq's chairman and CEO of Disruptive, in a statement. "Inference will without a doubt become the largest and most valuable segment of AI infrastructure."

---

## The Neocloud Bet

Groq's pivot places it squarely inside Nvidia's AI ecosystem rather than alongside it. While neoclouds like CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, and Render Networks have proliferated as demand for GPU compute outstrips supply, profitability remains an open question. These providers purchase Nvidia GPUs at scale, reselling access to enterprises and AI developers at margins that continue to compress as GPU availability increases.

Groq's relationship with Nvidia is now symbiotic rather than competitive — the company purchases Nvidia chips to power its cloud while positioning itself as an inference specialist optimized for latency-sensitive AI workloads.

---

## Valuation Context

The $3.5 billion valuation represents a roughly 50% haircut from Groq's September 2025 valuation of $6.9 billion. The company attributes the reset to changing market conditions and a strategic refocus rather than business deterioration. Groq reports serving 6 million developers across its cloud platform, though its financial details remain private.

The participation of Nvidia in this round signals continued alignment between the two companies — Nvidia benefits from Groq's GPU purchases while Groq leverages Nvidia's hardware for its inference cloud.

Groq plans to reach 200+ megawatts of capacity by 2027, scaling from its current 13 data center locations to meet growing enterprise demand for AI inference compute.
