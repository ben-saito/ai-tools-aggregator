# Can the AI Industry Persuade Data Center Opponents by Getting Rid of NDAs?

---

Amazon and Microsoft are abandoning confidentiality agreements in data center negotiations, but communities remain skeptical.

---

## The NDA Retreat

Major tech companies are reconsidering how they negotiate data center deals with local communities. Amazon recently announced it will no longer use nondisclosure agreements when negotiating with local governments over data center locations. Microsoft had previously made a similar move, signaling a shift toward greater transparency in these discussions.

The announcements came as both companies faced increasing pushback from communities concerned about the environmental impact, noise, and strain on local resources that large data center installations can create.

---

## The Business Case for Openness

The companies argue that data centers bring significant benefits to communities: well-paying technical jobs, increased tax revenue, and infrastructure improvements. However, community advocates point out that these benefits often come with substantial costs—increased electricity demand, water consumption for cooling systems, and potential impacts on property values.

The debate reflects a broader tension in how the AI industry is expanding its physical infrastructure. As training and inference workloads demand more computational resources, the need for new data center capacity continues to grow.

---

## What Happens Next

The tech industry's move away from NDAs represents a strategic pivot, but it remains to be seen whether transparency alone will be enough to address community concerns. The fundamental questions about resource consumption and environmental impact persist regardless of whether negotiations are conducted behind closed doors or in public view.

This shift comes at a time when data center opposition has intensified across multiple regions, with some municipalities imposing moratoriums on new installations while they develop more comprehensive regulatory frameworks.
