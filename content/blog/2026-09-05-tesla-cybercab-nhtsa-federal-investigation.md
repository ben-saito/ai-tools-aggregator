# NHTSA Opens Federal Investigation Into Tesla Cybercab Deployment Hours After Austin Launch

The United States' top automotive safety regulator opened a formal investigation into Tesla's Cybercab just hours after the first production vehicles hit public roads in Austin, Texas — marking one of the fastest federal regulatory responses to an autonomous vehicle deployment in recent history.

---

## NHTSA Investigation Launched Within Hours of Deployment

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) announced on Friday that it had opened a probe into Tesla's decision to launch the Cybercab — a purpose-built robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals — on public roads. The investigation was opened mere hours after Tesla deployed the first Cybercabs on Austin streets, according to regulatory filings.

Federal vehicle safety regulations currently require manual controls such as brake pedals and steering wheels. However, the Department of Transportation has recently proposed removing those requirements for vehicles designed to operate autonomously.

"NHTSA fully supports the safe development and deployment of automated vehicles. But as the federal regulator, we need to ensure that all of our laws are followed," said NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison in a statement. "Our approach of balancing innovation with safety oversight will allow the United States to maintain its global leadership in AV innovation."

---

## Self-Certification Under Scrutiny

NHTSA's filing indicates that Tesla self-certified the Cybercab as compliant with all applicable Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS). Automakers traditionally self-certify whether their vehicles meet FMVSS requirements — a process that places the burden of compliance verification on the manufacturer rather than requiring pre-market government approval.

The agency said it opened the investigation to "examine the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when certifying the Cybercab and related issues." NHTSA will consider whether Tesla's certification appropriately determined that certain federal motor vehicle standards are inapplicable to the Cybercab's design.

---

## Precedent From Zoox Investigation

NHTSA's approach mirrors its handling of a similar case involving Zoox, the Amazon-owned autonomous vehicle company. In 2022, Zoox self-certified its cube-shaped robotaxi — which also lacks traditional controls — and NHTSA subsequently issued a special order seeking additional information and formally launched an audit query.

While Zoox was primarily in the testing phase at the time, the inquiry slowed its path to commercialization. The Tesla investigation follows the same procedural framework, suggesting regulators are applying consistent scrutiny to the emerging class of purpose-built autonomous vehicles without conventional controls.

---

## Cybercab Design Raises Regulatory Questions

The Cybercab represents a departure from conventional vehicle architecture. With no steering wheel, no pedals, and no ability for a human to take manual control, the vehicle is designed to operate entirely through AI-driven decision making. This design choice has raised questions about how existing safety standards — written assuming a human driver — apply to vehicles that never anticipated human intervention.

Tesla CEO Elon Musk has positioned the Cybercab as a key pillar of Tesla's future revenue model, predicting that autonomous ride-hailing could eventually generate billions in annual revenue. The investigation adds regulatory uncertainty to that timeline.
