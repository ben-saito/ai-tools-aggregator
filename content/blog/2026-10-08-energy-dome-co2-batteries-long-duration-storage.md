# Energy Dome and the Rise of Carbon Dioxide Batteries

---

Italian startup **Energy Dome** is scaling its CO2-based long-duration energy storage system, positioning it as a cost-competitive alternative to lithium-ion batteries for grid-scale storage. The company's system uses thermodynamic cycles with carbon dioxide as the working fluid, storing energy as heat and pressure rather than electrochemical potential.

---

## How CO2 Batteries Work

Energy Dome's system charges by compressing CO2 gas using excess renewable electricity, which generates heat. The hot, compressed gas is then cooled and stored in liquid form. To discharge, the liquid CO2 is evaporated through the stored heat, driving a turbine to generate electricity.

The cycle is closed — CO2 circulates continuously — and the company claims round-trip efficiency comparable to lithium-ion, with potentially lower capital costs for long-duration applications (8-24 hours of storage).

---

## Grid-Scale Storage Context

The renewable energy transition requires storage that can shift multi-day or seasonal generation surplus. Lithium-ion batteries excel at 2-4 hour storage but face cost scaling challenges for longer durations. Compressed-air, flow batteries, and thermal storage systems like Energy Dome's target this gap.

Energy Dome has signed agreements with European utilities for commercial deployments, with construction underway on what the company describes as its first grid-scale facility.
