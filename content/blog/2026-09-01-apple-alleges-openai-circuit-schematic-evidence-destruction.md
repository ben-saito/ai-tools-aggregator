# Apple Alleges Former Employee Shared Circuit Schematics with OpenAI, Destroyed Evidence

Apple has filed what it calls "shocking evidence" in its ongoing lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging that a former Apple employee now working at OpenAI used a confidential Apple circuit schematic in his work at the AI company and enlisted a colleague to help destroy evidence when he learned he was under investigation.

---

## The Allegations: Circuit Schematics and Evidence Destruction

In its lawsuit against OpenAI, Apple claims former employee Chang Liu — who joined OpenAI after leaving Apple — used a confidential Apple circuit schematic at his new job. The company also claims that Liu referenced a tool sharing an internal Apple engineering application name while at OpenAI.

Apple further claims OpenAI was "well-aware" of Liu's access to Apple data. Most significantly, Apple alleges that when Liu learned Apple was investigating him, he enlisted OpenAI colleague Yu-Ting Peng to help destroy evidence in June.

"The MacBook represents the very limited information Defendants provided so far (and only after weeks of delay), and shows Apple is not conducting 'fishing expeditions' but that its trade secrets are being used and evidence is being destroyed," the filing reads.

---

## Text Messages Reveal Awareness of Access

Apple's new filings include text messages from Liu showing he was aware he still had access to Apple files. In messages punctuated with "crying laughing" emojis, Liu acknowledged he retained access to files he should no longer have been able to reach.

OpenAI has previously defended Liu, stating he only accessed Apple files after leaving the company to assist former colleagues who asked for his help. OpenAI argues Apple's own security practices and employee offboarding procedures are responsible for the residual access issue.

---

## Legal Context: A Broader Dispute

This new evidence emerges as part of a broader legal dispute between Apple and OpenAI that has been escalating throughout 2026. Apple originally filed the lawsuit alleging that OpenAI systematically recruited Apple employees to steal trade secrets. The case expanded in August when Apple sought a preliminary injunction to halt OpenAI's hardware product development and implicated 11 additional former employees.

The latest allegations add a new dimension: evidence destruction. If proven, this could significantly strengthen Apple's case by demonstrating knowing misconduct rather than incidental access.

---

## Developer Perspective

For developers, this case highlights several important considerations around employee mobility and trade secret protection:

- **Offboarding security**: OpenAI's defense points to a systemic issue with Apple's offboarding procedures — employees retaining access to systems after departure
- **Evidence preservation**: The allegation of evidence destruction carries serious legal implications that could affect damages calculations
- **Corporate IP boundaries**: The case tests how courts will treat proprietary information that employees access before switching employers

The case is being heard in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California.
