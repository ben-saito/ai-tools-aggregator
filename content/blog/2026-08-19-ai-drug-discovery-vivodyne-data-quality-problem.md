# AI Drug Discovery: Why "AI Will Cure Cancer" Claims Miss the Mark — Vivodyne’s New Approach

*TechCrunch, August 19, 2026*

A biotech startup called Vivodyne says the AI drug-discovery industry has a data problem — and that it has built a machine to fix it. On August 19, the company published its analysis of why current AI models fall short on cancer, and what it will actually take to make progress.

---

## Why "AI Can Cure Cancer" Is Not Credible Right Now

Vivodyne CEO and co-founder **Andrei Georgescu** asks: "Absent human testing, what are these AI models going to do? Will they cure cancer for us?"

He has a point. Even Anthropic CEO **Dario Amodei** wrote recently that claims AI will cure cancer have become more cliche than credible — "the thing that will kill you," he called it in one framing.

In reality, Alphafold was a major advance for understanding the building blocks of life, but it has yet to produce a new drug. A handful of AI-designed drugs have entered human trials, but the path from Phase I to widespread use is long and uncertain.

---

## HIVE — Growing 20 Types of Human Tissue Autonomously

Vivodyne’s solution is **HIVE** (Humane Integrated Virogenics Environment), a modular robotic lab that can grow 20 types of human tissue, then autonomously dose and monitor them. The goal is to generate the kind of causal biological data that current AI models are missing — data that today mostly comes from animal testing or studies of single cells and proteins, not living human tissue.

Current AI drug discovery relies on data from animal models and single-cell or protein studies. The problem: these don’t fully capture how drugs behave in living human biological systems.

Georgescu says the field needs "a sanity check" — existing models don’t have the data to capture the complexity of human biology. It’s a challenge that no amount of model scaling alone can solve.

---

## Summary

Vivodyne, spun out of the University of Pennsylvania in 2021 after Georgescu earned his PhD in bioengineering there, is taking a different approach. Rather than claiming AI will cure cancer with better models, the company is arguing that the bottleneck is data quality — and building the hardware to generate better data.

The takeaway: AI cancer cure claims are overblown in the short term. The real work is building the biological data infrastructure that future models will need.
