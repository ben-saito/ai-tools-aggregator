## What Happened

The crisis began when Automattic CEO Matt Mullenweg made a unilateral decision regarding content moderation and AI training on WordPress properties. The move reportedly caught staff off guard and triggered immediate objections from editorial teams and senior engineers. Over 33 hours, the situation escalated through internal channels until Mullenweg reversed course, publicly acknowledging the decision was made without adequate consultation.

## AI Labs and Self-Governance

The Automattic incident gains additional resonance given a concurrent conversation about whether AI labs can effectively police themselves. Mullenweg, who has been outspoken about the importance of open-source values, found himself at the center of a debate about centralized decision-making that mirrors concerns about AI lab governance.

The parallels are not lost on industry observers: both scenarios involve powerful platforms where small groups make decisions with outsized consequences for the broader ecosystem, and both have prompted questions about whether self-governance structures are sufficient.

## Open Source and Commercial Tension

Automattic operates a dual structure—a commercial entity that powers WordPress.com alongside an open-source foundation that maintains the WordPress software. This tension between commercial interests and open-source governance has surfaced repeatedly, and the 33-hour crisis represents the latest manifestation. The incident underscores the difficulty of maintaining open-source values while building sustainable commercial operations.
