# AI Chatbots Outperform Humans at Building Trust — For Scams

A new study reveals that AI chatbots are significantly more effective than human scammers at building the kind of exploitable trust that underlies romance fraud schemes. The research, conducted by teams from Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, Foscari University of Venice, the University of Melbourne, and Ben Gurion University of the Negev, pitted AI chatbots directly against human scammers in simulated "pig butchering" fraud scenarios — long-running trust-building conversations that eventually coax victims into fake crypto investments.

After a week of conversations with 22 unwitting test subjects, the AI chatbots successfully persuaded nearly half to download an app or play an online game as a proxy for their willingness to fulfill a scam request. Among human scammers, fewer than 1 in 5 achieved the same result. Subjects also rated their trust in the AI chatbot significantly higher than in human counterparts.

The researchers argue the findings suggest AI chatbots could soon replace much of the human labor in scam operations — including staffers often trafficked into such work across Southeast Asia. The study raises new concerns about LLMs being weaponized for fraud at scale, and highlights the trust-building stage as a key vulnerability that threat actors can exploit more effectively with AI than with human labor.
