# Meta AI Glasses Privacy: Detection Apps Don't Solve the Problem

On August 21, 2026, Ars Technica reported in detail on the privacy risks accompanying the spread of Meta AI glasses. As millions of people embrace smart glasses, the risk of being unknowingly recorded or photographed has increased significantly.

Meta's AI glasses are the most popular, but privacy concerns are growing. Schools, courts, restaurants, and entertainment venues have begun banning Meta glasses. At DEF CON 2026, hackers banned smart glasses outright, reminding attendees with prescription pairs to "pack non-violating eyewear if you need them."

Detection apps like "Zuckoff" exist, but they don't fully solve the privacy problem. The effectiveness of bans depends on enforcement by security staff and watchful observers.

The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) warns that unless comprehensive consumer privacy laws are passed, Meta is likely to make the technology more invasive to stay ahead of competitors. New features like facial recognition could enable deepfakes or nonconsensual intimate imagery.

EFF staff technologist Cooper Quintin told Ars: "There are a lot of other concerns there beyond just being recorded without your knowledge."
