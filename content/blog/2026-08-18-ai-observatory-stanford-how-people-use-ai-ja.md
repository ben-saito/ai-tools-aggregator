# AI Use Data Lacks Independent Verification, Stanford and MIT Researchers Say

Anthropic and OpenAI publish quarterly reports on how users interact with their AI products, but according to independent researchers, these reports offer an incomplete picture.

"There is no independent source to corroborate it," says Anka Reuel, a computer science PhD candidate at Stanford's Trustworthy AI Research (STAIR) Lab. Reuel is co-lead of the AI Observatory, a new public platform that aggregates and analyzes real AI conversations with popular models like Claude and Gemini, collected with user consent through seven existing datasets.

---

## Key Finding: 48% of Conversations Would Be Filtered by Anthropic's Methods

When AI Observatory researchers applied Anthropic's Economic Index methodology to their dataset, they found that nearly 48% of conversations would have been filtered out. Non-work-related conversations were more likely to involve health and relationships (44.2% versus 31.2% in Anthropic's own analysis).

Anthropic has released separate blog posts on AI companionship and sensitive use cases, but the researchers argue that a unified bird's-eye view analysis—rather than fragmented reports—helps the research community understand usage patterns more consistently.

---

## Model-Specific Usage Patterns

The AI Observatory found significant differences in how people use various AI platforms:

- **Grok and Gemini**: Higher usage for news and political information retrieval. Grok also showed higher concentration of misinformation
- **Anthropic (Claude)**: More frequently used for coding tasks
- **ChatGPT**: Dominant for homework assistance

Even different versions of the same model showed distinct patterns. ChatGPT conversations were shorter during the GPT-3.5 era, but became longer and more iterative with GPT-4o—a version known for driving emotional connections with users.

---

## AI Companionship Increasing, Sensitive Uses Decreasing

Analysis of WildChat data from 2023 to 2025 revealed a significant increase in small talk over time, suggesting growing use of AI for companionship. Meanwhile, AI assistants' self-disclosure (admitting they are AI chatbots) decreased.

Sensitive exchanges including harassment and hate speech became less frequent, potentially indicating that platforms are deploying more effective safeguards.

---

## Corporate Data vs. Independent Research

Anthropic's Economic AI Index covers 1 million Claude conversations; OpenAI's usage report analyzed 15 million ChatGPT conversations. However, the aggregation of data that companies control means their reports tend to focus on findings that paint them in the best light.

"When we want to ask, for example: is Anthropic's general-purpose AI system used mostly for good or mostly for bad, we don't have a way of answering that question because that information is proprietary," the researchers explain.

The AI Observatory's data will remain available to the research community, and the team plans to expand its datasets over time. The ideal solution, according to Reuel, would be for AI companies to share their data with independent researchers while protecting user privacy—but she notes that companies currently tend to focus on reports that present them favorably.
