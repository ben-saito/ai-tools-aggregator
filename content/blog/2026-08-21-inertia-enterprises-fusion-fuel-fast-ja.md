# Inertia Enterprises Reduces Fusion Fuel Filling Process from Week to Minutes

Fusion power startup Inertia Enterprises has reduced its fuel pellet filling process from a week or more to just minutes, TechCrunch exclusively reported.

The breakthrough addresses one of 10 hurdles the company must overcome to deliver its first commercial power plant. Inertia raised $450 million on the premise of commercializing technology from the National Ignition Facility (NIF), where fuel pellet manufacturing was painstakingly slow.

At NIF, making fuel pellets can take a week or more and cost a small fortune. "You're only making a handful of them a year," said Jeff Lawson, co-founder and CEO of Inertia. "I put on my commercial hat and was like, wait a minute, I know the word for this: Prototypes."

Inertia hired engineers from companies like Apple to solve mass manufacturing challenges. The fuel pellet consists of a spherical diamond shell with a thin layer of frozen deuterium and tritium just inside.

The company is using 3D printing to build robot prototypes being tested by select customers. Commercial versions are expected from the new dedicated facility by early 2027.
