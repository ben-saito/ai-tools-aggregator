# Child Monitoring Apps May Need a Reboot, MIT Technology Review Reports

AI-powered parental monitoring tools have grown into a $1.57 billion market in 2025, but researchers say these apps often do more harm than good—and a better approach exists.

---

## The Problem with Surveillance Apps

Most parental monitoring apps go beyond Screen Time limits. They scan children's texts, photos, emails, and chats, alerting parents whenever an algorithm flags something dangerous. Bark Technologies, the current leader, scanned 11 billion messages for 7.5 million children in the US in 2025. Its free school program covers one in 10 kids in the US.

But these tools have a dark side. Researchers scraped over 600,000 app reviews and talked to children who grew up under surveillance. Some reported false alarms that led to punishment, secrets revealed before they were ready, breakdowns of trust, and anxiety that persisted into adulthood.

One researcher—Pam Wisniewski, a principal research scientist at the International Computer Science Institute (UC Berkeley)—experienced the internet's dangers firsthand as a teenager. At 14, she shared her address with someone online who later turned out to be a murderer. That experience shaped her career studying online safety for families.

---

## AI Chatbots Are the Newest Threat

The next wave of growth is being marketed around AI. Companies position their tools as foils to chatbot companions and other AI risks. But critics say this approach—watching kids more closely—misses the point.

Child safety researchers point to two better approaches:

1. **Platform-level duty of care**: Force social media companies to build guardrails instead of leaving families to find their own. This approach is advancing in the UK, Australia, and several US states.

2. **Resilience-based safety**: Spend less effort watching kids and more effort helping them recognize risk, cope with it, and turn to trusted adults when something goes wrong. Wisniewski and others are developing tools that don't aggressively monitor children but train them in risk recognition.

---

## For AI Developers

For developers building AI safety tools, this debate highlights a key design tension: monitoring-based safety can undermine the trust it seeks to protect. The most effective AI safety tools for young users may be those that empower users rather than surveil them.

The broader market for parental control software is expected to nearly triple in value by 2034, making this both a safety and business challenge for AI companies targeting family audiences.
