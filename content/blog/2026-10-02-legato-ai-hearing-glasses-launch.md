# Legato Launches AI Hearing Glasses: Open-Ear Design, Directional Sound, Accessibility Focus

Hearing tech startup Legato has launched AI-powered hearing glasses, targeting the roughly 1.5 billion people worldwide with mild-to-moderate hearing loss who avoid traditional hearing aids due to cost, comfort, and social stigma.

---

## The Problem with Traditional Hearing Aids

Conventional hearing aids typically use directional microphones that focus on sounds coming from a specific direction. In practice, these systems struggle in noisy environments like restaurants or crowded spaces, where they amplify everything rather than isolating the speaker the wearer wants to hear.

Traditional hearing aids also carry a median price of several thousand dollars per unit, require professional fitting, and carry social associations that make many users reluctant to wear them consistently. Legato's founders identified these barriers as the primary reasons hearing care remains inaccessible to a large portion of those who could benefit.

---

## The Legato Approach: Open-Ear Design with AI Processing

Legato's glasses use an open-ear design — sound is not broadcast to people around the wearer. Instead, a dual-speaker system built into the frame directs audio toward the wearer's ear while actively canceling sound that would otherwise escape to the environment.

The AI processing layer is central to Legato's approach. Rather than simple directional microphone boosting, the glasses use machine learning to distinguish between foreground speech and background noise, continuously adapting the audio mix based on the acoustic environment. The system can isolate a conversation partner in a noisy room in real time.

The frames look like standard eyeglasses, addressing the stigma concern directly. Legato offers multiple frame styles to match different aesthetic preferences.

---

## Accessibility and Cost

Legato has not disclosed full pricing, but the company has stated its goal is to bring the cost well below that of traditional hearing aids, which often require audiologist visits and can cost $6,000 or more per pair. The glasses are designed to be self-fitted via a smartphone app, eliminating the need for professional appointments in many cases.

The startup is positioning the product as a consumer electronics category rather than a medical device, at least in its initial formulation. Regulatory pathways differ depending on how the product is classified, and Legato is navigating both the FDA's OTC hearing aid rules and state-level regulations.

---

## Competitive Landscape

Smart glasses with audio capabilities have been released by major technology companies — Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses, Amazon's Echo Frames — but these are primarily designed for music playback and voice assistant access. Legato is differentiated by its focus on hearing compensation specifically, applying AI signal processing to the problem of speech isolation in social settings.

Traditional hearing aid manufacturers like Phonak, Oticon, and Widex have also begun incorporating AI into their devices, but typically at price points and in form factors oriented toward the medical device market rather than consumer adoption.
