# New Mexico Judge: Meta Caused Public Nuisance — Must Fund $567M Abatement Fund

A New Mexico state judge on August 6, 2026 issued a Phase 2 judgment finding that Meta's platforms constitute a "public nuisance" under state law, ordering the company to fund a $567 million abatement program to address the youth mental health crisis its products helped create.

---

## Phase 2: The Abatement Judgment

Judge Bryan Biedscheid's Phase 2 ruling marked the second major finding against Meta in the same case. The Phase 1 jury trial in March 2026 had already found Meta liable for unfair practices violations and imposed $375 million in civil penalties. Phase 2 focused on the state's request for abatement — whether Meta must fund ongoing remediation efforts rather than simply pay fines.

"The Court finds that Meta is a cause of and has substantially contributed to a public nuisance in New Mexico and is required to abate that public nuisance," Biedscheid wrote in the judgment.

---

## The Fund Breakdown

The $567 million abatement fund is allocated across four categories:

- **$420 million** for treatment programs
- **$90 million** for screening and assessment
- **$33 million** for awareness and prevention
- **$24 million** for other costs

Biedscheid rejected a proposal from an expert witness that Meta fund entirely new community-based health centers, calling that remedy disproportionate. He also limited the fund's duration to five years rather than the 15 years proposed by the state.

"Treatment is a necessary component of addressing harms to mental health and safety," the judge wrote. "However, requiring Meta to fund entire new physical facilities goes beyond what remedies are necessary to abate the nuisance."

---

## Meta's Design Choices Cited

The judge found that Meta "implemented platform features that were designed to maximize engagement and time spent on its platforms, for all users and for teenage users in particular" — directly linking the company's product decisions to the documented rise in youth mental health crises.

Evidence presented included increased rates of suicide and eating disorders among New Mexico youth, alongside findings that "overwhelmed the systems in place for addressing such harms."

"Significant numbers of people in New Mexico experience harm from Meta's products due to risks of sexual exploitation, interference with education, and adverse mental health outcomes," Biedscheid wrote, including child sexual exploitation cases where "an online predator threatens or coerces a child for financial gain, typically involving a threat to expose nude or exploitative imagery."

---

## Meta's Response

Meta disputed the characterization, pointing to its existing safety measures and noting it has invested in tools for parents to monitor and restrict teen usage.

"We work hard to keep people safe on our platforms and have been transparent about the challenges of identifying and removing bad actors and harmful content," a company spokesperson said. "We will continue to defend ourselves against claims that mischaracterize our work."

The company reported $60.8 billion in revenue and $15.85 billion in net income in Q2 2026.

---

## Context: A Growing Legal Bill

The Phase 2 judgment adds to Meta's mounting liability from the New Mexico case. Combined with the March 2026 Phase 1 verdict, Meta now faces $942 million in total penalties from this single case. Separately, the company faces federal scrutiny and additional state investigations over similar allegations.

The case is being heard in Santa Fe County District Court. Phase 3 proceedings, which may address additional remedies or enforcement mechanisms, have not yet been scheduled.
