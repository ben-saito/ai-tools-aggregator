# Anthropic Releases Opus 5.5 with Lower Prices and Fable-Level Performance

Anthropic has released Opus 5.5, positioning the new model as the strongest the company has tested to date, while simultaneously cutting prices significantly. The release marks a deliberate shift for Anthropic following CEO Dario Amodei's recent embrace of calls to pace frontier AI development.

---

## Lower Prices, Stronger Performance

Opus 5.5 output tokens will be charged at $20 per million tokens, down from $25 for the previous Opus 5 model. The company claims the new release delivers performance comparable to Mythos, its most capable model, across biology and cybersecurity benchmarks.

The launch arrives just two months after Opus 5 debuted on July 24, a notably rapid update cycle compared to previous Anthropic release patterns. Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are scheduled for release "in the coming months," completing the 5.5 tier refresh.

---

## Communication Overhaul

Beyond raw performance, Opus 5.5 introduces significant changes to how the model communicates. According to Anthropic's announcement, the new model is less likely to use technical jargon and more likely to surface critical information at the beginning of its responses. This addresses a common complaint from enterprise users who found earlier Opus versions verbose or buried key details.

---

## Safety Restrictions Inherited from Mythos

Because Opus 5.5 performs comparably to Mythos in biology and cybersecurity capabilities, it inherits the same safeguards that limit Mythos's deployment. These restrictions limit what the model will assist with in high-stakes domains, reflecting Anthropic's increasingly conservative approach to capability deployment following the broader industry debate about AI pacing.

---

## Pacing Strategy in Practice

The release is notable because it comes shortly after CEO Dario Amodei publicly embraced calls to deliberately slow AI capability progress. Rather than a full deceleration, however, Anthropic appears to be threading a needle: releasing improved models that are meaningfully better at tasks while constraining the most dangerous capability domains.
