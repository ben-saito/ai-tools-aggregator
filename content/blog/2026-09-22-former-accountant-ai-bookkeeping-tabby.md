# Tabby: A Former Accountant Is Building AI to Make Bookkeeping Obsolete

Ahad Ali spent years overseeing a team of 20 people and handling more than 2,000 tax returns per year. But he grew frustrated with accounting software that was designed around the needs of the software, not the accountants using it. Now he's building Tabby — a real-time AI bookkeeping platform that automates the work of bookkeeping entirely.

---

## From Accountant to Founder: The Origin Story

In May 2025, Ali was running a successful accounting business. "We did bookkeeping, accounting, sales tax, taxes, everything," he told TechCrunch. But he noticed his work was increasingly dictated by the frictions of accounting software — tools like QuickBooks that he says were rarely designed with actual accountants in mind.

The frustration led to a realization: what if bookkeeping itself could be automated? Not just the data entry, but the entire conceptual framework of tracking a business's financial health in real time.

---

## Tabby's Technical Approach

Tabby is designed as a real-time bookkeeping interface. Using Plaid to import live bank account data, the platform builds an always-up-to-date dashboard showing a business's profit and loss. The AI handles the categorization of transactions, the generation of financial reports, and the ongoing maintenance of the books — all without the traditional setup friction of accounting software.

The key innovation is the shift from periodic (monthly or quarterly) reconciliation to continuous, real-time financial oversight. Small businesses get the kind of instant financial visibility that previously required either a dedicated accountant or complex spreadsheet workflows.

The team is also preparing to launch Tabby Talk — a natural language interface that lets business owners query their finances in plain English. Instead of navigating reports or exporting spreadsheets, users can ask questions like "What were our biggest expenses last month?" and get instant answers from their own financial data.

---

## Traction and Competition

Fourteen months in, Tabby has:
- **5,500 small businesses** using the platform
- Approximately **$100,000 in annual recurring revenue**
- A team of just **7 people** (Ali, a technical co-founder, 4 engineers, and a go-to-market specialist)

The company is currently raising a **$1 million pre-seed round**.

The accounting space is heating up with AI. Ali is targeting QuickBooks — still the biggest player in the market — but he'll face well-funded competitors like Sequoia-backed Rillet, plus the major AI labs' own finance tools. It's a crowded field, but Ali believes Tabby can win by focusing on the same small-to-medium-sized firms that his accounting business originally served.

---

## What This Means for Developers

Tabby represents a pattern emerging across vertical SaaS: AI-native products that don't just add an AI feature to existing workflows, but redesign the entire workflow around continuous AI oversight. The model — real-time data ingestion, automated categorization, natural language querying — could apply to legal document review, HR onboarding, and supply chain management.

For developers evaluating opportunities in the accounting space, Tabby's approach highlights a few key questions:
- Can AI eliminate the periodic reconciliation cycle entirely?
- Does real-time financial data unlock new business models for SMBs?
- How do vertical AI startups compete against incumbents with distribution advantages?
