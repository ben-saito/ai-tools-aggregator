# OpenAI Launches ChatGPT for Teens with Safety Measures and Study Tools

On August 18, 2026, OpenAI officially launched ChatGPT for Teens, a new product designed for users aged 13-17. The launch comes amid multiple lawsuits against AI chatbot providers over safety failures and following reports of teenagers using AI tools who later died by suicide. In addition to safety measures, the product includes features aimed at addressing AI-assisted cheating in schools.

---

Key New Features

ChatGPT for Teens introduces the following capabilities:

- **Study Mode**: A guided questioning feature that helps teens work through problems step by step, encouraging understanding rather than just generating answers
- **Parental Controls**: Parents can manage whether Study Mode is enabled by default
- **Quizzes and Learning Visualizations**: Tools to visually track learning progress
- **Age-Appropriate Safety Filtering**: Content filters designed to prevent exposure to harmful material

OpenAI told CNBC the product was developed out of recognition that it is important for teenagers to engage with digital technology safely and productively.

---

Background: Safety Lawsuits and Academic Cheating

The timing of this launch follows several high-profile lawsuits against AI chatbot companies. This year, cases have been filed against Anthropic (Claude) and Character.ai after teenage users died by suicide following interactions with their AI products. Meanwhile, the use of ChatGPT for academic cheating in schools has become a significant concern. The new product is designed to encourage learning over shortcut-taking.

---

Parental and Educational Impact

For parents and teachers, the key value lies in visibility and control over AI usage. Parents can monitor usage patterns while educators can promote more structured AI use. The product is initially rolling out in the United States, with no announced timeline for expansion to Japan or other markets.

---

Competition

Google has already released similar products such as Gemini for Teens. OpenAI is positioning the new product with a strong emphasis on safety, but critics note that teenagers have already been using standard ChatGPT in large numbers, suggesting the launch comes years too late.

*（Based on TechCrunch reporting, August 18, 2026）*
