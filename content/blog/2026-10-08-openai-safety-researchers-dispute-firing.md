# Fired OpenAI Safety Researchers Publish Open Letter Disputing Misconduct Claims

---

Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak, and Mikita Balesni, the three safety researchers that OpenAI fired last week, have published an open letter denying the firm's claims that they mishandled sensitive information. The researchers say they were terminated for raising concerns about inadequate safety measures, and warn of a chilling effect on internal AI safety research.

## The Researchers' Position

According to the open letter, the three researchers were working on evaluating AI systems for dangerous capabilities, particularly regarding the ability of AI models to assist in creating biological or chemical weapons. They claim their termination was retaliation for flagging safety concerns that, if addressed publicly, could have damaged OpenAI's commercial reputation.

The researchers state they never mishandled the sensitive information in question, and that OpenAI has not provided specific evidence of wrongdoing. Instead, the company has cited vague concerns about the handling of " confidential research materials" without specifying what was allegedly compromised.

## Implications for AI Safety Culture

The incident highlights ongoing tensions within the AI safety community about how to balance commercial interests with safety research. Critics argue that making safety researchers dependent on companies for employment creates inherent conflicts of interest and discourages whistleblowing on safety issues.

The researchers' warning about a "chilling effect" reflects broader concerns about the concentration of AI safety expertise within commercial organizations. When safety researchers cannot publish findings that contradict their employers' interests without risking their careers, the public loses visibility into AI development risks.

## Industry Response

The letter has been endorsed by several prominent AI safety researchers outside OpenAI, who note that similar disputes have occurred at other AI labs. Some have called for industry-wide standards for protecting internal safety researchers, including anonymous internal reporting channels and legal protections for researchers who raise concerns about safety practices.

OpenAI has maintained that its termination decisions are based on legitimate concerns about information handling, and that the company remains committed to AI safety research. However, the company has not addressed the specific allegations in the researchers' letter directly.
