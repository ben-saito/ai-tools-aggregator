# Toyota Orders Workers to Train Humanoid Robots, Insists Humans Won't Be Replaced

Toyota has announced a major expansion of its humanoid robot workforce, with plans to deploy 400,000 robots across its manufacturing operations by 2030, while simultaneously ordering human workers to train the machines to perform precise assembly tasks.

---

## The Investment Plan

Starting in 2028, Toyota Motor Group aims to invest $6.42 billion annually in upgrading factories with a new robotic workforce. The plan calls for 150,000 robots in Toyota's own automotive plants and another 250,000 at facilities operated by group companies that produce components and materials.

Toyota has already begun deploying its ELEY humanoid robots — machines that roll on wheels and feature precise hand movements — across assembly lines. Human workers are actively teaching these robots by wearing "jigs" modeled on the humanoids' fingers to guide them through tasks requiring fine motor skills.

---

## The Human Workforce Question

The announcement naturally raises concerns about the future of Toyota's 18,000 veteran workers across 60 factories worldwide, including 11 US manufacturing plants. However, executive vice president Hiroki Nakajima told Nikkei Asia that Toyota's vision is "a world where robots coexist with humans, rather than replacing them."

This framing positions Toyota's initiative as augmentation rather than replacement — though labor advocates note that similar promises have preceded major workforce reductions in other industries.

---

## The Industry Context

Toyota's push reflects a broader race among automakers to develop and deploy humanoid robots. The automotive industry has been a leading adopter of industrial robotics for decades, though traditional industrial robots are typically fixed robotic arms at workstations or mobile robots moving materials.

The new generation of humanoid robots differs in their ability to navigate varied environments and perform tasks originally designed for human bodies — raising both opportunity and disruption calculations for manufacturers.

China alone has deployed over 2 million industrial robots, and analysts expect global adoption to accelerate as humanoid designs mature and costs decline.
