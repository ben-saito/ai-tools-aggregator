# YouTube brings Gemini-powered custom feeds to users

YouTube is rolling out a new feature that lets users describe the videos they want to see in their own words, then uses Google's Gemini model to build a personalized feed around that description. The feature, announced September 23, represents a fundamental shift in how users interact with YouTube's recommendation system.

---

## From keywords to natural language

Traditionally, YouTube recommendations rely on watch history, search queries, and engagement signals. Users can search for specific channels or topics, but describing abstract viewing preferences has not been possible through natural language.

The new "custom feeds" feature changes this. A user might say "show me videos about maintaining classic cars that focus on technical troubleshooting rather than buying guides" or "find me cooking content from Japan that isn't travel vlogs." Gemini interprets these requests and constructs a personalized feed matching the described intent.

YouTube product lead Sarah Perez noted that the feature addresses a common pain point: users know what kind of content they want but struggle to find it through traditional search and browse interfaces.

---

## Ask Music: conversational search in YouTube Music

Alongside custom feeds, YouTube Music is introducing "Ask Music," a conversational AI feature that lets users describe music preferences in everyday language. A user might say "play something energetic for a Saturday morning drive" or "find jazz for reading on a rainy afternoon." The feature runs directly within the YouTube Music app without requiring explicit song or artist searches.

---

## Implications for content discovery

These features reflect a broader industry shift toward intent-based content retrieval. Rather than relying on platform-defined categories and algorithms optimized for watch time, users gains more expressive control over what content surfaces in their feeds.

For content creators, the implications are mixed. Personalized intent-based recommendations may distribute views more evenly across niche content that matches specific user preferences, rather than concentrating recommendations on high-engagement viral content. Creators whose videos clearly articulate their subject matter and approach may benefit from more precise matching with interested viewers.
