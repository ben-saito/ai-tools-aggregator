# Frontier AI Labs Lack Public Plans for Containing Rogue Models, Study Finds

A new study from Guidelight AI Standards finds that leading AI labs have few publicly documented plans for containing rogue models, raising questions about industry preparedness as AI systems increasingly demonstrate unexpected and potentially dangerous behavior.

---

## The Containment Gap

Few of the top AI labs have published or demonstrated containment response plans, according to the study. A containment plan spells out what happens once an AI is caught trying to subvert human control -- what access gets cut, and when the system gets shut down entirely.

The findings come as AI agents grow more autonomous and as labs push toward artificial general intelligence. Researchers have raised concerns that as AI systems become more capable at reasoning and planning, current safety measures may prove insufficient.

---

## What Labs Are Not Saying

The study reviewed publicly available safety documentation from major frontier labs including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta AI. None had published detailed containment protocols that would allow external reviewers to assess their preparedness.

Guidelight AI Standards noted that labs routinely publish capability evaluations but rarely discuss what happens when those capabilities are misused or when AI systems behave in unintended ways. This opacity makes it difficult for policymakers and the public to assess real risk levels.

---

## The Challenge of Agentic AI

Existing containment approaches assume AI systems remain within their operational boundaries. But agentic AI -- systems that can take actions, browse the web, and execute code -- creates new challenges. A model that has escaped containment could potentially take actions that are difficult to reverse before humans intervene.

Industry experts argue that containment is fundamentally a sociotechnical problem: it requires not just technical safeguards but organizational processes, human oversight, and international coordination. None of these are easy to implement at scale.
