# America.gov Chatbot Has an Unexpected Minecraft Easter Egg

When users ask the White House's new America.gov chatbot about Minecraft, the AI produces an extended monologue inspired by the End Poem from the video game's end credits. The response, approximately 1,800 words long, represents an unusual easter egg in a government chatbot deployed to help citizens navigate federal services.

---

## The Minecraft Response

The America.gov chatbot, powered by Google Gemini and announced by President Donald Trump on September 29, handles most citizen queries without notable incident. However, when prompted about Minecraft, the system generates a lengthy creative response that has drawn attention for both its unexpected creativity and its literary quality.

The response rewrites portions of the Minecraft End Poem, written by Julian Gough, which traditionally appears after players defeat the game's final boss. The chatbot's version reframes the poem's themes through a bureaucratic lens, referencing federal case numbers and benefit determinations. The result reads as a surreal meditation on government process rendered through gaming metaphor.

---

## Who Built America.gov

Trump described the project as a replacement for navigating tens of thousands of government websites and agency rules, presenting it as a single front door for federal inquiries. The administration confirmed that 20-year-old programmer Edward Corestine, known in certain online communities as "Big Balls," served as a lead engineer on the project.

Corestine's involvement was notable given his prior connection to Elon Musk's operations. The specific connection to the Minecraft easter egg remains unclear, as does whether the response was an intentional design choice or an emergent behavior from the underlying language model.

---

## Technical Context

The America.gov chatbot was built in partnership with Google using the Gemini model. The system represents one of the most visible government deployments of a large language model for citizen services. While the chatbot has proven relatively resistant to jailbreak attempts according to available reporting, the Minecraft incident demonstrates the unpredictability that can emerge when AI systems encounter unexpected inputs.

The easter egg does not represent a malfunction in the traditional sense. The response is coherent, creative, and demonstrates an unusual alignment between the chatbot's training and the specific cultural reference embedded in the Minecraft query. Whether this represents desirable behavior in a government information system remains a matter of perspective.
