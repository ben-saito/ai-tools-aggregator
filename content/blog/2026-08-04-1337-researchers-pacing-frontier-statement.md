# ~1,337 AI Researchers Sign Statement Asking US Government to Support International "Pacing" Mechanism for Frontier AI Development

*A new statement signed by senior representatives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, Safe Superintelligence, and others calls for governance tools to deliberately slow frontier AI development amid collective action challenges*

---

## Overview

A new statement has emerged with senior representation from all major Western AI labs — including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Thinking Machines, Meta, and Safe Superintelligence Inc — asking the US government to support an international effort to develop "the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development." The statement, signed by approximately 1,337 employees across the industry, represents one of the broadest calls yet for coordinated governance of advanced AI systems.

---

## The Core Request

The statement acknowledges that "the world's leading AI companies believe they could be close to automating AI research," and warns that "there is a real risk that capability development rapidly accelerates beyond our ability to understand or control the resulting systems." It requests that the US government support international coordination to build tools — both technical and governance-related — that would allow the industry to collectively slow down frontier AI development if needed.

The key challenge highlighted is **collective action**: each individual company and country faces intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow its own progress. Without coordination mechanisms, even companies that want to exercise caution are pressured to keep accelerating. "Today, the world lacks the technical and governance tools to deliberately pace frontier-wide progress," the statement reads.

---

## Why This Matters: RSI and the Control Problem

The statement comes as researchers increasingly discuss **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)** — the theoretical scenario where AI systems become capable of improving themselves, leading to rapid, potentially uncontrollable capability growth. The ~1337 signatories are asking for the ability to buy time — to slow down development at critical junctures so that society can "adapt to each rung up the intelligence ladder."

The statement acknowledges that there may be some levels of intelligence that are, "for now, too dangerous to reach for." This framing — from an industry that has historically been reticent to publicly discuss downside scenarios — marks a notable shift in the public discourse from leading AI labs.

---

## Connection to the Broader Pacing Debate

This statement follows on the heels of a recent TechCrunch Equity podcast episode featuring Sam Altman, during which the OpenAI CEO discussed his own calls for the industry to "pace the rate of AI development." The new statement can be seen as a more formal, industry-wide articulation of the concerns Altman raised — but goes further by directly asking government involvement in creating international governance mechanisms.

The statement is distinct from the podcast discussion in that it:
- Is signed by employees at **all major Western AI labs**, not just OpenAI
- Formally requests **US government support** for international coordination
- Specifically calls for building **technical and governance tools** for pacing
- References the **collective action problem** explicitly
