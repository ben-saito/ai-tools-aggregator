# Anthropic Operates Wet Lab for Biology Experiments as AI-Powered Drug Discovery Race Intensifies

Anthropic is running a wet laboratory where researchers conduct physical biology experiments, the company confirmed to Reuters on September 18, 2026. The lab, which Anthropic acquired when it purchased stealth biotech startup Coefficient Bio in April, represents a concrete step toward the company's ambition to use AI to accelerate life sciences research -- and potentially contribute to drug discovery.

---

## Why a Wet Lab Matters

The announcement places Anthropic directly in the physical biology research space, a move that separates it from most AI labs that limit themselves to computational work. Eric Kauderer-Abrams, Anthropic's head of life sciences, told Reuters: "We believe that to do biology, the final test is still, and will be for a while, in real lab work. We absolutely are doing that today."

The lab operates like a standard biotech facility, with Anthropic conducting some research internally while also collaborating with external partners. The primary focus is fundamental biology rather than drug discovery -- though the company has signaled it sees the two as interconnected.

---

## Competing with Pharma Partners Creates Tension

Anthropic has been careful not to position itself as a competitor to pharmaceutical companies, with whom it has major partnerships. The timing of the wet lab announcement is notable: just days earlier, Anthropic announced a partnership with Novo Nordisk on joint drug discovery. The company has faced criticism before for launching products that compete with its own customers' offerings.

The juxtaposition has not gone unnoticed in the industry. Investor and AI coding startup founder Chamath Palihapitiya posted on X with characteristic sarcasm: "The group behind such hits as: 'We're All Going To Die' and 'Regulate Me Now' are building a wet lab in SF. I do not recommend this."

---

## Safety Researchers' Warnings Loom Over the Announcement

The timing is politically delicate. Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon recently resigned with stark warnings about AI existential risk, stating that "the people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade." The company's own alignment lead put the odds of AI-caused human extinction at greater than 10% within the next decade.

CEO Dario Amodei published a widely-discussed essay calling for the industry to slow down and institute self-regulation, specifically naming bioterrorism as one of AI's biggest risks. The wet lab announcement comes against this backdrop of internal debate about the direction of increasingly powerful AI systems.

---

## Life Sciences Verification Program Launches Alongside

Anthropic also launched a Life Sciences Verification Program this week, giving vetted biological researchers access to its most powerful models. The company published recent research reports on accelerating protein design and uplifting bimolecular modeling -- technical work that supports drug discovery workflows.

The wet lab represents a significant expansion of Anthropic's physical footprint in the life sciences, combining AI model development with the ability to experimentally validate predictions generated by those models.
