# Why People Aren't Buying Mark Zuckerberg's AI Future

On August 16, 2026, TechCrunch published an analysis exploring why skepticism surrounds Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg's vision for a future powered by personal AI agents. The story appeared on the same day as TechCrunch reported that Stripe was acquiring OpenRouter for over $7 billion and that Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei had described the current AI backlash as a "fundamentally a crisis of trust."

---

## The Vision: "The Future Is for Everyone"

Zuckerberg published a 6,500-word essay this week declaring that "The Future is for Everyone" and painting an optimistic picture of a future powered by AI, where "everyone will have an exceptionally capable personal agent that understands you, your goals, and everything you care about." The essay outlined Meta's strategy for personal AI running on individual devices, managing schedules, drafting messages, and organizing files — always on, always available, with or without an internet connection.

---

## The Backlash: Meta's History of Overpromising

On the latest episode of TechCrunch's Equity podcast, reporters Kirsten Korosec, Rebecca Bellan, and Anthony Ha discussed why not everyone is buying Zuckerberg's vision. The core critique centers on Meta's track record: the same promises made about social media — that it would connect people and democratize communication — instead delivered "ragebaiting and advertisements, and not connection."

Rebecca Bellan observed that Zuckerberg is positioning himself "almost like an anti-Dario" — Anthropic's CEO Dario Amodei, who has publicly pushed back against accusations of AI doomerism. But the problem is credibility: "You look back to the social media days, [Zuckerberg] was saying that he wants to make sure that everyone has an outlet for talking to their friends and having a social network. And what do we have instead?"

---

## Meta's Half-Open AI Strategy

The podcast highlighted a contradiction in Meta's current AI approach. While the company released Muse Glimmer as a fully open-weight model that anyone can download and run locally, it simultaneously released Muse Spark — a more powerful model locked behind API access. The most capable models remain proprietary even as Meta champions "open" AI.

This creates a bifurcated strategy: Glimmer serves the open-source community and developer enthusiasm, while Spark maintains Meta's revenue stream from enterprise customers and large-scale users. Bellan described it as Meta "trying to win in a different way" — not through frontier model leadership, but through personal empowerment and device-level AI.

---

## The Consumer AI Credibility Gap

Anthony Ha noted that Meta rarely comes up in conversations about leading AI labs or popular consumer chatbots. While Meta AI tools are embedded in Instagram and Facebook, the company has not established itself as a destination for AI assistants the way OpenAI has with ChatGPT or Anthropic has with Claude.

"Meta isn't that yet, and so it feels, to a certain extent, this is an attempt to reposition them and try to claim space that they really haven't been that successful at before," Ha observed.

Zuckerberg's essay also built on promises from the previous year, when similar "The Future Is for Everyone" framing was tied to Meta's wearable AI glasses. The hardware strategy remains unclear — "it's not really clear what the final hardware form factor for this would be," Bellan noted.

---

## The Verdict

The Equity podcast's consensus was skeptical. Meta's AI vision is ambitious, but the gap between Zuckerberg's rhetoric and the company's historical execution — combined with a half-open model strategy and lack of consumer AI credibility — leaves reason for doubt. The question is not whether personal AI agents will exist, but whether Meta will be the company that delivers them.
