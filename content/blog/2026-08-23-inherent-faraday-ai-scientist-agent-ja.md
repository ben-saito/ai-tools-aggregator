# Inherent Faraday: DeepMind Alumni Build AI Scientist Agent that Outperforms Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5

DeepMind alumni are building an AI agent that can replicate published scientific research — and it's doing so at a fraction of the compute cost of larger frontier models.

---

## From Stealth to Frontier: Inherent's Faraday Agent

London-based AI lab Inherent emerged from stealth just weeks ago with a $50 million seed round. Founded by former Google DeepMind researchers, the company has been operating with relatively little public attention compared to better-funded rivals. But while those rivals have yet to show concrete results, Inherent is already sharing what it's built.

Its AI agent, Faraday, has outperformed Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 at a specific but meaningful task: independently reproducing the findings of published scientific papers without being told the answer in advance.

The achievement matters because paper replication is a standard training exercise for human scientists. Many PhD students begin their research careers by reproducing existing work to understand methodology. Inherent's cofounder and chief scientist Edward Hughes frames it this way: "Many PhD students actually start by doing this."

---

## Small Model, Big Results

What makes Faraday's performance notable is how it achieves these results. Both Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 are frontier-scale systems representing the state of the art in large language model development. Faraday runs on Qwen 3.6, a comparatively tiny model with just 27 billion parameters.

Parameter count is typically used as a proxy for model size and, by extension, training costs. A 27B model requires dramatically less compute to train and run than frontier models with hundreds of billions or trillions of parameters.

Inherent's bar for success went beyond simple accuracy. Beyond replicating results, the company wanted Faraday to demonstrate what it calls "research taste" — an instinct for what experiments are worth running and how to design them well.

---

## Reinforcement Learning as the Key Ingredient

Teaching something as intangible as "taste" is hard. Inherent's approach centers on reinforcement learning, a training method that rewards AI systems for good outcomes rather than spelling out rules for them to follow.

Rather than training agents primarily on the study of how science itself is conducted, Inherent leans on this reward-based approach. The company bets that reinforcement learning will generalize better to its longer-term goal: agents capable of contributing across many scientific fields.

"We're always guided by that north star of building an AI scientist agent and imbuing our agents with taste," Hughes said.

That focus has shaped what Inherent chooses not to build. Rather than developing its own coding tool, Faraday uses OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Codex instead — much the way human scientists lean on existing software rather than building everything themselves.

---

## The Ideal AI Teammate

Inherent is also trying to avoid building agents that simply tell users what they want to hear. Hughes describes the goal as modeled on his favorite kind of teammate: "the kind who comes back and says: I got curious about this, and I went off and I did these experiments. What do you think of these results?"

That collaborative instinct extends to how Inherent operates as a company. Its dozen employees all work in person out of an office in King's Cross — the London neighborhood that Google DeepMind's presence helped transform into one of the world's top AI hubs.

The company believes that London is the right place to build. Several other DeepMind spinoffs have chosen to stay in the area, creating a concentration of talent and expertise that Inherent is betting will continue to attract both employees and potential partners.

---

## Implications for AI in Science

While paper replication may sound like a party trick given Inherent's much loftier goal — building AI that discovers new scientific knowledge rather than just verifying old results — the capability represents a meaningful step.

The ability to independently verify scientific findings has value beyond the obvious: it could help address the replication crisis that has plagued academic research, where many published findings cannot be reproduced by independent researchers.

For now, Inherent is keeping its focus on the science. With Faraday demonstrating that smaller, more efficient models can achieve competitive performance on specific scientific tasks through better training approaches, the company is positioning itself at an interesting intersection of AI capability and practical utility.
