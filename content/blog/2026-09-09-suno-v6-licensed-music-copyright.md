# Suno Unveils Suno v6: Trained on Licensed Music as Copyright Suits Pile Up

AI music model maker Suno on Wednesday unveiled a new model family called Suno v6, developed using licensed data from music labels and distributors including Warner Music Group, BMG, and Believe — a stark departure from its earlier models trained on uncertain legal footing.

---

## From Copyright Battles to Licensed Training

Suno has faced multiple lawsuits from record labels that accused the startup of using copyrighted data to train its AI models. The company settled with Warner Music Group last year and struck a deal with BMG last month. With Suno v6, Suno explicitly stated that the new model is not trained using the data it used to train previous versions of its music-generating model.

The shift to licensed training data marks a significant strategic pivot as Suno seeks to insulate itself from ongoing legal exposure. The company is still facing lawsuits from Sony Music and Universal Music Group, as well as artist Jason Isbell, and users alleging the company ignored security concerns while chasing growth.

The announcement came a day after Suno admitted to training its models on YouTube videos — a disclosure that added fuel to the already raging copyright disputes.

---

## Suno v6 Model Family

Suno is releasing three versions of the new model:

- **Suno v6 base**: Available to paying users, described as reliable and steerable for controlled outputs
- **Suno v6 wild**: An experimental model for paying users, designed for ideation and unexpected results
- **Suno v6 mini**: A faster version available to all users

The company plans to retire its older models as the v6 family rolls out.

---

## New Features: Edit, Reference, Remix

The v6 lineup introduces new capabilities not present in earlier Suno models. Users can edit a specific part of a song using a prompt or a single word in the lyrics. The model also supports using text, images, or video as references to generate tracks. Users can separate an instrument from a sample and create a new beat with it.

Suno is also preparing to launch a remix feature — one that requires artists to opt in to a new program set up with music labels, allowing Suno to use their songs for AI-generated remixes.

Jack Brody, Suno's chief product officer, said the company believes the licensed approach creates new revenue opportunities for artists and stakeholders through derivative work.

---

## Still Fighting Lawsuits, Still Raising Capital

Despite the legal battles, Suno has raised over $819 million in funding to date, according to PitchBook data. The company has also introduced watermarking for AI-generated songs and new download limits based on account tier.
