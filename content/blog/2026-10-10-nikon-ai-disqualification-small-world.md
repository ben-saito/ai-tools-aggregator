# Nikon Small World Competition Disqualifies Entry for AI Use

---

Nikon has disqualified the winning entry in its Small World in Motion photomicrography competition after determining that AI image enhancement tools had been used in post-processing, violating contest rules that require images to be captured directly from microscopy without AI-generated modifications.

The winning video, which captured microscopic organisms in unprecedented detail, had initially impressed judges with its clarity and technical execution. The disqualification was announced after a routine review process identified characteristic artifacts consistent with AI upscaling and noise reduction tools commonly used in computational photography.

The decision has reignited debate about the boundary between computational enhancement and AI-generated content in scientific imaging. Contest organizers clarified that traditional computational methods — deconvolution, color correction, focus stacking — remain permitted, but any technique involving neural network-based image generation is explicitly prohibited.

Some contestants and microscopy researchers have argued the rule is difficult to interpret consistently, noting that modern scientific microscopes already incorporate significant onboard AI processing in capture hardware, and that the distinction between "AI in the instrument" and "AI in post-processing" is increasingly blurred.

The runner-up video has been elevated to first place, and organizers have announced they will publish updated guidelines clarifying permitted and prohibited techniques ahead of next year's competition.
