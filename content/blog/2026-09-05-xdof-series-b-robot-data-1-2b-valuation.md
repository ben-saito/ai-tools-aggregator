# Robot Data Startup XDOF in Talks for Series B at $1.2B Valuation

XDOF, a robot data startup that exited stealth just three months ago, is currently in talks to raise a Series B round at a valuation of $1.2 billion. The discussions were first reported by TechCrunch.

---

## Building the Data Infrastructure for Physical AI

The startup aims to build the data pipelines, collection tools, and annotation systems that frontier AI labs and robotics companies cannot easily build themselves -- essentially acting as an outsourced data operations partner for the physical AI industry.

XDOF was co-founded by UC Berkeley researchers Philipp Wu (CEO) and Fred Shentu (CTO) in 2024. As a PhD student, Wu was studying how robots learn from large datasets. One major impediment to his research was the lack of large-scale data to work with.

To solve this, Wu and Shentu developed GELLO -- a low-cost teleoperation system that allows a human operator to control a robotic arm remotely to generate training data. Their research formed the foundation for XDOF, which investors now describe as the Scale AI or Mercor for physical robotics.

Unlike purely software-based AI data operations, XDOF focuses on collecting physical-world training data for robotic systems. The startup combines remote robot teleoperation with human collectors who wear sensors to record everyday tasks like folding clothes and flattening boxes.

---

## Partnership with UC Berkeley AI Research

XDOF is partnering with UC Berkeley's AI Research lab to release what it believes is the largest collection of high-quality robot training data ever assembled, dubbed ABC.

The startup plans to hire and train teams of data collectors worldwide, including teleoperators who steer robots remotely and egocentric operators who wear body sensors to capture movement data.

Other startups in the robot training data space include Mecka AI, Scale AI, and Micro1. Unlike these competitors, XDOF's approach emphasizes physical task data collection at scale, combining both remote robotic control and human-worn sensors to capture the full range of real-world manipulation tasks.

---

## The Business Model

The company positions itself as infrastructure for the physical AI stack -- providing the training data that enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured real-world environments. By combining teleoperation with large-scale human data collection, XDOF creates datasets that can train robotic systems to handle everything from warehouse logistics to household chores.

The Series B discussions reflect growing investor interest in the physical AI sector, as AI labs increasingly recognize that high-quality training data is the bottleneck for developing capable robotic systems. The round terms have not been finalized, and the total capital being raised has not been disclosed.
