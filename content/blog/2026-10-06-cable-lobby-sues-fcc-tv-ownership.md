# Cable Lobby to Sue Trump FCC Over Repeal of National TV Ownership Cap

The cable industry has announced plans to sue the Trump FCC over its recent repeal of the national TV ownership cap, with the cable lobby arguing that the FCC lacks statutory authority to eliminate a congressionally mandated ownership restriction.

---

## FCC Repeal of the Ownership Cap

The FCC voted in August 2026 to eliminate the TV ownership rule that had long capped the percentage of American households a single broadcaster could reach. The repeal was part of the Trump administration's broader deregulatory agenda for the communications sector.

The commission finally published its final repeal order on October 1, 2026—an unusually prolonged delay that industry observers suggest was intended to provide additional runway for potential legal challenges before the order took effect.

## Cable Industry's Legal Argument

The cable lobby, representing major providers including Comcast and Charter, has filed suit arguing that Congress established the ownership cap as a statutory mandate and that the FCC cannot unilaterally repeal a legislative limitation through administrative action.

The industry's core position rests on separation of powers: FCC deregulatory authority extends to modifying or eliminating regulatory restrictions the agency itself created, the argument goes, but not statutory mandates enacted by Congress. The lawsuit seeks judicial review of whether the FCC exceeded its delegated authority.

## Charter-Cox Merger Context

The timing of the legal challenge is notable given recent consolidation in the cable industry. Charter Communications completed its acquisition of Cox Communications in August 2026, creating one of the largest cable operators in the United States. The merger had faced opposition from advocacy groups that argued the deal would create "unchecked gatekeeper power over Internet distribution" and enable the combined company to raise prices.

Consumer advocacy groups had challenged the merger before the FCC, arguing that the combined entity would have excessive market power—but their protests were rejected. The resolution of the FCC's authority to remove ownership caps could affect the regulatory framework under which future mergers are evaluated.
