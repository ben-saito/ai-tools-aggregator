# Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis: "We're Already Fighting Yesterday's Battle" on AI Policy

Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis issued a stark warning about the pace of artificial intelligence deployment, telling TechCrunch in a September 2026 interview that governments are fundamentally unprepared for the societal changes AI is accelerating. Speaking on the sidelines of a San Francisco waterfront event, Mitsotakis — who holds a master's degree from Stanford — described his Bay Area visit as a "homecoming" and a fact-finding mission that included meetings with Tesla and Sequoia Capital.

---

## Greece's Economic Comeback and the Tech Opportunity

Mitsotakis made a pointed economic case for why Greece deserves renewed attention from the technology industry. The country is paying down its debt at a record pace and borrowing at more favorable rates than it has in years. "I think it's another indication that the economy is doing well and that Greece is no longer treated as a special case," he said. The Greek 10-year bond yield sits around 4.3%, versus roughly 5% for U.S. Treasuries of similar maturity — a spread that reflects improved creditor confidence.

On the spending side, Mitsotakis said roughly 36 billion euros from the EU's post-COVID recovery fund has been directed into digital infrastructure, including an online portal for government services and broader digitization of public administration. Greece has also reformed its tax treatment of stock options, relaxed labor regulations, and introduced a returning diaspora tax regime that offers reduced rates for Greeks who move back for up to seven years.

---

## The Talent Drain and the Visa Opportunity

One of Mitsotakis's central goals is reversing the brain drain that accelerated during Greece's debt crisis, when hundreds of thousands of young Greeks left the country seeking opportunities abroad. He framed the current moment as a unique opening: as U.S. work visas become harder to secure, Greece is positioning itself as a viable alternative for tech talent with Greek heritage.

The government has expanded its visa programs for remote workers and returning professionals, though Mitsotakis acknowledged that processing times remain a bottleneck. "We still have work to do on that," he said.

---

## The Candid AI Warning: "Fighting Yesterday's Battle"

When the conversation shifted to AI's societal impact, Mitsotakis dropped the promotional tone. Greece is implementing a ban on social media for children under 15 starting in January 2027, a policy many countries are debating or adopting in some form. But Mitsotakis suggested the measure may already be outdated.

"Sometimes I feel that we're already fighting yesterday's battle," he said. "What does it mean for our kids to grow up with digital companions or boyfriends or girlfriends?"

The remark, reported by TechCrunch, crystallized a concern running through the statements of several world leaders at AI forums in 2026: that regulatory and policy frameworks are perpetually catching up to a technology that is reshaping labor, education, and social bonds faster than institutions can adapt.

---

## A Broader Pattern in Government AI Readiness

Mitsotakis is not alone in expressing anxiety about the speed of AI deployment relative to institutional readiness. At multiple conferences in 2026, heads of state and ministers have described similar gaps between the pace of AI capability advancement and the development of governance structures to manage it.

Greece's specific approach — combining economic modernization with targeted digital investments and cautious social safeguards — represents one government's attempt to thread that needle. Whether it proves adequate is a question Mitsotakis himself seems to doubt. "No government is ready for what AI is about to do," he told TechCrunch.
