# Meta AI Glasses Fuel Privacy Backlash as Detection Apps Emerge

The explosive growth of Meta AI glasses has triggered widespread privacy concerns, with schools, courts, restaurants, and entertainment venues beginning to ban the devices amid reports of secret recordings.

The hackers behind DEF CON 2026 reportedly banned smart glasses with no exceptions, reminding attendees with prescription pairs to "be sure to pack non-violating eyewear if you need them." Even tech-savvy venues are struggling to enforce the bans, as spotting the glasses can require trained security staff.

---

## Zuckoff: An App to Detect Meta AI Glasses

As demand for Meta AI glasses surges — with millions sold annually — a new app called "Zuckoff" has emerged to help users detect when they are being recorded by someone wearing the devices. The app represents one of several technical responses to the privacy threat posed by the always-on cameras.

The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) has warned that Meta appears prepared to make the technology even more invasive. Potential new features such as facial recognition could allow the devices to be used to create deepfakes or nonconsensual intimate imagery of people who may not realize cameras are rolling.

---

## The Recording Light Problem

Meta's sole safety feature — a tiny recording indicator light — is easily covered with a sticker, making it ineffective against determined bad actors. The devices have reportedly been used in immigration raids, to harass sex workers and retail workers, and for various unwanted recordings from pranks to bullying.

---

## Call for Legislative Action

EFF staff technologist Cooper Quintin told Ars Technica that comprehensive consumer privacy laws are needed to address the threat. The organization has released a guide for smart-glasses users on resisting facial recognition and developing responsible recording habits, while urging users to blur strangers' faces before posting videos online.
