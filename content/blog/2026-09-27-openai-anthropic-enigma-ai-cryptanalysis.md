# AI Models Astra and Opus Complete Alan Turing's Unfinished WWII Codebreaking Work

Frontier AI models from OpenAI and Anthropic have solved long-unsolved Enigma messages from World War II, accomplishing in days what would take human cryptanalysts years.

---

## A Historic Achievement in AI Cryptanalysis

Two frontier AI models -- OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 -- have successfully cracked Enigma messages that remained unsolved for over eighty years. The achievement, reported by cryptanalysts Carter Leffen and Jack Willis, marks a new milestone in applying large language models to complex mathematical and historical challenges.

The cryptanalysts used the AI models to search databases of unbroken Enigma messages, decode intercepted communications, and verify results against historical records. The models demonstrated the ability to function as virtual cryptanalysts, approaching the problems with methodological rigor.

---

## Turing's Legacy and AI's Continuation

Alan Turing and his team at Bletchley Park built the Bombe computer to crack Enigma-encrypted messages during WWII, shortening the war and saving countless lives. The newly cracked messages represent problems that remained beyond the reach of conventional computational approaches -- until now.

Frode Weierud, a retired electrical engineer and cryptology enthusiast who maintains the Crypto Cellar archive, noted that the AI models approached the problems with professional methodology. "GPT-6 Astra is behaving like a very professional cryptanalyst and archive researcher," Weierud wrote. "What it has achieved in two days would take a human years to accomplish."

Jack Willis, a cybersecurity executive, used Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 to solve a second message, demonstrating that the achievement was not limited to a single model or approach.

---

## Implications for Modern Cryptography

The successful application of LLMs to historical codebreaking raises questions about the future of cryptographic security. If AI models can solve Enigma messages that stumped human experts for decades, what does this imply for current encryption standards?

Security researchers note that Enigma's vulnerabilities were ultimately mathematical rather than computational -- modern encryption schemes like AES are designed with different principles that remain resistant to current AI capabilities. However, the pace of AI advancement suggests that cryptographic standards must continuously evolve.

---

## The Pace of Frontier AI Development

The simultaneous achievement by both OpenAI and Anthropic models reflects the intense competition in frontier AI development. Both companies have been explicit about "pacing" the frontier -- releasing models in rapid succession as capabilities advance. The Enigma achievement demonstrates that the latest generation of models has reached a new threshold of analytical capability.

For the cryptanalysis community, the results open new research avenues. The AI models' ability to search archival databases and cross-reference historical context suggests potential applications in historical research, document analysis, and systematic verification of historical records.
