# YouTube's new AI feature lets users build their own recommendation algorithm

YouTube is rolling out a new feature that lets users create custom video feeds by describing what they want to see in their own words. The prompt-based tool uses Google Gemini AI to generate a personalized feed, which is then pinned to the top of the user's home page in its own tab.

---

## How it works

Users type a description of the content they want into a prompt box — for example, a specific interest or theme — and Gemini builds a feed around that request. The resulting custom feed surfaces videos matching the description and remains accessible from a dedicated tab on the home page.

The feature is YouTube's answer to a broader trend of social platforms offering algorithmic customization. Decentralized social network Bluesky popularized the concept of user-built feeds, and this year introduced an AI tool called Attie that assists with feed building. YouTube is now applying the same logic to the world's largest video platform.

---

## Why it matters for creators

YouTube's recommendation system has historically been a black box — the algorithm decides what users see, with limited visibility into the logic. This new feature shifts some control to users, letting them define their own consumption patterns rather than relying solely on the platform's default ranking.

For creators, custom feeds could change discovery dynamics. If users are actively building feeds around specific interests, content targeting those interests may get more targeted exposure. The feature also gives YouTube a response to criticism that its algorithm prioritizes engagement over relevance.

---

## The AI personalization race

YouTube's move puts it in direct competition with other platforms that have introduced AI-driven personalization. The company argues that the vast catalog of videos on the platform makes manual exploration difficult, and AI-assisted discovery addresses that overwhelm.

The feature leverages Google DeepMind's Gemini model, reflecting Google's strategy to integrate its flagship AI model across consumer products. The move also signals Google's continued investment in AI-powered platform differentiation as competition for user attention intensifies across social and video platforms.
