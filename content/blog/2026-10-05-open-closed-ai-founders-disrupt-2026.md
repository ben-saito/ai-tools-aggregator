# Open or Closed? Founders Navigate AI Foundation Model Choices at Disrupt 2026

At TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, founders and AI practitioners are engaging in critical discussions about whether to build on open or closed AI foundation models — a decision that carries significant implications for product capabilities, cost structure, and competitive positioning.

---

## The Foundation Model Choice

Selecting an AI foundation model is one of the most consequential technical and business decisions for AI startups. The choice between open and closed models involves tradeoffs across multiple dimensions: upfront development costs versus ongoing API expenses, customization flexibility versus ease of integration, and data privacy versus managed service convenience.

Open models like Meta's Llama series and Reflection AI's Beam offer the advantage of local deployment and modification capabilities. Closed models from providers like OpenAI and Anthropic provide generally more advanced capabilities and managed infrastructure at the cost of vendor dependency.

---

## The Hybrid Approach

An emerging pattern among startups is using multiple foundation models rather than committing to a single provider. This approach allows products to leverage the strengths of different models for different tasks, potentially reducing costs while improving overall performance.

The ability to route requests between models based on task requirements, cost sensitivity, and latency constraints creates flexibility that single-provider strategies cannot match. However, managing multi-model architectures introduces additional complexity in integration and monitoring.

## Disrupt 2026 Programming

The Disrupt 2026 agenda includes dedicated sessions exploring these decisions, with founders sharing real-world experiences navigating the tradeoffs. ---

## Market Dynamics

The foundation model market continues to evolve rapidly, with new entrants and improved open models challenging the capabilities of established closed providers. This competition is forcing ongoing reassessment of the open-closed tradeoff across the industry.

For early-stage companies, the decision carries long-term implications for architecture and competitive strategy. The sessions at Disrupt provide a forum for practitioners to learn from peers who have navigated these choices at various scales and stages.
