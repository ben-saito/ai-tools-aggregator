# AI Systems Are Getting Better at Research Taste — And That Has Implications for Recursive Self-Improvement

---

AI systems are getting better at having good research taste. The better their taste, the higher the chance of recursive self-improvement. As AI systems become more capable of evaluating the quality of their own outputs and the outputs of other AI systems, they become better at identifying which research directions are most promising and which approaches are likely to fail.

Research taste — the ability to intuitively assess which problems are tractable, which methods are likely to generalize, and which questions are worth pursuing — has long been considered a distinctly human cognitive achievement. The fact that AI systems are developing something that resembles research taste has profound implications for how AI research itself will be conducted.

If AI systems can effectively evaluate AI research, they can identify promising research directions faster than human reviewers, spot flaws in proposed approaches more systematically, and accelerate the overall pace of AI progress. This creates a feedback loop where better AI begets faster AI improvement.

The connection to recursive self-improvement is direct: an AI system with good research taste can identify the highest-leverage interventions in its own architecture or training process, accelerating its own improvement in a pattern that could lead to rapid capability gains.
