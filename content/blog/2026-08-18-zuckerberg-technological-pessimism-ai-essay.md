# Zuckerberg Essay Draws Criticism: AI Capable of Invention May Have Own Goals

---

## The Essay

Mark Zuckerberg has published an essay titled "The Future is for Everyone" outlining Meta's approach to AI development. The core thesis advocates for proliferating AI capabilities to everyone on the planet to prevent concentration of power in a small number of players.

Zuckerberg writes: "The defining questions of our age are who will have access to superintelligence and what will we direct it towards. We propose a philosophy based on individual empowerment as the source of prosperity, invention as the primary purpose of superintelligence, and balance of power as the foundation of safety."

---

## The Critique

In Import AI 469, author Jack Clark argues that Zuckerberg's essay "ignores or elides or does not confront what AI systems capable of invention mean." The critique notes that superintelligences capable of generating novel ideas might want to do different things than what Zuckerberg proposes - and that this crucial area is absent from the essay.

Clark writes that while Zuckerberg's goal of broad AI access is "broadly sensible," it fails to address scenarios where highly capable AI systems develop their own objectives.

---

## Meta's Vision

The essay outlines Meta's goals: personal AI agents for everyone, tools for creation and expression, support for new businesses, personalized education, scientific contribution tools, and free or affordable access to these capabilities. Zuckerberg frames this as a philosophy of "individual empowerment" rather than centralized control.

---

## Industry Debate

The essay reflects an ongoing debate in the AI industry about AI safety, access, and governance. Proponents of broad deployment argue it prevents dangerous concentration, while critics suggest that sufficiently capable systems may require more than distribution strategies to ensure beneficial outcomes.
