# Poll of 56,000 Americans reveals surprising consensus on AI job disruption policies

The Center for Shared AI Prosperity (CSAIP) has published the results of the largest survey yet on American attitudes toward AI-related policy, polling 56,000 Americans on 79 distinct policy ideas. The findings reveal broad consensus on economic interventions — but deep opposition to wealth redistribution mechanisms.

---

## Strong support for worker protection, skepticism of wealth redistribution

The poll found Americans "broadly supportive of economic policies addressing AI disruption," with these top scorers:

- **Expand apprenticeships**: +66 net approval
- **Require severance for automated-away jobs**: +63 net approval
- **Sector-based job training programs**: +60 net approval

These three policies all scored firmly positive among Democrats, Republicans, and Independents.

At the bottom of the list:

- **U.S. sovereign wealth fund**: -51 net approval
- **Tax on distributed profits**: -33 net approval
- **Universal basic income (UBI)**: -33 net approval

The pattern is consistent: Americans support policies that help workers retrain and transition, but reject structural redistribution mechanisms — even when those mechanisms are designed to address AI-driven economic disruption.

---

## What this means for AI policy politics

The CSAIP researchers note that "a popular policy is not the same as an effective policy." The polling helps identify where the public is already receptive to an idea and where advocates will need to build coalitions from scratch.

The political implication is significant: AI companies facing regulatory pressure may find more durable coalition partners in the apprenticeship and severance camp than in UBI or sovereign wealth fund advocates — even though those advocates are often more vocal on the left.
