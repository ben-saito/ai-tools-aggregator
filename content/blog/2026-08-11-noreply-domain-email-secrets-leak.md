# Researcher Buys noreply.net Domain, Receives Thousands of Corporate Secrets

A security researcher purchased the noreply.net domain and immediately began receiving thousands of misdirected corporate emails containing passwords, security credentials, and other sensitive information. Ars Technica reports that the deluge included instructions for industrial sites in the Middle East and confidential business data from multiple organizations.

---

## 400,000 Emails in 18 Months

The researcher received approximately 400,000 emails over 18 months, with 28,365 containing attachments. Organizations sending to noreply addresses assumed these would go nowhere — digital trash cans. Instead, they were reaching an active recipient who began notifying companies of their misconfiguration.

---

## AI Company Among Leakers

Among the organizations inadvertently sharing sensitive data: an AI company using object recognition technology to monitor workers at industrial sites. Their technical emails to other parties were routed to the noreply.net domain owner, exposing details about their deployment operations.

---

## Systemic Problem

The incident highlights a systemic issue: organizations treat certain email domains as discardable, but those domains get re-registered and become active. The assumption that "nobody owns this address" is increasingly incorrect as domains become commoditized.
