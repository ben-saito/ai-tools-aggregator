# Meta Opens Muse to Hardware: Open-Source Gadgets Project Lets Developers Build Their Own AI Devices

Meta is expanding Muse beyond a standalone chatbot — the company announced an open-source hardware project called Muse Gadgets on October 2, 2026, giving developers the tools to build their own devices that connect to the AI agent.

---

## Open-Source Firmware and Linux SDK

Muse Gadgets provides open-source firmware — the low-level software that runs directly on hardware — along with a Linux software development kit (SDK). Meta is also sharing project ideas to help developers get started. The reference designs support low-cost hobbyist computers like the Raspberry Pi and off-the-shelf ESP32 boards, which developers can connect to displays, buttons, sensors, and other peripherals.

The company has set up a Discord channel to support the developer community building with the toolkit.

---

## Nat Friedman's Muse Home Link

Nat Friedman, head of product at Meta's Superintelligence Lab, posted on X that his team built a device called Muse Home Link — a USB-C gadget that connects Muse to physical hardware in the home. Meta manufactured 5,000 of these units and is giving them away for free to Muse subscribers while supplies last.

Friedman's post about the free device received significant attention, suggesting there is strong developer interest in bridging Muse's AI capabilities with physical computing.

---

## Part of a Broader Muse Ecosystem Push

The Muse Gadgets announcement fits into Meta's strategy to make Muse a platform rather than a single product. Earlier in the week, Meta introduced Muse for Small Business — a free tier (with usage limits) that connects Muse to tools including Shopify, Dropbox, and Slack. The company also launched a new business unit focused on enterprise Muse deployments.

The common thread across these announcements is integration: Meta wants Muse to work within existing software stacks and now physical hardware ecosystems, rather than competing solely as a consumer chatbot.
