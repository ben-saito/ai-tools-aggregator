# Thinking Machines in Talks for $1B Round at $40B+ Valuation Led by Accel

Thinking Machines, the AI research lab founded by former OpenAI chief technology officer Mira Murati in early 2025, is in discussions to raise approximately $1 billion in new funding at a valuation of at least $40 billion, according to a TechCrunch source and reporting by The Information. Existing investor Accel is in talks to lead the round, which would mark one of the largest fundraises in the AI sector this year.

---

## From OpenAI Pedigree to $40 Billion Valuation

Thinking Machines burst onto the AI scene with an extraordinary $2 billion seed round last year, one of the largest seed financings in history, valuing the company at $12 billion at inception. Andreessen Horowitz led that round, with participation from Nvidia, Google Ventures (GV), Lightspeed Venture Partners, and Conviction Partners. The investment was driven largely by the pedigree of Murati and the cohort of former OpenAI researchers who joined her at the new lab.

The company has since grown its revenue base to an annual run rate exceeding $100 million, according to a source with knowledge of its financials. At that revenue level, a $40 billion valuation implies an exceptionally high revenue multiple — a reflection of investor appetite for AI infrastructure companies rather than traditional software benchmarks.

---

## Inkling and the Tinker Platform

In July 2026, Thinking Machines released Inkling, an open-weight AI model designed to generate revenue through a usage-based compute model. Rather than charging flat licensing fees, Inkling charges organizations for the compute resources consumed when adapting the model on proprietary data using the company's Tinker platform. This architecture positions Thinking Machines as an infrastructure provider for enterprises that want to fine-tune frontier-class models on private datasets without surrendering data to third parties.

The open-weight release strategy contrasts with the approach of OpenAI and Anthropic, which have kept their most capable models proprietary. By releasing open weights, Thinking Machines enables researchers and companies to inspect, modify, and run the model locally, while the Tinker platform captures value through the inference compute layer.

---

## Departures and the Path to the Next Round

Despite the strong financial trajectory, Thinking Machines has experienced notable turnover. Several co-founders, including prominent researchers Lilian Weng and Luke Metz, have returned to OpenAI. These departures underscore the competitive labor market for top AI researchers, even at well-capitalized startups.

The new round under discussion would value the company below the approximately $50 billion valuation Thinking Machines reportedly sought to secure late last year, according to The Information. The gap reflects both the broader pullback in AI valuations since 2025 and the scrutiny applied to revenue multiples in the compute-intensive AI infrastructure sector.

---

## Investor Calculus in the Post-Foundry Era

The Accel-led talks arrive as venture investors recalibrate expectations for AI labs following the emergence of AI compute providers and model foundries as a distinct asset class. Companies like Crusoe, Nscale, and CoreWeave have raised billions to build out the physical infrastructure for AI training and inference, while AI labs face pressure to demonstrate revenue growth that justifies multi-billion dollar valuations.

Thinking Machines' hybrid approach — open-weight models paired with a proprietary fine-tuning platform — represents a middle path between fully open research and fully closed deployment. Whether that positioning can sustain a $40 billion valuation in a market that has grown more skeptical of pure compute narratives will depend on continued revenue acceleration and the competitive dynamics of the enterprise AI adaptation market.
