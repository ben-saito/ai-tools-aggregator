# AIUC Raises $40M to Bring Third-Party Safety Audits to Enterprise AI Agents

Two veterans of AI safety research have launched a startup that aims to apply the SOC 2 auditing model to the AI agent era. Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Company (AIUC) announced a $40 million Series A round led by Ribbit Capital, with participation from First Harmonic, to fund its third-party certification layer for enterprise AI deployments.

---

## The Problem: Smart AI Gets Harder to Control

The founding pair — an early Anthropic employee and the former COO of AI safety research organization METR — argue that as AI systems grow more capable, they simultaneously become harder to deploy safely and harder to control. "Banks, hospitals, governments and militaries no longer decline to deploy AI because a model isn't smart enough," said AIUC's co-founder. "They decline because they've made commitments to their own customers about what a system will and won't do, and nobody can currently guarantee that."

Unlike traditional software, where behavior can be formally specified and tested, AI agents can behave unpredictably when confronted with novel situations. The startup points to three recurring failure modes: jailbreaks, hallucinations, and data leaks.

---

## The Solution: AIUC-1 Standard and 5,000-Test Battery

AIUC has developed a certification standard called AIUC-1, modeled on the SOC 2 framework that became ubiquitous in cybersecurity. The company assembled a consortium of roughly 250 security and risk leaders — the enterprise buyers of AI agents — to define what "safe" means from a purchasing perspective.

The resulting test suite runs agents through approximately 5,000 distinct scenarios designed to probe boundary conditions. A passing agent receives a detailed report of roughly 100 pages identifying where it performs reliably and where it exhibits concerning behaviors.

The company counts Cursor, Lovable, Harvey, and ElevenLabs among its early customers.

---

## Connection to METR and the Broader Safety Conversation

The startup's approach mirrors thinking inside METR, where co-founder Dattani served as COO from 2024 to 2025 and remains a board member. METR has historically focused on performance evaluation — whether agents can reliably complete tasks — but has expanded into safety-focused red-teaming.

The timing is notable: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei recently called for frontier AI labs to pace development, citing a surge in bad-behavior incidents, and floated the idea of embedded third-party evaluators. AIUC positions itself as a commercial implementation of that vision, oriented toward enterprise procurement rather than lab oversight.
