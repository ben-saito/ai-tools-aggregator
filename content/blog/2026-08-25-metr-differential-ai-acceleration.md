# METR Study Reveals Uneven AI Acceleration Across Science and Technology

A new study from METR finds that AI is dramatically accelerating cybersecurity research while showing only minor gains in mathematics and no measurable impact on AI algorithmic progress itself.

---

## Differential Acceleration: Where AI Is and Is Not Helping

A new METR study analyzing where AI has accelerated scientific and technological progress reveals an uneven landscape. The research examined three domains — cybersecurity, mathematics, and AI research itself — and found dramatically different rates of acceleration.

**Cybersecurity: Major acceleration.** The rate of vulnerabilities reported across many projects has dramatically accelerated in 2026 compared with 2025, both for specific projects (cURL, OpenSSL, Firefox, and Microsoft) and for aggregate vulnerability databases (the US NVD and OSV). AI tools are enabling security researchers to find and report vulnerabilities at unprecedented rates.

**Mathematics: Minor acceleration, harder to measure.** AI is clearly contributing to more work being done — arXiv submissions have doubled in some areas in less than 12 months — but quantifying the value of those contributions remains difficult. Some math problems from prestigious lists have been solved, including the Jacobian conjecture from Smale's list and Problem 44 from Green's list. However, researchers say it is too early to determine whether this represents a sustained trend.

**AI algorithmic progress: No measurable acceleration.** When looking at algorithmic progress across seven significant problem areas — CIFAR-10, Hutter compression, Gurobi mixed-integer programming, MIPLIB, nanoGPT, Stockfish, and the matrix-multiplication exponent — there are few areas where LLM-attributable contributions have appeared. NanoGPT and CIFAR-10 show some AI-assisted progress, but the rate of increase is far less than in cybersecurity and mathematics.

---

## Why the Gap Exists

The differential acceleration pattern suggests that AI's impact depends heavily on domain structure. Cybersecurity vulnerabilities are well-defined, enumerable problems with clear success metrics — AI excels at enumerating and checking against known patterns. Mathematics, while having some enumerable components, involves more open-ended reasoning that is harder to accelerate. And AI research itself may be approaching limits that are simply harder for current AI to push further.

---

## What This Means

This research highlights how AI is causing advances in some parts of science and technology, but the effect is not unified across fields. There are pockets of dramatic acceleration in areas like cybersecurity, while other domains show more gradual progress. For organizations investing in AI-assisted research, this suggests that the technology does not accelerate all types of work equally — domain structure and problem definition matter significantly for whether AI tools provide an advantage.
