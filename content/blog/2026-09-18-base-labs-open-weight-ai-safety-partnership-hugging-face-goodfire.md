# Base Labs Launches Open-Weight AI Safety Partnership with Hugging Face and Goodfire

Base Labs, the research group spun up by Baseten earlier this year, has announced a new open-weight AI safety partnership with Hugging Face and Goodfire AI. The initiative aims to develop and publish methods for training and monitoring open models, providing safety evaluation infrastructure that can be built into how models are trained and deployed rather than added as an afterthought.

---

## The Abliteration Problem

The announcement comes as the AI safety community grapples with the growing problem of abliteration — a technique that allows bad actors to remove safety guardrails from open-weight models. The scale of the issue is significant: Hugging Face currently hosts over 6,000 abliterated models on its platform.

The Base Labs partnership represents an attempt to establish a transparent standard for open models that integrates safety directly into the training and deployment pipeline. Rather than bolting on safety measures after a model is built, the group is proposing a foundational approach where safety is embedded from the start.

"We believe openness to be an advantage for AI safety," the company said on X. "Openness provides more visibility into the behavior of models and, most importantly, greater means of turning safety research into action."

---

## Building Safety Infrastructure for Open Models

Baseten launched the new safety infrastructure standard alongside the Base Labs research arm on Wednesday. The collaboration brings together three organizations with distinct but complementary focuses: Base Labs' research capabilities, Hugging Face's platform reach, and Goodfire AI's safety expertise.

The partnership will publish methods for monitoring open models and develop evaluation frameworks that the broader AI community can use. By making these tools openly available, the group hopes to raise the baseline safety of open-weight models across the industry.

---

## Open Weights as a Double-Edged Sword

Open-weight models have become a cornerstone of the AI ecosystem, enabling developers worldwide to build on state-of-the-art foundation models without licensing fees. However, the same openness that accelerates innovation also makes it straightforward to strip away safety measures.

The Base Labs initiative addresses this tension directly, arguing that transparency and openness can actually enhance safety rather than compromise it. By making model behavior more visible and providing standardized monitoring tools, the partnership seeks to demonstrate that open models can be both capable and safe.

The $40 million OpenAI Foundation commitment to Public Data for Health represents a parallel effort to ensure AI safety extends beyond model architecture to the data that trains these systems.
