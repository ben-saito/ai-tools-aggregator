# ChatGPT-using lawyer punished for citing fake testimony from made-up witnesses

A New Mexico defense attorney has been formally reprimanded for submitting a legal brief to the state Court of Appeals that contained fabricated testimony and other errors generated by ChatGPT. The case represents one of the first judicial disciplinary actions arising from an attorney's use of AI for legal research.

---

## The case details

Attorney Stephen Aarons, who has practiced criminal defense in New Mexico for over 40 years, was hired by a defendant's family members to appeal a murder conviction. In August 2025, Aarons submitted a brief containing fake testimony and incorrect legal citations—all generated by ChatGPT without verification.

"I didn't know that AI could hallucinate facts," Aarons told the court. He admitted to the court that he did not verify the factual claims and legal authority in his AI-generated brief before signing it.

The New Mexico Court of Appeals rejected the brief and referred Aarons to the state's disciplinary board. The disciplinary action resulted in a formal reprimand, marking a significant moment in the intersection of AI tools and legal ethics.

---

## Implications for legal practice

The case highlights the growing risks of relying on AI systems for legal work without independent verification. Courts across the United States have begun grappling with how to handle AI-assisted legal filings, with several states introducing guidelines requiring attorneys to certify that they have reviewed any AI-generated content.

Legal experts note that while AI tools like ChatGPT can be useful for drafting and research assistance, they can also produce confident but incorrect information—a phenomenon known as "hallucination" in AI systems. This incident serves as a cautionary example for legal professionals.

---

## Key takeaways

- **AI hallucination in legal contexts**: ChatGPT and similar language models can generate plausible-sounding but entirely fabricated legal citations and testimony
- **Attorney responsibility**: Legal professionals remain ultimately responsible for the accuracy of all filings, regardless of how content was generated
- **Emerging regulation**: Courts are beginning to establish guidelines for AI use in legal practice, with certification requirements becoming more common
