# Elon Musk spent half his time on robots and AI in Tesla earnings calls

TechCrunch analysis reveals a significant shift in how Elon Musk allocates his attention during Tesla's quarterly earnings calls.

---

## The numbers don't match the narrative

Tesla shipped nearly 500,000 cars last quarter and 70% of its revenue still comes from car sales. Yet according to a new analysis by financial research firm Hudson Labs, Musk has spent the last several years using earnings calls to push AI and robotics narratives rather than discussing the core automotive business.

The analysis, which reviewed transcripts from S&P Market Intelligence dating back to 2019, used an AI tool called Co-Analyst to map what Musk and Tesla executives discussed across 28 quarterly earnings calls.

---

## From cars to Optimus

Musk has consistently framed Tesla as "really an AI and robotics company" during these calls, even as the company's finances remain dominated by vehicle sales. Projects like the Optimus humanoid robot and the promised "Full Self-Driving" robotaxi have taken up increasing airtime.

Hudson Labs' findings suggest this isn't just investor relations theater — it reflects where Musk's personal focus has shifted. The analysis tracked keyword frequency over time and found that references to AI, autonomy, and robotics have grown steadily, while traditional automotive terms have declined.

---

## What this means for investors

The earnings call rhetoric creates a disconnect with Tesla's actual revenue mix. Yet the stock is increasingly valued on robotics and AI potential rather than automotive fundamentals. For investors, the question is whether the AI narrative justifies valuations that incorporate aggressive future scenarios for both Optimus and robotaxi.
