# Open-Weight AI Models Near Frontier—But Safety Gap Widens

A new report from AI safety nonprofit SaferAI reveals that China's Z.ai has closed the capability gap with frontier labs faster than expected, while safety measures lag dangerously behind.

---

## GLM-5.2 Approaches GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7

Z.ai's open-weight model GLM-5.2 is only months behind OpenAI's GPT-5.5 and Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.7 on cyber and biology capabilities, according to SaferAI's evaluation. The model ran via Z.ai's public API completed all offensive cyber and dual-use biology tasks given to it—and refused none.

In contrast, Claude Opus 4.7 "refused so consistently that SaferAI could not complete CyberGym on it at all," the report notes.

---

## The Safety Gap Is Structural

The divide between open-weight and closed frontier models is not incidental. Frontier labs deploy safeguards including classifiers, refusal training, and API-level controls. Once weights are downloaded, those protections become unenforceable—users can modify safeguards, fine-tune the model, or strip system prompts entirely.

Far.ai found hundreds of universal jailbreaks that succeed against most harmful requests on frontier models like xAI's Grok 4.5 and Google DeepMind's Gemini. But open-weight models have no equivalent safeguards at all, by design.

---

## An Untenable Trajectory

The core concern: open-weight models are rapidly approaching the capability ceiling of the most advanced closed systems. If the trajectory holds, highly capable AI will be freely downloadable by anyone within the next year—without any safety architecture to constrain misuse.

SaferAI's Papadatos suggested pre-training data filtering as one approach: removing offensive cybersecurity information from training data before model training.
