# Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan argues US AI labs should distill frontier models freely

Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan has staked out a provocative position in the debate over AI model distillation: he believes US regulators should not restrict American AI labs from engaging in the practice of distilling knowledge from frontier models.

Distillation refers to the technique where a model maker extensively queries another model to learn how it works and reasons. While AI labs claim this practice can constitute intellectual property theft when used without permission, Tan argues the practice is fundamentally legitimate -- especially when done openly.

"I would do nothing," Tan told CNBC. "We could argue that there should be an American distillation regime."

Tan's argument is layered. First, he contends it is overreach for AI labs to dictate what customers do with outputs their models generate. Second, he notes that proprietary AI labs themselves vacuumed up vast amounts of human knowledge -- including copyrighted material -- without asking permission when training their own models.

Anthropic this week released its second report accusing Chinese AI labs of conducting "illicit distillation attacks," using hidden identities and stolen credentials to extract knowledge from frontier models without authorization. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has publicly called on US regulators to intervene.

Tan, who has described himself as having "cyber psychosis" from avid AI use, frames the issue as a question of market structure: he wants balance between open-weight AI labs and proprietary frontier labs.

"They are at the frontier and driving it forward. We want that to be fundable, and be a great business model ongoing," he said. "You want open weight models to give people freedom and access."

The doomer scenario, in Tan's view, is consolidation: "The nightmare scenario, the doomer scenario for AI is that there's just one company. It has the best access to capital. It has the best AI researchers. It runs away with it and suddenly there's one company that's monolithic. And that would be bad."

## Key Points

- **Distillation debate** -- Tan argues US labs should freely distill from frontier models; Anthropic argues Chinese labs conducting the same practice illegally
- **Double standard?** -- Tan notes proprietary labs ingested copyrighted material without permission during training
- **Market structure concern** -- Tan sees open-weight AI as a check against monopolistic concentration
- **Anthropic's position** -- released second report this week on alleged Chinese distillation attacks
