# Astra and Opus Pass Turing's Other Test: AI Cracks WWII Enigma Messages

---

## Frontier AI Models Successfully Decode Long-Unsolve D Enigma Communications

A pair of AI models from OpenAI and Anthropic have achieved what Alan Turing and his team of cryptanalysts accomplished during World War II: cracking Enigma-encrypted messages. Researchers have demonstrated that modern frontier AI systems can successfully decode previously unbroken Enigma messages, marking a significant milestone in the intersection of AI capabilities and historical cryptography.

While Turing and his contemporaries built the Bombe, an early mechanical computer that enabled the UK to translate Enigma messages during the war, the approach required extensive human expertise and specialized hardware. The new research shows that large language models can achieve similar results through entirely different means.

---

## How Modern AI Approaches Classical Cryptanalysis

The research team tasked OpenAI's Astra model with searching a database of Enigma-encoded messages for unbroken communications and decoding them. Unlike traditional cryptanalysis that relies on known weaknesses in the Enigma machine's wiring and rotor settings, the AI approach leverages pattern recognition and probabilistic reasoning learned from vast amounts of text data.

Anthropic's Opus model demonstrated similar capabilities in independent tests, with both models successfully decoding messages that had remained unbroken for decades. The results suggest that modern AI systems have developed novel approaches to codebreaking that differ fundamentally from classical cryptanalytic techniques.

---

## Significance for AI Capability Assessment

The ability to crack Enigma messages represents a practical test of AI reasoning capabilities that differs from standard benchmarks. While many AI evaluations focus on language understanding or mathematical reasoning, codebreaking requires a combination of pattern recognition, systematic reasoning, and the ability to work with constrained symbol systems.

The research adds to a growing body of evidence that frontier AI models are developing capabilities that emerge unpredictably from large-scale training, rather than being explicitly programmed for specific tasks.
