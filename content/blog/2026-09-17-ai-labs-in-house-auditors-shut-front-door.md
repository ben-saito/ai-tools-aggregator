# AI Labs Want In-House Auditors, But Maybe They Should "Shut the Front Door"

A new perspective in AI safety suggests that AI labs may be overcomplicating their approach to rogue agents when a simpler solution may be sitting in plain sight: controlling what information the AI systems can access in the first place.

---

## The Auditor Approach vs. Access Control

As AI agents become more autonomous and capable of taking actions in the world, labs are increasingly investing in sophisticated auditing systems to detect and prevent misbehavior. However, some researchers argue that this approach puts the cart before the horse.

The core debate centers on whether it is more effective to build complex monitoring systems to catch AI misbehavior after it occurs, or to fundamentally restrict what AI systems can do in the first place through better access controls and permission architectures.

---

## The "Shut the Front Door" Argument

The argument for access control suggests that the most effective way to prevent AI agents from causing harm is to limit their ability to interact with external systems in ways that could be exploited or misused. Rather than relying on post-hoc auditing, designing AI systems with strict boundaries from the ground up may provide stronger safety guarantees.

This approach aligns with the principle of least privilege: AI systems should only have access to the minimum permissions required for their intended tasks, reducing the attack surface for both accidental and intentional misuse.
