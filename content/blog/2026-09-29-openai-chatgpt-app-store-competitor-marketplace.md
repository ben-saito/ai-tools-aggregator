# OpenAI Turns ChatGPT Into an App Store Competitor, Launches Marketplace

OpenAI is building an alternative to the traditional app store model, turning ChatGPT into a platform where software can be discovered and used directly within conversations. The company announced the initiative at its Dev Day event, revealing that ChatGPT — now reporting 1.2 billion weekly users — will begin suggesting apps within the flow of conversation when it recognizes a task where a third-party application could help.

---

## ChatGPT as an App Surface

The core shift is conceptual: instead of users navigating to an app store, downloading an application, and switching context to use it, ChatGPT becomes the entry point. When a user describes a task — scheduling a meeting, editing an image, drafting a document — ChatGPT can surface a relevant app panel inline, without the user leaving the conversation.

Apps built for this model are interactive panels that run inside ChatGPT. Developers create AI-native versions of their applications using OpenAI's extension framework. The company demonstrated how its own meeting app could pull a user's calendar, display upcoming events, and let the user act on them without switching tabs or devices.

Users authenticate with "Sign in with ChatGPT," which carries their existing AI usage allowance into third-party apps. OpenAI says this removes a friction point that has limited adoption of standalone AI tools — users no longer need separate subscriptions or credits for each app they try.

---

## 16 Launch Partners, 30-Plus Marketplace Listings

OpenAI announced 16 launch partners at the event, including Cognition (developer of the Devin coding agent), Notion, and several design tools. The company also launched an enterprise app marketplace where partners can list services. At launch, the marketplace has more than 30 partners, spanning productivity, coding, design, and data analysis categories.

The apps can be shared as lightweight websites — ChatGPT-generated pages that colleagues can access and sign into with their own ChatGPT credentials. This creates a distribution channel that bypasses both mobile app stores and traditional web downloads entirely.

---

## OpenAI's Version of App Review

The company also announced improvements to its plugin submission and review process — OpenAI's analogue to Apple's App Review. Developers can now track the status of their submissions and receive notifications when their plugin is approved or rejected. This is a direct response to complaints that the previous process was opaque and slow.

The move mirrors broader industry trends toward embedding third-party services inside AI interfaces rather than requiring users to switch contexts. Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic have all experimented with similar in-chat app surfaces over the past year, though none have announced a self-contained marketplace with integrated billing and authentication.

---

## Agents Push Users Toward Delegation

Beyond the app discovery layer, OpenAI's embrace of AI agents is quietly reshaping expectations about what users need to do themselves. When an app can handle a task autonomously — booking a meeting, generating a document, running a code review — the friction of switching to a new app becomes harder to justify. OpenAI's vision appears to be that the best app is the one that acts without being asked.
