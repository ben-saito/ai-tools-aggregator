# AI Security Startup AIR Emerges from Stealth with $50M to Vet AI Agent Skills

---

## The Problem: AI Agents Multiply Attack Surfaces

As companies deploy more AI agents -- software that can autonomously execute tasks across systems -- a new security concern has emerged: agents rely on third-party skills and add-ons that expand their capabilities, but companies have no way to audit what those extensions actually do.

AI security startup AIR believes companies need to monitor that supply chain, and it is now emerging from stealth with $50 million raised across seed and Series A rounds from Sequoia, boldstart ventures, and other investors.

---

## What AIR Does

AIR's platform discovers agents running at a company, continuously vets any skills and add-ons they use, and blocks any unwanted behavior before it can cause damage. The company was founded by Yair Saban (CEO) and Niv Hoffman (CTO), veterans of Israel's Unit 8200 intelligence corps, where they worked on offensive cybersecurity and AI systems.

The core insight: AI agents are only as trustworthy as their least-reviewed extension. A single compromised or malicious skill can give an agent capabilities the company never authorized.

---

## Market Context

The AI agent market is exploding, with companies deploying agents for everything from customer service to software development. But the skills and plugins that extend agent capabilities -- often built by third parties -- represent a largely unmonitored attack surface.

AIR's approach treats skills as a supply chain problem: vet them continuously, block suspicious ones, and give companies visibility into what their agents are actually doing.
