# OpenAI Acquires Glass Imaging: $300M Purchase of Apple Portrait Mode Engineers

OpenAI has acquired Glass Imaging, a smartphone camera technology company founded by former Apple engineers who previously led the development of Apple's Portrait Mode feature, according to a Wall Street Journal report. The acquisition valued at over $300 million represents OpenAI's most concrete step yet toward building proprietary hardware.

---

## Former Apple Engineers Behind Computational Photography

Glass Imaging was founded in 2019 by Ziv Attar and Tom Bishop, both ex-Apple engineers who directed the team that created Apple's Portrait Mode. That background directly informs the company's approach to computational photography and image signal processing.

The company's Los Altos-based team brings specialized expertise in optics and camera software — areas where OpenAI currently lacks in-house capability. For a company known primarily for language models, acquiring veteran mobile imaging engineers represents a strategic move toward AI-integrated hardware.

---

## Portrait Mode to AI Photography

Glass Imaging's technology leverages computational photography techniques that separate subject from background, creating the distinctive depth effect characteristic of modern smartphone photography. The company has developed image signal processing technology that could be adapted for AI-powered visual understanding tasks.

The acquisition comes amid broader industry consolidation in computational photography. Google, Apple, and Samsung have all invested heavily in camera technology as a differentiator, and OpenAI's move suggests the company sees imaging as a key component of future AI systems.

---

## Strategic Rationale

The Glass Imaging acquisition fits OpenAI's reported hardware ambitions. The company has been rumored to be developing AI-native devices, and the expertise gained from acquiring a team with deep mobile camera experience could accelerate hardware development.

For OpenAI, camera technology represents an input modality — much like voice or text — that could enable new categories of AI-powered applications. The ability to process and understand visual information at the device level, rather than relying solely on cloud processing, could enable new use cases for AI assistants.
