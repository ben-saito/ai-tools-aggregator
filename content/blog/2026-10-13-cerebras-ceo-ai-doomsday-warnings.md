# Cerebras CEO pushes back on AI doomsday warnings

---

Cerebras Systems CEO and co-founder Andrew Feldman thinks the AI doomer narrative isn't doing the industry any favors. At TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, the AI chipmaker exec quipped that "some of the leaders in the AI world maybe shouldn't be allowed to talk to reporters anymore" after a series of high-profile warnings about existential AI risk from labs including Anthropic and OpenAI.

Feldman's pushback reflects a growing divide in Silicon Valley between what might be called "doom pessimists" — who believe advanced AI poses genuine catastrophic risk — and "build fast, fix later" optimists who think safety concerns are overblown and stalling progress.

"The narrative that we're building something that's going to destroy humanity — that makes it really hard to recruit engineers," Feldman said. "It makes it hard to raise money. It makes it hard to sell to customers who don't want to be associated with that framing."

Cerebras occupies an interesting position in the AI infrastructure landscape. Unlike Nvidia, which sells to anyone who will buy, Cerebras has focused on government and sovereign AI deployments. The company counts the US Department of Energy, several national laboratories, and undisclosed "Tier 1 sovereigns" among its customers.

Feldman also addressed the compute scarcity narrative, arguing that the industry will solve its FLOPs problem through a combination of model efficiency improvements and new hardware. "Every time people say we're hitting a wall, the wall moves," he said. "I expect that to keep happening.
