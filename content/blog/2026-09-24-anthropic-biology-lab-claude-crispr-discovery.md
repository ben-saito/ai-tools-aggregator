# Anthropic's AI Biology Lab Discovers Novel Enzyme System Resembling CRISPR

Anthropic has announced that its Bay Area wet biology lab, operational since spring 2026, has already made a significant discovery: a previously unknown enzyme system hidden in bacteriophage DNA that performs cutting, copying, and pasting operations similar to CRISPR. The findings were described by CEO Dario Amodei as a system with "properties reminiscent of CRISPR."

---

## Discovery Made "Mostly, Though Not Entirely, by Claude"

Anthropic confirmed last week that it operates a physical biology laboratory in the Bay Area where its AI models run actual experiments. This week the company announced that Claude discovered the new enzyme system in just 21 hours of focused work, using approximately 950 agents that processed 210 million tokens of data.

The system Anthropic found exists in bacteriophages, which are viruses that infect and replicate within bacteria. The enzyme performs DNA operations analogous to CRISPR's cutting and editing capabilities, though Anthropic acknowledges a Stanford team previously discovered a related system.

"Though Anthropic's lab has only been running for a few months, the team reported that it took Claude 21 hours of concerted effort," Amodei wrote. "This discovery was based on the work of others, and a team from Stanford previously discovered a system 'that is in some ways similar to the one Claude found.'"

The company is positioning the discovery as evidence that AI can meaningfully accelerate biology research, while emphasizing that human scientists remain in the loop. Anthropic declined to specify the exact location of its lab or partner institutions.

---

## Validation Pending From Broader Research Community

Anthropic's claims await verification from the scientific community. The discovery's significance whether it constitutes a genuinely novel finding or an incremental characterization of known mechanisms remains to be established through independent replication.

The announcement comes amid heightened scrutiny of AI companies' safety practices. Amodei and other frontier AI CEOs have publicly stated that model capabilities have reached levels requiring new safety-testing protocols, following statements by Anthropic employees earlier this month that AI poses existential risks.

---

## AI-Powered Scientific Discovery Accelerates

The Anthropic lab announcement adds to a growing list of AI-assisted scientific discoveries. The case illustrates a new pattern in research: AI systems conducting high-throughput analysis across vast datasets to identify candidates that human researchers then validate in physical experiments. Anthropic says this combination of AI agents and human oversight represents its approach to responsible AI deployment in sensitive domains.
