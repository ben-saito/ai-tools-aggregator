# Space Mirrors Could Jeopardize Night Sky, MIT Tech Review Reports

A company planning to deploy space mirrors—devices designed to reflect sunlight from orbit to Earth—could unintentionally brighten the night sky for millions of people, according to MIT Technology Review. The report raises concerns about the impact of such infrastructure on astronomy and natural darkness.

---

## Space Mirror Plans Advance

The company, which MIT Tech Review did not name in its report, is developing technology to deploy large reflective structures in orbit. These space mirrors would theoretically be capable of directing sunlight toward Earth, potentially providing renewable energy or illuminating specific regions at night.

The concept has attracted interest from multiple companies and governments as a potential solution for climate change and energy generation. By capturing sunlight in space, where it's more intense than at Earth's surface, space mirrors could theoretically generate significant clean energy.

---

## Impact on Night Sky Raises Concerns

However, astronomers and dark-sky advocates have raised alarms. Even small amounts of reflected sunlight in orbit could be visible from Earth as new "stars" in the night sky, interfering with astronomical observations and disrupting ecosystems that rely on natural darkness cycles.

MIT Tech Review's analysis suggests that even a single large space mirror could appear as one of the brightest objects in the night sky, rivaling the Moon in apparent brightness. This could have cascading effects on wildlife, human health, and scientific research.

---

## Regulatory Framework Lags Technology

Currently, there is no international regulatory framework governing space mirrors or similar large-scale orbital infrastructure. The Outer Space Treaty of 1967 addresses some aspects of space activity but was not drafted with technologies like space mirrors in mind.

Experts have called for international discussions about the governance of space-based solar infrastructure before deployment begins in earnest.
