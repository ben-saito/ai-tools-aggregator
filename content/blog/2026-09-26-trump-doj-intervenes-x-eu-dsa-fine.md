# Trump Administration Intervenes in X's EU DSA Fine Battle, Backs Musk

The Trump administration through the US Department of Justice filed an application to intervene in legal cases brought by Elon Musk and X seeking annulment of a 120 million euro European Commission fine under the Digital Services Act. The DOJ filing asserts the EU improperly expanded regulatory authority to reach American companies not operating within its jurisdiction.

---

## DOJ Intervention in EU Court

The DOJ application, to be heard at the EU's General Court in Luxembourg, argues the European Commission overstepped its authority in fining X under the DSA. Assistant Attorney General Brett Shumate stated the Commission "inappropriately attempted to expand its regulatory authority to reach American companies not present or operating within its jurisdiction."

"We will not tolerate the European Commission engaging in regulatory overreach to try and control American engines of innovation and economic growth," Shumate added.

---

## The DSA Fine and EU Investigation

The European Commission fined X 120 million euros in December for violating DSA transparency requirements, providing insufficient access to data, and deceptive design practices. The DSA, a comprehensive online safety regime, has become a major flashpoint between Brussels and US technology companies.

The DOJ filing specifically takes issue with the Commission calculating the fine based on the combined global annual revenue of all companies Musk controls rather than X's revenue within EU jurisdiction. The filing also asserts the DSA improperly extended scrutiny to Musk as a private individual.

---

## Broader US-EU Tech Policy Conflict

The intervention fits a broader pattern of the Trump administration supporting US companies in disputes with European regulators. Several senior administration figures including Vice President JD Vance have publicly criticized EU fines against X. Musk has donated substantially to Trump and favored Republican candidates ahead of upcoming elections.

Separately, the EU continues investigating X for other potential DSA breaches including whether Grok's integration into the platform for EU users has been properly assessed for associated risks. Apple is also appealing a separate 500 million euro DMA fine for app store anti-competitive behavior.

Trump has called EU penalties against US companies "overseas extortion" and a "form of taxation."
