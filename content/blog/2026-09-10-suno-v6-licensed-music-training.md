# Suno Replaces Its AI Music Generation Models With Suno v6, Trained on Licensed Music

Suno, the AI music generation startup, unveiled Suno v6 on September 9, 2026 — a new model family explicitly trained on licensed data from major music labels and distributors including Warner Music Group, BMG, and Believe. The release marks a direct response to the wave of copyright infringement lawsuits the company has faced over its earlier models.

---

## From Copyright Suits to Licensed Training

Suno gained popularity by letting users generate full songs from text prompts — complete with vocals, instrumentation, and arrangement. But that capability came at a legal cost. Record labels including Warner Music Group sued Suno, alleging the startup used copyrighted music without permission to train its models. Suno settled with Warner Music Group in 2025 and struck a deal with BMG last month.

Suno v6 is positioned as a clean break from those practices. According to Suno, the new model was developed exclusively using licensed data from music labels and distributors. The company has not disclosed the specific terms of its licensing agreements or the size of its training dataset.

---

## Market Context: AI Music Heats Up

The release comes as the AI music generation space grows increasingly competitive. OpenAI's Jukebox, Udio, and several other startups are all competing for creators' attention. Suno's shift to licensed training could give it a stronger legal footing as the industry grapples with ongoing IP disputes.

The company has not confirmed whether Suno v6 will replace its previous models entirely or coexist with them. Users of Suno's existing platform will likely see gradual transitions as the company deprecates older model versions.

---

## Industry Implications

Suno's move toward licensed training sets a potential precedent for other AI media generation companies facing similar legal challenges. By securing licensing agreements with major labels, Suno may be building a more defensible business model — though it remains to be seen whether licensed training will produce models of comparable quality to those trained on broader datasets.

The company has not disclosed whether Suno v6's output quality matches or exceeds that of its predecessors.
