# Google Releases a New Local-First Granola Competitor

---

Google has launched **AI Edge Foresight**, a new offline-capable meeting note-taker that directly challenges Granola's position in the AI-powered meeting notes market. The app transcribes conversations, generates summaries, and answers questions using entirely on-device AI — no cloud processing required.

The move signals Google's commitment to **local-first AI**, where model inference happens on the user's device rather than being sent to remote servers. For enterprise users, this addresses a key concern around data privacy and compliance, particularly in regulated industries where meeting content cannot leave the organization's infrastructure.

## Key Features

AI Edge Foresight brings:

- **On-device transcription** using Google's speech recognition models
- **Meeting summarization** generated locally without cloud connectivity
- **Cross-session memory** — the agent retains context from past meetings
- **Native integration** with Google Workspace (Docs, Drive, Calendar)

Unlike cloud-based alternatives that send audio to remote servers for processing, AI Edge Foresight runs entirely on-device, meaning meeting content never leaves the user's machine. This is a meaningful differentiator for organizations with strict data governance policies.

## Why Local-First AI Matters

Running inference locally requires efficient model architectures that can run on consumer hardware without sacrificing quality. Google's investment in AI Edge — a portfolio of on-device models — reflects a broader industry trend toward **edge AI**, where model compression techniques and specialized silicon (Google's Tensor chips, Apple's Neural Engine) make local inference increasingly viable.

For developers building in this space, the implications include:

- **Smaller, distilled models** become valuable IP, not just commodity weights
- **Privacy-preserving AI** opens enterprise markets that cloud-only products cannot access
- **Offline-first architecture** requires different system design patterns than cloud-native applications
