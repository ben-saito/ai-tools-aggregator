# Character.AI Files for Bankruptcy: A Tale of Hype, Lawsuits, and a Disney Fire Sale

---

Only five years into its journey, Character.AI—which lets users create AI characters and have conversations with them—has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, following a slew of lawsuits and struggles to scale its popular platform. In a court document, the company said it had assets and liabilities between $50 million and $100 million.

"Over the past year, Character.ai has taken meaningful steps to sustain and improve its business in the face of significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds. After a comprehensive review of the available alternatives, the board determined that this court-supervised sale process provides the Company with the best opportunity to maximize value for all stakeholders," Character.AI CEO Colin Adams said in a statement.

## The Rise and Fall of an AI Chatbot Pioneer

Character.AI was founded by former Google engineers **Noam Shazeer**, a co-author of the landmark Transformer paper, and **Daniel De Freitas** in late 2021. The startup initially focused on training foundation models and developing an AI chatbot platform that lets people create custom characters they could chat with.

By September 2022, the startup reached unicorn valuation with a $150 million fundraise on the back of high demand for its platform, with the company saying at the time that its users had exchanged more than **2 billion messages** with its bots in five months, and active users were averaging more than two hours on the platform daily.

By October 2024, the platform recorded **20 million monthly active users**. But the company had already begun to unravel: Google struck a $2.7 billion deal to license the startup's models in August 2024, and both Shazeer and De Freitas returned to the search giant.

## Legal Troubles and the Downward Spiral

The company's fortunes deteriorated rapidly after a lawsuit following the suicide of a 14-year-old Florida boy whose mother says he became obsessed with a chatbot on the platform. Texas launched an investigation shortly after, and the regulatory and legal headwinds proved insurmountable.

## The Disney Fire Sale

Character.AI has agreed to sell some of its assets to Disney for **$20 million**, along with a non-exclusive perpetual license for its technology. Disney will provide transition services via former Character.AI employees who joined the entertainment giant last month alongside its former CEO Karandeep Singh Anand.

Adams said the company would continue operating during the sale process, expected to close in late November or early December. However, there isn't yet a bid for the company's assets or a buyer—and if no actionable proposal emerges, the platform will be wound down entirely.

## What Went Wrong?

Several factors contributed to Character.AI's collapse: heavy legal costs from lawsuits involving minor users, regulatory scrutiny over AI safety and chatbot interactions with teenagers, and the fundamental challenge of monetizing a chatbot platform in an increasingly competitive landscape where Big Tech players like Google and Meta have entered the space with their own offerings.
