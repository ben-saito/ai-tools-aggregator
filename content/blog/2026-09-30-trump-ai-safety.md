# Trump Administration AI Safety Plan Relies on Voluntary Big Tech Commitments

The Trump administration secured a voluntary agreement from two dozen AI companies to implement safety controls recommended by the White House, including undergoing independent AI safety tests. The firms also agreed to regular meetings to discuss best practices and establish common AI safety standards and benchmarks.

Signatories include Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI — companies that had already committed to external audits under prior frameworks. The agreement introduces no new legal requirements and relies on self-policing mechanisms.

---

## What the Agreement Requires

Participating firms commit to:
- Implementing White House-recommended AI safety controls
- Submitting to voluntary safety tests
- Regular cross-company meetings on AI safety best practices
- Joint development of safety benchmarks

The commitment echoes the Biden-era AI Safety Institute framework but softens regulatory enforcement in favor of industry coordination.

---

## Industry Reception

Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI had previously adopted external audit regimes independently. The new agreement formalizes these practices across a broader set of companies, though critics argue voluntary commitments lack the enforcement power of binding regulation.

The Information reported that no new legal obligations were created, and several smaller signatories had not previously engaged with formal safety evaluation programs.
