# The Pentagon Wants $30 Million for an AI-Powered Lie Detector

The US government is seeking $30.3 million over five years to develop an improved AI-powered lie detection system called "Polygraph+," according to a Department of Defense budget request. The program aims to modernize federal polygraph and credibility assessment technologies to improve their accuracy.

---

## Background: Polygraph Testing Under Scrutiny at the Pentagon

The move comes at a time of high tension within the Department of Defense. Under Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, the Pentagon has been increasingly turning to polygraph tests in an attempt to find leaks and insider threats. Polygraph+ will be run by the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA), which conducts background checks for the federal government.

The budget document, which has not yet been made public in full, was first reported by Inside Defense. The project represents a significant investment in automated credibility assessment for national security purposes.

---

## The State of AI-Powered Credibility Assessment

While traditional polygraph tests measure physiological responses like heart rate, blood pressure, and skin conductivity, AI-powered approaches aim to analyze micro-expressions, voice patterns, linguistic cues, and other behavioral signals that may indicate deception.

According to the budget proposal, the program will "modernize federal polygraph and credibility assessment technologies" through machine learning and automated analysis. The goal is to improve detection rates while reducing false positives that have long plagued traditional polygraph examinations.

Other efforts at the Department of Defense offer potential clues about the direction of this research. In 2023, the agency's Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) ran an open submission process to find companies with products that could be used for credibility assessment, suggesting the Pentagon is looking to leverage commercial AI capabilities rather than building everything from scratch.

---

## Implications for AI in National Security

The investment reflects growing interest across the US government in applying AI to security screening and threat detection. Unlike traditional polygraphs, which require specialized operators and can produce inconsistent results, AI systems could theoretically scale across the department's extensive personnel screening operations.

However, the plan is likely to face scrutiny from civil liberties advocates who have long questioned the reliability and ethics of polygraph testing, whether AI-enhanced or traditional.
