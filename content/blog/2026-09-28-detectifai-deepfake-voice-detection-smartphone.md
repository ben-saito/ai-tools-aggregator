# Deepfake Voice Scam Inspires Founder to Build Real-Time Detection AI

After her grandfather received a distressing phone call from someone impersonating his brother using AI-generated voice cloning, Tarini Padmanabhuni founded DetectifAI — a startup building detection models that run directly on smartphones to flag synthetic voices in real time.

The scam followed a familiar pattern: a caller claimed to be a relative in distress, pressuring the grandfather for immediate financial help. The voice was convincing enough to fool the family member, and only after the fact did they realize it was an AI-generated deepfake. Padmanabhuni, a San Francisco-based founder, decided to fight back against this emerging threat by building detection technology specifically designed for consumer mobile devices.

---

## Why Mobile-First Detection Matters

Unlike enterprise-focused deepfake detection tools that require cloud processing, DetectifAI runs models directly on smartphones. This approach offers several advantages: lower latency for real-time detection, improved privacy since audio never leaves the device, and broader accessibility for everyday users who may encounter voice scams.

The technical challenge is significant. Voice synthesis models have become increasingly sophisticated, producing outputs that can mimic specific individuals with just a few seconds of audio samples. Detecting these fakes on resource-constrained mobile devices requires highly optimized model architectures that balance accuracy with computational efficiency.

## Startup Battlefield Competition

DetectifAI is competing in the Startup Battlefield at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, where hundreds of early-stage startups pitch their ideas to investors and industry leaders. The company represents a growing segment of the AI security market focused specifically on synthetic media detection as the technology becomes more accessible to bad actors.

The emergence of consumer-focused voice authentication and detection tools reflects a broader arms race between generative AI capabilities and defensive technologies. As voice cloning becomes cheaper and more accessible, the potential for malicious use in fraud, impersonation, and disinformation campaigns grows correspondingly.

---

## Market Context

Voice deepfake fraud has increased dramatically as generative AI tools have become widely available. Scammers can now clone a voice with minimal audio samples and integrate the technology into cheap phone-based fraud operations. This has created demand for detection solutions across consumer finance, elder care services, and enterprise security.

DetectifAI's approach — bringing detection directly to end users rather than relying on backend infrastructure — addresses a gap in current market offerings. By making detection functionality native to smartphones, the company aims to provide immediate, on-device protection without requiring users to understand technical details of how the detection works.
