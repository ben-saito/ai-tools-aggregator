# Huawei Plans Q1 2027 Launch of Ascend 960DT AI Chip as It Takes On Nvidia

Huawei is accelerating the launch of its next-generation Ascend 960DT AI chip, moving the release forward from Q3 2027 to Q1 2027 as the company intensifies its competition with Nvidia in the AI semiconductor market.

---

## Acceleration Against U.S. Restrictions

The Chinese tech giant confirmed the accelerated timeline, saying the Ascend 960DT is now expected to be ready in Q1 2027, with the spokesperson noting that "Ascend 960 chips are launching ahead of schedule, doubling performance and advancing year by year." The announcement comes just one week before U.S. President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet on September 24 in Washington, DC.

Huawei has continued advancing its chip ambitions despite strict U.S. restrictions on China's access to advanced semiconductor technology. Analyst Rui Ma noted that those restrictions are unlikely to stop China from pursuing self-sufficiency, saying "I think it's futile to stop China's development in semiconductors because the stakes for self-sufficiency are just too high at this point."

---

## Peerium Computing Architecture

Huawei is pursuing an ambitious approach called Peerium Computing Architecture, aimed at connecting hundreds of thousands -- and eventually millions -- of AI chips into a single giant computing system. The Atlas 950 SuperPoD and SuperCluster are the first systems built on this architecture, with the company stating that an Atlas 950 SuperCluster can connect up to 256,000 accelerator cards.

---

## Questions on Scaling

The accelerated chip timeline raises questions about Huawei's broader AI systems. Analyst Rui Ma noted on social media that Huawei had previously claimed its Atlas 960 SuperPoD would scale to significant compute capacity, but the practical implementation of connecting hundreds of thousands of chips into a unified architecture remains a significant technical challenge.

---

## Geopolitical Context

The timing of the announcement is notable given the upcoming Trump-Xi meeting. Trump has pushed back against calls from AI industry leaders to slow AI development over safety concerns, arguing the U.S. needs to maintain its lead over China. Meanwhile, Chinese AI development continues to advance under continued U.S. export controls targeting advanced chips and semiconductor manufacturing equipment.
