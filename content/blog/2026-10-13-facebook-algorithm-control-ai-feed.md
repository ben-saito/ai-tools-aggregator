# Facebook Gives Users More Control Over the Algorithm That Shapes Their Feed

---

Facebook is the latest social media platform to give users greater control over the algorithm that determines what appears in their feeds. Meta announced a set of new controls that will allow Facebook users to explicitly tell the platform what types of content they want to see more or less of, directly influencing how the recommendation algorithm ranks posts in their feed.

The new controls appear as a set of preference sliders within Facebook's settings that allow users to boost or reduce the visibility of content from specific categories: friends and family posts, Groups content, political content, and AI-recommended posts from accounts the user does not follow. The changes represent the most direct mechanism Facebook has offered for users to shape their algorithmic feed.

---

## Why Algorithmic Transparency Is Getting Real

For years, social media platforms offered only the thinnest layer of control over algorithmic content curation — typically limited to muting specific accounts or marking individual posts as "not interested." These controls had minimal impact on overall feed composition because the recommendation systems were designed to maximize engagement based on signals the user could not see or influence.

The new wave of controls goes a step further by giving users visibility into the dimensions the algorithm is actually optimizing for and allowing direct adjustment. This shift reflects a combination of regulatory pressure — the EU's Digital Services Act requires platforms to offer algorithmic choice mechanisms — and competitive pressure from platforms like Bluesky and Threads that have marketed themselves on algorithm transparency.

---

## Implications for Content Creators and Publishers

The new controls have significant implications for pages and creators who depend on algorithmic distribution to reach audiences on Facebook. When users can turn down AI-recommended content from accounts they do not follow, the reach of publisher content may decline even among existing followers. Conversely, content that generates active engagement — comments, shares, reactions — may become more important as a ranking signal.

Meta has indicated that it will provide publishers with aggregated data about how their content is performing under different algorithmic preference settings, giving them better visibility into how audience behavior is affecting distribution.
