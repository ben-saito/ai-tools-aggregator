# Study: One-Third of Web Pages Since ChatGPT Show Signs of AI Authorship

A new Pew Research study released August 20, 2026 found that over one-third of web pages published after ChatGPT's November 2022 release show signs of being written by or substantially edited by AI. The report arrives shortly after Cloudflare reported that bot web traffic has overtaken human web traffic.

---

## Pew Methodology and Findings

To compile the report, Pew used the Common Crawl web archive to collect nearly half a million English-language web pages from the past five years. Researchers used Open Pangram's technology to detect AI-authored content.

In a random sample of 10,000 web pages collected in July 2026, around 10% showed "significant signs of AI authorship." However, a random sample of pages most likely to have been created after ChatGPT's launch showed a much higher rate — over one-third exhibiting AI authorship signals.

---

## Implications: Bots Reading Bots

The overlap between AI-authored content and bot traffic creates a self-reinforcing loop. Much of the web is now bots reading web pages written by other bots. This has implications for training data quality, search relevance, and the overall information ecosystem.

---

## Context for AI Developers

For developers working with AI systems, this trend highlights:
- **Training data contamination**: Web-scraped training data increasingly contains AI-generated content
- **Detection tools**: Tools like Open Pangram are becoming necessary for distinguishing content
- **Search and retrieval**: Traditional search relevance metrics may degrade as AI-generated content proliferates
