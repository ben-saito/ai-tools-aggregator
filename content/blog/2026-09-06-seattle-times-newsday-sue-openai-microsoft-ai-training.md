# Seattle Times and Newsday Sue OpenAI and Microsoft Over AI Training Data

Two major American newspaper publishers, the Seattle Times and Newsday, have filed lawsuits against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging that their journalism was used without permission to train AI systems. The lawsuits, reported on September 5, 2026, represent the latest in a wave of legal challenges from media companies concerned about how AI laboratories source their training data.

---

## Details of the Lawsuits

The Seattle Times, one of the Pacific Northwest's largest newspapers, and Newsday, a major publication serving Long Island, New York, both allege that OpenAI and Microsoft used their copyrighted articles, headlines, and editorial content to train large language models without licensing agreements or compensation.

The lawsuits focus on the alleged unauthorized use of journalistic content to develop commercial AI products. Both publications argue that this use undermines their ability to monetize their reporting and damages their relationship with readers who may turn to AI chatbots instead of visiting their websites.

---

## Context: A Growing Legal Battle

The Seattle Times and Newsday lawsuits are part of a broader pattern of media organizations pushing back against AI companies. Publishers have argued that AI systems trained on journalism create competing products that threaten the economic viability of original reporting.

Microsoft and OpenAI have not yet issued detailed public responses to the specific allegations in these lawsuits. The companies have previously argued that AI training on publicly available data constitutes fair use in some circumstances, though courts have yet to rule definitively on that question in the context of news content.

---

## Implications for the AI Industry

The outcome of these cases could shape how AI companies source training data going forward. If courts side with the publishers, AI laboratories may face pressure to negotiate licensing agreements with media companies or develop alternative approaches to training their models.

The lawsuits also highlight ongoing tensions between the AI industry and traditional media. As AI-generated content becomes more prevalent, news organizations are seeking legal frameworks to protect their role in the information ecosystem.
