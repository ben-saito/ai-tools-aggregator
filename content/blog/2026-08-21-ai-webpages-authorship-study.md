# A Third of Web Pages Published Since ChatGPT Show Signs of AI Authorship, Study Finds

Over one-third (35%) of web pages published after the release of ChatGPT show signs of AI authorship, according to a new study by Pew Research Center. The findings highlight how rapidly AI writing tools have transformed web content creation since November 2022.

---

## Pew Methodology

Pew researchers analyzed nearly half a million English-language web pages from the Common Crawl archive, collecting pages from the five years surrounding ChatGPT's launch. In a random sample of 10,000 pages collected in July 2026, approximately 10% showed significant signs of AI authorship overall.

However, this sample included older pages predating AI writing tools. When researchers filtered to only pages published after ChatGPT's release, the AI authorship rate jumped to 35%.

---

## Domain-Level Variations

The study revealed stark differences across domain types. URLs with .com domains showed AI authorship at roughly 10x the rate of .edu or .gov domains. Educational and government domains maintained approximately 1% AI authorship rates, while .org domains fell at 4.6%.

This disparity likely reflects the different incentives across sector types. Commercial websites have strong economic motivation to scale content production quickly, while academic and government institutions typically maintain stricter editorial standards.

---

## Bot Traffic Acceleration

The Pew findings arrive shortly after Cloudflare reported that bot web traffic had overtaken human traffic—a milestone reached sooner than anticipated. Notably, Pew's data focuses on what is being browsed (AI-authored content), distinct from who is browsing (bot vs. human traffic).

The dynamic creates a recursive loop: bots increasingly read web pages written by other bots, generating data that trains future AI models on AI-generated content.

---

## Implications for Web Ecosystem

The proliferation of AI-authored content raises questions about information quality, search relevance, and the long-term composition of the web's knowledge base. As AI-generated text becomes normalized, distinguishing human-authored original research from synthetic content grows increasingly difficult for both readers and automated systems.
