# Google Wants to Host Your Personal Vibe-Coded Apps in the Play Store

---

Google is planning a new way for users to distribute AI-generated apps directly from AI Studio to the Play Store, bypassing traditional developer requirements. The company announced that users who have built apps using vibe-coding AI tools will soon be able to send their creations to Google's app marketplace right from AI Studio.

## AI-Powered Discovery

Google has already added several AI features to the Play Store, and more are coming. The previously released Ask Play feature is expanding to offer more AI-powered discovery options. Google is also using AI to build a recommendation engine called Game Finder, which leverages usage data from 1.2 billion people who play games on Android.

Game Finder builds a user profile called "Gamer DNA" and matches players with others who have similar gaming preferences. Users can tune their DNA profile by rating games they've played, leading to more accurate recommendations. Game Finder also lets users add or remove tags to further adjust game suggestions.

## Vibe-Coded Apps Pipeline

Despite slower progress in coding-focused AI models lately, Google is continuing to push AI as a core feature of its products. Anyone who has been tinkering with vibe-coded apps in AI Studio will soon have a direct pipe into the Play Store.

Adding a vibe-coded app to the Play Store creates a "Personal Library" accessible from the app store client on your device. This is available only to you by default, providing an easy way to install and manage apps via Google's system. Google will let users keep their vibe-coded AI Studio projects in a private Play Store repository.

## Sharing and Distribution

Google says users can share these apps with "a small group" of friends and family without needing a developer account. Google has not decided exactly how many people count as a small group yet. For broader distribution, Google plans to have an onboarding process to set users up with a developer profile to publish their vibe-coded apps — at which point they become subject to the same app review guidelines as traditional developers.

Private apps are not reviewed by Google. The private-to-public app migration option will be available in the coming months. Private vibe-coded apps in the Play Store begin rolling out today in the US and select other markets.
