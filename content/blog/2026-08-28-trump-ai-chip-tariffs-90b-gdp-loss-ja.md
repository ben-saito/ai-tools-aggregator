# AI Industry Slams Trump's Plan to Tax AI Chips as "Single Dumbest Way Imaginable"

The Trump administration is planning sweeping new semiconductor tariffs that the tech industry warns could cost the US approximately $90 billion annually in GDP losses and delay hundreds of data center projects. The proposed tariffs on chips and chip-making equipment have drawn sharp criticism from across the AI industry.

---

## A Policy That Could "Doom" AI Innovation

According to Politico, a wide range of new semiconductor tariffs could be imposed in the coming months. The tech industry expects the measures will "doom" artificial intelligence innovation in the US. About eight people familiar with the Trump administration's plans, granted anonymity to discuss the framework, said the approach may change as it develops.

The scenario is the tech industry's nightmare. Since Trump took office, trade groups have warned that imposing tariffs on both semiconductors and the many downstream products that use semiconductors would significantly increase costs for data center construction and operation.

---

## The Economic Impact

Most absurdly, given the priorities of Trump's own AI Action Plan, that tariff approach would cost the US about $90 billion annually in GDP losses and cause approximately 20 percent of data center projects planned over the next five years to be cancelled or delayed, according to industry estimates.

The proposed tariffs would affect not just the chips themselves but the entire supply chain of semiconductor manufacturing equipment, memory, and networking components that go into AI data centers.

---

## Industry Pushback

Major tech companies and industry groups have privately lobbied against the measure, arguing it would hand competitive advantages to other nations building out AI infrastructure, particularly China. The tariffs would raise the cost of building AI infrastructure in the US while foreign competitors continue to expand.

The administration has framed the tariffs as a way to strengthen domestic chip manufacturing, but critics say the approach is too broad and would penalize the very industry the government is trying to accelerate.
