# Dario Amodei Outlines Plan to "Pace the Frontier" of AI Development

A week after an Anthropic researcher issued a doomsday warning about AI risks, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has published his own essay outlining a framework for coordinating AI development across frontier labs. The proposal, titled "Pacing the Frontier," advocates for independent safety evaluators and international coordination among AI labs in democratic countries.

---

## The Proposal

Amodei's framework rests on two pillars: independent safety evaluations conducted by third parties rather than the labs themselves, and coordination agreements between AI companies in democratic nations similar to arms control frameworks. The goal is to slow the most dangerous capabilities at the frontier while allowing beneficial AI development to continue.

The essay has already attracted support from several AI safety researchers and policy experts. However, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang offered pointed criticism, arguing that such coordination could amount to "coordination to slow down," potentially ceding AI leadership to less safety-conscious actors.

---

## Context: The Safety Debate Intensifies

The timing of Amodei's essay follows a controversial warning from Anthropic researcher Paul Christiano, who argued that current AI systems pose near-term risks of causing significant harm through misuse or misalignment. Amodei's response frames his coordination proposal as a pragmatic middle ground between unchecked development and blanket pauses.

The tension between safety-focused and capability-focused AI development strategies has become one of the central debates in the field. Amodei's essay attempts to reconcile these perspectives by proposing verifiable, enforceable limits rather than voluntary moratoriums.

---

## Industry Reception

Reactions from other AI labs have been mixed. Some smaller labs have publicly endorsed the framework, viewing it as a way to level the playing field against larger competitors who can afford more aggressive development timelines. Others remain skeptical, arguing that any coordination framework would be difficult to verify and could formalize competitive advantages for incumbents.

Huang's criticism reflects a broader view in Silicon Valley that artificial safety limits on AI development could prove counterproductive, especially given geopolitical competition with China in AI capabilities.
