# DOJ Investigates Andreessen Horowitz Over Potential Conflict of Interest Violations

The U.S. Department of Justice has opened an investigation into Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), one of Silicon Valley's most prominent venture capital firms, according to a TechCrunch podcast report. The inquiry focuses on potential conflicts of interest arising from the firm's partners sitting on boards of competing companies simultaneously.

The DOJ's scrutiny centers on two specific cases: Partner Ben Horowitz serves on the board of Databricks, while Partner Martin Casado sits on the board of a company that competes with Databricks in the data infrastructure space. The arrangement raises questions about whether confidential information from one investment could flow to benefit a competitor through shared board representation.

Venture capital firms routinely invest across competitive landscapes, but board seats at companies that directly compete create heightened scrutiny. Securities law prohibits board members from using non-public information to benefit competing interests, and federal investigators are examining whether the firm violated disclosure requirements or anti-corruption statutes.

a16z has not publicly commented on the investigation. The firm is one of the largest venture investors in AI, with significant holdings across Anthropic, Databricks, Stripe, and numerous other AI infrastructure and application companies.

The investigation reflects increased regulatory attention on the venture capital industry's role in AI markets. As AI companies consolidate around a small number of dominant infrastructure providers, the conflicts embedded in investment structures receive closer examination from antitrust and securities regulators.

The DOJ investigation could result in enforcement actions requiring structural remedies, such as forcing a16z to relinquish board seats or impose stricter information barriers between portfolio companies. Such outcomes would mark a significant shift in how major VC firms manage their portfolio governance.

## Regulatory Environment for VC Board Conflicts

Venture capital firms occupy a unique regulatory position relative to public company boards. Unlike corporations subject to Sarbanes-Oxley requirements, VC firms face fewer formal restrictions on cross-portfolio information sharing. However, when portfolio companies are publicly traded or near-public market valuations, the rules tighten considerably.

The Databricks situation is particularly relevant given that company's role as a data infrastructure provider for AI training and inference. Any appearance that a16z could use board-level access to favor Databricks over competitors would constitute a material conflict given the firm's AI investment thesis.

Industry observers note that the DOJ's focus on a16z suggests a broader pattern of scrutiny aimed at major AI investors. The concentration of AI investment among a small number of VC firms -- a16z, Sequoia, and Thrive Capital collectively account for a majority of late-stage AI deal flow -- makes conflict-of-interest management increasingly complex.
