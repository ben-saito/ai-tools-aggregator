# Pluto Security Raises $42M as AI Creates a New Attack Surface for Enterprise

---

The problem with giving everyone in a company access to AI tools is that people will use them in unexpected ways. And that fact is steadily seeding fresh attack surface across corporate environments in ways that traditional security tooling was never designed to catch. Pluto Security, a startup building a security platform specifically for AI-era risks, has raised $42 million in Series B funding to expand its detection and response capabilities.

The core insight behind Pluto Security is that AI adoption in the enterprise has fundamentally changed the threat landscape. When every employee has access to AI tools that can process sensitive documents, query internal databases, and interact with external APIs, the attack surface expands dramatically. Traditional data loss prevention tools were built for a world where sensitive data lived in known repositories — file servers, databases, email — not in the inputs and outputs of AI systems scattered across a company's workforce.

---

## Detecting the New Class of AI-Native Threats

Pluto's platform monitors how employees interact with AI tools and identifies patterns that indicate security risks: a user忽然 suddenly querying large volumes of customer data through an AI assistant, or an AI tool that has been granted database access returning unusual result sets. The system builds behavioral baselines for normal AI usage within an organization and alerts security teams when those baselines are breached.

What makes Pluto's approach distinct is that it treats the AI system itself as part of the security perimeter. Traditional security tools focus on protecting endpoints and networks; Pluto focuses on the interface between human users and AI systems, monitoring the information flows that pass through that interface.

---

## Why Investors Are Paying Attention

The $42M raise reflects a broader recognition in the security industry that AI adoption is creating a category of risks that existing tools cannot address. Large language models processing sensitive corporate data, AI agents taking actions on behalf of users, and AI-powered social engineering attacks are all relatively new phenomena that most enterprise security stacks were not built to handle.

The funding will be used to expand Pluto's detection capabilities beyond the current focus on data exfiltration and toward a broader set of AI-specific threat categories.
