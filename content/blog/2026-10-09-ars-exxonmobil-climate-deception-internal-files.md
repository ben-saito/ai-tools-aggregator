# Ars Technica — ExxonMobil Internal Files Reveal Decades of Climate Deception

---

Court filings released in recent days have exposed new details about **ExxonMobil's internal climate deliberations**, revealing that the company's own scientists privately doubted the climate solutions Exxon was publicly promoting. The documents, described in an Ars Technica investigation, show a decades-long gap between Exxon's internal scientific understanding and its public-facing messaging on climate change.

The filings add to a growing body of evidence supporting allegations of **climate deception** — a pattern that has made ExxonMobil the subject of multiple state investigations, congressional inquiries, and private litigation. The documents reportedly show that Exxon's internal research team had concluded that certain carbon capture and emissions reduction approaches the company promoted as viable were scientifically questionable — conclusions that were not reflected in Exxon's public communications.

---

## Key Findings

- **Internal scientific doubt**: ExxonMobil scientists privately expressed skepticism about the efficacy of climate solutions the company was simultaneously promoting in public forums
- **Decades of documentation**: The newly released files span multiple decades, showing the gap between internal knowledge and external messaging was sustained over time
- **Legal implications**: The filings are being used in multiple ongoing legal proceedings against the company, including state attorney general investigations

## Broader Context: AI in Climate Science

While this story is primarily about fossil fuel industry practices, it intersects with AI in important ways. AI and **machine learning** are increasingly used in:
- Climate modeling and prediction
- Carbon capture technology assessment
- Energy system optimization

The reliability of AI-driven climate insights depends heavily on the quality and integrity of training data — a reminder that **data provenance** matters enormously in scientific AI applications.
