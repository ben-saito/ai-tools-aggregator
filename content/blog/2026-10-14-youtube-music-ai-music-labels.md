# YouTube Music Rolls Out AI-Generated Music Labels Across Platform

---

YouTube Music on Wednesday announced the rollout of a new labeling system aimed at improving transparency around songs created with the help of generative AI. Now listeners will be able to easily identify tracks created using AI, including those involved in composition and vocals. The label will appear on the player page, and clicking on it provides additional information about the specific role of AI in the track's creation. Notably, these labels will also extend to the main YouTube app.

YouTube said it collaborated with various music partners and deployed its own detection tools to identify AI-generated music. According to its help page, partners using YouTube can declare their work's use of generative AI through one of three designations: **Fully Gen AI**, **Partly Gen AI**, or **No Gen AI**. "Fully Gen AI" applies to tracks that are entirely generated by AI, while "Partly Gen AI" refers to works where artists may have incorporated AI-generated components but added their own live elements. Tracks edited using pitch-correction tools such as Auto-Tune fall under the "No Gen AI" category.

## The Scale of the AI Music Problem

The move comes as AI-generated music rapidly expands across streaming services, and many platforms are adopting their own approaches. For instance, Apple Music has plans to introduce "Made With AI" labels as part of its AI Transparency Tags later this year. Spotify also has AI labels, as well as a music spam filter. Deezer has taken one of the stricter stances, reporting in July that it was receiving nearly **90,000 fully AI-generated tracks daily**. The platform labels detected AI-generated music and excludes it from algorithmic recommendations and editorial playlists, while also withholding royalties from fraudulent streams.

TIDAL also labels recordings it identifies as entirely AI-generated, allows listeners to disable them, and excludes these recordings from royalty attribution. YouTube, by contrast, is focusing on transparency through disclosure rather than outright blocking or filtering.

## Industry-Wide Shift Toward AI Transparency

This development reflects a broader shift in the streaming industry as platforms grapple with the explosive growth of AI-generated content. With tools like Suno and Udio enabling anyone to generate professional-sounding music in seconds, the volume of AI-produced tracks flooding platforms has grown exponentially. YouTube's approach of labeling rather than filtering represents a middle ground — giving users the information to make their own choices while still allowing AI music to exist on the platform.

The labeling system also creates new considerations for artists and rights holders, who must now decide how to disclose AI involvement in their creative process. As AI becomes more deeply integrated into music production, these transparency frameworks will likely become standard across the industry.
