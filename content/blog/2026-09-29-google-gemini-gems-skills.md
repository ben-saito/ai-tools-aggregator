# Google Kills Off Gemini Gems in Favor of Skills

Google is shutting down Gemini's Gems feature, replacing it with a new system called "skills" starting November 17, 2026. The change marks another adjustment to Google's AI product strategy as competition in the personal AI assistant market intensifies.

---

## From Gems to Skills

Gems launched in 2024 as a way for users to create custom AI assistants tailored to specific tasks without needing to repeat instructions each session. Google offered pre-made Gems for common use cases including learning coach, writing editor, and problem solver.

The transition to skills consolidates these capabilities under a streamlined model. A message in the Gemini app informs users that their existing Gems will automatically migrate to the new skills format. Going forward, users will access skills by entering a forward slash "/" in the task field, similar to command-line interfaces.

---

## Competition From Meta and Instinct

The timing of the change coincides with growing competition from Meta's Muse and Instinct, both of which have gained traction as all-in-one AI agents that handle diverse tasks through natural conversation. Google's approach with Gems required users to select and configure a specific assistant for each use case, adding friction compared to the conversational flow offered by competitors.

Industry analysts have noted that Google has struggled to maintain consistent branding and user experience across its AI product lineup. Gems is the latest example of a Google AI feature being discontinued or rebranded after initial launch.

---

## What Skills Means for Users

For existing Gem users, the migration should be seamless. Google has confirmed that all custom instructions and configurations will carry over to the skills format. The underlying AI capabilities remain largely unchanged, with the primary difference being how users access and activate specialized assistants.

The shift also signals Google's broader strategy to simplify its AI product surface. Rather than maintaining separate products for different interaction patterns, the company appears to be consolidating toward a unified assistant interface that handles both general conversation and specialized tasks.
