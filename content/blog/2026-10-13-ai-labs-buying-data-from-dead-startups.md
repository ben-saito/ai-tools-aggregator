# AI Labs Are Buying Data from Defunct Startups to Train Models — Raising Legal and Ethical Questions

---

A new industry has emerged at the intersection of AI training data and startup graveyard economics: companies that aggregate, clean, and sell datasets from failed startups to AI labs. These data brokers operate in a legal gray area, acquiring datasets through various contractual arrangements with bankrupt companies and then licensing them to model trainers at prices that can reach into the tens of millions of dollars.

**The economics:** When startups fail, their data assets — customer databases, proprietary collections, behavioral logs — often go unsold in bankruptcy proceedings. Data brokers have moved into this gap, negotiating with bankruptcy trustees to acquire these assets, then curating them for AI training use cases. Some brokers specialize in specific data types: medical records, code repositories, financial transaction logs, or social media archives.

AI labs purchasing this data argue they are operating within the law, as the data was legitimately acquired through bankruptcy proceedings. Critics contend that the original data subjects — the customers whose information was collected — never consented to their data being used to train AI systems. This is particularly concerning for sensitive categories like health or financial data, where the original consent frameworks were designed for specific service provision, not algorithmic training.

The practice has drawn attention from regulators in the EU and U.S., with the FTC recently opening an inquiry into data broker practices in AI training supply chains. Several class action lawsuits are also pending, alleging that using personal data from bankrupt companies for AI training constitutes a violation of original consent agreements.
