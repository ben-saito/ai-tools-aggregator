# Intology Achieves 44.7% on PostTrainBench — AI Systems Outperforming Human Baselines on AI R&D Automation

AI startup Intology, whose stated goal is to automate R&D, has released a new version of Locus — software designed to take open-weight language models and improve their performance beyond baseline. The new results, externally verified by PostTrainBench authors, show Locus achieving 44.7% on the benchmark, surpassing all frontier-agent baselines.

---

## PostTrainBench: Measuring AI's Ability to Improve AI

PostTrainBench, first introduced in March 2026, measures how well AI systems can take an open-weight model and improve its performance above its baseline through automated post-training. The benchmark tests whether AI systems can serve as capable researchers in the AI development process itself.

When PostTrainBench was first released, the highest-scoring system was Opus 4.6 at 23.2%. By September 2025, Claude Sonnet 4.5 achieved 9.9%. The latest results show Locus with Opus 5 achieving 44.7% — a dramatic jump that suggests AI systems are becoming significantly more capable at improving other AI systems.

---

## Key Results

Locus with Opus 5 achieves 44.7%, compared to 34.1% for Opus 5 without the specialized Locus harness. Even notably, Locus surpasses Fable 5, which scores 41.8%. The results underwent stringent contamination and cheating checks and were externally verified by the PostTrainBench authors.

---

## PostTrainBench+: Exceeding Human Baselines

Intology also built a variant of the benchmark that exceeds the standard 10-hour wall-clock limit on a single GPU. With over 4,000 hours of H100 GPU time, the system achieves 51.6% — surpassing the human baseline of 51.1% on PostTrainBench v1.1.

This suggests that within the current year, AI systems could exceed human performance on the core task of automating AI R&D.

---

## Real-World Deployment

Beyond benchmarks, Locus has already been deployed in production at Bubble, a no-code app-development startup. Locus discovered and trained a language model that now runs at approximately 2.8x lower error, 5.4x lower latency, and 105x lower cost than previous solutions.

---

## Why This Matters

These results add further evidence that AI systems are about to start building themselves — a trend that has significant implications for AI governance, safety, and the future of the technology workforce. The gap between frontier AI labs and automated AI R&D is narrowing faster than many anticipated.

The researchers estimate that the current human baseline on PostTrainBench will be exceeded before the end of 2026.
