# Nvidia Raises Shield TV Pro Price to $299, Citing AI-Driven Component Costs

Nvidia has raised the price of its Shield TV Pro media streaming box from $199 to $299, with the company directly blaming rising component costs driven by AI hardware demand. The price increase takes effect October 2, 2026.

---

## The Shield's History and AI Connection

The Nvidia Shield TV Pro first launched in 2019 running Android TV with AI-powered video upscaling and hardware decoding for nearly any media format. Despite running an aging Tegra X1+ processor, the device remained competitive for streaming media because of its extensive codec support and software capabilities.

For years, the Shield earned a dedicated following among home media enthusiasts who valued its premium features: Plex server functionality, 4K HDR streaming, and AI upscaling that could improve lower-resolution content. The device launched at $199.99.

Nvidia confirmed the price change in a statement to Ars Technica: "Starting October 2, SHIELD Pro will be priced at $299. The cost of components, including memory, has increased substantially across the industry."

The company declined to specify which components drove the increase, but memory and NAND flash prices have risen significantly as AI data center deployments compete with consumer electronics for the same semiconductor supply chain.

---

## The Irony of AI Pricing Pressure

The price increase carries an ironic edge because Nvidia has been the primary beneficiary of the AI boom that is driving up component costs across the industry. Nvidia GPUs and accelerators power the hyperscale AI infrastructure built by Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta. That demand has tightened supply across the semiconductor supply chain, raising costs for everything from HBM memory to basic NAND flash used in consumer devices.

When asked about the Shield as a product line earlier this year, Nvidia's Andrew Bell described it as a passion project. Bell noted that Nvidia still actively manufactures the Shield and sells every unit it makes -- suggesting the device was never a major revenue driver but remained in production to serve a dedicated user base.

At the old price point, manufacturing new units had likely become marginal or loss-making as component costs rose. At $300, Nvidia can sustainably continue production for the enthusiast market that values the device's codec support and media server capabilities.

---

## Market Context and Alternatives

The Shield TV Pro was already difficult to find in recent months, with stock slim at retail. The price increase likely reflects a deliberate decision to keep the device in production rather than discontinue it entirely.

For most users, the economics of the Shield no longer make sense at $299. Competing streaming devices from Roku, Amazon, and Google offer 4K streaming at a fraction of the price, and while they lack the Shield's advanced codec support and AI upscaling, those features only matter for specific use cases like home media server setups or playback of unusual video formats.

The Shield remains out of stock at Nvidia's direct store but is available at the new $299 price from Best Buy.
