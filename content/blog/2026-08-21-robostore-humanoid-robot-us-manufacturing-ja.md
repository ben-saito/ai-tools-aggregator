# US Distributor of China's Most Popular Humanoid Robots Pivots After FCC Import Ban

RoboStore, the main North American distributor for Unitree Robotics' humanoid and quadruped robots, has pivoted to U.S.-based manufacturing following an FCC ban on foreign-made robots.

RoboStore had sold over 1,500 robots to universities including Harvard and MIT, tech companies like Amazon and Nvidia, and over 150 universities for research and development purposes.

On August 10, RoboStore announced plans to open a 66,000-square-foot manufacturing facility by Q1 2027 under a new company called Robo Inc. The shift was accelerated by U.S. government moves to restrict or ban foreign-made robots, including legislation like the American Security Robotics Act (Senate, March) and the GUARD Act (House, June).

In July, the FCC banned imports of new models of foreign-made robots, including Unitree's humanoid robots and robot dogs, citing national security concerns over cybersecurity vulnerabilities.

Robo Inc. is developing a humanoid torso on a wheeled chassis for healthcare and concierge services, a specialized quadruped for security applications, and a humanoid robot platform for educators and researchers.

"We're reaching out to all these customers, and we have incredible interest now as we go through the process of raising money," said Teddy Haggerty, founder and CEO of RoboStore. The company has spent years as a bootstrapped business without outside investor funding.
