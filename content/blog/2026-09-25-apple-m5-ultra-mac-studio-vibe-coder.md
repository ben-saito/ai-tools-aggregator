# Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studio Targets Local AI Developers at $9,499

Apple's new M5 Ultra Mac Studio has arrived with a bold positioning: a local AI development machine for developers who want to run open-weight models without relying on cloud APIs. The review from Ars Technica paints a picture of a workstation caught off guard by its own popularity in the AI community, with Apple scrambling to meet demand from developers running tools like OpenClaw and local language models.

---

## From Intel to Apple Silicon: Why Local AI Changed Everything

When Apple was using Intel processors and AMD GPUs, the idea of an expandable and upgradeable tower made sense. But Apple Silicon chips brought the CPU, GPU, and memory interface under one roof, making that traditional upgradeability impossible. What emerged instead was a system perfectly suited for a modern "pro" workload that really pushes high-end hardware: running language models and agents locally.

Apple was caught off guard by the demand for these systems from people running software like OpenClaw and running open-weight models locally. Combined with the AI-fueled memory shortage, this is why it has been nearly impossible to buy most of these systems for months.

---

## The M5 Ultra: Two Generations in One Jump

The M5 Ultra jumps two processor generations in a single refresh (there was no M4 Ultra). The chip includes up to 12 super CPU cores, up to 24 P-cores, up to 80 GPU cores, 32 Neural Engine cores, two video decoding engines and four encoding engines, and a little over 1.2TB/s of memory bandwidth.

What's unique this generation: the M5 Pro and M5 Max are both already a pair of chiplets attached together via silicon interconnect, meaning the M5 Ultra is actually four distinct bits of silicon packaged together. The M1 Ultra, M2 Ultra, and M3 Ultra were essentially a pair of Max chips strapped together using a high-speed interconnect. The M5 Ultra maintains this approach but with chiplet-based building blocks.

In general-purpose CPU and GPU tests, the Ultra's CPU outruns the M5 Max by 80 to 90 percent, and the GPU is between 50 and 80 percent faster. The GPU scaling is consistent with past Ultra chips where the CPU comes closer to 2x scaling than the GPU.

---

## The Price Problem: $9,499 for the Base Ultra

Apple has raised prices across the board. The basic M4 Max Mac Studio started at $1,999; the M5 Max Studio starts at $2,499, a $500 increase. The M3 Ultra Mac Studio with 96GB of RAM started at $3,999. The M5 Ultra with 256GB RAM and 1TB storage that would have cost $5,599 eighteen months ago now costs $9,499.

The M5 Ultra is, by almost every measure, the fastest Apple Silicon processor built so far. It only loses in single-core CPU performance where the Mac mini's M6 very narrowly beats it, though the gap is small.

---

## AI Developer Reality Check

For developers running local AI workloads, the M5 Ultra offers substantial neural engine capacity and unified memory architecture that works well for running open-weight models. The 32 Neural Engine cores provide dedicated AI acceleration, and the unified memory architecture allows the CPU, GPU, and neural engines to share memory without copying overhead.

However, the Handbrake video encoding test revealed unexpected behavior: both the M5 Max and M5 Ultra consume about 75W during the test, and the Max was occasionally faster at completing H.264 encodes. This suggests that software optimization for the new chip architecture may still be catching up.

---

## Developer Takeaway

The M5 Ultra Mac Studio represents Apple's clearest statement yet that it sees AI developers as a core professional audience. The pricing reflects both the hardware complexity and the reality that demand for local AI compute has driven prices up across the industry. For developers committed to running models locally without cloud dependencies, the M5 Ultra delivers the fastest Apple Silicon chip ever made, but at a cost that requires serious commitment to the use case.
