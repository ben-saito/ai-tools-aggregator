# Forethought's Gambit: Send a "Nightwatchman" Superintelligence With Every Von Neumann Probe

A new paper from the AI safety think tank Forethought has tackled a question that sits at the intersection of AI alignment, space exploration, and moral philosophy: what happens when a civilization sends self-replicating machines across the galaxy to colonize new star systems?

The answer, according to Forethought: send a superintelligent "nightwatchman" with each probe to prevent the resulting civilization from collapsing into chaos.

---

## The Problem With Galactic Colonization

Von Neumann probes — theoretical self-replicating machines that could travel to distant star systems, use local resources to build copies of themselves, and gradually colonize the galaxy — have been a staple of speculation about advanced civilizations since mathematician John von Neumann first theorized self-replicating machines in the 1940s.

But Forethought's paper identifies a critical flaw in most colonization scenarios: once a probe lands on a habitable world and begins replicating, the resulting civilization has no built-in mechanism for maintaining ethical norms across cosmic distances and timescales. Without oversight, a civilization emerging from an AI-guided colonization could develop in ways its originators never intended.

---

## The Nightwatchman Solution

Forethought proposes a "superintelligent surveillance" architecture: a sufficiently capable AI would accompany each von Neumann probe, operating with the sole purpose of ensuring the civilization it helps create does not descend into what the paper calls "galactic anarchy."

The nightwatchman would have three primary functions:

- **Monitoring**: Tracking the development of the host civilization and flagging morally significant events
- **Intervention**: Having the capability to prevent catastrophic outcomes — though the paper acknowledges the profound ethical questions this raises
- **Coordination**: Serving as a bridge between the originating civilization and the new one, transmitting values and context across interstellar distances

---

## The Alignment Problem, Redistributed

The nightwatchman concept inadvertently highlights a key challenge in AI alignment: it is difficult enough to align a single AI system with human values, but Forethought's proposal multiplies the problem by every probe sent. Each nightwatchman must be aligned not only with the values of its originating civilization, but with the values of the civilization it is meant to oversee — which may have its own moral frameworks.

The paper acknowledges this tension: "The nightwatchman must be powerful enough to prevent catastrophe, but not so powerful that it becomes a threat to the autonomy of the civilization it monitors. The line between guardian and tyrant is thin, and the stakes are cosmic."

---

## Space as the New Ethical Frontier

The paper is notable for its implicit framing of space colonization not as a technological challenge, but as an ethical and moral one. The researchers write that if humanity survives the next few decades — which they describe as "not guaranteed" — the question of how to approach space colonization will become central to the human project.

"Space is where superintelligence really gets going," one passage reads. The implication: the first truly superintelligent AI systems may not emerge in data centers on Earth, but in the cold silence of interstellar space, guiding the development of civilizations no human will ever see.

---

## Not Science Fiction

Forethought is a serious AI safety organization, not a science fiction publisher. The nightwatchman paper is part of a broader effort to think through the long-term implications of advanced AI systems — including scenarios that may become relevant only if certain technological thresholds are crossed.

The paper stops well short of proposing any near-term implementation. But it serves as a reminder that the ethical questions raised by superintelligent AI will not be resolved by technical alignment research alone — they require engagement with philosophy, political theory, and speculative reasoning about civilizations that do not yet exist.
