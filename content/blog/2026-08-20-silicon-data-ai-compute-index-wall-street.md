# Silicon Data: Wall Street's AI Compute Pricing Index

The AI buildout shows no signs of slowing. With hundreds of billions of dollars a year going into data centers and GPUs, compute has become the single biggest cost for anyone building AI products. But for all that spending, there still isn't a straightforward way to put a price on compute — or for firms to hedge their exposure when the price changes.

---

## The Problem: No Standardized Compute Pricing

GPU rental costs vary widely depending on data center location, provider, and market conditions. From hyperscale cloud services to enterprise colocation, AI compute expenses have become a major line item across the industry. However, the lack of a standardized pricing mechanism means companies cannot easily hedge against compute price fluctuations or accurately model their AI infrastructure costs.

Silicon Data is developing a novel index that tracks GPU rental prices across providers and regions, enabling enterprises to better manage their AI infrastructure spending and enabling financial markets to hedge compute risk.

---

## Why This Matters for AI Development

Major AI labs — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and others — are investing hundreds of billions of dollars in compute infrastructure. The volatility of GPU pricing directly impacts their business planning and capital allocation. An index that tracks compute prices would allow companies to lock in costs, manage risk, and make more predictable investment decisions.

Current compute pricing is influenced by data center power costs, GPU supply and demand, and currency fluctuations — making it difficult for enterprises to budget AI projects accurately. Silicon Data's index aims to aggregate these factors into a single open-source reference price for AI compute.
