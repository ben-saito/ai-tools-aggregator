# Amazon Texas Data Center Plan Would Make It Largest U.S. Climate Polluter

---

## On-Site Fossil Power Plant for AI Compute

Amazon is planning a massive data center in Pecos County, Texas — and the company is simultaneously building an on-site power plant that would become the largest source of climate pollution in the United States. According to the New York Times, the plant would burn natural gas and is permitted to release 33 million tons of carbon dioxide per year.

This exceeds the emissions of any existing power plant in the country. The project reflects a broader trend where AI compute demand is driving data center infrastructure to resort to direct fossil fuel generation rather than relying on grid upgrades.

---

## Amazon's Climate Pledge Undermined

Amazon has co-founded the Climate Pledge and has publicly committed to net-zero carbon by 2040. However, the company's carbon emissions rose 16% last year — a direction fundamentally at odds with those commitments. An Amazon spokesperson said "the world looks different now than when we co-founded the climate pledge" while claiming the commitment remains intact.

The disconnect between stated climate goals and actual infrastructure investment highlights the tension between AI industry growth and environmental accountability.

---

## AI's Growing Carbon Footprint

The data center would be powered by on-site generation that Amazon claims will not raise electricity costs for Texas grid customers. But the scale of the project — 33 million tons of annual CO2 — represents a significant step backward for corporate climate commitments in the AI era.
