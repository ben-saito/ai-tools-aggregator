# Trump Killed the Digital Equity Act—But Courts Forced Part of It Back

The Trump administration eliminated the $1.25 billion Digital Equity Act, but a federal court has ruled that at least a portion of the broadband funding must be restored after finding that race-based provisions in the original program were unconstitutional.

---

## The Digital Equity Act

The Digital Equity Act was established to provide federal funding for broadband internet access in underserved communities. The program aimed to close the digital divide by directing resources to schools, libraries, and community organizations that lacked reliable internet connectivity.

The Biden administration implemented the program with provisions that explicitly considered race in determining funding allocations. The Trump administration moved to eliminate the program entirely, arguing that the race-based criteria were unconstitutional.

---

## Court Ruling

A federal judge ruled that while the race-based provisions were indeed unconstitutional, eliminating the entire program was not the appropriate remedy. The court ordered that funding be restored under neutral criteria that do not consider race.

"The court recognized that there are legitimate constitutional concerns with the original implementation," a Justice Department spokesperson said. "But the solution is to fix those provisions, not to eliminate assistance to communities that need it."

The administration has been forced to restart parts of the program while working to develop race-neutral criteria for distributing funds.

---

## What Comes Next

The episode highlights the ongoing legal battles over affirmative action and race-conscious policies in federal programs. It also underscores the political complexity of addressing digital equity without using demographic criteria.

Broadband advocates warn that the uncertainty has delayed critical infrastructure projects in rural and low-income areas.
