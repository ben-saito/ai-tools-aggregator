# OpenAI Fought Dirty on Career-Making Math Problem, Says NYU Mathematician

NYU mathematics professor Tristan Buckmaster announced three proofs on Tuesday with a preliminary finding on one of the major unsolved problems in theoretical mathematics. The findings, made in collaboration with Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge and using both Codex and Claude AI models, are significant in themselves — but they are also accompanied by an unusual controversy surrounding OpenAI attempts to solve the same problem.

---

## The Controversy

While Buckmaster and Alpöge were finalizing their results, they learned that information about their progress had been passed to OpenAI. When they contacted OpenAI about this, they were told that OpenAI had already achieved a full proof of the central problem. But when they asked followup questions about when OpenAI had begun its research and how much human input was involved, the answers became more evasive.

It emerged that an entire team had been working on the problem, Buckmaster says, and that an insane amount of compute had been used. Eventually, it was agreed that the first prompt had been sent in the past few days, after information about their work had reached OpenAI.

If true, that would suggest the OpenAI team had become convinced that Buckmaster and Alpöge approach was the right one, and decided to use its material advantage in computing resources to reach a formal proof first.

Sebastian Bubeck, who leads OpenAI mathematical research, says those claims are false and inflammatory. To clarify, I came into the discussion following academic norms, and I am disappointed that it has come to this, he wrote. Anyone who knows me knows that academic standards are of the highest importance to me.

---

## The Navier-Stokes Problem

The dispute centers on the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize problems — a set of major unsolved math problems, each carrying a $1 million bounty from Clay Mathematics Institute. The Navier-Stokes equations are widely used in fluid mechanics but poorly understood in theoretical terms. A solution would represent a significant advance in the collective understanding of mathematical physics.

Although the problem is widely pursued among mathematicians, the specific tactic taken by Buckmaster and his collaborator is far less common. As a result, Buckmaster found it suspicious that OpenAI ended up taking the same approach at the same time.

The route to the Clay problem through a smooth force, options c and d in Fefferman statement of the problem, is the route Luis and Diego opened and the one Levent and I had quietly chosen to attack, Buckmaster wrote. Almost nobody else I know of was working on it. It is not the direction one arrives at in a few days by giving a model the problem statement.

## Codex Data and Conflict of Interest

While Alpöge is employed by Anthropic, he was not conducting this research on the company is behalf. As a result, the duo used a mix of models, relying primarily on OpenAI is Codex in their work. Even so, Alpöge is affiliation with a rival lab seems to have been a sore point for OpenAI, and Buckmaster alleges that Bubeck asked him to remove Alpöge is credit as part of a proposed compromise.

When Buckmaster pushed to make the dispute public, he says that Bubeck replied: Why would you ruin your career? When Buckmaster pushed back, Bubeck followed up with: If you do not want me to be nice, then I do not have to be nice.

Buckmaster also raised concerns that, because he used Codex extensively in assembling the project, information from his work could have informed OpenAI is own efforts to solve the problem. OpenAI reserves the right to train models on Codex interactions, although users are able to opt-out. If the OpenAI team used a model trained on Buckmaster is own Codex interactions, it is plausible that it could have regurgitated his work when faced with a similar problem.

Regardless, the issue is likely to reignite the ongoing debate about AI is role in mathematical research, and OpenAI is specific incentives. For his part, Buckmaster seems to believe the best answer is to get as much information about the research out into the public eye.

I have not seen OpenAI is proof, Buckmaster wrote. I do not know what their model did, or how. I do not know whether our data was used. I am not accusing anyone of anything. I am stating what I was told, when, and what was proposed to me. I am stating it because the alternative is to let a sequence of announcements say something I know to be false.

## Technical Context

The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem concerns whether solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations can exist for all time, or whether they can develop singularities in finite time. This is one of the most important open questions in mathematical fluid dynamics. The problem is particularly challenging because the equations behave differently depending on the initial conditions, and certain configurations can lead to infinite velocities or pressure at a single point.

The use of AI systems in mathematical research has been controversial. While AI has helped discover new proofs and verify existing ones, questions remain about the role of training data, the potential for data contamination, and the incentives created when large companies compete for mathematical prestige and the associated financial rewards.
