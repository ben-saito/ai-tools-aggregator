# Meta Ads Pushed AI "Nudify" Apps Targeting Teens — 332 CSAM Ads Found This Year Alone

The Tech Transparency Project (TTP) has uncovered a disturbing pattern on Meta's platforms: at least 332 advertisements containing child sexual abuse material (CSAM) ran on Facebook and Instagram in 2026, with the "vast majority" promoting AI-powered "nudification" apps. The ads exploited real photos of young girls found online, some depicting preteens. Meta removed the flagged ads after TTP's disclosure, but the incident has renewed scrutiny of the company's ad review systems.

---

## How the Scandal Unfolded

TTP's investigation, published September 8, built on an earlier collaboration with Wired that identified 53 CSAM ads in August. After that initial batch was flagged, Meta claimed it had cleaned house — but TTP's follow-up investigation found hundreds more, including multiple CSAM ads matched to real photos of children that had appeared online.

The problem extends beyond CSAM. Wired independently identified "hundreds of other related 'nudification' ads containing explicit content of adults" on Meta's platforms, likely shared without consent. Nudification apps — which use AI to remove clothing from photos — have faced regulatory pressure globally, but enforcement on platforms hosting their advertisements has remained inconsistent.

TTP's findings call into question the integrity of Meta's ad approval process. If the system cannot detect and reject obviously horrific content before publication, "then Meta's whole ad review system should be called into question," the group warned in a press release.

---

## Meta's Inconsistent Response

After TTP flagged the 332 ads, Meta quickly removed approximately half from its archived ad library. But TTP noted curious omissions: many of the ads were not marked as violating Meta's policy on child sexual abuse material, a gap that "suggests Meta may be undercounting instances of child sexual abuse."

Some ads ran for days after being reported. TTP cited cases where Meta took "at least a week to review the reports and often took no action" despite clear violations involving minors. Other ads were linked to fraudulent business pages violating Meta's policies on inauthentic behavior.

One particularly glaring example: a Facebook page called "Divine Productions" — falsely claiming to represent the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints — ran a CSAM ad that escaped Meta's notice entirely. The ad was only removed after TTP's disclosure.

---

## Broader Pattern of AI-Enabled Harm

This incident reflects a broader challenge platforms face as AI-generated content proliferates. The Justice Department has clarified that AI-generated CSAM is illegal under federal child pornography laws, but the technical means to detect and prevent such content in advertising ecosystems have not kept pace. Meta is not alone in grappling with these issues, but its position as the world's largest digital advertising platform gives any failures outsized impact.

The TTP investigation arrives as legislators in multiple jurisdictions consider stricter regulations on AI-generated intimate imagery. In the United States, proposed federal legislation would criminalize the creation and distribution of AI-generated nude images without consent, with enhanced penalties when minors are depicted.

---

## What Comes Next

Meta has removed the specific ads flagged by TTP and Wired. The company maintains it has policies prohibiting CSAM and AI-generated intimate imagery and says it works to enforce them. But critics argue that reactive removal after public disclosure is insufficient — and that Meta's advertised review processes are not catching harmful content at the point of submission.

For developers building AI content moderation systems, the Meta case illustrates a difficult technical problem: distinguishing AI-generated imagery that sexualizes minors from legitimate AI applications requires sophisticated visual analysis and context understanding that current automated systems handle poorly. The gap between policy and enforcement remains wide.
