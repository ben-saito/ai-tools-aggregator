# Florida Asks Court to Halt OpenAI's Frontier Model Development

Florida has filed a new legal motion asking a court to stop OpenAI from developing its most advanced AI systems, arguing that frontier AI models represent an existential threat to civilization. The motion, filed Monday morning, escalates a civil lawsuit the state originally filed in June, when it argued ChatGPT was "a threat to the public safety of Floridians" — particularly to vulnerable populations.

---

## "The Greatest Public Nuisance Ever Created"

The new filing introduces stronger language, calling large language models "the greatest public nuisance ever created." Florida's legal team argues that OpenAI's frontier development poses an extinction-level risk that outweighs any benefits the technology might provide. The state is seeking an injunction that would halt OpenAI's most advanced research until adequate safety measures are in place.

The legal theory represents a novel application of public nuisance law to AI development. Traditionally, public nuisance claims have been used against physical activities like pollution or noise — not software development. OpenAI is expected to challenge the filing as legally baseless.

---

## Prior Arguments: Children and Vulnerable Adults

Florida's original June lawsuit focused on more immediate harms: the potential for ChatGPT to prey on children, spread misinformation, and harm vulnerable adults. The new filing expands this to a civilization-scale argument, citing recent AI safety research and high-profile incidents involving AI systems behaving unexpectedly.

Legal experts are divided on the merits. Some argue Florida's theory could open the door to broader regulatory actions against AI companies. Others say courts are unlikely to grant an injunction based on speculative future harms.

---

## Industry Reaction

The AI industry has broadly criticized Florida's legal strategy. Industry groups argue that such lawsuits could chill innovation and push AI development to other countries. OpenAI has declined to comment specifically on the pending litigation but has defended its safety practices.

The case is likely to wind through the courts for years. Whatever the outcome, it signals a new front in the ongoing legal and political battle over how to govern advanced AI systems.
