# Epic Games Reportedly Pivots to "AI First" Game Development Model

---

Epic Games is shifting to an "AI first" development strategy for its collaboration with the Walt Disney Company, letting AI models write all the code and guide all the artwork for an upcoming shooter. That's according to a Bloomberg report, which cites unnamed people familiar with the project who commented on the significant pivot in internal development at the Unreal Engine maker.

Human developers will be tasked with managing and guiding code and art assets generated by models such as Anthropic's Claude, according to internal Epic employee messages viewed by Bloomberg. That means "no more hand-written code" and art assets "generated with full AI workflows," according to the report.

## A $1.5 Billion Collaboration on Hold

The $1.5 billion collaboration between Epic and Disney, announced in early 2024, was slated to release a Marvel- and Star Wars-themed shooter in March 2027. That project has now been delayed indefinitely amid the AI-related shakeup and understaffed, slower-than-expected development thus far.

"We're embracing AI-accelerated development, led by Epic developers who are the visionaries and creative drivers for everything we do," an Epic spokesperson said in a statement.

Epic CEO Tim Sweeney wrote on social media: "We have a huge pivot underway here at Epic, moving developers around to retool for maximum AI acceleration. The aim isn't to do the same stuff with fewer people, but to enable everyone here to build MORE and BETTER stuff."

## AI in Game Development: Industry-Wide Trend

Sweeney and Epic have long been bullish on AI in game development. In an interview with IGN in June, Sweeney saw AI enabling games with "the possibility of infinite dialogue with a really simple setup for creators," where small teams could create games with "immense amounts of characters and immense and interactive worlds."

A survey of over 2,300 game industry professionals ahead of this year's Game Developers Conference found that 36 percent used generative AI tools as part of their jobs. However, those tools were used more for research and brainstorming (81 percent of AI-using respondents) than for code assistance (47 percent) and prototyping (35 percent).

Epic laid off over 1,000 employees in March, citing a "downturn in Fortnite engagement." Fortnite Senior External Development Manager Stephanie Arnette said the goal of AI tools is to "make us more efficient," not to replace jobs.

Last May, Epic and Disney faced embarrassment when an AI-voiced Darth Vader character added to Fortnite started repeating curse words before a hotfix was rolled out.
