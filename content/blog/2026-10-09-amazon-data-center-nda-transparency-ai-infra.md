# Amazon and Tech Giants Drop Data Center NDA Curtains — What Changed?

---

In a notable shift for the **AI infrastructure** arms race, Amazon and several other major **tech companies** have abandoned their long-standing practice of requiring strict **non-disclosure agreements** around data center procurement deals. The change, reported by TechCrunch, signals a new phase of transparency in the massive capital expenditures being funneled into **AI model training** and inference infrastructure.

## Why NDAs Became Standard

For years, cloud providers and hyperscalers kept data center agreements confidential for competitive reasons. Announcing a deal for 100MW of power or 50,000 GPU servers was effectively telegraphing your AI training capacity to rivals. The opacity also shielded suppliers from scrutiny and gave buyers leverage in negotiations.

The pattern was particularly pronounced after the **GPT-4** launch in early 2023 triggered an industry-wide scramble for **H100** and **A100** GPUs from Nvidia. Companies like Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud locked in capacity via multi-year agreements, with both parties having strong incentives to keep terms secret.

## What's Driving the Shift

Several factors appear to be pushing companies toward disclosure:

1. **Regulatory pressure**: Antitrust authorities in the US and EU have grown increasingly interested in AI infrastructure concentration. Voluntary transparency may head off mandatory disclosure requirements.

2. **Investor expectations**: Public market shareholders want clearer visibility into massive capex commitments. Amazon's AWS capital expenditures have surged past $20B quarterly, and analysts want to understand the AI-driven demand profile.

3. **Supplier relationships**: Some data center operators and power providers now prefer public deal announcements as a signal to markets and municipalities about their own growth trajectories.

4. **Competitive recalibration**: With the compute lead now widely understood to belong to Microsoft/OpenAI and Google DeepMind, the marginal value of secrecy has decreased. The race is no longer about hiding capacity — it's about demonstrating capability.

## The AI Infrastructure Buildout Context

The move toward transparency reflects the sheer scale of the ongoing buildout. Microsoft has committed to $80B+ in AI infrastructure for fiscal 2026. Google has pledged similar magnitudes. Amazon's AWS, while more diversified, has dramatically accelerated its AI-specific capex.

Power availability has become the primary constraint. **Nvidia's Blackwell** GPU families require unprecedented power density per rack, pushing data center designs toward liquid cooling and custom power delivery. The competition for power purchase agreements with utilities has become as strategic as the compute procurement itself.

## Implications for the AI Developer Ecosystem

For developers and companies building **AI applications**, the shift matters in practical terms:

- **Pricing transparency**: As infrastructure costs become more visible, SaaS and application layer pricing may rationalize around clearer cost structures.
- **Vendor evaluation**: Enterprises can now benchmark infrastructure commitments when choosing AI platform providers.
- **Supply chain visibility**: A clearer picture of who has committed to what capacity helps the market anticipate GPU and inference availability timelines.

The days of secretive data center deals may be numbered — driven as much by the sheer scale of the buildout as by any philosophical commitment to openness.
