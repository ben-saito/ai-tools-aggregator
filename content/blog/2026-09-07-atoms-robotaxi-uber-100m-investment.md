# Travis Kalanick's Atoms Reportedly in Talks With Uber for Robotaxi Technology

Uber founder Travis Kalanick's robotics company Atoms is reportedly preparing to enter the robotaxi business, according to a Financial Times story cited by TechCrunch. The report details Atoms' ambitions to become a major player in the autonomous vehicle industry through a hiring spree and acquisitions — months after raising $1.7 billion in a funding round led by Andreessen Horowitz.

---

## Atoms and Uber: A Strategic Fit

According to the FT report, Atoms has held discussions with Uber about how the ride-hailing giant could integrate Atoms' robotaxi technology into its platform. Uber has already established partnerships with multiple autonomous vehicle companies, but Atoms represents a potentially significant addition to that network — particularly given Kalanick's history with Uber and his stated goal of pursuing "unfinished business" through Atoms.

Uber has invested $100 million in Atoms, a figure previously confirmed by TechCrunch, signaling the ride-hailing company's strategic interest in the robotics startup's technology.

---

## Acquisition of Pronto Connects the Dots

Atoms' robotaxi ambitions align with its earlier acquisition of Pronto, an autonomous mining startup led by Uber's former self-driving chief Anthony Levandowski. Levandowki was convicted of stealing trade secrets from Google, sentenced to 18 months in prison, and later pardoned by President Donald Trump. His expertise in autonomous systems — now channeled through Pronto under the Atoms umbrella — provides a technical foundation for the company's broader autonomous vehicle aspirations.

---

## Kalanick's "Unfinished Business"

The robotaxi direction fits Kalanick's description of the $1.7 billion raise as pursuit of "unfinished business" — a reference to his contentious departure from Uber in 2017. The intersection of Atoms' robotics technology, Levandowski's autonomous driving pedigree, and Uber's existing AV partnerships creates a coherent strategic picture: Kalanick is building a vertically integrated autonomous vehicle business that could eventually compete in the same robotaxi market Uber has been cultivating through partnerships.
