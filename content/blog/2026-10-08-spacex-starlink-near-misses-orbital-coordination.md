# SpaceX Calls for Better Coordination in Orbit After Near-Misses with Starlink

---

SpaceX has formally communicated to the FCC that recent close encounters between its Starlink satellites and other spacecraft demonstrate an urgent need for improved orbital traffic coordination. The company described conjunction events — instances where objects in orbit pass within meters of each other — ranging from "tens of meters to hundreds of meters," calling the situation "too close to comfort."

## Conjunction Events: What the Data Shows

SpaceX's filing documented multiple close approaches involving Starlink satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO). Conjunctions at orbital velocities are qualitatively different from terrestrial near-misses: at 7.8 km/s (the speed of objects in LEO), even a hundreds-of-meters miss represents a very small angular separation from the perspective of orbital mechanics.

The company argued that current conjunction assessment processes are insufficient for the density of traffic in LEO, which has increased dramatically with the growth of Starlink, Amazon Kuiper, and other constellation operators.

## The Broader Context: A Congested Orbit

The near-miss events highlight a structural problem in low Earth orbit governance. The Outer Space Treaty predates commercial satellite internet constellations by decades. FCC orbital debris guidelines were written for a fraction of the current traffic levels. As more operators launch large constellations, the probability of conjunctions — and the importance of coordination protocols — grows correspondingly.

SpaceX's call for improved coordination mechanisms may also be strategically timed: clearer FCC coordination requirements could impose compliance burdens on competing constellation operators like Amazon's Project Kuiper.

## Developer and Entrepreneur Perspective

For developers and entrepreneurs building in the space technology sector, orbital congestion represents both a practical risk and a business opportunity:

- **Launch providers** and satellite operators need better conjunction monitoring and prediction tools
- **Insurance underwriters** are increasingly focused on collision risk in dense orbital shells
- **In-space servicing and debris removal** startups have a growing addressable market as the problem intensifies

The SpaceX filing is likely to accelerate regulatory discussions about mandatory conjunction assessment sharing and minimum separation standards for large constellations.
