# Viral AI Agent Instinct Raises $1B Series C at $10B Valuation

Instinct, the AI agent startup known for its personal AI assistant that operates through SMS and phone calls, has closed a $1 billion Series C funding round at a $10 billion valuation, the company confirmed this week.

The fundraise comes just months after earlier reports by The Information suggested the company was seeking capital at a high valuation. The rapid progression from earlier funding rounds to a billion-dollar raise reflects intense investor appetite for consumer AI agents that can actually complete tasks on behalf of users.

Instinct's AI assistant distinguishes itself by operating through a dedicated phone number and computer system, allowing it to interact with the world through voice calls and text messages. Recent product expansions include a "concierge" feature capable of making phone calls to manage tasks on behalf of users.

"We're building Instinct to be the best personal agent that can get things done for you," founder Noah Shinn said in a company statement. "We're just getting started."

The company's viral adoption has been driven by its ability to actually execute tasks rather than merely answer questions. Users can delegate phone calls, appointment scheduling, and other real-world interactions to the AI assistant.

However, the capabilities come with scrutiny. Users must disclose significant personal information to grant the agent access to complete tasks, raising questions about data privacy and the trade-offs between convenience and security.

Instinct now faces fresh competition from Meta's Muse, which launched with deep integration into Meta's ecosystem and has climbed to the top of U.S. app stores with millions of downloads. While Instinct relies on SMS and texting, Muse offers a more integrated experience within Meta's platform.

---

## Competitive Landscape

The personal AI agent market has rapidly evolved from question-answering systems to agents capable of executing real-world tasks. Instinct's approach of giving each user a dedicated AI agent with phone and computer access represents a significant capability expansion over traditional chatbot interfaces.

Meta's entry with Muse has intensified competition. Muse's deep integration with Meta's social and messaging platforms gives it advantages in user reach and ecosystem lock-in, while Instinct's independent phone-number-based approach offers portability but requires users to manage a separate service.

---

## Technical Capabilities

Instinct's agent architecture is designed to interact with legacy systems that lack native AI interfaces. By operating through standard communication channels like phone and SMS, the agent can complete tasks on websites and services that have not been specifically designed for AI integration.

The "concierge" feature extends this capability to voice calls, allowing the AI to manage tasks like scheduling appointments, making reservations, and handling customer service interactions on behalf of users.

---

## Privacy and Security Considerations

The trade-off between capability and privacy has become a central question for personal AI agents. Granting an AI assistant access to one's phone, computer, and personal information enables powerful task execution but also creates significant data exposure.

Instinct's privacy practices and data handling have been subject to scrutiny as users weigh the benefits of AI assistance against the personal information requirements for access.
