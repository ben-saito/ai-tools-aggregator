# AstroForge Adopts Transformer Models for Autonomous Spacecraft Control

AstroForge plans to launch its AI-controlled autonomous spacecraft Autonomy-1 aboard the first Stoke Space rocket flight in 2027. The mission, backed by NASA, is expected to gather scientific data about the sun. The company, developing asteroid mining technology, is taking a different approach from traditional spacecraft that rely on ground-based flight controllers.

---

## Background: Ground-Based Constraints for Startups

When NASA sends spacecraft to explore the solar system, they build in redundancy and automation. But much of the work is done by teams of flight controllers communicating with the vehicle from Earth. For startups like AstroForge, such infrastructure is not readily available.

Building a global ground network with five dishes around the world would cost approximately $200 million plus ongoing operations costs. Instead, AstroForge chose to remove this dependency entirely through AI autonomy.

---

## Autonomy-1: Spacecraft Autonomy Stack Architecture

Autonomy-1 will carry a small, transformer-based AI model taking direct charge of the spacecraft. According to Armand Awad, AstroForge's head of flight software, the company decided to leverage advances in transformer models driven by frontier labs.

The AI stack includes:
- Hybrid configuration with traditional control systems
- Autonomous anomaly resolution capabilities
- Self-reliant position recovery when attitude is lost
- Correlation analysis of power anomalies and communication loss

CEO Markau Giacich explained: "Would that have been recoverable with all the data on the spacecraft? I don't know, but I can tell you nothing onboard tried it, and I would love something onboard to try if the spacecraft is unrecoverable."

---

## Lessons from the Odin Spacecraft

Founded in 2022 and backed with $56 million in venture funding, AstroForge has launched two prototype spacecraft. Both suffered anomalies that prevented them from achieving most of their missions.

The loss of the Odin spacecraft spurred the company to consider alternatives: Could they put sufficient intelligence onboard for the spacecraft to solve its own problems?

---

## Technical Challenges and Approach

Most spacecraft autonomy depends on traditional control algorithms due to concerns about the unreliability of neural networks. AstroForge, constrained by capital, chose full AI autonomy.

"I am not making general spacecraft autonomy or general autonomy for the world," Giacich said. "I am making constrained autonomy at a very low sensor input, following basic training patterns."

In theory, the agent controlling the spacecraft will perform tasks like anomaly resolution, realizing it has lost track of its position in space and correlating power anomalies to diagnostic data.

---

## Future Plans

AstroForge's third vehicle, DeepSpace-2, is set to launch alongside Intuitive Machines' third moon mission, expected to head to space by the end of 2026. Solo will be onboard, demonstrating new autonomous capabilities.

Autonomy-1 is scheduled for 2027 on Stoke Space's first rocket launch, carrying the historic mission of becoming the first truly autonomous spacecraft with NASA backing.
