# Apple Releases iOS 27 and macOS Golden Gate 27: Siri AI Takes Center Stage

Apple has released its annual major operating system updates — iOS 27, macOS 27 "Golden Gate," watchOS 27, visionOS 27, and tvOS 27 — with Siri AI as the headline feature across every platform except tvOS. The update marks the most significant overhaul of Apple's AI strategy since Apple Intelligence was first announced.

---

## Siri AI: A Complete Overhaul

The new Siri is powered by a large language model-based architecture that brings context-awareness and natural language understanding to a level well beyond the previous generation. Users can now ask Siri to explain what is on their screen, search across personal history (including old emails and text messages), and chain multiple commands into a single contextual session.

The assistant can also generate Shortcut automations and Safari extensions from a prompt — a capability previously requiring manual configuration. A dedicated Siri app has been added to iOS, resembling in structure the approach other AI assistants have taken as chat interfaces.

Apple's on-device AI models — collectively branded as the AFM family — drive these features. On-device processing is handled by AFM 3 Core, a 3-billion-parameter model, while more complex reasoning tasks route to larger models in Apple's Private Cloud Compute infrastructure.

---

## Liquid Glass: Visual Design Language Evolves

The most visible aesthetic change in iOS 27 and macOS Golden Gate is the expansion of the "Liquid Glass" design language first introduced in iOS 18. Users now have a slider to control the transparency and blur intensity of the Liquid Glass effect across the operating system — from near-opaque to highly translucent.

The Liquid Glass effect applies to system panels, app backgrounds, and notification banners, creating a layered depth effect that Apple describes as a "physical material that responds to light and motion." The feature is available across iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

---

## Apple Intelligence for Developers

Third-party developers gained access to Apple Intelligence capabilities through an updated App Intents framework announced at WWDC 2026. The framework allows apps to build workflows around photo context, natural language understanding, and task automation without shipping their own foundation models.

This has already produced a wave of AI-native app updates, including Daydream's visual shopping feature (see separate article), which uses Apple Intelligence to identify garments in user photos and surface matching products.
