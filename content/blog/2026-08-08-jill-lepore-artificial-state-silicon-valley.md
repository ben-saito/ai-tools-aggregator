# Jill Lepore's "Artificial State": Why Silicon Valley Leaders Are Bad Science Fiction Readers

Historian Jill Lepore has a theory about why tech companies often use soaring language to describe their products — almost as if they're forming a new government. And whether you're thinking of Twitter's old "town hall in your pocket" or Anthropic's Claude Constitution, it's a theory that doesn't paint Silicon Valley in a very flattering light.

---

## The Rise and Fall of the Artificial State

In Lepore's upcoming book, "The Rise and Fall of the Artificial State," the Pulitzer Prize-winning Harvard historian and New Yorker writer argues that tech companies are gradually taking on the functions of democratic government. In her view, these leaders often mistake technological advancement for political legitimacy.

The book examines how platforms like Twitter and Facebook have displaced local news, distorted political discourse, and accumulated power traditionally held by governments — without any of the accountability mechanisms that democratic institutions are supposed to provide.

---

## Tech Leaders as Fantasy Politicians

On the TechCrunch Equity podcast, Lepore discussed her book with Anthony Ha, arguing that today's AI leaders — who promise a future where AI makes government more efficient, more responsive, and maybe even unnecessary — are selling an old fantasy in new packaging.

The "artificial state" of the title refers both to the nation-state that tech companies are gradually displacing, and to the artificial intelligence they are building to manage it.

---

## Implications for AI Governance

Lepore's analysis is particularly relevant as AI systems increasingly make decisions previously made by governments: content moderation, credit allocation, criminal justice, and access to information. The book raises questions about who governs these systems and what accountability looks like when the providers are private companies with no democratic mandate.
