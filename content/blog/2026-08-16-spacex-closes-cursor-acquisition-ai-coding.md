# SpaceX Officially Closes Cursor Acquisition, AI Coding Startup Joins Musk's Portfolio

---

## Deal Finalized: Cursor Now Part of SpaceX

SpaceX has officially closed its acquisition of Cursor, the AI-powered coding startup, according to an announcement on the Cursor blog. The deal, which was announced in April as a partnership with an option to acquire, has now been completed, making Cursor a wholly owned subsidiary of Elon Musk's space and AI conglomerate.

Cursor had previously announced it would partner with SpaceX to develop technology together, leveraging SpaceX's massive computing infrastructure — including the GPU fleet the company has been renting to customers such as Anthropic and Google.

---

## Access to World's Largest GPU Fleet

In its announcement confirming the deal's closure, Cursor emphasized the strategic advantage of joining SpaceX's ecosystem. "SpaceX is building the computing capacity needed to scale intelligence far beyond what exists today," the company stated. "Cursor will be one place where that intelligence becomes useful."

The startup highlighted that becoming part of SpaceX gives it "access to the largest fleet of GPUs in the world" — a significant competitive advantage in the AI coding tool market, where compute availability directly impacts model training and inference capabilities.

---

## Context: A Busy Year for Musk's AI Holdings

The acquisition follows SpaceX's earlier purchase of xAI earlier this year and represents a continued consolidation of AI assets under the Musk umbrella. Cursor, known for its AI pair programming tool that integrates with development environments, will now operate as part of SpaceX's broader AI infrastructure strategy.
