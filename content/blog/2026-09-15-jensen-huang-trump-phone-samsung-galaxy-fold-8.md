# Jensen Huang Takes Live Call from Trump at All-In Conference, Uses Samsung Galaxy Fold 8

At the All-In Summit in Los Angeles on September 14, 2026, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang received an unexpected live phone call from former President Donald Trump during his on-stage appearance. The moment quickly became a talking point across tech and political circles — but Huang was also seen using a Samsung Galaxy Fold 8 to take the call, a detail that drew significant attention given Nvidia's deep ties to the AI chip industry.

---

## The Call and Its Context

Huang was mid-presentation at the All-In Summit, a technology investment conference known for featuring prominent venture capitalists and tech figures, when Trump dialed in live on stage. Huang answered on a foldable phone handed to him by an assistant, putting the call on speaker so the audience could hear.

Once connected, the conversation quickly turned to artificial intelligence. Trump and Huang appeared to align on the view that mounting public fears about AI constitute what Trump called a "hoax." Trump argued that the real winners of the AI panic are China and "political people," and that AI is too transformative — "bigger than the internet," in his words — to slow down over safety concerns.

"Did you hear that? Thousands of people are clapping," Huang told Trump during the exchange, seemingly confirming the audience's reception of the remarks.

---

## Nvidia's Position in the AI Chip Market

Nvidia's business model depends heavily on AI labs and cloud providers purchasing its GPU chips — primarily the H100 and newer Blackwell architecture accelerators — to train and run large language models. From that perspective, downplaying existential risk from advanced AI aligns with Nvidia's commercial interests. Nvidia shares remain up approximately 33% over the past year, though they dipped a few percentage points on the day of the conference.

Huang's remarks put him at odds with other prominent AI industry leaders. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has been among the most vocal advocates for slowing the pace of frontier AI development, arguing that the risks from sufficiently capable AI systems warrant a more cautious approach. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has also signaled some support for decelerating the rate of capability advances.

Huang's direct rejection of that framing — telling Trump and the audience that he would not support efforts to slow the industry — represents a clear departure from the "decelerationist" position gaining traction among some of his peers.

---

## A Correction on the Device Used

An earlier version of TechCrunch's report incorrectly identified the device Huang used to take Trump's call as Apple's iPhone Duo. The publication later corrected the record, clarifying that the device was in fact a Samsung Galaxy Fold 8. The error stemmed from the Fold 8's similar form factor and the speed of the initial reporting.

The Samsung Galaxy Fold 8 is Samsung's latest flagship foldable smartphone, featuring a large internal display that can function as a compact tablet when unfolded. Its use by Huang at a high-profile tech conference adds a layer of irony given the ongoing competition between Android device makers and Apple in the premium smartphone market.

---

## The Broader Debate Over AI Development Pace

The exchange between Huang and Trump highlights a deepening rift within the tech community over how fast AI should advance. On one side are researchers and executives who argue that the risks of advanced AI — including potential misuse, autonomous systems, and societal disruption — warrant a slower, more deliberate approach to development. On the other side are those who argue that deceleration would hand strategic advantages to geopolitical rivals, particularly China, and that the transformative potential of AI outweighs the risks of moving quickly.

Trump's framing of AI fear as a "hoax" driven by political motivations echoes a broader narrative within certain segments of the tech industry that views safety concerns as overstated or politically motivated. Huang's apparent agreement with this position, whether出于 genuine conviction or commercial instinct, underscores how the AI chip business creates a structural incentive to favor continued rapid expansion.

## Political Optics and the Tech-Venture Capital Nexus

The All-In Summit regularly features venture capitalists who are close Trump advisors, including Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, and David Sacks. The conference has become a venue where tech industry leaders engage directly with political figures on issues ranging from AI regulation to economic policy.

The fact that Trump chose to call into Huang's presentation — rather than attending in person or calling a different tech executive — reflects the symbolic importance of Nvidia in the current AI ecosystem. Nvidia's GPUs are the foundational infrastructure for virtually every major AI lab, making Huang a de facto representative of the AI industry in political conversations.
