# Survey: Americans who use AI every day are still worried about it

A new report finds that greater exposure to AI applications does not reduce public concern about the technology. Americans who use AI daily report similar or higher levels of unease compared to those with minimal AI experience, challenging the assumption that familiarity breeds acceptance.

---

## Key findings

The survey, conducted by a nonpartisan research organization, found that:

- **Daily AI users show persistent concern**: Americans who use AI-powered applications every day are no less worried about the technology than those who use it rarely or never
- **Regulation support crosses demographic lines**: Support for AI regulation is strong across age groups, political affiliations, and levels of AI exposure
- **Trust gap between users and developers**: While AI companies emphasize the benefits of their products, public trust has not increased proportionally to adoption rates
- **Concern about AI outpaces understanding**: Many respondents expressed worry about AI but could not articulate specific concerns beyond general unease

---

## The familiarity paradox

The findings present a puzzle for AI companies that have argued increased usage would demonstrate the technology's value and reduce skepticism. If anything, the data suggests the opposite: daily users may have more firsthand experience with AI failures, limitations, and unexpected outputs than occasional users.

One respondent described using AI writing tools daily while remaining uncertain about how the model generates its suggestions. Another frequent AI image generator user expressed concern about the rapid improvement in synthetic media quality. Direct experience with AI capabilities appears to inform concern as much as comfort.

---

## Policy implications

The continued public concern despite increased adoption has direct implications for AI policy. Lawmakers face a public that supports stronger oversight without a clear consensus on what form that oversight should take. The gap between general support for "more AI regulation" and specific policy proposals remains wide.

For AI companies, the findings suggest that透明度 and explainability initiatives may be as important for public relations as for technical alignment. If users cannot understand how AI systems make decisions, their concern is unlikely to diminish through exposure alone.

---

## Survey methodology

The survey sampled 2,400 American adults across urban, suburban, and rural areas. AI usage was measured by self-reported frequency of interaction with AI-powered products including chatbots, image generators, voice assistants, and recommendation systems. The margin of error is +/- 2.8 percentage points.
