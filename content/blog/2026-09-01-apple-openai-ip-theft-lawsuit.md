# Apple Alleges Former Employee Stole Trade Secrets for OpenAI

Apple has filed what it calls "shocking evidence" in its lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging that a former employee stolen confidential data after joining OpenAI. The case reveals new details about how one of Apple's own circuit schematics may have been used at OpenAI, and that evidence destruction allegedly occurred when the employee learned he was under investigation.

---

## Evidence Destruction Allegations

According to the filing, Chang Liu — who previously worked at Apple and now holds a position at OpenAI — handed over his Apple work laptop for investigation earlier this month. Apple's legal team claims this laptop contained evidence that Liu used a confidential Apple circuit schematic in his work at OpenAI, along with a tool sharing a name with an internal Apple engineering application.

Apple further alleges that Liu enlisted OpenAI colleague Yu-Ting Peng to help destroy evidence in June after learning Apple had opened an investigation into his conduct.

"The MacBook represents the very limited information Defendants provided so far (and only after weeks of delay), and shows Apple is not conducting 'fishing expeditions' but that its trade secrets are being used and evidence is being destroyed," the filing stated.

---

## OpenAI's Knowledge of theAlleged Theft

Central to Apple's case is the claim that OpenAI was "well-aware" of Liu's access to Apple data. The company argues that OpenAI's knowledge of the stolen materials makes the AI company complicit in the alleged trade secret misappropriation, not merely a beneficiary of an individual employee's actions.

The case is the latest in a series of high-profile intellectual property disputes involving leading AI companies, following Sony and Warner Music's lawsuit against Anthropic over similar allegations, and reflects growing tension over talent mobility between traditional tech giants and AI startups.

---

## Background on AI Industry Talent Practices

The lawsuit comes as the AI industry faces increased scrutiny over how it acquires talent and proprietary technology. Apple — which has historically been secretive about its AI research — is now actively pursuing legal action rather than allowing the matter to remain internal.

This case follows a broader pattern in the technology sector where companies use litigation not only to seek damages but to signal enforcement of non-compete and confidentiality agreements as AI talent shortages intensify.
