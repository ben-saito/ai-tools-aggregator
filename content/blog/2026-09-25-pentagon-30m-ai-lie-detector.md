# Pentagon Requests $30 Million for AI-Powered Lie Detection System

The Pentagon has requested $30 million in fiscal year 2027 funding to develop an artificial intelligence system capable of detecting deception during interrogation and security screening, according to MIT Technology Review reporting published September 25, 2026. The program represents a significant expansion of AI applications in military and intelligence contexts.

## Project Details

The system, being developed under the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA), uses machine learning models trained on physiological signals including facial micro-expressions, voice stress patterns, and thermal imaging of facial blood flow. Unlike traditional polygraph tests, the AI approach claims to identify deception markers without physical contact sensors, potentially enabling covert screening scenarios.

## Technical Approach

The underlying model was trained on a dataset of recorded interrogations and known ground truth — cases where investigators later confirmed whether subjects were being truthful based on independent evidence. The AI looks for subtle inconsistencies in behavioral patterns that human investigators might miss, according to briefing documents reviewed by MIT Technology Review.

The system is designed to assist, not replace, human interrogators. Pentagon officials emphasized that any deployment would require human review of AI-generated assessments and would be subject to legal review under existing interrogation and detention frameworks.

## Controversy

Polygraph-based lie detection has faced sustained scientific criticism. The American Psychological Association has noted that stress-based lie detection methods produce significant false positive rates, potentially incriminating truthful individuals. AI-based approaches may improve accuracy but could also amplify existing biases if training data skews toward particular demographic groups.

Civil liberties organizations have raised concerns about automated deception detection in military and security contexts, arguing that the technology could lower the evidentiary threshold for detention and interrogation decisions.

## Next Steps

The $30 million request covers two years of continued development and evaluation. If funded, the program would include controlled trials comparing AI detection rates against professional human interrogators and polygraph operators.
