# Judge orders OpenAI to turn over hiring records in Apple trade secrets case

---

A judge has ruled that Apple will gain expedited access to OpenAI's hiring materials in the trade secrets-focused lawsuit the iPhone maker filed against the AI company last month. The order requires OpenAI to produce internal recruitment and hiring documents related to the positions Apple claims were used to misappropriate its confidential information.

The lawsuit centers on Apple's allegations that OpenAI recruited former Apple employees with access to sensitive AI research and then used information from those hires to develop competing products. Apple specifically claims that OpenAI's language models were built in part using techniques pioneered at Apple under its former "Project Greymatter" initiative.

OpenAI has denied the allegations, calling them "a transparent attempt to slow down AI development" and arguing that its models were built independently using publicly available research and its own engineering effort. The company has filed motions to dismiss several claims.

The judge's order for expedited discovery suggests the court believes Apple has presented sufficient evidence to proceed with at least some of its claims. Hiring records could reveal communications between OpenAI recruiters and former Apple employees, potentially illuminating what specific knowledge those hires were expected to contribute.

The case is being watched closely as one of the first major trade secret disputes involving frontier AI models. The outcome could establish precedent for how courts handle claims that model capabilities themselves constitute protectable intellectual property.
