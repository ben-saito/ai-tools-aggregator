# Nvidia, 70% Growth Expected for 2027 — Jensen Huang

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang continues to project unstoppable momentum for the AI chipmaker, forecasting 70% revenue growth for fiscal year 2027 at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology conference.

---

## Nvidia's AI Dominance Extends Through 2027

Founder, CEO, and tireless Nvidia hype man Jensen Huang told attendees at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology conference on Thursday why his company's AI domination — and revenues — will continue its record-breaking growth streak through the end of next year.

"There's been endless hand-wringing over whether Nvidia's party will end as it faces increasing competition for GPUs and AI chips from all directions," Huang said. But the numbers tell a different story.

"Most people think Nvidia builds a chip. I mean, you need airplanes to ship what we build," Huang said, adding that the company continues to battle a supply shortage rather than a demand problem.

## Massive Orders and AI Ubiquity

He added that orders for just one product — a computer system that combines 36 Grace CPUs with 72 Blackwell GPUs — are currently experiencing 27% month-over-month growth. Huang didn't limit his bullish view to current sales.

"I think we could grow 70% year over year. We're confident about that," Huang said. Analysts expect the company to end its current fiscal year with approximately $130 billion in revenue.

## Why Huang Is Confident

Huang explained why he's confident: His company is so embedded in every area of AI that he believes he can see the future. "Nvidia runs every model. Every single lab can use us," the CEO said, mentioning that this includes models from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google.

The company's CUDA ecosystem, years of software optimization, and supply chain relationships create moats that competitors struggle to cross. While AMD, Intel, and custom silicon from Google and Amazon compete for specific workloads, Nvidia's universal appeal remains strong.

## Valuation Concerns Ease — For Now

Despite concerns about the company's P/E ratio and whether AI spending will sustain its pace, Huang's track record of accurate forecasting gives investors reason for optimism. Nvidia stock has rewarded holders handsomely, with shares up significantly over the past two years.

The 70% growth projection would represent an even more dramatic jump than the company's recent performance, suggesting Huang sees no end to enterprise and cloud AI spending in sight.
