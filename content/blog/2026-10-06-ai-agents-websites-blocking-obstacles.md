# AIエージェントのウェブサイトへのアクセス——次の障壁は「ボット対策」

---

Personal AI agents promise to shop, book flights, and make reservations on behalf of users—but deliberate blocks and anti-bot defenses are creating friction, leaving consumers caught in the middle. A new proposed standard aims to provide a framework for websites to safely allow agent access.

---

## The Bot Defense Problem

Websites deploy CAPTCHAs, rate limiting, and fingerprinting to block bots and scrapers. These defenses inadvertently block AI agents, which often mimic bot-like behavior to navigate at scale. The conflict is structural: the same properties that make agents useful (speed, scalability, automation) are the behaviors sites work to prevent.

Terms of service also present a legal gray area. Many platforms explicitly prohibit automated access, creating liability questions when agents act on a user's behalf. This leaves agent developers in a bind—user-delegated actions look like violations even when authorized.

---

## Toward a Protocol for Agent Access

A new standard proposed by industry groups would give websites a way to declare agent-friendly policies, similar to how robots.txt governs crawler access. Under this model, sites could explicitly permit certain agent behaviors, creating a consent layer between platforms and autonomous assistants.

The proposal reflects a broader recognition that as AI agents proliferate, the current web infrastructure lacks adequate primitives for agent-platform negotiation. Resolving this is critical: the value of personal agents scales directly with the breadth of actions they can perform, and that breadth depends on site cooperation.
