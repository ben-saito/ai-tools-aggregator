# Seattle Times and Newsday Sue OpenAI and Microsoft Over AI Training Data

Two more major American news organizations have filed lawsuits against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging that the companies used their journalism to train AI systems without permission or compensation. The Seattle Times and Newsday joined a growing list of publishers taking legal action against AI companies over copyright concerns.

---

## A Growing List of Plaintiffs

The lawsuit describes generative AI as "a snake eating its own tail" that could "destroy the very organizations" that produce the content it trains on. The filing states: "AI products like ChatGPT and CoPilot are touted as producers of content, but in fact they are rapacious consumers, devouring human-authored content and delivering back to the world copies and derivative imitations of that same original content they consumed to achieve their commercial objectives."

This lawsuit follows the landmark 2023 case where The New York Times sued OpenAI and Microsoft over similar copyright issues. Other publications have since filed their own suits as the case continues through the courts.

---

## A Notable Plaintiff

The Seattle Times lawsuit carries particular significance because Microsoft and OpenAI have previously funded some of the organization's journalism projects and fellowships. The defendants' prior financial relationship with the plaintiff adds an unusual dimension to the case.

Microsoft told GeekWire that the company is "surprised by the lawsuit" but expressed willingness to "sit down and explore solutions to this type of dispute."

---

## Industry-wide Implications

The lawsuit warns that without legal intervention, the journalism industry could become "broken beyond repair" due to AI's ability to consume and replicate published content at scale. As AI companies continue to build large language models on news archives and web content, publishers argue they deserve compensation for the use of their work.
