# Apple Tightens macOS Full Disk Access Controls as AI Agents Pose New Security Risks

Apple announced plans to tighten controls around macOS's Full Disk Access permission, citing growing risks from increasingly capable AI agents that can access users' files, messages, mail, and browsing history. The move comes after reports that AI applications—including ChatGPT's Mac app and Meta's Muse—could potentially access sensitive user data through the permission system.

---

## The Full Disk Access Problem

Full Disk Access is a macOS privacy feature that grants applications elevated permission to read system files, including sensitive data like messages, emails, and browsing history. Traditionally, this permission was designed for legitimate system utilities and professional software like backup tools and development environments.

AI agents have changed the calculus. When a user grants an AI agent access to their desktop, the agent may need to read files and interact with applications to complete tasks. Some developers have used Full Disk Access in ways that could expose everything on a user's system without their full knowledge or explicit consent, Apple said in a statement.

The timing coincides with reports that a flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app could have allowed hackers to access sensitive data through the Full Disk Access permission. Additionally, TechCrunch reported that Meta's Muse AI knew the content of a user's private messages even when the user only intended to share specific content.

---

## Apple's Response

Apple says it will add new controls to limit how apps can use Full Disk Access, particularly for AI applications. The company did not specify a timeline for the changes but indicated they would arrive in a future macOS update.

"We have seen apps using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk," Apple said in its announcement. "We are working to ensure that users have more granular control over what their applications can access."

The announcement signals a broader reckoning for the AI industry as desktop AI agents become more capable. Unlike cloud-based AI services where data processing happens on remote servers, desktop agents operate locally and often require deep system access to be useful.

---

## Industry Implications

The development reflects growing tensions between AI capability and user privacy. AI agents need broad system access to be useful—they must read files, send emails, and interact with applications. But each permission granted is also a potential attack surface.

Security researchers have noted that AI agents represent a new category of risk because they can take actions on behalf of users, not just read data. A compromised AI agent with Full Disk Access could theoretically read sensitive documents, intercept communications, and monitor user behavior.

For AI developers, Apple's tighter controls may force redesigns of how agents request and use system permissions. The era of broad, open-ended permissions may be ending as the industry matures.
