# Authors Push Back as Publishers and Agents Seek Share of Anthropic Settlement Payments

Some authors expecting their share of Anthropic's $1.5 billion copyright settlement received surprising emails this week -- notifications that someone else was making a claim on their payments. The dispute centers on how settlement funds are being allocated among authors, publishers, and literary agents.

---

## Background: The $1.5 Billion Settlement

Anthropic settled a major copyright class action last year, after a judge ruled that training AI models on copyrighted material is legal under fair use doctrine, but pirating that material is not. The deal received final court approval in July 2026, clearing the way for payments to proceed.

Under the settlement terms, authors of nearly 500,000 titles are entitled to $3,000 per pirated work. For books still in print with a traditional publisher, the payment is split 50-50 between author and publisher. For self-published works, or books where rights have reverted to the author, the full amount goes to the author.

---

## Publishers Claiming More Than Their Fair Share

Writers took to social media to report that publishers appear to be claiming payments beyond their entitled share. Mystery and thriller author April Henry posted that HarperCollins claimed a book on the Anthropic Settlement even though the rights had reverted to her at least 17 years ago -- and on the same day she received a credit alert saying the publisher had been added as her employer, which she said was never the case.

Victoria Strauss of the Writers Beware blog documented two categories of complaints: publishers seeking payment for works where rights have legitimately reverted, and publishers claiming the full 100% payment when they should only receive 50%.

Strauss noted that while some publishers have attributed these errors to poor recordkeeping and asked Anthropic to correct them, the pattern of errors suggests something more systemic.

"The fact that authors are reporting the exact same errors over and over suggests to me that these aren't routine glitches, but something much more widespread and systemic," Strauss wrote.

---

## Literary Agents Joining the Claims

Beyond publishers, authors reported that literary agents are also making claims on settlement payments -- despite not being rights holders in most cases. Author Courtney Milan was direct in her criticism on Bluesky, writing: "Apparently some agents are trying to claim percentages on the Anthropic settlement, and I do not REMOTELY think they should do this, what the fuck, stop that shit!"

Authors Guild CEO Mary Rasenberger told The New York Times she does not view this as a deliberate grab by publishers, instead attributing it to confusing settlement processes and bad record-keeping.

---

## How Authors Can Dispute

Authors can challenge their payment allocations, but face procedural hurdles. To claim 100% of a settlement payment, authors must prove that rights reversion occurred before August 10, 2022 -- the download date used in the settlement calculation.

The dispute highlights the complexity of untangling rights records across decades of publishing agreements, and raises questions about how Anthropic is administering the settlement process for what may ultimately prove to be a fraction of the originally filed claims.
