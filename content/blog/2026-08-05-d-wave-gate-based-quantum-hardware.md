# D-Wave Shows Off Gate-Based Quantum Hardware, Expanding Beyond Annealers

D-Wave, best known for its quantum annealers, is showcasing new gate-based quantum hardware. The company acquired a startup called Quantum Circuit Inc. (QCI) and is testing entanglement on dual-rail qubits built with fluxonium technology.

---

## Why it matters

D-Wave has long been the most visible player in quantum annealing, a specific approach to quantum computing that excels at optimization problems. The move into gate-based quantum computing — the kind that IBM, Google, and others are pursuing for general-purpose quantum algorithms — represents a significant expansion of D-Wave's ambitions.

The dual-rail qubit architecture uses two coupled resonators to encode a qubit, a design that differs from the transmon qubits common at IBM and Google. D-Wave claims this approach offers advantages in coherence time and fault tolerance.
