# OpenAI Parts Ways With Three Safety Researchers Amid Internal Investigation

OpenAI has terminated three safety researchers after an internal investigation found they mishandled sensitive company information, according to reports by The Wall Street Journal. The departures come amid heightened scrutiny of OpenAI's safety practices and just days after reports that executives had brushed aside employee warnings about safety procedures.

---

## What We Know

OpenAI confirmed in a statement that three individuals had "parted ways" with the company for violating policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information. The company did not name the researchers, their organization, or the specific information involved.

"We have parted ways with three individuals for violating our policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information," an OpenAI spokesperson said.

The company did not respond to questions about whether the researchers had raised safety concerns through internal channels before allegedly sharing information outside the organization.

---

## Context: Executive Warnings

The departures follow a New York Times report indicating that OpenAI executives had dismissed employee warnings about the company's safety practices. The timing suggests internal tensions over how OpenAI handles sensitive safety-related information.

OpenAI has faced ongoing scrutiny over its approach to safety governance. The company's structure—originally established as a nonprofit with a capped-profit arm—has evolved as it pursues commercial development of advanced AI systems. Critics have long argued that the commercial pressures conflict with the company's stated safety mission.

---

## Safety Governance Under Spotlight

The incident highlights the broader challenge of managing safety research within AI organizations. Safety teams must have access to information about system capabilities and limitations to do their jobs effectively. But that same information can be sensitive if disclosed publicly or shared with competitors.

AI safety researchers often face difficult questions about what information they can appropriately share, both internally and externally. The line between legitimate oversight and policy violation can be unclear, particularly in organizations where commercial interests and safety priorities are in tension.

The company did not specify whether the separated researchers had worked on front-of-mind safety concerns or more routine information handling issues.
