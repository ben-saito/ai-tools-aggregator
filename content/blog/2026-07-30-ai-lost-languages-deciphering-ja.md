# AI and the Deciphering of Lost Languages: Human Insight as the Key

While AI excels at pattern recognition, a recent case demonstrates that human intuition remains essential for deciphering lost languages. Ars Technica reports on a collaboration between an AI engineer and linguists that advanced the understanding of Linear A.

Linear A is classified as a language isolate with no confirmed connection to any known language. Similarly, Etruscan remains only partially understood despite centuries of study.

---

## AI as Research Assistant, Not Replacement

In June 2026, a self-taught AI engineer made a breakthrough on Linear A by starting from a single hypothesis: that one unknown word in a prayer inscription derived from a Semitic root meaning "to dwell" or "to inhabit." The engineer then used AI-built scripts to test this hypothesis across the Linear A corpus.

Crucially, the idea came from the human, not the AI. The AI functioned as a "research assistant" that rapidly verified the human hypothesis against thousands of characters - work that would have taken a human months to complete manually.

AI's real strength lies in large-scale pattern testing. It can verify whether a hypothesis about one sign holds across an entire archive in minutes, and identify repeated sequences that human eyes would miss.

---

## Cross-Lingual Transfer and Unknown Scripts

AI also employs a technique called "cross-lingual transfer," where a model trained on a known language can infer patterns in a related unknown one. This is analogous to how knowledge of Spanish helps with Portuguese.

Researchers have successfully applied this approach to scripts like Ugaritic, an extinct Semitic language of ancient Syria, where the language family was already known.

---

## Conclusion

For deciphering lost languages, the division of labor is clear: human intuition generates hypotheses while AI rapidly tests them at scale. AI does not replace the linguist - it supercharges their hunches.
