# Hertha Metals Founder Laureen Meroueh Takes on Steel Industry Decarbonization

A former MIT mechanical engineering PhD is tackling one of the most stubborn problems in industrial decarbonization: cleaning up steelmaking without driving up costs. Laureen Meroueh, 34, founded Hertha Metals to commercialize a new furnace design that simplifies the chemistry of steel production, potentially eliminating a significant portion of the industry's carbon footprint.

---

## The Steel Problem

The steel industry generates roughly 7% of global carbon emissions -- about as much as the fashion industry. Very little has changed about purifying iron ore since the process was invented and commercialized in the 1850s. The majority of steelmakers melt solid iron ore at extremely high temperatures inside blast furnaces, where material reacts with gases to remove oxygen. The process relies on coal and has proven difficult to decarbonize because profit margins are thin and capital requirements are enormous.

## Hertha's Approach

Meroueh's company developed a new furnace that simplifies the chemistry behind steel production. Rather than relying on the traditional blast furnace approach with its multi-step reactions and coal dependency, Hertha's system focuses on what can be fixed about the process in the near term -- targeting the electrochemical reactions directly rather than waiting for zero-carbon energy systems to become broadly available.

The company's pilot facility can produce one metric ton of steel per day. Rajesh Swaminathan, a partner at Khosla Ventures and an investor in Hertha, said the company's scale-up is "impressive," especially given how little the team has spent compared to competitors. "Competitors have created far less steel with $50 million or $100 million in funding," he noted.

## From MIT to Clean Steel

Meroueh's path to steel decarbonization runs through mechanical engineering and green hydrogen. At 12, she was accepted into a program to take college-level courses through Florida Atlantic University. Despite rigorous coursework, she spent hours surfing and sitting in trees, fostering an appreciation for nature and a desire to safeguard it.

After finishing her PhD in mechanical engineering at MIT, she led a green hydrogen startup before founding Hertha in 2022. The company's approach focuses on what it can fix about the steel industry now, rather than waiting for complete decarbonization of energy systems.

Iryna Zenyuk, director of the National Fuel Cell Research Center at UC Irvine, said of the potential: "There's huge value in reducing the size of this production system." The technology, if it scales, could provide a transitional path toward cleaner steel without requiring a complete overhaul of manufacturing infrastructure.
