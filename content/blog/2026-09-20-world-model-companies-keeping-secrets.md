# World Model Companies Are Keeping a Lot of Secrets

The world models space—anchored by AMI Labs (Yann LeCun) and World Labs (Fei-Fei Li)—is sitting on significant funding and buzz, but press for details on actual products and commercialization plans yields only vague non-answers. This was the finding from a TechCrunch analysis published September 20, 2026.

---

## A Panel at All In Reveals the Pattern

TechCrunch AI Editor Russell Brandom moderated a world models panel at the All In conference and came away with a consistent observation: nobody in the space wants to talk about what they are actually building. The major players—AMI Labs and World Labs—are both well-funded and generating substantial media attention, yet both rank "pretty low on the trying-to-make-money scale," Brandom writes.

When pressed on specific product plans, Michael Rabbat, co-founder and VP of World Models at AMI Labs, responded: "We'll talk about it when we're ready to talk about it." Over email, he clarified: "We're still in a research and building phase, so we're not talking publicly about any product plans or timeline."

World Labs' Marble platform represents what appears to be the most fully developed offering in the space. Its demos showcase media creation, building explorable environments for video games, and CGI effects. Robotics use cases are also demonstrated, but the overall impression is of a platform designed to showcase capabilities rather than ship products.

---

## Secrecy Extends to the Supply Chain

The opacity extends beyond the model companies themselves to their data suppliers. Alex de Vigan, CEO of Physicl—a data supplier for world model companies—told TechCrunch he knows his data has been useful for unnamed projects, but remains in the dark about specifics. "I wish they would tell us more. We could build more useful data if we knew what they were working on," de Vigan said.

This supplier-side secrecy is symptomatic of a broader strategic posture. World models are inherently versatile: the same modeling approach that helps a Waymo navigate traffic could equally power a humanoid robot handling warehouse logistics or convert video footage into explorable 3D environments. AMI Labs has already explored manufacturing, biomedicine, robotics, and AI-assisted medical software through its Nabia partnership.

---

## The Dark Forest Theory of AI Secrecy

Brandom draws a parallel to Cixin Liu's "dark forest" concept: in an environment where competitors can also fundraise easily, announcing a breakthrough risks alerting rivals. The logic is straightforward—as long as funding remains abundant, there is no pressure to commit to a specific vertical. And committing to one vertical means potentially losing the advantage of exploring others.

"If AMI announced tomorrow that they had built a humanoid robot or a next-generation Hollywood rendering system, a lot of other labs would suddenly be very interested in the space," Brandom writes. The competitive dynamics reward silence, at least for now.

---

## What World Models Actually Are

At its core, world modeling refers to systems that automate spatial intelligence—the kind of spatial reasoning that allows an autonomous vehicle to navigate complex environments or a robot to manipulate objects in physical space. The commercial applications span robotics, interactive media, and advanced driver-assistance systems.

Despite the secrecy, analysts note that the field is moving rapidly. The combination of large-scale visual data, advances in generative models, and improvements in simulation infrastructure has made it feasible to build systems that can predict and simulate physical environments with increasing fidelity.

The question of when—or whether—these systems will reach market in meaningful ways remains unanswered. For now, the companies involved appear content to let their demos speak for themselves.
