# Amazon Uses Twitch Streamers' Content for AI Training by Default

Amazon's Twitch streaming platform will now use creators' content to train generative AI models by default, requiring users to manually opt out if they don't want their streams used.

---

## "If This Was Opt-In, Nobody Would Opt In"

In a live stream addressing nearly 3,000 concerned users, Twitch Chief Product Officer Mike Minton explained the reasoning bluntly: "Well, there's an honest answer — If this was opt-in, nobody would opt in. That's honestly the answer."

The move has sparked immediate backlash from the Twitch community. Streamers are concerned they may be contributing their content to Amazon's AI training without realizing it, since opt-out is not the default.

When one user asked whether their past videos had already been used for training, Minton admitted: "I don't actually know the answer to that question because I don't know what Amazon has done in terms of model training and what they've used and not used."

---

## How to Opt Out

To opt out, users must navigate to their channel settings (not the creator dashboard), select the security and privacy tab, scroll to "training for generative AI," and toggle it off.

---

## Why This Matters

For Amazon, Twitch's archive represents thousands of hours of audio and video content — valuable training data for AI models. Twitch framed the change as "adding a setting that lets you opt out," rather than announcing a default shift toward AI training use.

Twitch's streamer community has historically been opposed to generative AI, given that most AI products are trained on scraped web content without creator consent.
