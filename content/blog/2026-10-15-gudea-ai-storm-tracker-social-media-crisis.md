# Gudea's AI "Storm Tracker" Aims to Predict Social Media Crises Before They Erupt

---

Every day, conversations unfold across social media and websites around the world. A seemingly insignificant post—whether it involves AI-generated misinformation, hate speech, or a damaging rumor—can quickly snowball into a much larger public controversy. Gudea, a startup that describes itself as the "storm tracker for the internet," is using AI to help organizations spot trouble brewing online before it turns into a crisis.

## Beyond Traditional Social Listening

Gudea pitched its mission at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, positioning itself as a fundamentally different approach to brand and reputation monitoring. Traditional social listening platforms track metrics like mentions, hashtags, and impressions. Gudea's AI analyzes behavior, relationships, and the pathways through which information travels across more than 500 mainstream and fringe websites and platforms, including X, Reddit, and TikTok.

The distinction matters because metrics like "number of mentions" don't capture whether a conversation is building toward a crisis or merely generating noise. "A lot of the chief communications officers and chief media or marketing officers, they're using legacy tools to try to figure out what's happening," CEO Keith Presley said. "Those tools were only ever designed to tell you the number of hashtags or number of impressions."

## The Intelligence Community Heritage

Gudea's approach has roots in national security intelligence work. The team began with the George Washington Initiative, a nonprofit established after the 2020 election, which adapted strategies and tactics that the intelligence community has used overseas to disrupt online networks tied to groups like Al-Qaeda and ISIS. They applied those methods to domestic information campaigns.

The nonprofit phase revealed a gap: existing tools didn't show how information was actually moving across people, groups, and platforms. "That gap became the opening for Gudea," Presley said.

## AI and the Disinformation Scale Problem

The rise of generative AI has dramatically worsened the problem Gudea is tackling. "When we first started in 2021, and then as a company in 2023, we maybe saw one out of four things we looked at have inauthentic activity around it," Presley said. "Now it's probably nine out of ten things that we look at have this inauthentic activity."

AI tools have lowered the cost and effort required to generate content and coordinate online campaigns to the point where sophisticated influence operations no longer require a large organization—effectively, one person with a laptop can now execute campaigns that previously required teams.

## The Client Portfolio

Three years after founding, Gudea has nearly 40 clients, including a Fortune 50 telecommunications company, a Fortune 50 retailer, "one of the world's largest AI labs," a major band, public relations agencies, and government agencies. The presence of an AI lab as a client suggests Gudea's technology is relevant to companies concerned about AI-generated misinformation about themselves—a growing concern as synthetic media capabilities advance.

## What's Next: Geolocation for Narratives

Gudea plans to expand its geolocation capabilities for narratives, targeting a launch next year. The technology would allow users to identify where conversations are originating with far greater precision—down to the city-block level. The company also wants to give customers API access to run advertising campaigns through Gudea's AI model before launching them, predicting potential negative outcomes before they occur.

Gudea recently announced $7 million in seed funding co-led by Mudita Venture Partners and Silicon Road Ventures, bringing total funding to approximately $15 million.
