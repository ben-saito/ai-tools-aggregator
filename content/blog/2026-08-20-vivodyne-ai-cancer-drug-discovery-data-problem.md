# Vivodyne: Why AI Isn't Close to Curing Cancer

A biotech startup called Vivodyne says the AI drug-discovery industry has a data problem — and that claims about AI curing cancer are overblown until the data issue is solved. The company opened what it calls the world's largest "human data center" near San Francisco last week, aiming to generate the causal biological data that today's AI models are missing.

---

## The Data Problem in AI Drug Discovery

AI drug-discovery companies have made bold claims about curing cancer, but the reality is that current models lack the data to capture the complexity of human biology. Existing models are trained on data from animal testing, single-cell studies, or protein structures — not living human tissue.

"Absent human testing, what are these [AI] models going to do?" asks Andrei Georgescu, Vivodyne's CEO and co-founder. "They're going to cure cancer in mice."

Even Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei wrote recently that claims AI will cure cancer have become "more cliche than credible." The pharmaceutical industry faces a 90% failure rate: drugs that work in animal testing often fail in human clinical trials.

---

## Vivodyne's Approach: HIVE Labs

Vivodyne was spun out of the University of Pennsylvania in 2021. The company's HIVE (Human In-Vitro Experimentation) modular robotic labs can grow 20 kinds of human tissue, then autonomously dose and monitor them, generating causal biological data at scale.

The company claims its liver cells have 94% predictive accuracy compared to human trials, its airway tissue matches real human tissue 96% of the time, and its bone marrow has achieved 100% concordance in tests of 20 different chemotherapy drugs.

Georgescu sees autonomous biology labs as key to generating data that can train AI models to understand human biology — a prerequisite for meaningful progress in healthcare AI.
