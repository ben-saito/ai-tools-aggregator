# OpenAI Acquires Smartphone Camera Maker Glass Imaging for Over $300 Million

OpenAI has purchased Glass Imaging, a smartphone camera technology company founded by former Apple engineers who previously led the development of Portrait Mode, according to a Wall Street Journal report. The acquisition valued at over $300 million, comes as OpenAI continues its push into hardware, with rumors swirling about the company's plans for AI-native devices.

---

## Former Apple Engineers Behind Portrait Mode

Glass Imaging was founded in 2019 by Ziv Attar and Tom Bishop, both ex-Apple engineers who directed the team that created Apple's Portrait Mode photography feature. That background directly informs the company's approach to computational photography and image signal processing.

The company's Los Altos-based team will bring specialized expertise in optics and camera software — areas where OpenAI currently lacks in-house capability. For a company known primarily for language models, acquiring veteran mobile imaging engineers represents a concrete step toward building AI hardware.

---

## OpenAI's Hardware Ambitions

The acquisition aligns with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's stated interest in consumer AI devices. In 2025, Altman and former Apple design chief Jony Ive revealed a joint startup called io, which OpenAI subsequently acquired for $6.5 billion. That deal brought Ive's industrial design team into the OpenAI ecosystem.

The Glass Imaging purchase suggests OpenAI is pursuing a two-track hardware strategy: premium devices through the Ive partnership, and underlying imaging technology through targeted acquisitions of specialized teams.

---

## Strategic Rationale

Smartphone cameras have become the primary interface through which users capture and share visual information. For OpenAI, controlling camera technology could enable novel AI features impossible to achieve through software-only integration — real-time visual reasoning, AI-powered computational photography, and integrated multimodal interactions that current smartphone platforms cannot support.

The $300 million price tag positions this as a strategic talent and technology acquisition rather than a market-play move, similar to how Google acquired HTC's Pixel team in 2017 to build its own phone hardware.
