# Anthropic Wins First Court Victory Against Pentagon Supply-Chain Risk Designation

A federal judge in California ruled on Thursday that the Trump administration's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was illegal. U.S. District Judge Rita Lin said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's labeling of Anthropic as a risk to national security signified "unlawful retaliation" in violation of the First Amendment, and that the decision was "arbitrary and capricious." Lin also found Anthropic was denied due process as required under the Fifth Amendment.

---

## Background: The Pentagon's Supply-Chain Risk Label

Earlier this year, Hegseth and President Trump labeled Anthropic a supply-chain risk, a designation that carried implications for the company's ability to secure government contracts and partner with federal agencies. Anthropic challenged the designation in court, arguing it was politically motivated and lacked factual basis.

This is Anthropic's second lawsuit against the Pentagon. The company's first victory in this case marks a significant legal win as its litigation continues in Washington.

---

## Court Ruling Details

Judge Lin's ruling specifically cited the First Amendment's protection against government retaliation for political speech, finding that the administration's actions appeared designed to punish Anthropic for positions it had taken on AI safety and policy. The ruling also noted that the due process requirements of the Fifth Amendment were not met, as Anthropic was not given adequate opportunity to respond to the allegations before the designation was made.

The decision represents a rare successful challenge to a government's supply-chain security designation and sets a precedent for how AI companies can contest similar actions in the future.

---

## Industry Implications

The case highlights the growing legal and political tensions between AI companies and government agencies over the classification of AI systems as potential national security risks. As AI capabilities advance, the question of how to appropriately regulate and designate AI companies in the supply chain remains unsettled.
