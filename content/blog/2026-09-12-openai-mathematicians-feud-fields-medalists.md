# OpenAI and Mathematicians feud escalates as 25 Fields Medalists sign open letter

Twenty-five leading mathematicians, all Fields Medal recipients, have signed an open letter accusing AI labs of threatening mathematical research culture. The dispute centers on how AI companies handle breakthrough proofs and whether proper attribution is being given to human mathematicians.

The conflict intensified this week when NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster accused OpenAI of pressuring him not to credit a collaborator employed by Anthropic for work on an important mathematical problem. Buckmaster also raised concerns that OpenAI may have used their collaborative research with Codex to generate its own competing proof.

OpenAI further escalated tensions by withdrawing its sponsorship of a mathematics event at CalTech following criticism from researchers at the university.

The mathematicians' letter argues that while AI models solving outstanding mathematical problems could benefit humanity, this is only true if the solutions can be understood and verified by the mathematical community. The signatories warn that rushed announcements leave no time for proper documentation, isolation of new methods, or citation of prior work.

"Often these solutions are announced in a rush, leaving no time for a proper writeup, the isolation of new methods and ideas, and citing relevant previous work of others," the letter states. OpenAI's proof remains unverified, they note.

The concerns extend beyond individual attribution. Mathematicians are increasingly worried that their use of AI coding tools like Codex may be feeding directly into training data for competing models, creating a dynamic that incentivizes secrecy. If frontier labs can spend tens of millions of dollars using LLMs to beat original researchers to discoveries, the culture of open mathematical research faces existential threat.

This letter follows the Leiden Declaration, released in June by a working group grappling with how LLM-generated proofs will change mathematical practice.

## Key Points

- **25 Fields Medalists** signed the letter, representing the highest echelon of mathematical achievement
- **NYU professor** Tristan Buckmaster accused OpenAI of pressuring him to not credit an Anthropic collaborator
- **OpenAI withdrew** from a CalTech mathematics event after criticism from researchers
- **Unverified proof** remains a flashpoint -- OpenAI's solution to a major problem has not been independently confirmed
- **Cultural threat** -- mathematicians fear the open research culture that trains future generations is at risk

---

## Broader Context

Mathematics has historically valued the intellectual super-structure that nourishes students, finds new questions, and integrates discoveries into broader human understanding. The mathematicians argue that AI-conceived ideas cannot become "fully alive" without human mathematicians to develop, communicate, and teach them.

The dispute reflects a larger tension in AI development: the gap between what is technically possible and what is academically responsible. As one signatory noted, the value in mathematics is not just the proofs and who gets credit, but the crucial human transmission chain between generations of researchers.
