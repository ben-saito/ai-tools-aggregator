# White House Rebrands AI as "Super Intelligence" in New Executive Order

The Trump administration signed an executive order officially redesignating artificial intelligence as "super intelligence," a move accompanied by a gathering of major tech CEOs who signed what the President called a "morally binding" AI safety pledge. The announcements mark a significant shift in how the US government frames artificial intelligence policy.

---

## The Executive Order

The executive order formally adopts the term "super intelligence" to describe advanced AI systems, replacing the more commonly used "artificial intelligence" in official government communications. The order reflects growing concern among policymakers that existing terminology fails to capture the potential risks of increasingly capable AI systems.

The document outlines principles for ensuring that advanced AI development proceeds safely while maintaining American leadership in AI research and deployment. Specific regulatory mechanisms remain to be defined through subsequent rulemaking.

---

## The CEO Summit

The White House convened leaders from major technology companies including Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Elon Musk, and Anthropic's Dario Amodei for the announcement. The assembled executives signed a voluntary safety pledge committing their companies to responsible AI development practices.

Trump described the pledge as "morally binding," though the commitment lacks formal legal enforcement mechanisms. The voluntary nature of the accord has prompted questions about its effectiveness in practice, particularly given competitive pressures in the AI industry.

---

## Industry Reactions

The rebranding initiative has received mixed reactions from the AI research community. Supporters argue that "super intelligence" better communicates the transformative and potentially existential nature of advanced AI systems to policymakers and the public. Critics suggest the terminology shift risks confusing technical discussions and could complicate international regulatory coordination.

The distinction between "AI" and "super intelligence" remains undefined in technical terms, with the executive order focusing on aspirational rather than definitional criteria.

---

## What Comes Next

Implementation of the executive order will require sustained interagency coordination across commerce, defense, and research agencies. Congressional action would be needed to create binding regulatory authorities, meaning the practical impact of the policy shift depends heavily on future legislative developments.
