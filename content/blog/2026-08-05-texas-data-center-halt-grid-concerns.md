# Texas Governor Halts New Data Centers Amid Grid Overwhelm

Texas Governor Greg Abbott announced Monday that all new data center projects will require audits by the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) and the state's grid operator, ERCOT, as the interconnection queue reaches unprecedented levels.

---

## Grid Queue Grows Fivefold in Six Months

In January, ERCOT had 233 gigawatts of projects waiting to connect to its grid. That figure has more than doubled to 474 gigawatts today, with approximately 90% of those requests coming from data center developers, according to the grid operator.

The scale of proposed development far exceeds Texas's current electricity needs. The interconnection queue today represents more than five times ERCOT's total peak demand, raising concerns about grid stability and the state's ability to maintain reliable power supply.

Tech companies have increasingly turned to Texas for data center construction, attracted by loose regulations and abundant power supply. Only Virginia hosts more data centers than Texas. Major operators including Google and Microsoft have established significant operations in the state, drawn by ample natural gas reserves and growing renewable energy capacity.

---

## AI Compute Demand Drives Surge

The rapid expansion reflects growing AI compute demand, with companies securing land and grid connections ahead of actual construction. Many developers file interconnection requests as a first step, creating a queue where many projects may ultimately fizzle out before reaching completion.

While Texas has historically maintained adequate electricity supply through natural gas, wind, and solar resources, the pace of data center growth threatens to outstrip the grid's capacity to absorb new load without significant infrastructure investment.

Abbott's executive action marks a rare instance of regulatory intervention in Texas's traditionally hands-off approach to data center development, signaling growing concern about the state's ability to manage explosive demand for AI infrastructure.

---

## Audit Requirements and Timeline

Under the new directive, all proposed data center projects must undergo review by PUCT and ERCOT before advancing. The audits will assess grid impact, infrastructure readiness, and alignment with state energy policy objectives.

The announcement sent ripples through the commercial real estate and technology sectors, where Texas has become a focal point for data center investment. Analysts expect the audit requirement to delay some projects while others may be reconsidered entirely.
