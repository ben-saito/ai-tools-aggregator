# OpenAI and mathematicians feud escalates as 25 Fields Medalists sign open letter

Twenty-five leading mathematicians signed an open letter arguing that AI labs are threatening their intellectual work as labs race to one-up each other with solutions to famous math problems.

---

## The open letter and its signatories

Each signatory has been awarded the Fields Medal, considered the most prestigious prize in mathematics. The letter argues that while AI models' ability to solve the world's outstanding mathematical challenges could be a boon to humanity, that will only be the case if solutions can be understood and communicated by the math community and, ultimately, the rest of the world. The signatories specifically criticized the pace at which AI labs announce results "often in a rush, leaving no time for a proper writeup."

---

## NYU professor accuses OpenAI of pressuring collaborator

NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster accused OpenAI of pressuring him not to credit a collaborator who works for Anthropic for solving an important math problem. Buckmaster wondered if the company had used their work with Codex to produce its own groundbreaking proof over a marathon weekend of inference.

OpenAI has not publicly responded to the specific accusation, but the company pointed to its history of publishing research and collaborating with academic mathematicians.

---

## OpenAI withdraws sponsorship amid criticism

On Thursday, OpenAI withdrew its sponsorship of a math event at CalTech after the company was criticized by researchers at the university. The withdrawal came as tensions between the AI lab and the academic mathematics community continued to escalate.

---

## Industry context: AI and mathematics

AI labs have increasingly positioned mathematical reasoning as a key benchmark for frontier models. OpenAI's Codex, Anthropic's Claude, and Google's Gemini have all demonstrated capabilities in formal mathematics. The competition to claim "first" on famous mathematical problems has intensified, with academic mathematicians arguing that the rush to announce results undermines the careful verification process that has traditionally governed the field.

The dispute highlights a broader tension between the speed of AI development and the slower pace of academic peer review and verification.
