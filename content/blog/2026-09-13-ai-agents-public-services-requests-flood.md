# AI Agents Flooding Public Services With Requests

Government agencies are reporting a surge in requests powered by AI agents, raising questions about access equity and the future of public-facing bureaucracy.

---

## The Agent Revolution Hits Government

The vast majority of cases we find are people who are entitled to claim for something, claiming for that thing — but they're using AI agents to do it faster. That's according to researchers studying the impact of AI on public services, who found that AI-assisted benefit claims are rising sharply.

The phenomenon represents both an opportunity and a challenge for government services designed around human-scale request volumes.

## Efficiency Gains vs. Fairness

AI agents can file benefits claims, appeal denied applications, and navigate bureaucratic processes at speeds impossible for human users. For individuals entitled to services but frustrated by complex application processes, this can be genuinely helpful.

However, researchers worry about an arms race dynamic: as some claimants use AI agents, agencies may need to deploy their own AI systems to process the increased volume and detect fraudulent claims. This could disadvantage less sophisticated users who lack access to AI tools.

## Case Study: Benefits Applications

In pilot programs where AI agents were allowed to assist with unemployment benefits applications, approval rates increased and processing times decreased. But agencies also reported difficulty distinguishing between legitimate AI-assisted claims and attempts to game the system.

Some jurisdictions are now debating whether to require disclosure when an AI agent files a claim on someone's behalf. Others are exploring limits on the number of automated filings permitted per user.

## Implications for Digital Equity

The rise of AI agents in public services highlights a broader concern about technology access. Those with AI tools get faster, more competent navigation of bureaucracy — potentially widening outcomes gaps between tech-savvy users and those without such access.
