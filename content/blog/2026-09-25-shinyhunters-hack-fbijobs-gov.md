# ShinyHunters Claims FBIJobs.gov Hack, Demands Removal of "Exaggerated" FBI Advisory

A threat actor known as ShinyHunters has claimed responsibility for a breach of FBIJobs.gov, stealing approximately two to three terabytes of data including names of current and former FBI agents and applicants. The group is demanding that the FBI remove or edit an advisory that described ShinyHunters as sometimes using "exaggerated claims" to extract payments.

---

## The Breach and the Demand

On Tuesday, ShinyHunters took down the agency site and posted a banner on the homepage reading "THIS SITE HAS BEEN SEIZD BY SHINYHUNTERS." The group told The New York Times that none of the stolen data has been leaked yet, but it included "names of current and former agents as well as applicants."

The motive was not extortion, ShinyHunters claimed. Instead, the strike was meant to force the FBI to either remove or edit a May advisory warning about ShinyHunters that the group found insulting.

In a message posted on the dark web reviewed by Ars, ShinyHunters said it was "severely offended" that the FBI alleged the group sometimes uses "exaggerated claims" to extract payments from victims. ShinyHunters was also upset that the FBI claimed the group conducts swatting attacks against corporate workers and makes sextortion threats. That "never" happens, ShinyHunters said.

To get the advisory changed, ShinyHunters gave FBI Director Kash Patel and the Assistant Director of the FBI Cyber Division, Brett Leatherman, one week to comply with demands.

---

## How the Breach Occurred

Not many details have been released on how ShinyHunters gained access to the data. ShinyHunters told the NYT only that "it had weaponized a zero-day, or previously undiscovered computer bug within the system."
