# Elon Musk Spends Half His Time Talking Robots and AI on Tesla Earnings Calls

A new analysis of seven years of Tesla earnings calls reveals that Elon Musk has progressively shifted his attention away from Tesla's core automotive business toward AI and robotics — even as the company continues to ship nearly 500,000 cars per quarter and derives 70% of its revenue from vehicle sales.

---

## The Shift: From Cars to AI

The analysis, conducted by TechCrunch in partnership with Hudson Labs, a financial research firm, sourced transcripts of Tesla's quarterly earnings calls dating back to 2019 from S&P Market Intelligence. The firms used Hudson Labs' Co-Analyst AI tool to map what Musk and other Tesla executives discussed across more than seven years of calls.

The findings show a measurable decline in discussion of traditional automotive topics — manufacturing scale, vehicle sales, supply chain — alongside a corresponding rise in focus on AI projects including the Optimus humanoid robot and fully autonomous robotaxi ambitions.

---

## The Message vs. The Numbers

Musk has spent years publicly positioning Tesla as "really an AI and robotics company" rather than a carmaker. The analysis provides quantitative backing for that claim: despite Tesla's automotive business generating tens of billions in quarterly revenue, Musk's verbal focus on earnings calls has increasingly migrated toward long-horizon AI projects.

The disconnect between Tesla's revenue composition (predominantly automotive) and Musk's stated strategic priorities (AI and robotics) has been a recurring point of scrutiny from investors and analysts.

---

## Implications for Tesla Investors

For shareholders, the analysis raises questions about executive attention allocation. Tesla's automotive business remains highly profitable, but if Musk's focus is genuinely shifting toward unproven robotics and autonomy ventures, investors are effectively gaining exposure to a venture capital-like profile while holding what is priced as a high-growth automotive stock.
