# Lightspeed targets $250M for new India fund, focusing on early-stage AI

The Silicon Valley venture capital firm Lightspeed is targeting $250 million for a new India-focused fund, with an emphasis on early-stage artificial intelligence companies, according to a TechCrunch report published September 24, 2026.

---

## A New Approach to India Investing

For the first time, Lightspeed is aligning its India fundraising cycle with its global fund timeline. The firm is also shifting toward a shorter investment period, a strategic pivot that reflects the accelerating pace of AI deal flow in the region.

The new fund signals Lightspeed's renewed commitment to the Indian market at a time when early-stage AI startups are attracting significant attention from global investors. The firm's India portfolio has historically covered consumer technology, enterprise software, and financial services — categories now being reinvented through AI-native products.

---

## Why India, Why Now

India's startup ecosystem has matured considerably over the past several years, producing globally competitive companies across fintech, healthtech, and infrastructure. The emergence of large language models and AI-native development tools has lowered the barrier for building sophisticated AI applications, creating opportunities for early-stage founders in sectors from enterprise automation to agricultural technology.

Lightspeed's India team has been active in evaluating AI opportunities across the spectrum, with early-stage deals attracting particular interest given the availability of talent and the scalability of infrastructure across the country.

---

## Implications for the AI Startup Landscape

The size of the fund — $250 million — positions Lightspeed to make meaningful early-stage investments while maintaining the ability to follow on in later rounds. For founders building AI companies in India, the new fund represents another credible late-seed and Series A option beyond traditional US-based investors.

The alignment of the India fund with Lightspeed's global cycle also suggests the firm is treating India not as a regional outpost but as an integral part of its worldwide investment strategy.
