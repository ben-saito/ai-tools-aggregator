# NASA Swift Observatory Rescue Mission Called Off

NASA has called off the mission to rescue the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory, a gamma-ray telescope that was facing an uncontrolled reentry into Earth's atmosphere later this year. Katalyst, the startup contracted to build and launch the rescue satellite named Link, announced on August 20, 2026, that "ongoing attitude control issues" would prevent the rescue from succeeding.

The Link satellite launched on July 3, 2026, carrying three robotic arms designed to capture Swift and boost it into a higher, safer orbit. The spacecraft is approximately the size of a refrigerator and uses xenon-fueled electric thrusters for propulsion.

Despite the mission failure, Katalyst stated it would attempt to extract what technical value remains from the spacecraft, potentially demonstrating the satellite's close-in navigation capabilities near Swift before the observatory's eventual reentry.

NASA awarded Katalyst a $30 million contract less than a year ago, with the tight timeline of launching before summer 2026 to reach Swift before atmospheric reentry. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman acknowledged the setback, stating "NASA should be willing to move quickly and take smart risks when the potential return is worth it, and that is exactly what we did with this mission."

The Swift Observatory has operated since 2004, making important contributions to gamma-ray burst research. Without rescue, the spacecraft is expected to reenter and burn up in Earth's atmosphere later this year, ending its 22-year mission.
