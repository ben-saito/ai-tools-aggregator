# World Model Companies Are Keeping a Lot of Secrets — TechCrunch Reports on the Opaque AI Race

---

## The Promise and the Mystery

World models — AI systems designed to understand and simulate physical environments, enabling applications from robotics to autonomous vehicles — represent one of the most competitive frontiers in artificial intelligence. Companies like World Labs, Mesheur, and numerous well-funded startups are racing to build systems that can navigate, manipulate, and predict across physical space. But as the business and technical press cover launches and funding rounds, the actual state of the technology remains remarkably opaque.

TechCrunch's Russell Brandom reported on September 20, 2026, that when he began pressing companies on where world model technology would actually be commercialized, the responses became conspicuously vague. The article, titled "World model companies are keeping a lot of secrets," documents a pattern of deliberate obfuscation across the sector.

---

## Data Suppliers Are Also Tight-Lipped

The secrecy extends beyond the model builders themselves to their data suppliers. Alex de Vigan, CEO of Physicl — a company that provides training data for world model companies — declined to specify which firms he works with or what those partnerships entail. "The closest thing I found to an authority was Michael Rabbat, a co-founder of AMI Labs," Brandom notes, "but AMI is less than a year old, so it's fair enough to keep quiet."

World Labs' Marble product was described as "probably the most fully developed" world model publicly known, yet even that system is largely black-boxed in terms of architecture, training data, and benchmarks.

---

## Why the Secrecy? Versatility as a Shield

Part of the mystery comes from the inherent versatility of world model technology. At its simplest, a world model is a navigable map of the world — similar to systems that already power self-driving cars. But the same underlying technology could be applied to robotics, interactive video generation, scientific simulation, or defense systems. No one doubts there are viable businesses to be built, and as long as fundraising remains easy, there's no pressure to narrow the focus.

This creates a situation where investors, partners, and even potential customers cannot easily evaluate competing claims. Companies can position themselves as leaders in an unspecified race without revealing benchmarks, deployments, or verified capabilities.

---

## Implications for the AI Industry

The world model secrecy pattern reflects a broader trend in frontier AI: companies increasingly treat internal capabilities as competitive moats rather than open research contributions. While OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind publish papers and release model specifications, the world model sector operates closer to traditional defense-tech confidentiality norms.

For developers and investors trying to assess the landscape, the practical consequence is reliance on secondary signals — funding announcements, job postings, and partner statements — rather than verifiable technical claims.
