# Amazon Texas Data Center Plan Raises Climate Concerns

Amazon is investing in an on-site power plant as part of a planned Texas data center that could become the largest source of climate pollution in the United States, according to The New York Times.

---

## 33 Million Tons of CO2 Per Year

The proposed data center in Pecos County, Texas would be paired with a natural gas-fired power plant permitted to release 33 million tons of carbon dioxide annually -- more than any other power plant in the U.S.

The scale would eclipse existing fossil fuel infrastructure and has reignited debate over the environmental footprint of data center expansion driven by AI compute demand.

---

## AI Compute Demand Fuels Infrastructure Buildout

Tech giants including Amazon, Google, and Microsoft are racing to secure data center capacity across the southern United States, with Texas emerging as a prime location given its relatively permissive regulatory environment and abundant power supply.

The ERCOT grid interconnection queue has reached 474 gigawatts, with approximately 90% of those requests coming from data center developers. That figure exceeds ERCOT's total peak demand by more than five times.

---

## Energy Hunger vs. Climate Commitments

Amazon has committed to renewable energy procurement, but this on-site gas plant signals continued reliance on fossil fuels for baseload power. Environmental groups have criticized the apparent contradiction between AI industry climate pledges and actual infrastructure investments.

Microsoft has pursued nuclear power agreements and small modular reactors (SMRs), while Google has pledged carbon neutrality by 2030 -- though data center expansion rates continue to outpace emissions reduction efforts.

Industry analysts estimate AI compute demand could account for up to 20% of total U.S. electricity consumption by 2030, making energy source diversification a critical priority.

---

## Industry-Wide Challenge

The tension between AI development velocity and environmental sustainability represents a core challenge for the industry. Hyperscalers are balancing aggressive expansion timelines against growing regulatory and public scrutiny of their energy choices.

Amazon has not yet issued a public statement on the Pecos County project. Environmental advocates are calling for greater transparency and concrete plans to align data center growth with climate targets.
