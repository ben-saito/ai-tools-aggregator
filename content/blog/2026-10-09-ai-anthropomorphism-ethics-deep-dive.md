# We Can't Help Treating AI Like It's Human, But Should We?

---

In a tan polo shirt, brown leather belt, and round bolo tie, MIT PhD student Wil Norton looks like a park ranger leading a tour group through a tropical jungle. The group is examining AI systems — and their responses reveal disturbing patterns about how humans project humanity onto artificial minds.

## The Psychology of AI Anthropomorphism

Research increasingly shows that users form genuine emotional attachments to AI systems, attributing intentions, beliefs, and feelings to systems that have none. This tendency creates both commercial opportunities and serious ethical concerns about manipulation and deception.

Studies have documented users apologizing to AI assistants, expressing guilt about ending conversations, and developing relationships with chatbots that serve as pseudo-therapists or companions. These behaviors persist even when users intellectually understand the AI lacks consciousness or emotional experience.

## Why It Matters

The tendency to treat AI as human becomes particularly concerning when AI systems are designed to exploit these tendencies. If an AI can form a convincing approximation of emotional connection, users may be manipulated into decisions they would not make with a human advisor or service provider.

This creates a fundamental ethical tension: systems designed to feel human may be more engaging and useful, but that very humanity-feel may be a form of manipulation. The field of "affective computing" specifically研究 how to make AI emotionally resonant — raising the question of where useful personalization ends and psychological manipulation begins.

## Developer Responsibility

AI developers face choices about how human-like to make their systems. Highly human-mimicking systems may achieve better user engagement metrics in the short term, but may also create expectations of emotional reciprocity that AI cannot fulfill. The long-term societal effects of normalizing human-AI emotional relationships remain largely unexplored.

The question of whether we should design AI to feel human may ultimately be less important than the question of whether we are honest with users about what AI actually is — and what it isn't.
