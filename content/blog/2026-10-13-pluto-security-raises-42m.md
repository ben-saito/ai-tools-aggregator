# Pluto Security Raises $42M as AI Gives Enterprise Security Teams a New Headache

---

The problem with giving everyone in a company access to AI tools is that people will use them in unexpected ways. And that fact is steadily seeding fears that companies now have new, gaping security holes in their systems — or at least, will soon.

Illustrating the pace of AI tool adoption within enterprises driving such fears, Shahar Bahat, CEO and co-founder of Pluto Security, says a newly announced AI tool can show up inside companies' environment within a mere two hours of its release.

Pluto Security has raised $42 million in Series B funding to help enterprises manage the security implications of widespread AI adoption.

## The AI Security Visibility Problem

Traditional security tools were designed for a world where software came in discrete packages, installed by IT, and ran on company-owned infrastructure. AI tools subvert all three assumptions. Employees subscribe to AI services with credit cards. AI tools process sensitive corporate data. And the behavior of AI systems is inherently harder to predict and audit than traditional software.

Pluto's platform gives security teams visibility into which AI tools employees are using, what data those tools have access to, and what the AI systems are doing with that data.

## Rapid Deployment, Rapid Risk

The two-hour deployment claim speaks to a broader dynamic in enterprise AI adoption: business units are moving faster than IT and security teams can track. When an AI tool can be subscribed to and running on company data within two hours, traditional approval processes become moot.

Pluto's approach is to assume that AI tools are already in the environment and work backwards — finding where sensitive data is flowing, what exposures exist, and how to contain them without disrupting productivity.
