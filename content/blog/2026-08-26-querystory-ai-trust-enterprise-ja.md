# QueryStory Emerges from Stealth with $6M Seed to Bridge AI Trust Gap

QueryStory came out of stealth with $6 million in seed funding and a plan to use LLMs and cybersecurity expertise to make AI queries coherent for enterprise decision-makers. The startup was founded by Mehdi Naghibzadeh, who spent years at Google building tools for security analysts to query complex data.

---

## From Cybersecurity to Enterprise AI

Naghibzadeh's background in tracing cyberattacks through disparate networks taught him the value of verified knowledge. The costly and time-consuming nature of that work led him to see an opportunity to apply similar techniques to enterprise data analysis using LLMs.

"You get this pattern of an investigation -- you ask a bunch of questions of the data, and after you have been able to ask a number of questions, you assemble that together into a narrative," Naghibzadeh explained. QueryStory's platform bridges the trust gap for AI to give enterprises answers they can act on, replacing the need for armies of forward deployed engineers or data science teams.

---

## Enterprise Trust in AI Outputs

The core problem QueryStory addresses is that enterprise users often cannot trust AI-generated analysis because they cannot verify how the AI arrived at its conclusions. By applying cybersecurity principles -- traceability, verification, and audit trails -- QueryStory aims to make AI outputs trustworthy for business decisions.

QueryStory raised its $6 million seed round in late 2025 from Brightmind Ventures and New York Life Ventures at a $60 million valuation. Early customers include decision-makers seeking ground truth from complex, disparate data sources without dedicated data science teams.

---

## The Future of AI-Assisted Decision Making

Tim Del Bello, a partner at New York Life Ventures, is using the platform to replace several analysts and produce quarterly business reviews. "The product was built for people like me: decision-makers seeking the ground truth who need to work with complex, disparate data sources but don't have a data science or BI team at their disposal," he said.
