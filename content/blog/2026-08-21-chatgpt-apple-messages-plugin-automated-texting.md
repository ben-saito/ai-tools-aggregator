# ChatGPT Can Now Send Texts for You With New Apple Messages Plugin

OpenAI has launched an Apple Messages integration for ChatGPT that allows the AI to send texts on behalf of users — raising questions about convenience, privacy, and the boundaries of AI-mediated communication.

The plugin allows ChatGPT to access a user's message history (with permission) and compose responses that it can send through the Messages app. Users specify the tone and length; ChatGPT generates the text; and with a single tap, the message goes out under the user's name.

The use cases are straightforward: composing difficult messages, handling high-volume customer service replies, or sending routine updates without stopping what you're doing. The feature works across theMessages ecosystem, not just iMessage.

Privacy advocates have flagged concerns. Granting an AI access to read and send messages is a significant trust decision: the conversation history could reveal sensitive personal information, and the AI's access, while user-initiated, creates a data trail that might be accessible to third parties.

OpenAI has emphasized that the feature requires explicit user consent at every step, and that message content is not used for training unless the user opts in. But the broader pattern — AI systems acting as intermediaries in personal communication — is new enough that norms and regulations have not caught up.

The Messages integration is the latest example of AI systems moving from passive advice to active agency: executing tasks, sending communications, and taking actions on behalf of users. As these capabilities expand, the line between "AI assistant" and "AI delegate" continues to blur.
