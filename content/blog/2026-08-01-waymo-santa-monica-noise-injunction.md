# Judge Orders Waymo to Stop Overnight Charging in Santa Monica Amid Noise Complaints

A Los Angeles Superior Court judge has granted a preliminary injunction requiring Waymo to halt overnight charging operations at its Santa Monica facilities, following complaints from residents about persistent noise disturbances from the autonomous vehicle fleet.

---

## Background: Residents Allege Year-Long Disturbance

The City of Santa Monica filed a public nuisance lawsuit against Waymo in November 2025, alleging that vehicles operating at charging stations on Broadway at 12th and Euclid Streets generated excessive light, sound, and traffic congestion. The facilities, which Waymo began using in January 2025, sit across the street from a private school and are surrounded by apartment buildings and residences.

Christopher Potter, a neighbor who lives adjacent to the lots, described the disturbance in court filings: "The constant 'beep beep beep' sound as the autonomous vehicles back out of their spaces is an incessant disturbance that hinders both our tranquillity during the day and our peace during the night."

Another resident, who requested anonymity, noted that the sound was particularly disruptive late at night. "At 2am when the bars let out, that's when [they had been] full bore. The sound of the Waymos, yes, but the acceleration and deceleration sound that EVs have, makes it sounds like you have Tron outside your window. It [was] one after another!"

---

## Court Order: 11pm to 6am Prohibition

Judge Bradley S. Phillips ruled in favor of the City of Santa Monica's motion for preliminary injunction, prohibiting Waymo from operating the charging lots between 11pm and 6am. Mayor Caroline Torosis said in a statement: "Our residents' ability to sleep peacefully in their own homes is not negotiable. This ruling affirms what our neighbors have been telling us for more than a year."

Waymo has indicated it will comply with the order while the case proceeds. The company has also filed a counter-suit against the city. The case is expected to continue for several months as both parties present their arguments.

---

## Implications for AV Operations

The ruling highlights growing pains for autonomous vehicle companies as they scale operations in urban environments. While Waymo has operated in San Francisco and Phoenix without similar restrictions, Santa Monica's residential density and proximity to schools and housing created conditions that the court found constituted a public nuisance. The case may set precedent for how AV charging infrastructure is regulated in mixed-use urban areas.
