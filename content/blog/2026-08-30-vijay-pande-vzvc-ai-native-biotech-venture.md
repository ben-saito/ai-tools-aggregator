# Vijay Pande Departs a16z to Launch VZVC: A New AI-Native Venture Model for Biotech

Vijay Pande, who built and led Andreessen Horowitz's roughly $4 billion biotech practice for a decade, has left to start VZVC — a much smaller, AI-native venture firm built around a handful of concentrated bets per year. The move marks a sharp departure from the traditional venture model and signals a broader shift toward AI-driven biotech investing.

---

## From a16z to VZVC: A Strategic Pivot

Pande joined a16z in 2015 after a tenure as a Stanford chemistry professor, best known for building Folding@home — the distributed computing project that turned millions of home PCs into a supercomputer for disease research. At a16z, he oversaw the firm's biotech portfolio, which grew to approximately $4 billion in invested capital.

In June of last year, Pande walked away from the traditional venture model to co-found VZVC with longtime investor Zach Werner. The new firm is designed around a fundamentally different philosophy: instead of spreading capital across dozens of investments per year, VZVC focuses on a small number of concentrated bets, has no associates, and relies heavily on AI for day-to-day operations.

"We're not doing 30 bets a year," Pande told TechCrunch this week. "A handful of concentrated bets — that's how you build something real."

---

## Biology: From Discovery Science to Engineering

The core thesis driving Pande's new venture reflects a broader transformation in biotech. Pande argues that biology is transitioning from what he calls a "science of discovery" — characterized by serendipitous findings and high failure rates — to an "engineering" discipline where AI and machine learning enable precise control over drug development pipelines.

"For a lot of the way drugs have been developed, there was very much a fortuitous aspect to it," Pande explained. "What's shifted is that AI and machine learning allow computers to wrap their type of understanding around something very, very complicated — to try to figure out what targets you want your drugs to hit, for specific diseases, to be able to make those drugs, and now even to help in the clinical trials."

Clinical trials remain the most expensive phase of drug development. Pande notes that while AI has begun reducing the cost and time to reach clinical trials, running a full trial can still cost hundreds of millions of dollars. The probability of a drug successfully progressing from Phase 1 to the end of Phase 3 is just 20%.

---

## The Data Problem: Why Biotech Data Is Different

One of the most significant challenges in AI-driven medicine, according to Pande, is the nature of biological data itself. Unlike text scraped from the internet, biological data cannot be freely gathered at scale. Nearly every biotech company ends up building its own proprietary, walled-off dataset — creating a fragmentation problem that contrasts sharply with the open, web-scale data that powers large language models.

This data siloing has profound implications for AI advances in medicine. Shared, open datasets could accelerate progress dramatically, but competitive pressures incentivize companies to guard their data jealously. Pande's VZVC appears to be built around the thesis that the most successful AI-biotech investments will be those that find ways to leverage shared data infrastructure rather than operate in isolation.

---

## The Changing Face of Venture Capital

Pande's departure from a16z and the launch of VZVC reflects a broader reckoning in the venture capital industry. Traditional VC models — built on wide fund deployment across large portfolios — are being tested by an era where AI reduces the need for large teams and where concentrated, thesis-driven bets may outperform spray-and-pray strategies.

VZVC's model, with its small team, minimal headcount, and heavy AI integration, represents a template that could reshape how biotech venture capital operates. The firm is, in essence, applying the same AI-native principles to venture that Silicon Valley's best-funded startups apply to product development.

## Key Takeaways

- **VZVC** is a new AI-native venture firm co-founded by Vijay Pande and Zach Werner, focused on a small number of concentrated biotech bets per year
- Pande argues biology is transitioning from "discovery science" to "engineering," enabled by AI and machine learning
- The **data siloing problem** in biotech — where each company builds proprietary datasets — remains a fundamental challenge for AI-driven medicine
- VZVC's minimal-team, AI-heavy operating model represents a new template for biotech venture capital
