# Hugging Face Releases $399 Open Source Robot Duck, Microduck

Hugging Face unveiled the Microduck on Thursday, a cute duck-like robot priced at $399 that ships before Christmas. The 25-centimeter-tall robot can waddle, pick up objects with its beak (up to 800 grams), get back up when it falls, crouch, and even roller skate.

Clem Delangue, CEO of Hugging Face, said the Microduck is an "open-source robot you can teach new tricks with reinforcement learning." The robot perceives the world with a camera, lidar sensors, and two IMUs (inertial measurement units that measure and report an object's movement).

"Welcome to the era of open-source affordable robots to democratize physical AI and world models!" Delangue said in a post on X.

Hugging Face acquired French startup Pollen Robotics in April 2025 to build affordable open source AI hardware. A couple of months later, the two launched Reachy Mini, a small desktop robot. The Microduck represents their next step in consumer-friendly robotics.

The Pollen Robotics team noted that the Microduck's behaviors can be trained in simulation and directly deployed on the robot. Developers can fine-tune the bot, retrain it, and redeploy it. The SDK, simulation, and full RL training stack are available on GitHub.

The Microduck is available for pre-order now at $399, with shipping scheduled before the end of the year.

---

## Open Source Robotics Meets AI Agents

The Microduck launch signals Hugging Face's ambition to become a major player in physical AI, not just software. By combining open source hardware with reinforcement learning tooling, Hugging Face is enabling developers to experiment with embodied AI at a price point far below traditional research robots.

The robot's design prioritizes accessibility: it is small enough for a desktop, durable enough to survive falls, and versatile enough to learn new tasks through trial and error. For developers interested in world models and physical AI, the Microduck provides a ready-made platform for experimentation.

The full software stack, including simulation environments and training pipelines, is available on GitHub under an open source license.
