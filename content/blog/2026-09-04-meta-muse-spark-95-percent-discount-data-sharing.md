# Meta Offers 95% Discount on Muse Spark in Exchange for User Data

Meta is offering its new Muse Spark coding agent model at a steep discount -- roughly 95% -- to users who agree to share their prompts and model outputs with the company for future AI development. The pricing structure is designed to gather training data that Meta believes is critical for improving agentic AI tools.

---

## The Pricing Model

Under the contributor pricing program, one million input tokens costs just 10 cents, compared to $1.25 under a standard agreement. Output tokens drop from $4.25 per million to a similarly reduced rate. The discount is explicitly tied to the user's consent to have their interactions fed back into Meta's model training pipeline.

The Muse Spark model is designed for operating coding and other agents, meaning it is optimized for task completion rather than pure conversation. This class of AI tool has seen rapid capability improvements, which Meta attributes partly to the availability of high-quality interaction data from tools like Claude Code.

---

## Data Collection Challenges

Meta has faced difficulty obtaining training data through conventional means. An earlier initiative to track the computer usage of its own employees was paused after attracting internal criticism. The company's head of AI research has noted that large companies have shown reluctance to have their data used for model training, often preferring token-billed Enterprise plans even when consumer subscription tiers are significantly cheaper.

Princeton computer science professor Arvind Narayanan observed that there is good evidence that large companies do not want their data used for model training -- a preference that manifests even when the cost savings of consumer plans would otherwise favor their adoption.

---

## Industry Implications

The data collection challenge reflects a broader tension in AI development: the most capable models require vast amounts of real-world interaction data, but the most valuable sources of that data -- businesses and individuals with sensitive workflows -- have strong incentives to keep it private.

Meta's approach of discounting model access in exchange for data represents one attempt to resolve this tradeoff, though it remains to be seen whether the model outputs gathered will be of sufficient quality to meaningfully improve next-generation systems.
