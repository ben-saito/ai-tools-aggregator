# Anthropic Wins First Court Challenge Against Pentagon Supply-Chain Risk Designation

A federal judge in California ruled on Thursday that the Trump administration's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was illegal, marking the company's first legal victory in its ongoing dispute with the Defense Department.

---

## Ruling Details

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's labeling of Anthropic as a risk to national security constituted "unlawful retaliation" in violation of the First Amendment, and that the decision was "arbitrary and capricious." Lin also found that Anthropic was denied due process under the Fifth Amendment.

Earlier in 2026, Hegseth and President Donald Trump designated Anthropic as a supply-chain risk and ordered all federal agencies — even those outside defense — to cease working with the company that makes Claude models.

---

## Origins of the Dispute

The conflict centered on Anthropic's safety guardrails, which the company maintained would prohibit the Pentagon from using its models for fully autonomous weapons or mass surveillance of American citizens. The Pentagon disputed these restrictions and alleged that Anthropic was attempting to control how the military utilized models it had purchased.

In her ruling, Lin highlighted what she described as inconsistency between the government's actions and its stated rationale. She noted that Hegseth had proposed applying the Defense Production Act to Anthropic — which would indicate the company was essential to national security rather than a threat to it. She also pointed to the Defense Department's continued pursuit of a contract with Anthropic and collaboration with the company's new Mythos model for cybersecurity purposes.

Lin additionally noted that Anthropic "undisputedly lacks" any backdoor access to its technology once delivered to the Defense Department.

"The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics," Lin wrote.

---

## Broader Legal Battle

Anthropic filed two complaints against the Department of Defense in March — one in California and one in Washington, D.C. The California case, which resulted in this week's ruling, is the first to conclude. The D.C. litigation remains ongoing.

The case represents a significant test of the government's authority to designate technology companies as supply-chain risks and the procedural requirements that must be followed in making such determinations.
