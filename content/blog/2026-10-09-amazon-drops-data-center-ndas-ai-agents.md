# Amazon drops data center NDAs, and AI agents want your credit card

---

Amazon says it will stop using NDAs when negotiating data center deals with local governments, following a similar move from Microsoft earlier this year. Secrecy has fueled community backlash against AI infrastructure, with opposition leading to hundreds of proposed and existing data centers being delayed or blocked outright.

The announcement was part of a broader blog post making the case that data centers are actually good for communities — creating jobs, boosting local tax revenue, and supporting small businesses. Amazon's post acknowledged that the NDA practice had "created the impression that something was being hidden."

On the same podcast episode, the TechCrunch Equity team also explored how AI agents are increasingly being designed to handle financial transactions. Several startups featured on the show are building agents that can make purchases, subscribe to services, and manage recurring payments — all with access to user credit cards and payment systems.

The dual developments reflect a broader industry push to address two major friction points in AI adoption: local community opposition to data center construction, and the challenge of giving AI agents real purchasing power. Both are seen as critical to the next phase of AI infrastructure and agent deployment.

Amazon's move follows Microsoft's similar announcement earlier in 2026. Together, the two largest cloud providers have now publicly committed to greater transparency in their data center siting negotiations.
