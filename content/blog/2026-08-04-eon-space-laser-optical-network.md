# EON Wants to Move the Data Superhighway from Ocean Fiber to Space Lasers

Endeavour Optical Networks is planning to launch the fastest space laser communications system ever built. The company aims to replace transoceanic fiber cables with laser-based data transmission across the globe.

---

## Background: Limitations of Ocean Fiber

The current internet backbone relies on fiber optic cables laid across the Pacific and Atlantic ocean floors. However, high installation costs, difficulty repairing faults, and capacity limits have been persistent challenges. EON sees space-based laser communication as the solution to this infrastructure bottleneck.

---

## Advantages of Space Laser Communication

Space lasers use precise light beams to enable data transfer speeds 10 to 100 times faster than wireless or conventional satellite communications. EON's system plans to build optical links between low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites, creating an end-to-end data route that bypasses submarine cables entirely.

Laser communication is also less affected by weather conditions on the ground. Technology to minimize signal loss from fog and rain has advanced significantly, making practical deployment increasingly viable.

---

## Competitor Comparison

SpaceX's Starlink leads in existing satellite internet, but EON's approach focuses specifically on high-speed, low-latency dedicated optical links. It has the potential to solve the latency issues that current Starlink users face, with demand expected from military and financial trading sectors.

---

## Implementation Timeline

EON aims to launch its first demonstration satellite in the second half of 2026. Initial months will offer limited coverage, gradually expanding to a global network. Commercial service is expected to begin after 2027.

---

## Industry Impact

If space laser communication reaches practical use, data transfer costs between cloud providers could drop significantly. How major players like AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure will respond — partnering with emerging infrastructure companies like EON or building their own space communication networks — will be the next focus.
