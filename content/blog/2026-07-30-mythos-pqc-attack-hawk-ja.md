# Mythos Cryptographic Attack Knocks Post-Quantum Algorithm HAWK Out of NIST Competition

---

## A New Attack Breaks a Promising PQC Candidate

In a significant development for post-quantum cryptography, a new attack called Mythos has successfully broken the HAWK algorithm, removing it from consideration for NIST's post-quantum cryptography standardization process. Ars Technica reports that HAWK, which had withstood years of testing, fell to the novel attack technique.

This marks a setback for the ongoing NIST PQC standardization effort, which aims to establish quantum-resistant cryptographic algorithms before large-scale quantum computers become practical. HAWK was one of the third-round candidate algorithms being evaluated for standardization.

---

## Understanding the Attack

This Mythos attack -- unrelated to the Anthropic AI model of the same name -- represents a mathematical cryptanalysis technique. The attack exploits previously unknown weaknesses in HAWK's underlying cryptographic structure, demonstrating that even algorithms that have been thoroughly vetted may harbor vulnerabilities.

For developers working on cryptographic systems, this news reinforces the importance of maintaining cryptographic agility: the ability to swap out algorithms as new vulnerabilities are discovered.

---

## Implications for PQC Migration

The HAWK elimination adds to concerns about the readiness of post-quantum cryptography deployment. Organizations currently planning PQC migrations should ensure their architectures can support algorithm substitution as the standardization process continues to evolve.

NIST is expected to continue evaluating remaining candidates, with final standards anticipated in the coming years. Security teams should monitor NIST's ongoing announcements and begin planning for hybrid cryptographic approaches that combine classical and post-quantum algorithms during the transition period.
