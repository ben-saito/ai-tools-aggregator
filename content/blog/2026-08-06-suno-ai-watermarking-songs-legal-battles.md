# Suno to Begin Watermarking AI-Generated Songs Amid Ongoing Legal Battles

Suno, the AI music generation startup, announced on August 6, 2026 that it will begin watermarking songs created on its platform. The watermarking system, developed in partnership with lyrics provider Musixmatch's Sentinal copyright detection system, is designed to identify AI-generated music without affecting the listening experience.

---

## Watermarking Principles and Design

In a blog post, Suno co-founder and CEO Mikey Shulman outlined core principles for the watermarking initiative:

「These tools are designed to be durable and resistant to tampering, without affecting the listening experience. They are also not intended to pass judgment on whether a song is good, meaningful, or sufficiently human. Ultimately, we believe it should be up to artists and platforms to decide what they want to disclose. Our role is to build tools that give them transparency options and make it easier to collaborate across the industry.」

Suno also updated its community guidelines to explicitly prohibit "deceptive audio presented as real" and "using a real person's voice or likeness without permission" — a direct response to concerns about AI-generated music being passed off as human performances.

The company plans to add a new download policy to prevent mass distribution of AI-generated content on streaming platforms, though full details were not disclosed.

---

## Legal Battles on Multiple Fronts

The watermarking announcement comes as Suno faces significant legal challenges. The company is being sued by Universal Music Group and Sony Music Group in a case coordinated by the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA), following a lawsuit filed in June 2024. Additionally, a German court ruled in favor of a government body in late July regarding Suno's data sourcing practices.

Suno raised $400 million in Series D funding in June 2026, giving it substantial resources to pursue both legal defense and product development. The watermarking system represents an attempt to address industry concerns about transparency while the legal landscape remains unsettled.
