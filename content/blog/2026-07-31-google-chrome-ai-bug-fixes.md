# Google Says It Fixed More Chrome Bugs in June Than Over the Past Two Years, Thanks to AI

Google announced that its security team patched more vulnerabilities in Chrome during June 2026 than in the entire preceding two-year period combined, attributing the acceleration directly to the integration of AI-assisted code review and bug detection tools.

The company said AI systems are now embedded throughout its browser security workflow, from initial code analysis to regression testing. The result has been a qualitative shift in how quickly vulnerabilities are identified and resolved, before they can be exploited in the wild.

---

## How AI Is Changing Browser Security

Traditionally, browser security teams have relied on a combination of static analysis tools, manual code audits, and external bug bounty reports. Google says AI has augmented each of these steps. Large language models trained on Chromium's codebase can flag suspicious patterns that might escape rule-based scanners, while generative tools help developers write more secure patches faster.

The company stressed that AI assists rather than replaces human security researchers. The acceleration comes from triaging and prioritizing findings more efficiently, not from fully automated exploitation of vulnerabilities.

---

## Implications for the Industry

Microsoft has reported similar results with AI-assisted security workflows in Windows and Azure. The pattern suggests that as AI tools mature, the economics of security research are shifting — vulnerabilities that would have gone undetected for months are now being caught during the development cycle itself.

This has downstream effects for enterprise security posture. If major platforms are genuinely becoming harder to exploit at the source, attackers may shift their focus to supply chain attacks and social engineering, where human behavior remains the weakest link.

---

## Expert Reactions

Some security researchers have welcomed the acceleration while cautioning that AI-generated patches can introduce new vulnerabilities if not carefully reviewed. The speed of AI-assisted development creates pressure to deploy quickly, which can conflict with the deliberate testing cycles that security-critical software requires.

Google says it has not reduced human review requirements for AI-generated patches and that its internal red team continues to probe for regressions before any patch reaches stable channels.

*This article is based on information from July 30, 2026.*
