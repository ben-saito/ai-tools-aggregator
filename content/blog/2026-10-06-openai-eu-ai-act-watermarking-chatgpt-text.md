# OpenAI Will Start Watermarking ChatGPT Text in the EU

OpenAI will begin watermarking text generated by ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union to comply with the AI Act, according to an announcement made on October 5, 2026. The invisible watermarks will mark AI-generated content, though the company warns that editing can make the marks harder to detect.

---

## EU AI Act Compliance Push

The EU AI Act, which took effect in 2025, requires providers of general-purpose AI models to implement technical measures that allow output to be identified as AI-generated. OpenAI's watermarking initiative is a direct response to these regulatory requirements, making it one of the first major AI companies to implement system-level content attribution for EU users.

The watermarking system works by embedding subtle statistical patterns into AI-generated text that are invisible to the human eye but detectable through specialized analysis tools. This approach differs from visible watermarks or metadata tags, which can be easily stripped by users.

---

## Editing Complicates Detection

OpenAI acknowledged a significant limitation: text editing can degrade or eliminate watermarks. Casual paraphrasing, copy-pasting into different contexts, or using AI rewriters can make the invisible marks undetectable. This creates a gap between the technical capability and practical enforceability of the AI Act's attribution requirements.

The company emphasized that watermarking is one component of a broader content authenticity strategy, which includes provenance tracking and disclosure labels for users who interact with AI-generated material.

---

## Industry Implications

The move positions OpenAI as a leader in AI governance compliance ahead of the AI Act's enforcement deadlines. Other major AI providers are expected to announce similar measures in the coming months as the compliance window narrows. The watermarking standard could become a de facto international norm if the EU approach is adopted by other jurisdictions.

The watermarking of Codex code output is particularly significant for developers using AI-assisted coding tools, as it raises questions about how attribution requirements will affect code licensing and open-source contributions made with AI assistance.

---

*Watermarking implementation reflects a growing regulatory consensus that AI-generated content requires technical identification mechanisms, with the EU leading the way on mandatory attribution standards.*
