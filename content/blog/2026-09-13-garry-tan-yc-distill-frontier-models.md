# Y Combinator's Garry Tan proposes "American distillation regime" for frontier AI models

Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan has told CNBC that U.S. regulators should not restrict American AI labs from using distillation techniques to learn from frontier models, and suggested the U.S. should develop its own "American distillation regime" to compete with Chinese AI labs.

---

## Tan's position on distillation

Speaking in an interview earlier this week, Tan said: "I would do nothing. We could argue that there should be an American distillation regime." He elaborated to TechCrunch that he wants smaller, American open-weight AI labs to use the same kind of training techniques on American frontier AI labs, giving the U.S. a more robust set of open-weight options that aren't Chinese.

Distillation is when a model maker extensively prompts another model in order to learn how it works and reasons. It is a commonly and legitimately used technique by AI labs to help train new models.

---

## Anthropic's reports on alleged Chinese distillation attacks

Tan made the comments in the context of Anthropic releasing its second report alleging that Chinese labs are engaged in "illicit distillation attacks," hiding their identities to distill without permission and relying on fraud and stolen credentials to do so. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei had previously publicly called on U.S. regulators to intervene.

Tan, however, appeared skeptical of regulatory restrictions, suggesting instead that U.S. labs should adopt similar techniques to maintain competitiveness.

---

## Policy implications

The proposal raises questions about intellectual property rights in AI training, the boundaries between legitimate knowledge transfer and unfair competitive practices, and the appropriate role of government in regulating AI development techniques. The debate contrasts with the more restrictive approach some have advocated, which would treat unauthorized distillation as a form of IP infringement requiring regulatory action.
