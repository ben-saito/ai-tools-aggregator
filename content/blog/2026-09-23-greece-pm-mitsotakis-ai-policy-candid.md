# Greek Prime Minister: "We're Already Fighting Yesterday's Battle" on AI Policy

Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis visited San Francisco this week with a dual mission: promoting Greece as an investment destination and sounding an alarm about artificial intelligence that few heads of state have been willing to voice publicly.

Speaking at an event hosted by Endeavor Greece in San Francisco, Mitsotakis told TechCrunch in an interview that no government is prepared for what AI is about to do to society. His remarks, delivered before an audience of roughly 250 founders, investors, and operators, represented a rare instance of a sitting head of state engaging directly with the AI policy debate rather than relying on prepared diplomatic language.

---

## A Bay Area Fact-Finding Mission

Mitsotakis, who holds a master's degree from Stanford, described his Bay Area visit as partly a fact-finding mission. He spent Monday morning touring Tesla and Sequoia Capital, among other stops, before heading to the U.N. General Assembly in New York. The trip was also explicitly designed to recruit tech companies to consider Greece as a base.

Greece is set to regain developed market status from MSCI index provider next year, a milestone that reflects the country's economic recovery following its debt crisis. Mitsotakis noted that Greece has paid down its debt at a record pace and that much of the roughly EUR 36 billion received from the EU's post-COVID recovery fund went into digital infrastructure.

But despite the economic optimism, Mitsotakis was candid about the gaps in his government's understanding of AI's societal implications.

---

## The Governance Gap

"I think it's another indication that the economy is doing well and that Greece is no longer treated as a special case," Mitsotakis said of his country's progress. But on AI policy, he acknowledged a broader uncertainty among world leaders.

"I don't think any government has answers to many of the AI questions that we're all more privately debating," he said. The remark stood out because trade missions typically produce upbeat announcements rather than admissions of policy gaps.

The Greek prime minister's warning reflects a growing tension in global AI governance. Regulatory frameworks in the European Union, United States, and elsewhere are still taking shape, while AI capabilities continue to advance faster than the policy process can track. Mitsotakis's comments suggest that even leaders who follow the issue closely see themselves as perpetually behind the curve.

---

## Greece's Position in the AI Race

Greece occupies an unusual position in the global AI landscape. The country has produced a notable number of AI researchers and startup founders relative to its population, but lacks the domestic tech giants that shape AI development in the United States and China. Mitsotakis's engagement with the Bay Area ecosystem reflects an effort to attract the infrastructure and talent needed to compete in an AI-driven economy.

Whether his public candor about AI governance gaps translates into concrete policy remains to be seen. But in a room full of founders and investors, his willingness to acknowledge uncertainty stood in contrast to the confident pitches more typically heard on the tech conference circuit.
