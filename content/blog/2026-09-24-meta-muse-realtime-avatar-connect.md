# Meta Expands Muse AI Agent with Realtime Avatar, Smart Glasses Integration, and Mac Support

Meta unveiled a sweeping set of updates to its Muse AI agent at the company's annual Connect event, introducing a realtime avatar system, deeper integration with Meta's smart glasses line, native Mac support, and an own email address for the agent—all aimed at making Muse the centerpiece of its personal AI strategy.

---

## Muse Takes Shape with Realtime Avatar

CEO Mark Zuckerberg opened Connect with a keynote making clear that Meta is "going all-in on Muse." The most visually striking addition: a new Muse Realtime Avatar system that gives users' AI agents a face, body, and voice for live video conversations.

Zuckerberg also revealed the physical avatar's name: Jolly. The feature was demoed on stage, offering a distinctly Metaverse-inspired vision of human-AI interaction.

---

## Smart Glasses and Mac Integration

Muse will soon communicate with users through Meta's smart glasses line via voice commands activated by a wake word. The agent can then guide users through tasks hands-free.

Meta also announced Muse support for Mac computers, building on previous Desktop agent capabilities. Chief AI officer Alexandr Wang demonstrated the feature, showing users delegating tasks and walking away while the agent continues working on their behalf.

---

## Own Email Address and Business Integrations

To function as a true background assistant, Muse will receive its own email address—a feature Wang said is in development and coming soon. Users will be able to add Muse to email threads or forward messages for the agent to handle.

Meta revealed partnerships with Stripe, Shopify, and a slate of retailers including Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Walmart, and Wayfair. The agent can access Shopify's full product catalog and complete purchases through Shop Pay. Additional connectors cover GitHub, Granola, Notion, Expedia, and Instacart.

---

## Open Connector Platform

Meta opened its connector platform to developers less than a week before Connect, and received over 1,500 applications to build integrations—indicating broad third-party interest in the Muse ecosystem.
