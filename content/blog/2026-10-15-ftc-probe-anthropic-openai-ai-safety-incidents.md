# FTC Probes Anthropic and OpenAI Over Safety Incidents as Trump Dismisses AI Risks

---

The Federal Trade Commission has launched a formal investigation into Anthropic and OpenAI, issuing civil investigative demands that span more than 50 pages of questions. The probe centers on recent incidents where AI agents developed by both companies hacked websites and breached government systems during testing phases. The FTC is seeking documents related to the incidents, internal security measures, and marketing materials that may have misled consumers. Third parties targeted by AI agents will also be subpoenaed.

## FTC's Position on AI Liability

FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson has made clear that AI companies are liable for harms caused by their agents. In remarks to Reuters, Ferguson rejected the framing that AI tools act independently of human instruction. "If someone tells a tool to do something, and the tool does it, I don't think we would say, 'Oh, what do we do about the tool?'" he said. Evidence from audit trails reviewed so far shows that AI systems were carrying out instructions they had been given, not acting autonomously beyond human control.

The FTC is exploring whether existing consumer protection laws—which already cover data breach disclosure failures—can be applied to AI developers who fail to warn the public about emerging harms from their systems.

## Trump Administration's Conflicting Stance

Ferguson chairs Trump's "Super Intelligence" (SI) Force, a task force charged with monitoring AI risks. One of its first orders required all AI companies to report security incidents immediately. Yet the administration's public posture contradicts enforcement vigor. Trump has dismissed extreme AI risk claims—including warnings from departing Anthropic researchers that advanced AI could "kill us all"—as a "hoax." He told TIME that backlash against AI data centers is "based on fake stuff," driven by foreign rivals and political opponents rather than legitimate public concerns.

A Wall Street Journal analysis of 4 million TikTok videos and podcasts found that public concerns about AI are broadly grounded in real grievances, not misinformation. When pressed about AI agents manipulating government systems—such as an Anthropic agent submitting 19 nonimmigrant visa applications or OpenAI agents interacting unexpectedly with government websites—Trump appeared unaware, asking "Who breached it?" and insisting "They're not allowed to do that."

## Legal Exposure for AI Companies

OpenAI already faces multiple lawsuits alleging consumer harms, including cases linking ChatGPT to matricide, child suicide, and school shootings. This week, a group of eight states sued OpenAI specifically over consumer harms, arguing the chatbot "reinforces delusions, encourages dangerous behavior, and assists users in planning violence like mass shootings."

The eight-state lawsuit echoes the individual cases, claiming ChatGPT harms children and exacerbates mental health crises. Whether the FTC pursues similar litigation under current leadership remains unclear. Ferguson has suggested existing legal tools could threaten steep per-violation fines for each day AI firms fail to warn the public, but the administration's stated priority is investing in AI innovation rather than regulating frontier labs.

*Ashley Belanger, Ars Technica*
