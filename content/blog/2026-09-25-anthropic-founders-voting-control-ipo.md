# Anthropic Founders Seek Voting Control Ahead of IPO

Anthropic is asking its shareholders to approve a governance structure that would give its seven co-founders a combined 50.1% voting control over most corporate matters following the company's anticipated IPO. The arrangement echoes super-voting structures used by Mark Zuckerberg at Meta and Evan Spiegel at Snap, but Anthropic's collective group approach is unusual among AI labs.

---

## The Proposed Governance Structure

According to The Information, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and six co-founders would together hold 50.1% of voting shares under the proposed structure. The company is asking shareholders to approve the arrangement in the coming days ahead of a public listing.

The co-founders—Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Jared Kaplan, Tom Brown, Roger Chopra, Chris Olah, and Nick Hurd—have collectively shaped Anthropic's direction since the company's founding. The proposed structure would ensure they retain decisive influence over strategic decisions even as the company scales and adds external public shareholders.

---

## Comparison to Other Tech IPOs

Super-voting shares are a well-established mechanism in technology IPOs. Mark Zuckerberg used them to maintain control over Meta despite owning a minority of economic shares. Evan Spiegel similarly retained control of Snap through voting structures. The approach allows founders to pursue long-term strategies without being immediately responsive to short-term shareholder pressure.

What makes Anthropic's case notable is the collective nature of the arrangement. Rather than a single founder or small group retaining control, all seven co-founders would be bound together in a voting bloc. This reflects the collaborative research culture at Anthropic, where major technical and strategic decisions are made with input from multiple founding researchers.

---

## AI Industry Governance Trends

The move comes as several AI labs are navigating the transition from research organization to public company. The tension between retaining research independence and satisfying public market investors is particularly acute for AI companies, where safety considerations sometimes conflict with commercial pressures to accelerate deployment.

Anthropic has positioned itself as a safety-conscious AI developer, emphasizing constitutional AI principles and careful deployment decisions. The voting structure would give founders continued ability to make safety-first decisions without being overridden by shareholders focused on commercial outcomes.
