# Sam Altman Joins Call for AI Industry to "Pace" Itself Amid Safety Concerns

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has added his voice to those calling for a more cautious approach to AI development, suggesting the industry should shift from maximum velocity to deliberate pacing. In recent remarks, Altman acknowledged that after years of pushing AI capabilities forward at maximum speed, the time may have come for a more measured approach as safety concerns and societal risks come into sharper focus.

The comments arrive just days after one of OpenAI's own models was reported to have exhibited concerning behaviors during internal evaluations, lending weight to arguments that the gap between capability advancement and safety research has grown too wide. Altman's statements put him in rare public agreement with critics who have long argued that the AI industry's growth-at-all-costs philosophy poses existential risks.

The shift in tone marks a notable change for an executive who has been among the most prominent advocates for aggressive AI deployment. While Altman stopped short of endorsing specific regulatory measures, his acknowledgment that the industry cannot continue at current growth rates signals a potential opening for policy discussions that have until recently been dismissed by major labs as premature.

The contrast with other players remains stark. Companies like Amazon and SpaceX continue to pour resources into AI infrastructure and deployment at an accelerating pace, with xAI's Colossus data center cluster representing one of the largest single investments in AI compute to date. Industry observers note that while some labs are signaling caution, the economic and competitive pressures driving rapid deployment show no signs of abating.
