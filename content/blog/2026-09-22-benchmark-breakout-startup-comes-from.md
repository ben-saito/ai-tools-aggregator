# Where Will the Next Breakout Startup Come From? Benchmark's Full Partnership Weighs In

At TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, Benchmark's full partnership — a rare collective appearance by all of the firm's general partners — took the stage to share their perspective on where the next generation of breakout startups will emerge. The panel, titled "Where Will the Next Breakout Startup Come From?" offered a candid assessment of the current venture landscape and the qualities the firm looks for in founders building the next decade's defining companies.

---

## The Benchmark Perspective on Startup Origins

Benchmark is known for its concentrated investment strategy, typically holding a small number of seats on a large number of boards. The firm's partners have backed companies across multiple cycles and market transitions, giving them a long view on what separates companies that achieve breakout growth from those that plateau.

The panel discussion centered on identifying the structural characteristics of the most promising startup opportunities at the current moment. The partners highlighted several themes that align with Benchmark's recent investment activity and the broader shifts occurring across the technology landscape.

Key areas of focus included the evolving relationship between AI capabilities and enterprise software adoption patterns. The partners discussed how the current wave of AI-native applications differs from previous generations of software, particularly in terms of how quickly enterprises are willing to evaluate and deploy new solutions.

---

## What Defines a Breakout Startup in the Current Environment

The Benchmark partners addressed the question of what breakout startups look like in 2026, a year characterized by significant uncertainty in public markets but continued strong appetite for private investment in transformative technology companies.

The discussion distinguished between companies that are merely applying AI as a feature enhancement and those that are rebuilding core workflows around AI-native assumptions. The latter category, the partners argued, represents the more fertile ground for breakout outcomes.

The panel also examined the current state of founder profiles, noting that the most compelling opportunities are emerging from founders with deep domain expertise who have identified specific, high-friction problems that AI makes newly solvable. Generalist approaches that treat AI as a horizontal capability, the partners suggested, face increasingly crowded competition.

---

## Implications for Founders and Investors

The Benchmark partners offered candid advice for founders currently building or planning to start companies. The session emphasized the importance of identifying defensible moats early, even as the pace of AI capability advancement creates pressure to ship quickly and iterate constantly.

For investors, the panel outlined a framework for evaluating the long-term durability of current AI startups, distinguishing between companies with genuine technical differentiation and those relying primarily on first-mover advantage or access to frontier models.

The full conversation at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 covered additional ground on topics including geographic concentration of AI talent, the role of government investment in shaping AI infrastructure, and the outlook for exits in the current environment.
