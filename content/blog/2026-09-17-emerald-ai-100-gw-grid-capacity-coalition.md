# Google, Nvidia, Anthropic Form Coalition With Emerald AI to Secure 100 GW of Grid Capacity for Data Centers

A new industry coalition backed by Google, Nvidia, Anthropic, and grid infrastructure startup Emerald AI announced plans to locate 100 gigawatts of new data center capacity -- enough to power approximately 75 million homes -- across locations with available electrical grid infrastructure. The initiative, announced at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, represents one of the most coordinated private-sector efforts to address the power constraints that have become a primary bottleneck for AI infrastructure expansion.

---

## The Grid Bottleneck Becomes a Strategic Problem

As AI training and inference workloads scale, the availability of reliable electrical power has emerged as a limiting factor for data center siting decisions. Unlike compute resources, which can be procured through hardware acquisitions, grid capacity requires coordination with utility providers, regulatory approvals, and often years of infrastructure development. The coalition's target of 100 GW exceeds the total electricity generation capacity of many mid-sized nations.

Emerald AI's role in the coalition centers on the company's grid optimization technology, which maps available transmission capacity across North American utility networks and identifies candidate sites where power can be delivered within accelerated construction timelines. The company has been building what it describes as a "grid intelligence" platform that combines real-time transmission data with machine learning models trained on infrastructure permitting patterns.

---

## Why This Matters for AI Development Trajectory

The concentration of AI infrastructure within a small number of data center campuses has raised concerns about both resilience and competitive dynamics. When major AI labs are competing for a finite pool of available grid power, the allocation decision itself becomes a form of strategic positioning. The coalition's approach -- aggregating demand signals from multiple labs to secure capacity that no single company could justify independently -- represents a structural response to this coordination problem.

Nvidia's participation reflects the GPU leader's interest in ensuring that the AI infrastructure buildout does not face power constraints that would reduce demand for its hardware. Google and Anthropic bring the demand-side perspective of major AI research organizations whose training workloads are directly constrained by available compute.

---

## Infrastructure Constraints as AI Policy

The formation of the coalition also reflects a broader trend: the private sector responding to infrastructure bottlenecks that public planning processes have not adequately addressed. Grid expansion in the United States typically operates on timelines measured in years to decades, while AI infrastructure demand has accelerated on a scale of months to quarters. The gap between these planning horizons is driving direct investment in grid intelligence and site selection as a competitive necessity.

The Energy Department has signaled interest in accelerating permitting for data center infrastructure, but concrete policy responses remain in early stages. In the meantime, the coalition approach represents the industry's own answer to a problem that public infrastructure planning has not kept pace with.
