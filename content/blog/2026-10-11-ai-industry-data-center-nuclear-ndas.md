# Can the AI industry persuade data center opponents by getting rid of NDAs?

---

Amazon recently followed Microsoft's lead and said it will no longer use nondisclosure agreements when negotiating with local governments over data centers. The TechCrunch Equity podcast explored whether this shift toward transparency will be enough to address growing community opposition to AI infrastructure.

The episode featured a panel discussion examining why data center opposition has intensified, what role secrecy played in fueling distrust, and whether transparency measures can meaningfully change public perception. The consensus was nuanced: ending NDA practices is necessary but not sufficient.

Data center opponents have raised concerns including environmental impact (water usage, energy consumption), property value effects, noise pollution, and the concentration of AI infrastructure in select communities. Many of these concerns were amplified by the lack of transparency in negotiations.

TechCrunch's analysis noted that the AI industry is at an inflection point where community relations strategy may be as important to growth as model capability. Several major data center projects have faced multi-year delays or outright cancellation due to local opposition, with some communities organizing formal referendums on proposed facilities.

Microsoft's earlier announcement to end NDA practices was described as a "first step" by industry observers. Amazon's follow-through gives the practice broader legitimacy across the two largest cloud providers. Whether smaller providers and municipalities will adopt similar practices remains an open question.

The deeper challenge is that opposition to AI infrastructure often reflects broader skepticism about AI itself — not just concerns about specific facility siting. The industry's ability to rebuild public trust will likely require more than transparency in negotiations.
