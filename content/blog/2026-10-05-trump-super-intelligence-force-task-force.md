# Trump Unveils Super Intelligence Force: New Task Force to Coordinate US AI Policy

President Donald Trump has announced the formation of a new Super Intelligence Force, a dedicated government task force designed to ensure American leadership in artificial intelligence. The announcement, made via Truth Social on Sunday, marks the latest evolution in the Trump administration's approach to AI governance following a series of executive actions rebranding AI as "super intelligence."

---

## Task Force Leadership and Structure

The Super Intelligence Force will be led by National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton, effectively making him the administration's new AI czar. The Wall Street Journal reports that Federal Trade Commission Chair Andrew Ferguson, Undersecretary of War for Research and Engineering Emil Michael, and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor will serve as vice chairs.

"The Super Intelligence Force is tasked with coordinating the effort of the Federal Government to ensure that America continues to lead the World in Super Intelligence, which many say is bigger than the Industrial Revolution, and the Internet, and will protect the interests, and improve the lives, of all Americans," Trump said in his announcement.

The task force was first promised in September, when Trump responded to an ongoing AI safety debate that he has largely dismissed as a "hoax created by Radical Left Dumocrats" by saying he would form an AI Force and appoint an AI czar, noting that "Only High I.Q. individuals need apply!"

---

## 120-Day Report and Policy Framework

The Super Intelligence Force has been given 120 days to produce a comprehensive report on the risks and opportunities presented by AI. According to its charter, the task force will "develop plans for responding to SI-enabled threats to our society, while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation and competition."

This framing positions the task force as balancing national security concerns with economic competitiveness, a theme that has dominated recent policy discussions in Washington.

---

## Clayton's Position: "Not Being First" as Primary Risk

Jay Clayton, who previously chaired the Securities and Exchange Commission before returning to government service, has been outspoken about the competitive risks of pausing AI development. Earlier statements suggested it would be "irresponsible for the United States to back away from AI while countries like China advance." His comments to the WSJ reinforced this stance, identifying "not being first" as one of the biggest risks facing the nation.

This approach contrasts with the voluntary safety commitments signed by major tech executives at the White House earlier this week, which lack enforcement mechanisms.

---

## Background: From AI Safety Debate to Super Intelligence

The formation of the Super Intelligence Force follows a series of executive actions that have reshaped federal AI policy. An executive order officially redesignated AI as "super intelligence" in government terminology, accompanied by a gathering of tech executives including Mark Zuckerberg, Jeff Bezos, Elon Musk, and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei at the White House to sign what Trump called a "morally binding" safety pledge.

The pledge itself drew attention for an unusual typo in its text, underscoring the hastily arranged nature of the announcement. The practical implications of the commitments remain unclear, as they represent voluntary measures rather than binding regulation.

The Super Intelligence Force represents the operational arm of this new policy framework, translating the rebranding effort into an institutionalized government structure with specific deliverables and timelines.

---

## Developer Perspective

For developers and AI practitioners, the task force's 120-day report will be a critical document. The stated goal of "preventing overregulation and regulatory capture" suggests the administration aims to maintain a favorable environment for AI development, but the national security framing around "SI-enabled threats" indicates that certain applications and international collaborations may face increased scrutiny.

The involvement of the Undersecretary of War for Research and Engineering signals substantial defense sector investment in AI capabilities, potentially creating new contracting opportunities for AI developers with security clearances. Meanwhile, the FTC's participation hints at continued attention to competition and market dynamics in the AI sector.
