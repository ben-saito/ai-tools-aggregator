# Mapping the "Uncensored" AI Model Landscape: More Distributors Than Makers

---

Startup 10a Labs has mapped out the community that has formed around building and distributing uncensored AI systems, finding that the ecosystem has grown more diversified than commonly understood — with significantly more distributors than original model creators.

The term "uncensored" in AI refers to models that have not been aligned to refuse potentially harmful requests, or that have had their alignment deliberately removed or reduced. These models occupy a contested space in the AI ecosystem, positioned as alternatives for users who find commercial AI refusal mechanisms overly restrictive.

## The Ecosystem Structure

According to 10a Labs' analysis, the uncensored AI model space has developed a distinct structure:

**Model creators** are the relatively small number of organizations that train base models from scratch. These include academic groups, open-source collectives, and a handful of startups.

**Distributors** are more numerous — organizations that take existing models (often fine-tuned or quantized versions of open-weight models) and provide them through their own platforms, interfaces, or hosting services.

**Tooling providers** form a third layer, offering the infrastructure that makes it easier to deploy and use uncensored models.

## Why This Matters

The concentration of distributors relative to creators has implications for understanding the actual flow of uncensored AI capabilities. If most users access these models through a small number of hosting platforms, those platforms become chokepoints for any potential intervention.

It also raises questions about the sustainability of the ecosystem — if most players are intermediaries rather than primary innovators, where does the underlying capability development happen?

## Character of Uncensored Models

Most current uncensored models are fine-tunes of open-weight base models (particularly those from Meta's Llama series). They tend to excel at tasks involving creative writing, roleplay, and other applications where commercial AI services are most restrictive.

The technical capability gap between uncensored and aligned models has narrowed in recent months, as alignment techniques have become better understood and as open-weight base models have grown more capable independently.
