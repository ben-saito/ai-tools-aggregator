# The Download: Military Fake Blood, Future Batteries, and an OpenAI Engineer Arrest

---

This is today's edition of The Download, our weekday newsletter that provides a daily dose of what's going on in the world of technology.

**The US military's race to make fake blood.** In February 2007, a US military helicopter carrying blood to a field hospital was shot down in Iraq, killing all seven crew members. The incident illustrates a persistent challenge for military medicine: getting blood products to where they are needed under combat conditions is extraordinarily difficult. Blood expires. Cold chains break. Now, the US military is funding research into synthetic blood products that could survive the extreme conditions of the battlefield and be manufactured on demand. The work sits at the intersection of synthetic biology and battlefield medicine — and increasingly, AI is playing a role in accelerating the design of these products.

**Plus: The FBI has arrested an OpenAI engineer accused of snooping.** In other news, an OpenAI engineer was arrested by the FBI on charges related to unauthorized access to company systems. The case raises questions about insider threat controls at AI labs that are increasingly handling sensitive data and model weights.

**The Download: open model trade-offs and geothermal power's promise.** Yesterday's edition explored the ongoing debate about open-source versus closed-source AI models, examining the real security and safety trade-offs that companies like Meta and Mistral are navigating as they release increasingly powerful models. Also covered: how geothermal energy is emerging as a surprising contender in the race to power AI data centers.
