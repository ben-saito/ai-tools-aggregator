# SpaceX to Build Power Plant for xAI Colossus Data Centers While Unpermitted Turbines Remain

SpaceX is constructing a new power plant to serve xAI's Colossus data center cluster, but the company will not remove existing unpermitted turbines for at least another year, according to regulatory filings and reporting on the situation.

The power infrastructure push is directly tied to xAI's ambitious compute expansion plans. The Colossus cluster has been xAI's centerpiece for training and inference at scale, and the company has been racing to secure power capacity ahead of demand. The new power plant is part of that effort to ensure reliable electricity supply for the growing GPU cluster.

The complication involves turbines that were installed without complete regulatory approval. Rather than immediately decommissioning these units, SpaceX is seeking to keep them operational while the new power plant is completed. The timeline of approximately one year before full compliance is achieved reflects the practical reality of data center power infrastructure development.

For AI developers and infrastructure teams, the situation illustrates a recurring theme in the current AI compute boom: power availability has become a critical bottleneck. Companies building large-scale GPU clusters are finding that power procurement, permitting, and infrastructure development are as challenging as acquiring the GPUs themselves. The xAI-SpaceX relationship gives xAI an unusual advantage in this space through vertical integration.

The regulatory dimension also highlights how fast AI infrastructure deployment is outpacing the regulatory frameworks designed to govern it. Turbines and power plants that would normally undergo lengthy approval processes are being pushed through on compressed timelines, creating ongoing compliance questions that remain unresolved.
