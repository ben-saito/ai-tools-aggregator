# Patient-Zero Drill Exposes Gaps in Healthcare Facility Readiness

Health officials in New York conducted a regional test of healthcare facilities to assess their ability to identify and respond to a patient presenting with a mystery infection that could indicate an emerging pandemic threat. The results revealed significant weaknesses: 40% of facilities failed the drill.

The exercise centered on "professional patient actors" who staggered into healthcare facilities feigning symptoms of an H5 bird flu infection. To pass the test, a facility needed to mask the patient and place them in an isolation room within one minute.

---

## Drill Design and Scope

The drill ran between January and June 2026. Actors visited 73 healthcare facilities across New York — 52 in New York City and 21 in the rest of the state. Most visits were to emergency departments (42 of 73), with others targeting hospital outpatient clinics (19) and urgent care centers (12).

The drill evaluated whether staff could recognize unusual symptoms, implement appropriate infection control measures, and follow protocols for isolating potentially infectious patients.

---

## Key Findings

The results showed that 40% of facilities failed to meet the standard for early identification and isolation. Officials noted needs for improved training of healthcare workers to achieve faster recognition of potentially pandemic-capable pathogens.

"These drills identified needs for health care workers and health care facility staff member training to achieve earlier identification, masking, and isolation of patients presenting with unusual symptoms," officials said in a statement.

---

## Pandemic Preparedness Implications

The H5 bird flu scenario was chosen because avian influenza remains a top concern for pandemic preparedness experts. The drill highlights the operational challenges of responding to a novel respiratory pathogen — challenges that extend beyond clinical protocols to include supply chain management for personal protective equipment and coordination between facilities.

The findings suggest that even well-resourced healthcare systems in major metropolitan areas need ongoing training investments to maintain readiness for emerging infectious disease threats.

---

*Healthcare facility drill results were reported October 2, 2026.*
