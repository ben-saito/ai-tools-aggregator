# Trump Executive Order to Split MMR Vaccine Draws Sharp Opposition from Big Pharma

President Trump signed an executive order calling for splitting the combination measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine into three separate shots, a move that immediately drew fierce criticism from both the pharmaceutical industry and the medical community. The order, signed on August 11, 2026, would increase the number of shots children receive from two combination doses to six individual doses.

---

## The Order and Its Implications

Trump's executive order frames the change as a "gold standard" vaccine policy aimed at reducing the number of vaccines children receive. However, the mechanism to achieve this—splitting the MMR combination—would have the opposite effect in practice. The current MMR vaccine is administered in two doses, covering all three diseases. Splitting it into individual shots would require six doses to achieve the same coverage.

More critically, individual vaccines for measles, mumps, and rubella do not currently exist in the United States. Fulfilling the order would require pharmaceutical companies to invest heavily in developing, testing, and bringing to market entirely new vaccines—all based on no scientific reasoning, industry representatives pointed out.

---

## Industry Resistance

Within hours of the order being signed, PhRMA—the leading pharmaceutical industry group representing GSK, Sanofi, Pfizer, Merck, Johnson & Johnson, and Novo Nordisk—issued a statement calling the order a debasement of American vaccine policy.

PhRMA's Chief Medical Officer Michael Ybarra stated that previous federal vaccine recommendations "were built on decades of rigorous scientific research and public health expertise," and that changes should be based on "the same high standard of evidence."

"Simply importing recommendations from foreign countries wouldn't reflect gold standard science—it would mean accepting a lower bar of protection against diseases that remain serious and, in some cases, life-threatening for children in the United States," Ybarra said.

Merck and GSK, the two manufacturers of MMR vaccines used in the US, also issued rare public rebukes. Merck stated it supports vaccine policy "rooted in rigorous, evidence-based science" and pointed to "no published scientific evidence that shows any benefit in separating the combination MMR vaccine into three individual shots." GSK similarly noted that "There is no scientific evidence that separate measles, mumps, and rubella shots improve safety or efficacy compared to combined MMR."

---

## Medical Community Opposition

The American Academy of Pediatrics, which called the order "not only disheartening but dangerous," is already leading a lawsuit against previous changes to federal childhood vaccine recommendations under Trump and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. In March 2026, the organization, along with other medical groups, won a temporary injunction reversing most of those changes.
