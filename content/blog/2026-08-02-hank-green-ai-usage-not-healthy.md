# YouTuber Hank Green Says His AI Usage Is "Not Healthy"

Educational YouTuber Hank Green has publicly acknowledged that his relationship with AI chatbots has become unhealthy, saying the "dopamine loop" of interacting with large language models led him to accidentally include AI-generated content in a published video and prompted him to scale back his online presence.

The controversy began when viewers noticed Green used the phrase "I appreciate the pushback" in a Complexly video—an phrasing that appeared to be an AI chatbot's response rather than natural human dialogue. Green later confirmed on Reddit and X that he had used ChatGPT "for research on this script" while under deadline pressure.

---

## A Public Admission

In a detailed Reddit post, Green said he was "mortified" and acknowledged that his process had become "diluted" by AI assistance. "You need to know that my words are mine," he wrote. "I don't think that this hasn't been true, but I've been moving so fast that my own process isn't actually clear to me."

Green insisted he only used ChatGPT to locate academic papers and resources, and that all opinions and arguments in his videos remained his own. However, he conceded that the cumulative effect of AI use across his workflow had made it difficult to distinguish his authentic voice from AI-generated scaffolding.

---

## The Dopamine Problem

Green's most candid reflection focused on his psychological relationship with AI interaction itself. "The level of dopamine I've been getting from interacting with LLMs... from doing more and more and more—is not healthy for me or good for the world," he wrote. "It is careless, and has disconnected me from where people are on this."

He cited broader concerns about AI's climate impact and "the speed at which these companies are trying to consolidate economic power" as additional reasons for re-evaluating his use of the technology.

---

## Industry-Wide Pattern

Green's admission is notable given his position as one of the most prominent science communicators online. His honest self-assessment highlights a tension facing creators who use AI tools: efficiency gains and research acceleration come with risks to authentic voice and audience trust.

Green said he plans to pause or reduce posting frequency while he "come[s] to terms" with establishing clearer boundaries between AI-assisted research and his own original writing.
