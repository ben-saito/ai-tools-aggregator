# Stoke Space Raises New Funding as Reusable Rocket Ambitions Scale Up

Stoke Space, the aerospace startup pursuing fully reusable orbital rockets, has closed another significant funding round as it targets its first orbital launch. The company revealed plans for a larger rocket beyond its initial vehicle, signaling accelerating confidence from investors in its approach to reusable launch infrastructure.

---

## Reusability: The Full Stack Approach

Stoke Space's core thesis is that full reusability — not just first-stage recovery but complete vehicle reuse — is the only viable path to the launch frequency the space economy requires. The company has been developing its vehicles with this constraint baked in from the start, rather than adapting existing architectures.

Unlike companies that have demonstrated partial reusability, Stoke's designs target the complete lifecycle: landing, refurbishment, and relaunch without major vehicle-level disassembly. The technical challenge is substantial, requiring materials and systems that can withstand multiple atmospheric reentries and be turned around quickly.

---

## The Space Economy Bottleneck

The company's framing is direct: the space economy grows exactly as fast as launch capacity allows. Satellites for communications, Earth observation, and emerging applications require reliable, frequent access to orbit. Traditional expendable launch vehicles impose a ceiling on how rapidly the orbital infrastructure can expand.

Stoke joins a cohort of next-generation launch providers — including SpaceX, which has proven reusability at scale — working to remove that ceiling. The investor appetite for these bets reflects both demonstrated market demand and the increasingly clear economics of reusable launch.
