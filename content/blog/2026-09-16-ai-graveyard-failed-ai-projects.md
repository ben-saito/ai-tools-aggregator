# The AI Graveyard: A Running List of AI Projects That Did not Make It

Apple's repeatedly delayed Siri AI. OpenAI's messy "super app" launch. Humane's AI Pin. The Rabbit R1. A new TechCrunch analysis documents the growing list of high-profile AI failures — a graveyard of projects that missed expectations or shut down entirely.

---

## The List of AI Casualties

**Apple Siri AI** — When Apple unveiled Apple Intelligence in 2024, an improved Siri was one of the highlighted features. The pitch promised a Siri that could understand context and perform complex tasks. However, Apple repeatedly pushed back the release, citing engineering issues. Siri AI finally appeared in the iOS 27 beta in July 2026, rolling out to English-language users this month.

**Microsoft Recall** — Announced at Build 2024, Recall was pitched as an AI-powered "photographic memory" for Windows PCs. The announcement triggered immediate backlash over privacy and security concerns. Critics argued Recall could create a searchable archive of user activity. A cybersecurity researcher recently built a tool that could extract and display Recall data, and controversy has not subsided.

**OpenAI Super App** — OpenAI attempted to pull everything under one roof with a unified AI assistant experience. The redesign quickly drew criticism from users who found the new interface confusing and cluttered. OpenAI rolled back the change soon after.

**OpenAI Operator** — An AI agent that could browse the web and complete tasks on a user's behalf. OpenAI shut it down as the company increasingly integrated agentic capabilities directly into ChatGPT.

**OpenAI Sora** — The video-sharing platform struggled with high operating costs and user-retention challenges, shutting down in March 2026.

**Humane AI Pin** — One of the most well-known failures in AI hardware. Designed to provide AI features through a wearable device, the product struggled with performance. Humane warned customers to stop using its charging case due to safety issues.

**Rabbit R1** — Unveiled at CES 2024, the AI companion was designed to perform tasks on behalf of its owner. Early reviews described the device as unfinished with unreliable performance. The company has continued updating R1, repositioning it as a computer controller.

**Notion Mail** — Launched April 2025 as an AI-focused email product. The company noted declining user adoption and shut it down.

**Yupp** — A free playground letting users compare responses from hundreds of AI models side by side. At its peak, Yupp let users test more than 800 models. The platform shut down as larger platforms began offering similar experiences.

**Figgs AI** — Operated from 2023 to 2024, letting users create and interact with customizable AI characters for roleplay and storytelling. The platform representatives cited changing market dynamics.

**Relay** — A startup that was outpaced by bigger platforms and is currently struggling.

---

## Why So Many AI Failures

The pattern cuts across both startups and tech giants. Products were announced with ambitious promises, faced unexpected technical or market challenges, and either shut down or quietly folded back into existing platforms. The AI graveyard highlights the gap between what AI can theoretically do and what users actually find useful in practice.

Even OpenAI, one of the most valuable private companies in the world, has had its own share of casualties — multiple standalone apps shut down and features consolidated, alongside the abortive super app redesign.

---

## The Infrastructure Angle

The failures come amid a broader reckoning in AI infrastructure investment. While companies pour hundreds of billions into data centers, questions are mounting about whether the AI applications being built on top of that infrastructure will deliver the returns needed to justify the capital deployed. Some of the same dynamics that sank consumer AI products — overpromising, underdelivery — may be at play at the infrastructure level as well.
