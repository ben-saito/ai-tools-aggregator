# Meeting Notetaker Circleback Adds Free Tier to Attract More Customers

Circleback, a Y Combinator-backed meeting notetaker startup, has introduced a free subscription tier to let users experience its product before committing to a paid plan. The company faces intensifying competition from Granola, Read AI, Fireflies, Wispr, and Calendly's new note-taking tools.

---

## Free Tier Details

The free plan allows users to record meetings, access mobile and Apple Watch apps, use AI to query transcripts, and integrate with Linear and Slack. However, free users can only access their meeting history for the last 30 days. Paid plans starting at $14 per month (billed annually) unlock unlimited history, all integrations, and full API and MCP access. Circleback previously had no free tier, with plans starting at $20.83 per month.

---

## Company Background and Metrics

Founded in 2023 by Ali Haghani and Kevin Jacyna, Circleback raised $2.5 million in 2024. The company says it has been profitable since then and is currently generating run-rate revenue of over $1 million per employee with a team of eight people — roughly $8 million in total annual recurring revenue. Haghani said the company has seen significant user drop-off with its limited trial period, motivating the introduction of a free tier as a marketing substitute rather than spending on Google Ads or Meta Ads.

---

## Competitive Landscape

The meeting note-taker market has become increasingly crowded. In recent weeks, dictation app Wispr launched its own note-taker and scheduling tool, while Calendly added a similar capability. Dedicated players like Granola, Read AI, and Fireflies have raised substantial venture funding. Despite competing against much larger and better-funded companies, Haghani said Circleback has been consistently winning customers and is not immediately looking to raise additional capital given its growth trajectory.

---

## Technical Integration

Circleback differentiates through deep integration with developer and productivity tools. Its MCP (Model Context Protocol) access enables programmatic interaction with meeting transcripts, while Linear and Slack integrations position it for engineering and operations teams. The eight-person company's $8 million ARR implies strong per-employee productivity, a metric Haghani highlighted as evidence of efficient execution.
