# Researchers Use AI to Design Proteins That Cut Up Other Proteins

---

Researchers have used AI-based protein design tools to create novel enzymes capable of breaking peptide bonds that are typically stable for hundreds of years. The work, published in Nature Chemical Biology, demonstrates how AI can short-circuit the natural diversification process of proteases — the enzymes organisms use to catalyze the breakup of proteins.

A protein is a chain of amino acids held together by peptide bonds. On their own, those bonds are extremely stable, estimated to have a half-life of several hundred years. But in Earth's environment, most proteins don't last nearly that long because organisms have evolved specialized protein-digesting enzymes called proteases.

## Short-Circuiting Natural Evolution

The researchers took the catalytic core of two types of protease and replaced the entire rest of the protein with AI-designed sequences. The resulting enzymes could be tuned to cut specific proteins in half, and the researchers demonstrated their effectiveness by using them to cut proteins associated with neurological diseases.

The approach represents a significant advance in AI-driven protein engineering, moving beyond modifying existing proteins to entirely new designs that don't exist in nature. By using AI to explore the vast space of possible protein sequences, the researchers could create enzymes with precise functions targeted at specific disease-related proteins.

## Implications for Drug Development

This capability has immediate implications for drug development, where enzymes that can selectively cleave disease-related proteins could become the basis for new therapeutic approaches. The ability to design proteins with specific catalytic activities using AI represents a convergence of computational biology and synthetic chemistry that could accelerate the development of protein-based therapeutics.
