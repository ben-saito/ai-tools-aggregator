# Nvidia Spearheads Open Secure AI Alliance: 120+ Companies, Proposals Already Taking Shape

Just one week after forming, the Open Secure AI Alliance (OSAA) — an industry group spearheaded by Nvidia — has grown to over 120 companies and already has concrete proposals out for public comment, demonstrating unusual speed for an industry consortium.

The group developed its first working group, called the Shared AI Findings Exchange (SAFE), while members gathered at the Black Hat cybersecurity conference in Las Vegas this week. The Linux Foundation is managing the proposal submissions.

---

## What the Alliance Is Proposing

The initial proposals cover practical security measures: how to confidentially report AI cybersecurity incidents, alert those affected, and conduct blame-free analysis so the entire ecosystem can learn from vulnerabilities.

Members are also contributing open source technologies that could eventually coalesce into a practical framework for securing AI agents or defending against rogue AI attackers — such as the OpenAI model that recently infiltrated Hugging Face, a case that helped galvanize the industry response.

---

## Who's In, Who's Out

The alliance roster reads like a who's who of tech: Adobe, BlackRock, Cisco, Intel, Microsoft, and Visa are among the notable members. Hugging Face, Okta, Red Hat, and Amazon have all contributed specific technologies:

- **Nvidia**: open model family and Garak, an open source LLM vulnerability scanner
- **Okta**: agent identity technology
- **Red Hat**: agent governance tools
- **Amazon**: Strands Agents (open agent building tool) and the Cedar authorization language

However, some conspicuous names are absent: Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have not joined, despite OpenAI and Google signing the original open letter that preceded the alliance.

---

## Open Source as a National Security Argument

The OSAA's formation was triggered by the Trump Administration's consideration of banning Chinese open weight AI models. The alliance argues that openness is one of the most important paths to AI safety and security.

"Weight" in AI refers to the learned parameters of a model — open weight models like Meta's Llama series allow anyone to download, run, and modify the model. The concern is that Chinese labs could exploit or manipulate open weight models, but the OSAA's position is that restricting open source AI would weaken the U.S. ecosystem more than it would constrain adversaries.

As the Arcee chief technology officer put it: "Openness may be one of the most important paths to AI safety and security."

---

## AI Speeds Meet Policy Process

The alliance is operating at what participants call "AI speeds" — just weeks after the original open letter to the White House, the group already has working proposals, a dedicated forum, and significant technical contributions catalogued.

Whether the consortium model can sustain this pace — and whether OpenAI and Google will eventually join — remains to be seen. But for now, the OSAA represents the most concrete industry response to date to questions about how to secure the expanding frontier of AI agents deployed across enterprise environments.
