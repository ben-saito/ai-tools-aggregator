# Brian Chesky: AI Agents Need Their Own Operating System

In a new interview, Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky has outlined his vision for an "AI-native operating system" — a platform layer where AI agents can operate independently of existing app ecosystems. The remarks come as Airbnb integrates agentic AI features into its platform, but Chesky remains cautious about conversational interfaces that sacrifice conversion rates.

---

## Chesky's Vision: An OS for AI Agents

Speaking to TechCrunch, Chesky argued that the current smartphone paradigm — built around app downloads and siloed experiences — is poorly suited for an agentic AI future. Instead, he believes the industry needs an "AI-native operating system" where agents can discover, authorize, and execute tasks across services without user intervention at every step.

This vision aligns with Airbnb's broader product direction. The company recently added AI-powered search functionality that lets users enable a toggle for natural language property queries, with dynamic filters surfacing relevant amenities based on preference signals like "baby" or "ski equipment."

---

## The Airbnb Agent Strategy

Chesky framed Airbnb's agentic ambitions in terms of platform control. Rather than building a chatbot-first interface that might degrade the booking conversion rate — which he noted is the hard part of AI-powered search — Airbnb is introducing AI features incrementally within its existing UI framework.

"Anyone can vibe-code a search function, but to do something that doesn't kill conversion rate, that's the hard part," Chesky said, distinguishing Airbnb's approach from rapid agentic deployments by competitors.

Beyond search, Airbnb is using AI to generate comparative insights across wishlisted properties and expanding into adjacent services including meal delivery, laundry, baby-gear rental, ski and boat rentals, and enhanced grocery delivery in select markets.

---

## Consumer AI Beyond the App Store

The interview positions Chesky as a skeptic of the current app-store model for AI experiences. He noted that consumers increasingly want seamless, cross-platform agent interactions rather than managing multiple dedicated apps — a view that underpins Photon's messaging-layer thesis and Meta's App Store-topping Muse launch.

For Airbnb, the key question is how agents will discover, trust, and act on behalf of users in high-stakes transactions like travel bookings. Chesky's proposed OS layer would address authentication, authorization, and transaction guarantees across services — infrastructure that does not yet exist in commercial form.
