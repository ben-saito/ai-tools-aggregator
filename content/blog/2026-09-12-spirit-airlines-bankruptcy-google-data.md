# Panic Grows Over Spirit Airlines Bankruptcy Data Sale to Google — "Bankruptcy Cannot Become AI Data Grab"

As Spirit Airlines proceeds through bankruptcy proceedings, former technology provider Springshot filed an objection on September 10 with the bankruptcy court, warning that data that could be sold to Google may improperly include Springshot's intellectual property. The core issue is that among the data Spirit claims to hold, Springshot's proprietary technology and IP are likely co-mingled.

---

## Background of the Dispute

Springshot is an airline efficiency optimization platform startup founded in 2011. Its technology has been used by hundreds of airports globally and has supported Spirit Airlines' technology stack for the past three years.

As part of its bankruptcy proceedings, Spirit is preparing to auction off a large dataset, with Google emerging as a prospective buyer.

Springshot argues that the data sale agreement's terms are inadequate. Categories such as "productivity and collaboration data," "core business systems and business application data," and "workflow and process data" are vaguely defined. Even if these categories would sweep in Springshot's data, the agreement fails to clarify that third-party IP would be excluded.

---

## "Bankruptcy Cannot Become the New Land Grab for AI"

The most notable aspect of Springshot's filing is its argument that "bankruptcy cannot become the new land grab for AI." The company's CEO, Kreuzkamp, stated:

"Possession of IP is not ownership of IP. If the bankruptcy court sanctions this unauthorized acquisition and use of trade secrets, it risks setting a precedent where startups see massive amounts of IP transferred to the world's richest and most monopolistic companies via bankruptcy courts."

Google has stated it has no intention of using bankruptcy proceedings to acquire data, but Springshot's concerns are not easily allayed. The underlying fear is that Google, unlike other AI companies, could leverage large data assets to further strengthen its AI training competitive advantage.

---

## Court Proceedings

Springshot is requesting that the court pause the data sale until a transparent forensic process establishes what data Google would actually be acquiring. It remains unclear whether the bankruptcy court will grant this request.

Industry analysts note that the outcome of this case could set standards for how AI-related data is handled in bankruptcy proceedings going forward. With AI companies on high alert over rivals acquiring data through bankruptcy proceedings, the stakes are high.

---

## Google's Position

Google has not provided official comment on the transaction. The company has stated only that it is working cooperatively with the bankruptcy court to ensure the data sale proceeds appropriately.

---

## Implications for the AI Industry

This case is relevant to the AI industry in three key ways:

- **Bankruptcy procedures and AI data**: The phenomenon of bankrupt companies' data being used as training material for AI companies has emerged as a new front in AI data strategy
- **Intellectual property boundaries**: Cases are emerging where the boundary between a bankrupt company's client IP and its own IP becomes ambiguous
- **Regulatory needs**: Discussion is arising over whether bankruptcy courts need to regulate AI companies' acquisition of others' data
