# NHTSA Opens Investigation into comma.ai After Multiple Crashes

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) has opened an investigation into comma.ai, the aftermarket driver assistance company, following multiple crashes and injuries involving vehicles equipped with its devices. Federal regulators are aware of five crashes where cars using comma.ai devices hit slow or stopped vehicles.

---

## The Investigation

NHTSA's investigation focuses on the safety of comma.ai's advanced driver assistance systems, which are marketed as low-cost alternatives to OEM-provided features like Tesla's Autopilot. The agency is examining whether the company's technology properly handles scenarios with stationary or slow-moving vehicles.

Comma.ai sells a device that attaches to a car's windshield and uses camera-based machine learning to provide lane centering and adaptive cruise control. Unlike OEM systems, it relies on after-market hardware and software.

---

## Safety Concerns in Aftermarket ADAS

The investigation highlights broader questions about the safety of aftermarket advanced driver assistance systems. Regulators have grappled with how to oversee companies that retrofit autonomous driving features onto vehicles not designed for them.

Industry observers note that the incident underscores the challenge of deploying driver assistance in real-world conditions where stationary vehicles, debris, and edge cases are common.
