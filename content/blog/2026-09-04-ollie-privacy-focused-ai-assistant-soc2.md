# Ollie AI Assistant Pursues Privacy-First Strategy with SOC 2 Compliance

Ollie, a family-focused AI assistant, is positioning privacy as its primary differentiator in a crowded market, announcing SOC 2 compliance as a key milestone that sets it apart from competitors. The San Diego-based startup has raised $7.5 million in seed funding from Khosla Ventures and AI House, betting that trust and data sovereignty will win over consumers wary of handing over personal information to AI companies.

---

## SOC 2 Compliance as a Trust Signal

Ollie is among the first mainstream family-focused AI assistants to achieve SOC 2 compliance -- a framework that provides independent assurance a company is handling user data responsibly. The certification is more commonly associated with enterprise software vendors, making its adoption by a consumer-facing AI product notable.

"We fundamentally think that trust and privacy are absolutely imperative, and that is why our business model is a subscription, because we want our users to know that Ollie works for you," said the company's co-founder in a statement.

---

## The Competitive Landscape

Ollie enters a market with multiple text-based personal assistants, including Poke (acquired by Cognition), Fambot, Ohai, Folk, and Saner.ai, among others. These tools aim to integrate with users' daily lives through text-message interfaces, offering calendar management, task reminders, and general assistant functionality.

The company acknowledges that consumers face a tradeoff: personal AI assistants require access to meaningful personal data to be useful, but that same data could be used for model training, advertising, or other purposes users may not anticipate. Ollie's response is to promise it will not use customer data for training or share it with third parties, and to back that promise with a subscription revenue model.

---

## Funding and Growth

Compared to some competitors that have raised significantly larger rounds, Ollie's $7.5 million seed is modest. The company appears to be betting that its privacy-first positioning will resonate with a segment of users who have grown skeptical of free AI services built on data extraction.
