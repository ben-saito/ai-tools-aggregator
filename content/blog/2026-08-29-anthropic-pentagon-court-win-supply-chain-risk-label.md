# Anthropic Gets First Court Win Against Pentagon Supply-Chain Risk Label

A federal judge in California ruled on August 28, 2026, that the Trump administration's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was illegal. U.S. District Judge Rita Lin said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's labeling of Anthropic as a national security risk signified "unlawful retaliation" in violation of the First Amendment, and that the decision was "arbitrary and capricious." Lin also found Anthropic was denied due process under the Fifth Amendment.

---

## Background of the Dispute

Earlier this year, Hegseth and President Donald Trump labeled Anthropic a supply-chain risk and ordered all federal agencies to stop working with the company behind Claude. The dispute stemmed from Anthropic setting hard lines on safety guardrails that would prevent the Pentagon from using its models for fully autonomous weapons and mass surveillance of American citizens.

The Pentagon denied it would misuse Anthropic models and alleged that Anthropic was trying to control how the military used models it had purchased. Anthropic maintained that it would not provide models that could enable mass surveillance or lethal autonomous weapons.

---

## The Court's Finding

In her ruling, Lin stated that the government's "words and deeds confirm that the challenged actions were based on a desire to make a public example out of Anthropic for its 'arrogance' in criticizing the government." She pointed out inconsistencies, noting that Hegseth's proposition to apply the Defense Production Act to Anthropic would have meant the company was essential to national security rather than a threat to it.

This marks Anthropic's first court victory in what is now a two-part lawsuit against the Pentagon. A separate lawsuit continues in Washington addressing the core contractual and procurement issues between Anthropic and defense agencies.

---

## Industry Impact

The ruling is significant for the AI industry because it establishes that the government cannot arbitrarily label AI companies as supply-chain risks without due process. It also signals that courts may be willing to scrutinize the factual basis behind national security designations affecting AI developers. The case sets a precedent that AI companies can successfully challenge government actions they view as retaliatory or legally baseless.
