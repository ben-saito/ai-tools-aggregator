# SpaceX's Secret Foundry: Musk's Bet to Bypass the Turbine Blade Oligopoly

Elon Musk has confirmed that SpaceX is building a secret foundry in Bastrop, Texas to cast its own gas turbine blades and vanes — a manufacturing process currently controlled by only four companies worldwide, all of them at capacity through 2030. By bringing turbine blade production in-house, Musk says SpaceX can bring gas power online up to 18 months faster than the industry standard, potentially giving xAI a decisive advantage in the race to secure AI data center power infrastructure.

---

## The Casting Bottleneck

The core problem is that gas turbine blades operate at temperatures around 3,000 to 3,600 degrees Fahrenheit — roughly 800 degrees hotter than the melting point of the metal alloys used to make them. This is possible only because of internal cooling channels, thermal-barrier coatings, and the specific way each blade is cast as a single, unbroken crystal grown slowly inside a vacuum furnace. Just four companies worldwide have mastered this process at industrial scale, and all are effectively sold out through 2030 due to AI infrastructure demand, according to GE Vernova.

"SpaceX and Tesla are each building 100GW per year of solar production capacity as fast as possible," Musk wrote on X, "but natural gas will still be needed to supplement and bootstrap solar for several years. The limiting factor for natural gas turbine production is casting the blades and vanes. By doing in-house casting at SpaceX, we can accelerate natural gas turbines coming online by up to 18 months, which is a profound game-changer."

---

## Why This Matters for AI Infrastructure

GPU shortages remain an issue — Nvidia's newest Blackwell chips still carry lead times of several months — but a second constraint has emerged alongside it: the physical power grid. The International Energy Agency projects global data center electricity use will roughly double by 2030, and gas turbine makers are sold out through the decade. Building private gas-fired plants next to data centers, rather than waiting on the grid, has become the default strategy for hyperscalers including Amazon, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and Microsoft.

If SpaceX successfully brings its turbine blade foundry online, it would hold a manufacturing capability that every other AI infrastructure builder currently depends on a small oligopoly for — giving SpaceX and xAI an edge that well-funded but non-manufacturing competitors cannot quickly replicate.

---

## Pollution Concerns: The Fight Already Playing Out in Memphis

The other side of the coin is environmental impact. In Memphis, where xAI has run gas turbines to power its Colossus data centers since 2024, the NAACP has repeatedly accused the company of operating turbines without the permits or pollution controls required by federal law. The concern is that turbines emit smog-forming compounds and hazardous chemicals including formaldehyde — pollutants linked to asthma, respiratory disease, and certain cancers. University of Memphis researchers said in their own limited analysis that air pollution in the area grew "slightly worse" because of the data center.

In Virginia's "Data Center Alley," a study commissioned by the Piedmont Environmental Council using the EPA's COBRA health-impact model found that emissions from a single facility's eight gas turbines could reach more than 2.5 million people across multiple counties, causing an estimated 3.4 to 6.5 additional premature deaths annually, translating to $53 million to $99 million in health-related damages. The heaviest impact would fall on already-marginalized communities.

The same regulatory and health battles are playing out anywhere gas turbines have become the default solution for data center power shortages — and faster turbine deployment through SpaceX's foundry would mean more turbines coming online more quickly.
