# Meta Executive Departs for OpenAI Amid Intensifying India Competition

Sandhya Devanathan, Meta's top executive in India, is leaving the social media giant to join OpenAI, where she will oversee operations across Southeast Asia and Australia. The departure comes as Meta faces growing regulatory scrutiny in India, one of its largest markets.

Devanathan's move signals the intensifying competition for AI talent between established tech giants and frontier AI labs. OpenAI has been expanding its international presence as it seeks to compete with Chinese AI companies across Asian markets.

At Meta, Devanathan was instrumental in navigating India's complex regulatory environment, which has included debates over data localization, content moderation, and AI governance frameworks. Her departure leaves a gap as Meta continues to face pressure from Indian authorities over various platform practices.

---

## Strategic Implications

The executive shift highlights the magnetic pull that AI labs now exert on experienced technology executives. Meta, despite its own AI investments and the success of its Llama open-weight model series, has found itself competing with frontier labs for talent in critical markets.

OpenAI's expansion into Southeast Asia and Australia suggests the company is pursuing a broader international strategy beyond its core US market. India represents a particularly important battleground given its large population, growing AI talent pool, and regulatory environment that is still taking shape.

For Meta, the loss comes at a time when its AI glasses and Llama models are generating significant attention. The company recently updated its Ray-Ban smart glasses to limit nonconsensual recording, responding to privacy concerns that have drawn regulatory attention in multiple markets.

---

## Industry Context

The movement of executives from social media platforms to AI labs reflects broader shifts in the technology industry. As AI capabilities become increasingly central to technology strategy, companies with leading AI products are able to attract talent with experience in scaling platforms and navigating complex regulatory landscapes.

Meta has not yet announced Devanathan's replacement, and the company faces ongoing questions about its AI strategy beyond the Llama model family and its consumer AI hardware products.
