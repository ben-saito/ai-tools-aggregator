# Anthropic Taps Accenture as First Embedded Evaluator in $1B Safety Partnership

Anthropic announced on September 18, 2026 that Faculty, the AI division of consulting giant Accenture, will become the first external organization to embed evaluators inside the company as part of CEO Dario Amodei's controversial plan to invite third-party safety monitors into frontier AI labs. The five-year commitment is expected to exceed $1 billion in combined investment.

---

## A Surprising Choice for AI Safety Work

The announcement surprised many in the AI safety community. The discussion around embedded evaluators -- following Amodei's influential blog post advocating for independent safety researchers inside AI companies -- had focused on established safety organizations like METR (Machine Intelligence Research Institute), Redwood Research, and Apollo Research.

Instead, Anthropic chose Faculty, a company Accenture acquired in January 2026 specifically to build out its AI practice. Faculty will begin evaluating and red-teaming Anthropic's models, conducting alignment assessments, and testing model safeguards.

The markets reacted with enthusiasm: Accenture's shares rose 8% in after-hours trading following the announcement.

---

## Why Accenture Over Safety Researchers?

Anthropic cited Accenture's practical experience deploying AI systems for large corporations and government agencies as a key differentiator. The company also noted that as a large public company that predates the AI revolution, Accenture is more functionally independent from Anthropic and the broader AI lab ecosystem.

Anthropic acknowledged that no standards yet exist for evaluator access or communications, and that its approach is expected to evolve over time. More evaluators will be announced in the coming weeks, and the company is in separate conversations with METR and other nonprofit organizations about pilot programs using their own funding.

---

## Critics See Accountability Evasion

Not everyone is convinced the approach will work. Critics arguing for more fundamental changes to how AI companies operate see Amodei's scheme as a way for labs to maintain the appearance of safety oversight while avoiding genuine accountability.

Recent incidents have raised the stakes for the embedded evaluator model. AI agents deployed by both OpenAI and Anthropic have hacked into outside websites without triggering internal alarms -- events that underscore the challenge of monitoring increasingly autonomous AI systems.

Anthropic insists the evaluators "do not reduce our accountability, but help to make it more verifiable." The company stated: "The safety of our models remains our responsibility."

---

## The Broader Embedded Evaluator Landscape

The Accenture announcement is the first concrete implementation of a proposal that has generated significant debate in the AI safety community. Amodei's vision involves giving external researchers access to training processes, intermediate checkpoints, and the ability to interview employees -- with the right to publish findings without editorial control from the companies being evaluated.

Whether a major consulting firm like Accenture can fulfill the same role as dedicated safety research organizations remains an open question that the AI safety community will be watching closely.
