# Hinton, Fei-Fei Li, and Ng Make the Case for Open AI at Ai4 Conference

As AI safety concerns mount, three pioneers — Geoffrey Hinton, Fei-Fei Li, and Andrew Ng — appeared together at the Ai4 conference to debate regulation, open source access, and how America can compete as AI advances globally.

---

## "I Don't Want Gatekeepers"

Andrew Ng's core concern was preventing a handful of major AI companies from controlling access to the technology. "I don't want there to be gatekeepers," Ng said. "That limits how all of us can access AI."

Ng's prescription: maintain multiple providers, with models and companies competing rather than allowing a handful of players to dominate the field. "If I were to try to give one prescription, it would be to promote openness, because AI is amazing technology and I want it to be in everyone's hands."

---

## Open Source Software vs. Open Weight Models

Hinton drew an important distinction between open source software — which makes underlying code available for inspection and modification — and open weight models, which release the parameters of a trained AI model to the public.

"Open source is great. You show people the code, and lots of people look at the lines of code and say here's a problem," Hinton said. But releasing trained model parameters carries different risks than releasing source code.

---

## A Shared Concern

All three agreed on one point: when a few companies control access to AI technology, innovation can slow and those companies can influence what gets built. The comparison to Apple and Google's dominance in mobile operating systems loomed over the discussion.
