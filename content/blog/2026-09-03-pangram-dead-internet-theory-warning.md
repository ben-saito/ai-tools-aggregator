# Pangram CEO Warns: Internet is "Dangerously Close" to Dead Internet Theory

Max Spero, CEO of AI detection startup Pangram, has warned that the internet is "dangerously close" to fulfilling the "dead internet theory" — the hypothesis that most online content is now generated by bots rather than humans. Speaking on the TechCrunch podcast, Spero argued that the proliferation of AI-generated text and images across job applications, product reviews, and insurance claims is accelerating the erosion of online trust.

The "dead internet theory" is a conspiracy theory positing that most internet content is generated by bots and that human activity online is largely simulated. While the theory has historically been dismissed as paranoid, Spero argues that recent advances in AI generation have made the distinction between human and AI-generated content increasingly difficult to detect.

"The internet has a trust problem," Spero said. "It's not just because social media feeds are filling up with AI slop. AI-generated text and images are now making their way into job applications, product reviews, and even insurance claims, leaving platforms and users alike scrambling to figure out what's real."

---

## Why AI Detection Is Harder Than "Real or Fake"

Pangram's core product is an AI detection system designed to identify AI-generated content across multiple modalities. Unlike simple watermark-based detection systems that rely on statistical artifacts left by generative models, Pangram's approach attempts to analyze the semantic coherence and contextual patterns that distinguish human writing from AI output.

Spero explained that the challenge is fundamentally harder than the "real or fake" framing suggests. "The question isn't just whether something was generated by AI," he said. "It's whether the content is authentic — whether it reflects genuine experience, real opinions, and authentic communication. AI can now generate text that is contextually appropriate and semantically coherent, but it lacks the lived experience that gives human communication its texture."

The detection problem is compounded by the fact that AI systems are increasingly being fine-tuned to evade detection. Just as antivirus companies face adversarial evasion techniques from malware authors, AI detection systems must contend with models specifically optimized to produce outputs that appear human.

---

## Implications for Online Trust

The erosion of online trust has practical consequences beyond philosophical concerns about authenticity. Job applications containing AI-generated cover letters undermine the hiring process, product reviews that are AI-generated distort consumer purchasing decisions, and AI-generated insurance claims could fraudulently obtain payouts based on fabricated incident reports.

Pangram is one of several startups that have emerged to address the AI detection problem, including companies focused on specific verticals like academic integrity, content moderation, and financial fraud detection. The market for AI detection technology is expected to grow as regulatory requirements and platform policies increasingly mandate disclosure of AI-generated content.

---

## Technical Approaches to Detection

Current AI detection approaches fall into several categories: statistical watermark detection (identifying patterns introduced by specific generative models), perplexity-based analysis (measuring how unexpected or "surprising" text is to language models), and semantic coherence analysis (examining whether content exhibits the logical inconsistencies typical of AI-generated text).

Spero suggested that no single detection method is sufficient on its own. "The most robust detection systems combine multiple signals," he said. "But the fundamental challenge is that AI systems are getting better faster than detection systems can adapt. It's a cat and mouse game, and right now the mice are winning."
