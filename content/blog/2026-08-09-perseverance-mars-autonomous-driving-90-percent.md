# NASA's Perseverance Rover Logs 90% Autonomy on Mars, Sets New Distance Record

*NASA's Perseverance rover has driven 45.16 km across Mars with 90% autonomy, eliminating the need for Earth-based navigation commands for most of its journey*

---

## Overview

NASA's Perseverance rover has achieved a new distance record on Mars, traversing 45.16 km (28.06 miles) across the Martian surface since landing in February 2021. What sets this record apart is that approximately 90 percent of that distance was covered autonomously, without waiting for navigation commands from Earth.

---

## How Autonomy Changed Mars Exploration

The key enabling technology is Perseverance's auto navigation system, according to Steven Lee, Perseverance project manager at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

Like self-driving cars on Earth, Perseverance uses sophisticated onboard cameras to image surrounding terrain, which are then processed by its Vision Compute Element. This system allows the rover to navigate independently rather than waiting for Earth to send navigation instructions.

By contrast, Curiosity — Perseverance's predecessor launched nine years earlier — had similar imaging capability but limited onboard computing power, requiring much more Earth-based guidance.

---

## Scientific Benefits

The autonomy has significantly improved Perseverance's operational efficiency. With less time spent waiting for Earth commands, the rover has been able to cover more ground and spend more time on scientific investigation.

"The auto navigation system is very enabling for the science," said Vivian Sun, the mission's deputy project scientist.

Perseverance is studying some of the oldest rocks in the Solar System in Jezero Crater, searching for signs of past microbial life as part of the Mars 2020 mission. The rover also collects and caches rock samples intended for eventual return to Earth.
