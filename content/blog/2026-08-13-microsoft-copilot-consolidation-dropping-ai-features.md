# Microsoft Copilot Consolidation: Dropping AI Podcasts, Group Chats, Deep Research

Two years after positioning AI as a "generational shift" worth leading, Microsoft is folding its consumer and business Copilot apps into one, and ditching a raft of underperforming AI features by August 18, 2026.

---

## Personal and Professional AI Use Overlap — and Copilot Was Too Complex

Microsoft will merge the consumer-facing Copilot app with Microsoft 365 Copilot, acknowledging that the boundary between personal and professional AI use has blurred. The prior setup — separate apps competing with ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini — was too fragmented to win users.

The consolidation follows a broader industry trend. Claude merged Cowork into Chat. OpenAI folded Operator into ChatGPT. Google stitched deep research and web browsing into Gemini. The AI app market is simplifying.

---

## Features Being Dropped

Consumers lose access to:

- **Group Chats** — shared AI conversation threads
- **AI-generated podcasts** in Copilot
- **Copilot Labs** experimental features
- **Deep Research** (consumer tier)

For professional users, Microsoft will offer Researcher as a replacement for Deep Research functionality. All files from the standalone Copilot app migrate to OneDrive.

The company is also retiring Mico, an animated floating blob character that was essentially Clippy reimagined as an AI — widely seen as emblematic of Copilot's identity confusion.

---

## Internal Pressure: "Earn the Right to Exist"

In July 2026, EVP Jacob Andreou, who oversees Copilot, circulated an internal memo stating that Copilot needed to "earn the right to exist" — a blunt acknowledgment that the product had lost its way. The cleanup is a direct response.

The August 18 deadline marks the end of the transition period.

---

## Context: Broader AI App Consolidation

The move fits a pattern. The AI assistant market has shifted from launching dozens of specialized features toward fewer, more capable integrated products. Microsoft is catching up to what competitors already realized: users want one good tool, not many mediocre ones.
