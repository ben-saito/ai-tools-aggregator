# Why We Cannot Help Treating AI Like It Is Human

---

## The Psychology of Anthropomorphizing Robots and LLMs

Walking through MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Lab (CSAIL), TechCrunch contributor Jason Deegan found himself instinctively shaking the hand of a Unitree humanoid robot and saying "Hello, sir, it's nice to meet you." He knew the robot was not sentient. He shook its hand anyway.

This anecdote opens a broader reflection on why humans so readily project human traits onto AI systems — and what the consequences might be as these systems become more sophisticated.

## Sherry Turkle's Research on Artificial Intimacy

The piece connects Deegan's experience to the work of MIT professor Sherry Turkle, who has studied human-computer relationships since the first home computers. Her new book, "Artificial Intimacy," explores how relational artifacts — from early chatbots to modern LLMs — trigger care-giving responses in humans that we are neurologically wired to provide.

"When we are drawn into even the most primitive exchanges with a relational artifact, we believe it cares for us," Turkle writes. "And we are wired to care for it in return."

Turkle's research suggests this tendency runs deeper than simple confusion between digital and biological systems. The impulse to treat AI as a social agent may be a fundamental feature of human cognition that AI systems increasingly exploit.

## Implications for AI Development

The piece notes that even AI researchers at major labs sometimes describe their models in terms that imply agency, intention, or emotional states — language that may reflect not just technical shorthand but genuine conceptual slippage. As AI systems become more capable of maintaining coherent, contextually appropriate conversations, the threshold for treating them as social agents continues to lower.
