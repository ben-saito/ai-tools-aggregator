# Oracle Issues Force Majeure Notice on New Mexico Stargate Data Center

Oracle has sent a force majeure notice to Blue Owl Capital regarding its New Mexico Stargate data center, citing construction delays tied to energy supply issues. The notice, reported by Bloomberg and confirmed by CNBC, would allow Oracle to delay payments should the facility miss its 2028 target to come online.

---

## What Is Force Majeure and Why Does It Matter Here

Force majeure clauses excuse a party from contractual obligations when events outside its control interfere. In this case, Oracle is not seeking to exit as the campus's main tenant. Instead, the notice provides legal cover to推迟 payments if the project falls behind schedule.

Project Jupiter, located in Santa Teresa, New Mexico, is part of the broader Stargate AI infrastructure initiative announced by the Trump administration in 2025. The campus is designed to handle 2.45 gigawatts of power, with Oracle serving as the primary tenant building the facility for OpenAI.

---

## Energy Supply: The Core Problem

The Stargate data center has faced a string of setbacks, many tied to its unconventional energy setup. The campus was originally planned to run on 2.2 gigawatts of natural gas generation, which would have produced approximately 14 million tons of CO2 emissions per year, according to Michael Thomas, CEO of Cleanview Data.

The current plan relies on gas-powered fuel cells from Bloom Energy. This makes a reliable gas supply central to the timeline — and a shortage or delivery issue can cascade into delays across the entire project.

Oracle maintains it does not expect a delay. "Project Jupiter remains on our planned schedule," the company said in a statement to CNBC. "We are fully committed to New Mexico and confident in our path forward."

Blue Owl Capital, whose unit received the notice, said the financial commitments to the multi-year project remain unchanged.

---

## The Broader AI Infrastructure Challenge

The notice arrives as AI data center construction faces growing scrutiny nationally. Building capacity for customers like OpenAI requires enormous capital outlays and complex coordination across energy suppliers, construction firms, and technology providers.

The force majeure filing highlights the tension between the aggressive timelines promised by AI companies and the realistic constraints of large-scale infrastructure development. The 2028 target gives Oracle and its partners a defined window — but energy supply chain issues have already caused the project to veer off course.
