# OpenAI Reportedly Completes $7 Billion Employee Tender Offer at $852B Valuation

OpenAI has completed a $7 billion employee tender offer, buying back shares from employees at a valuation of $852 billion — the same as its most recent fundraising round in March, which added $122 billion to the company's war chest.

The deal, reported by Bloomberg, comes as OpenAI also filed confidentially with the Securities and Exchange Commission in June to prepare for a potential IPO later this year. However, the tender offer suggests that an IPO may not be forthcoming soon.

---

## Providing Liquidity to Employees

With many tech companies remaining private longer than previous generations of startups, private tender offers have proven a useful way for firms to allow employees to realize the value of their stock compensation without the complexities that come with a public offering.

OpenAI did not respond to a request for comment by publication time.

---

## Sam Altman: "We Did Not Have Our Best 12 Months"

Last month, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman wrote that "we did not have our best 12 months ever, which is mostly my fault." The tender offer comes amid a period of significant internal and external scrutiny of the company's direction and pace of development.

The tender offer size — $7 billion — is substantial, representing one of the largest liquidity events for employees of a private company in the AI sector. It signals that OpenAI's secondary market valuation remains high despite competitive pressure from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta.

---

## IPO Preparations vs. Tender Offers

The dual signals of IPO preparation and employee liquidity via tender offer reflect a broader tension in the AI industry. While companies prepare for potential public listings to satisfy investor demands for liquidity, they simultaneously use tender offers to retain talent by giving employees ways to cash out without requiring an IPO.

OpenAI's March 2026 funding round reportedly valued the company at $852 billion, making it one of the most valuable private companies in the world. The consistency of this valuation across both the fundraising round and the current tender offer suggests investor confidence in the company's trajectory, even as the competitive landscape intensifies.
