# Hyperscalers' Natural Gas Bet: AI Data Centers Face Energy Price Risk

After years of investing heavily in wind and solar, the major hyperscalers—Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft—are making an unexpected pivot to natural gas. A new research report from energy firm Noreva suggests this bet could backfire badly, with natural gas prices potentially tripling in some parts of the United States as AI-driven demand collides with constrained supply and rising LNG exports.

---

## The Gas Rush

Cheap natural gas has driven hyperscalers to lock up significant power capacity. In March 2026, Meta announced plans to build a 7.5-gigawatt natural gas power plant in Louisiana to serve its Hyperion data center. Shortly after, Microsoft and Google each unveiled plans for gigawatt-scale gas plants in Texas. Amazon rounded out the wave with a 7.6-gigawatt gas plant, also in Texas.

For companies that historically avoided large capital expenditures in physical infrastructure, the AI data center boom has pushed them deep into energy markets—territory where they have limited experience.

"They're doing things that are not normal for an off-taker to do," said Peter Gardett, CEO of Noreva, noting that investors have expressed surprise at the level of natural gas price risk hyperscalers are willing to take on.

---

## The Price Shock Scenario

Noreva forecasts natural gas prices could surge above $10 per million BTUs at certain trading hubs—up from today's range of $2 to $4.50 per million BTUs, with Henry Hub currently priced just under $3. The widely traded futures market currently shows stable prices, suggesting the market doesn't anticipate major shifts. Gardett isn't convinced.

"I think everyone in the energy markets has been lulled into a sense that gas prices can't go up," Gardett told TechCrunch. "You just need simple arithmetic to get to a much tighter gas market than you were in just a few years ago."

The financial implications are significant. Fuel represents roughly half the operating cost of electricity generation at large power plants. A doubling or tripling of natural gas prices could substantially increase the operating costs of "bring your own power" AI data centers—facilities designed to operate independently of the grid.

---

## Potential Consequences for AI Economics

Higher energy costs could flow directly into AI service pricing. Token costs for inference workloads might rise, or hyperscalers could be forced to connect to the broader electrical grid—potentially driving up electricity prices for other consumers in the process.

The timing is particularly delicate. AI inference workloads are already under pressure to become more cost-effective, and energy costs represent a major component of data center operating expenses. A sustained natural gas price surge would fundamentally alter the economics of AI service deployment at scale.

For now, futures markets remain calm. But if Noreva's analysis holds, hyperscalers may find their natural gas bet to be considerably more expensive than they planned when they locked in these deals.
