# Neocloud Lambda Secures $1B in Debt to Buy More AI Chips

Lambda, an AI cloud company that buys computing chips and rents them out to businesses, has raised $1 billion in private, short-dated debt to buy Nvidia's AI chips that it will lease to Microsoft, Bloomberg reports.

The terms of the deal, which Bloomberg says was arranged by JP Morgan Chase, signal that Lambda is betting it will be able to quickly deploy the chips and start generating revenue from them, letting it repay the debt fairly quickly using that incoming cash.

---

## Latest in a String of Loans

This is the latest in a series of loans Lambda is using to fund GPU infrastructure for specific customers. In May 2026, it closed a $1 billion secured credit facility, and this week it announced the closing of a $926 million loan to fund Nvidia GB300 GPUs — one of Nvidia's newest chip models — for a deployment it's under contract to provide to Nvidia.

The $1 billion private debt deal comes as Lambda is reportedly in talks for a $3 billion pre-IPO round. The company last November raised $1.5 billion in venture capital at a $5.43 billion post-money valuation, per PitchBook data.

---

## AI Infrastructure Debt Boom

Lambda isn't the only company relying on debt to fund the AI boom. According to data Bloomberg compiled, banks and tech companies have raised over $400 billion in AI-related debt globally in 2026 so far.

This reflects the enormous capital requirements of building AI infrastructure. GPU clusters, cooling systems, and land for data centers require billions in upfront investment, and debt financing allows companies to spread that cost while scaling rapidly.

---

## Growing AI Cloud Market

Lambda competes with other AI cloud providers like CoreWeave, Cirrascale, and Groq Cloud. The company's business model centers on purchasing large quantities of Nvidia GPUs and leasing access to them on a contract basis.

The deal with Microsoft is notable because it shows major hyperscalers are willing to sign multi-year lease agreements for GPU capacity — effectively outsourcing some of their chip procurement to specialized cloud providers.

---

## Nvidia's Position

For Nvidia, the proliferation of AI cloud providers creates a steady demand for its chips regardless of which company ends up winning the AI race. Whether customers are building models, running inference, or leasing capacity to third parties, they need Nvidia GPUs.

The company's H100, H200, and upcoming GB300 chips remain the industry standard for AI training and inference workloads.
