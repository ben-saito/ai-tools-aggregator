# Bill Gates Proposes Robot Tax and "Human Reserved" Jobs to Mitigate AI Labor Impacts

Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates published a lengthy essay on his Gates Notes site on August 26, 2026, laying out specific policy proposals to address AI's labor market disruption.

---

## Gates's Position on AI Development

Gates aligns broadly with the "Responsible AI" camp, expressing skepticism that the "Pacing the Frontier" open letter—signed by AI employees pushing for an industry slowdown—would be effective. At the same time, he voiced enthusiasm for AI's benefits to science and healthcare, while acknowledging serious concerns about workforce displacement.

---

## The "Robot Tax" Proposal

One of Gates's more novel ideas is a robot tax. Currently, employers pay payroll taxes on human wages, but robots can typically be written off immediately as a business expense. Gates argues this creates an uneven playing field that accelerates job displacement.

A robot tax would close this gap, effectively raising the cost of AI adoption and slowing displacement.

---

## "Human Reserved" Job Designation

Gates also proposes setting aside certain roles as "Human Reserved"—essentially barring AI from specific tasks by law. The goal would be to protect roles where AI-driven displacement would be economically devastating and where retraining options are limited.

"We might set something aside as Human Reserved for economic reasons," Gates wrote, citing roles where the social cost of displacement would exceed the productivity gains.

---

## Industry Impact

Both proposals would significantly dent the profits of major AI labs, which may explain why they have not gained traction until now. Whether policymakers can overcome industry opposition remains an open question.
