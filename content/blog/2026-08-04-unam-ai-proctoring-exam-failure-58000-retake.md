# UNAM AI-Proctored Exam Failure: 58,000 Students Must Retake Test After Suspicious Score Surge

Mexico's largest university has ordered a full retest for roughly 58,000 applicants after an AI-supervised remote entrance exam produced wildly anomalous results — with top scores jumping fivefold compared to previous years.

---

## The Anomalous Results

The Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM) administers a standardized entrance exam to roughly 100,000 applicants annually. Between 2021 and 2025, just 3.5 percent of test takers scored 100 or more out of 120 questions. This year, that figure surged to 16.3 percent. At the highest score range (110+), the rate jumped from 0.9 percent to 5.5 percent — a more than sixfold increase.

The exam was administered remotely. Applicants were required to install LockDown Browser from Respondus (which locks down the browser environment) and a proctoring system from Territorium that monitored test-takers via webcam, watching for signs of impersonation, phone or earphone use, and leaving the frame.

---

## The Investigation

The surge in top scores triggered accusations of widespread cheating. UNAM appointed a commission of experts — formally named "la Comisión Técnica de Personas Expertas para la Revisión del Proceso de Selección de Ingreso a Licenciatura para el Ciclo Escolar 2026-2027/1" — to investigate.

The commission's findings, due to the nature of the exam format, could not pinpoint exactly how cheating occurred. The exam was multiple-choice, making it difficult to detect typical AI-cheating patterns like instantly pasted complete answers. Tips circulating online before the test advised students to position monitors outside the webcam frame where they could access ChatGPT or other AI models. UNAM canceled nearly 2 percent of exams for conduct violations, but the system clearly failed to catch the majority of cheating attempts.

The commission recommended a control exam: all affected applicants — roughly 58,000 people — must retake the test in person, supervised by humans. UNAM's rector apologized to honest applicants who did nothing wrong.

---

## What UNAM's Case Reveals About AI Proctoring

The UNAM incident is a concrete illustration of the gap between what AI proctoring systems promise and what they deliver. Both Respondus LockDown Browser and Territorium's AI monitoring are commercial products used by universities worldwide. Yet UNAM's experience suggests that determined test-takers can circumvent both.

AI expert Rafael block commented on the difficulty of detecting AI-assisted cheating in this format. With tips circulating before the exam and a remote format that allowed monitor repositioning, students had ample opportunity to use ChatGPT or other AI models for answers.

The broader implication: remote proctoring by AI may be fundamentally inadequate for high-stakes, multiple-choice assessments. Universities and testing bodies relying on these systems may be getting false assurance.

---

## Affected Applicants Face Months of Uncertainty

The retest will apply not only to those who secured admission based on this year's exam, but to everyone who would have been admitted based on minimum passing scores in their program of study since 2021. Approximately 58,000 people's admissions now depend on a future in-person exam, for which dates have not yet been set.

UNAM described the retest as "necessary to give certainty and guarantee equity in admissions." For the thousands of honest applicants caught in this collapse of exam integrity, the delay and uncertainty are the direct cost of AI proctoring's failure.
