# DC Circuit Court Rules Pentagon Can Blacklist Anthropic

In a 2-1 ruling issued by the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit on September 25, 2026, a panel of judges upheld the Trump administration's ability to blacklist Anthropic from federal contracts. ## The Ruling

The appellate court found that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth did not exceed his authority under the Supply Chain Security Act or the Constitution when ordering federal agencies to stop using Anthropic's products. The court acknowledged the case "raises profoundly difficult questions about the appropriate military uses of an almost unimaginably powerful new technology."

The ruling centered on which law applies to the blacklisting decision. A district court in California had previously ruled the action violated 10 U.S.C. Section 3252, which limits supply chain risks to malicious actions by adversaries. However, the DC Circuit reviewed the decision under a different statute, 41 U.S.C. Section 4713, which carries fewer restrictions.

---

## Anthropic's Response

"We respectfully disagree with the court's decision," an Anthropic spokesperson told CNBC. "Another federal court has already held the government's parallel designation unlawful. We remain confident in our position and are considering all options, including further review."

Anthropic may seek an en banc review by all appeals court judges or appeal to the Supreme Court. The company sued the Trump administration in March 2026 after being designated a supply chain security risk and banned from federal and defense contracting.

---

## Split Legal Outcomes

This ruling creates a split in federal courts on the Anthropic blacklisting question. The Northern District of California ruled in August 2026 that the blacklisting was illegal retaliation under the First Amendment, finding that the action was punishment for Anthropic's refusal to support lethal autonomous warfare.

The DC Circuit did not dispute the district court's primary finding but said the appeals court has exclusive jurisdiction to review decisions made under Section 4713. Congress specifically granted the DC Circuit this authority.

---

## Background

The Trump administration blacklisted Anthropic after the company refused to enable certain Claude features for autonomous military applications. Anthropic has maintained that its models are designed with safety guardrails that prevent use in lethal autonomous weapons systems.

The blacklisting has significant financial implications for Anthropic, which was valued at $18.5 billion following its latest funding round.
