# I Created an Interactive Digital Avatar of Myself -- and You Can Talk to It

A TechCrunch writer details their experience with Synthesia's new interactive avatar product, exploring the implications of AI-powered digital twins for enterprise training and personal representation.

---

## The Offer I Couldn't Refuse

When Synthesia invited me to their new office opening in New York this September, I didn't hesitate when they asked if I wanted my own AI avatar. The U.K.-based startup, known for its enterprise-focused digital avatar platform, had just launched a product called Roleplay Sessions that lets employees practice difficult conversations -- performance reviews, sales calls, sensitive HR situations -- with an AI version of themselves as the conversation partner.

I said yes. Of course I would.

---

## Building My Digital Twin

To create my avatar, I entered a mini film studio tucked inside Synthesia's office. The process involved taking numerous photos and capturing a two-minute voice recording. The team then trained my interactive avatar using a combination of AI models: a voice-to-text model converts spoken words into text, an agentic language model interprets the text and can take actions based on it, and a text-to-speech model generates the avatar's responses.

The result is a digital twin that can discuss specific topics -- in my case, venture fraud -- and interact with users in real time.

---

## Mixed Feelings About AI Clones

My experience with the avatar left me with complicated feelings. On one hand, the technology is impressive -- the avatar captures mannerisms and voice characteristics with surprising accuracy. On the other hand, creating an AI version of yourself raises uncomfortable questions about authenticity, consent, and what it means to delegate your voice to a machine.

Synthesia positions Roleplay Sessions as an enterprise training tool, allowing employees to rehearse high-stakes conversations in a safe environment. The product represents a significant step in making AI avatars practical for professional use cases, moving beyond novelty demonstrations toward genuinely useful applications.

---

## The Broader Trend

Interactive avatars represent a convergence of several AI capabilities: speech recognition, natural language understanding, voice synthesis, and video generation. As these technologies mature, the line between interacting with a person and interacting with their AI representation continues to blur.

For enterprises, the appeal is clear: scalable training, consistent messaging, and the ability to practice difficult conversations without another human in the loop. For individuals, the implications are more personal -- what does it mean to have an AI version of yourself that can represent you in your absence?

---

## Looking Ahead

The venture fraud use case is telling. Training an avatar to discuss financial fraud requires a specific knowledge base and the ability to engage thoughtfully with complex questions. The fact that my avatar could be trained on this narrow topic suggests the technology is moving toward genuine task completion rather than simple pattern matching.

Whether that represents progress or a new set of risks remains to be seen.
