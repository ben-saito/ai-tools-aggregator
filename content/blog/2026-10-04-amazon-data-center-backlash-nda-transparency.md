# Amazon data center backlash: AWS CEO breaks silence on NDAs and community impact

Amazon Web Services CEO Matt Garman has responded to growing data center backlash, announcing that Amazon no longer uses non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) in conversations with communities hosting data center facilities. The statement marks a shift in how Amazon approaches the public and regulatory scrutiny surrounding its infrastructure expansion.

---

## The backlash against data centers

Environmental activist Erin Brockovich recently identified NDAs as one of the primary complaints she hears about data center developments. Communities near proposed or existing facilities have raised concerns about noise, water consumption, energy demand, and lack of transparency in approval processes.

In response, New York state announced a one-year moratorium on permits for large data centers, and Garman noted that more than 100 similar moratoriums are currently under consideration across the United States. The scale of opposition has forced major technology companies to reconsider their approach to community engagement.

Garman's blog post, while brief, attempted to reframe data centers as positive contributors to local communities, arguing that facilities bring economic benefits and technological infrastructure. However, the lack of detailed responses to specific concerns has drawn criticism from environmental groups and local officials.

---

## The NDA question

Non-disclosure agreements have been a flashpoint in the debate over data center expansion. Critics argue that NDAs prevent communities from understanding the full scope of what they are agreeing to, particularly regarding environmental impact assessments and long-term operational commitments.

Amazon's decision to abandon NDAs represents a notable reversal. The company had previously used such agreements to restrict what local officials and residents could disclose publicly about data center plans, a practice that opponents said created an imbalance in information access.

The moratorium movement shows no signs of slowing. With more than 100 proposed restrictions pending nationwide, technology companies face increasing pressure to demonstrate that data center growth can coexist with community interests and environmental protection.

---

## Industry implications

The tech industry's approach to data center infrastructure is under broader scrutiny as AI workloads drive unprecedented demand for compute capacity. Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and other providers are racing to build new facilities, often in regions with favorable tax incentives and electricity costs.

Garman's statement suggests Amazon recognizes that its standing with regulators and local communities may depend on greater transparency. Whether the removal of NDAs alone will satisfy critics remains uncertain, but it signals a willingness to engage on terms that opponents have demanded.
