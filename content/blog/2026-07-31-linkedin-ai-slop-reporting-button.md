# LinkedIn adds a button to report AI-generated "slop"

LinkedIn is introducing new ways to reduce low-quality AI-generated posts, including a "seems like AI slop" reporting option. The professional network is also replacing its own AI writing feature with a proofreading tool, signaling a shift away from AI-generated content generation toward AI-assisted editing.

---

## The problem with AI-generated professional content

LinkedIn has seen an influx of AI-generated posts designed to boost engagement rather than provide genuine professional insight. These posts often follow a recognizable pattern: generic advice, motivational clichés, and engagement-baiting phrasing that AI-generated content excels at producing at scale.

The new reporting option allows users to flag content that appears to be AI-generated slop — low-effort content created primarily for algorithmic amplification rather than professional value. LinkedIn's trust and safety team will review these reports and take action against repeat offenders.

---

## From generation to proofreading

Perhaps more significant is LinkedIn's decision to replace its standalone AI writing assistant with a proofreading tool. Rather than generating posts from scratch, the new tool helps users refine and edit their own writing. This represents a philosophical shift: LinkedIn is acknowledging that AI-generated content, even when polished, often lacks the authenticity that professional networking demands.

The proofreading-first approach also sidesteps some of the liability concerns around AI content generation — if professionals are still writing their own posts and only using AI for grammar and clarity checks, the platform avoids the perception that it is encouraging inauthentic engagement.

---

## Platform moderation in the AI era

This move reflects broader tensions across social platforms as they grapple with AI-generated content at scale. Unlike text-based platforms, LinkedIn's professional context makes authenticity particularly important — connections and recommendations carry real-world career implications.

The "AI slop" button gives users a voice in moderation but also signals LinkedIn's recognition that AI content detection remains imperfect. The feature likely relies more on user reports than automated detection, acknowledging the current limitations of machine learning-based content classification.
