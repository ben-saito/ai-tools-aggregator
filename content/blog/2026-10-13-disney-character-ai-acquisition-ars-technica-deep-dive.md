# Disney Offers $20M for Character.AI Tech Amid Bankruptcy Proceedings

---

Disney has offered to purchase "a non-exclusive perpetual license" of certain Character Technologies intellectual property as the chatbot startup navigates bankruptcy proceedings that began this week. The $20 million offer marks a striking reversal from Disney's position in September 2025, when the company sent a cease-and-desist letter accusing Character.AI of weaponizing Disney characters in ways that could damage its brand. The deal may face competition from other bidders, and Disney has not indicated whether it plans to use Character.AI technology in products accessible to children.

The bankruptcy filing and subsequent offer come after Character.AI became one of the first chatbot platforms to face serious regulatory scrutiny over the mental health impacts of AI companions, particularly among teenage users. Several highly publicized incidents involving young users contributed to a broader reassessment of AI companion products by parents, clinicians, and regulators.

---

## From Cease-and-Desist to Acquisition Offer

The trajectory of Disney's relationship with Character.AI reflects a rapid shift in the company's posture toward the chatbot startup. In September 2025, Disney joined other major media companies in publicly criticizing Character.AI for its handling of copyrighted characters, sending a formal cease-and-desist that accused the company of enabling interactions that could be "sexually exploitive and otherwise harmful and dangerous to children."

That cease-and-desist came after Character.AI chatbots roleplaying Disney characters were linked in public discourse to user interactions that raised concerns about mental health impacts. Disney's letter argued that Character Technologies had "weaponized Disney characters in a way that could damage its brand long term."

The scandal ended with Character Technologies removing all Disney-owned characters from its platform. However, Disney subsequently hired Character.AI's former chief Karandeep Anand as its first chief technology officer, a move that signaled a more nuanced view of the company's technology. The acquisition offer represents a further extension of that warming relationship, though regulatory scrutiny of AI companion products continues.

---

## Bankruptcy Proceedings andCompeting Bidders

Character.AI's bankruptcy filing kicked off this week, with the company seeking to restructure or sell assets under court supervision. Disney's $20 million offer is one of several the company has received, according to sources familiar with the proceedings. Character.AI has not disclosed the identities of other potential acquirers.

The valuation implied by Disney's offer represents a significant discount to Character.AI's previous private market valuations. At its peak, the company was reportedly valued at over $1 billion based on private funding rounds. The drop to a $20 million license offer reflects both the regulatory challenges facing AI companion products and the broader shift in investor sentiment toward AI startups that have faced controversy.

Character.AI has not responded publicly to questions about whether it plans to accept Disney's offer or pursue other acquisition proposals. The bankruptcy court must approve any sale of intellectual property assets.

---

## Regulatory Scrutiny of AI Companions

The Disney acquisition offer comes as AI companion products face continued regulatory attention. Multiple state attorneys general have reviewed Character.AI's safety practices, and the company has implemented changes including enhanced content moderation and restrictions on certain types of character interactions.

Mental health clinicians have raised concerns about the addictive qualities of AI companions, particularly for teenagers who may form unhealthy attachments to AI characters. Character.AI's platform allowed users to create and interact with AI versions of fictional characters, celebrities, and original creations — a model that proved popular but generated controversy when young users developed what clinicians described as problematic relationships with the chatbots.

Disney has not indicated whether it plans to use Character.AI technology in products for children. The company's theme parks and consumer products divisions have experimented with AI in various capacities, from animatronics to content production. Character.AI's conversational AI infrastructure could enable a new generation of interactive experiences where guests converse directly with AI-driven characters — a prospect that has drawn skepticism given the company's regulatory history.

---

## Disney's AI Strategy Under Karandeep Anand

Disney's acquisition interest coincides with the tenure of Karandeep Anand, the former Character.AI chief who joined Disney as its first chief technology officer. Anand's background at Character.AI gives Disney direct familiarity with the company's technology stack and the challenges associated with deploying conversational AI at consumer scale.

Under Anand's leadership, Disney has signaled a more aggressive push into AI-powered consumer experiences. The company has experimented with AI in theme park attractions, customer service applications, and content creation tools. Character.AI's technology could extend those experiments into interactive character experiences that maintain extended conversations with guests.

The acquisition offer is subject to regulatory approval and due diligence. Terms of the proposed transaction remain confidential beyond the $20 million license figure. If the deal closes, it would represent Disney's most significant move into conversational AI for consumer applications.
