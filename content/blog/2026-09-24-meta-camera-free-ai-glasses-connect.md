# Meta Unveils Camera-Free AI Glasses at $349 With 12-Hour Battery Life

Meta has officially introduced its first camera-free smart glasses at the company's annual Connect event in Menlo Park, California, on September 23, 2026. Developed in partnership with EssilorLuxottica, the glasses are designed to compete directly with audio-first AI wearables while addressing privacy concerns that have challenged Meta's camera-equipped products.

---

## A Direct Answer to Privacy Criticism

Meta's camera-equipped Ray-Ban Meta glasses have sold well, but they have also attracted criticism — particularly after incidents where wearers used the devices to record people without consent, earning the product the unflattering nickname "perv glasses" in some quarters. The new camera-free model represents Meta's most direct response to that backlash.

"Once you get used to high quality audio, it's just a thing that you want all the time, right?" CEO Mark Zuckerberg said during the keynote, explaining the company's reasoning for building an audio-only device. Meta's internal research, he said, showed there was demand for an AI-wearable focused purely on sound and voice interaction.

The glasses do not record video. They connect to Muse, Meta's AI assistant, through Bluetooth when paired with a smartphone, giving users a voice-first interface to the AI without the social friction of a camera pointed at the world.

---

## Technical Specifications

The camera-free design allows for a significantly lighter frame. The glasses weigh just 43 grams — lighter than most of Meta's camera-equipped models. Key specifications include:

- **Weight:** 43 grams
- **Battery life:** Up to 12 hours on a single charge
- **Charging:** Included charging case (capacity not disclosed)
- **Starting price:** $349
- **Styles:** 23 color and lens combinations
- **New frame options:** Retro-inspired "Clubmaster" Ray-Ban style, and "Burbank," described as a classic timeless rectangle shape
- **Fit features:** Adjustable temple tips, swappable nose pads, overextension hinges

---

## Audio AI as a Platform

The glasses run Muse, Meta's personal AI assistant, which means they support real-time voice conversations with the AI without requiring the user to unlock their phone or open an app. A tap on the fingerprint sensor in the corner wakes the device.

Users can also order the glasses with prescription optical lenses, positioning the product as an everyday wearable for people who need vision correction. This distinguishes it from earlier audio-only glasses that were primarily fitness or convenience devices.

Meta frames the glasses as a platform for its broader AI strategy. "All of Meta's AI glasses will, of course, feature access to its newer personal AI assistant, Muse," the company said in its announcement.

---

## Expanded Lineup and Brand Partnerships

Alongside the camera-free model, Meta announced the next generation of its camera-equipped Ray-Ban Meta glasses (Generation 3). New styles include the Aviator and the Zena, a cat-eye frame shape. The existing Ray-Ban Meta Wayfarer continues as well.

Meta also expanded its celebrity brand partnership program. Following its earlier deal with Kylie Jenner, the company announced a new partnership with singer Lisa, whose identity was not disclosed in the initial announcement. The glasses will roll out to more countries globally as part of Meta's international expansion.

---

## Competitive Landscape

The camera-free glasses place Meta in competition with Apple's Siri-powered AirPods, which represent Apple's approach to audio-first AI interaction. Meta's advantage is a dedicated glasses form factor rather than earbuds, along with a lower price point than the AirPods Max.

The broader smart glasses market has struggled with adoption due to usability questions, cost, and privacy concerns. Meta's camera-equipped glasses remain the market-leading product in the space by most estimates, and the camera-free model gives the company a second entry point for consumers who want AI capabilities without the camera.

Meta's Reality Labs division, which develops the glasses, has been losing money, according to earnings reports. The expanded product lineup is part of Meta's strategy to reach sufficient scale to make the hardware business sustainable.
