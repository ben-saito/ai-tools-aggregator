# Nvidia Expands Halos to Robotics: Physical AI Now Driving Nearly $10B in Annual Revenue

Nvidia has expanded its Halos safety architecture to robotics, announcing **Halos for Robotics** in June 2026 as the company positions itself at the center of the physical AI revolution. The full-stack safety solution, originally developed for self-driving vehicles, now targets warehouses, humanoid robots, and surgical robots — with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang revealing the physical AI business already generates nearly $10 billion in annual revenue.

---

## From Robotaxis to Humanoids: Halos Expands

Nvidia's Halos system first launched in 2025 as a comprehensive safety architecture for autonomous vehicles. The platform combined hardware and software tools to help developers implement guardrails preventing self-driving cars from harming nearby people. Now, Halos for Robotics extends this approach to a broader range of physical AI applications.

"We're seeing the convergence of capable AI models and capable robot hardware, and safety is emerging as the next critical bottleneck," said Amit Goel, Nvidia's head of robotics ecosystem and edge computing, in an interview with Ars Technica. "That's why we launched Halos for Robotics — to unlock the full potential of these systems."

**Key deployment targets for Halos for Robotics:**
- Autonomous mobile robots in warehouse environments
- Humanoid robots operating inside factories
- Surgical robots requiring precise safety guarantees

---

## The Business Case: $10B and Growing

During a recent appearance, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang disclosed that the company's physical AI business — encompassing robotics, autonomous vehicles, and related technologies — already contributes nearly $10 billion annually. This figure represents a significant milestone for a segment that remains secondary to Nvidia's core data center GPU business.

The robotics market represents a strategic bet on the next wave of AI deployment. While AI data centers capture headlines and investor attention, Nvidia has quietly built a portfolio of technologies targeting real-world AI: robots, self-driving vehicles, and automated manufacturing systems.

---

## Safety as a Competitive Moat

Nvidia's approach treats safety architecture as a competitive differentiator. Rather than selling isolated components, Nvidia offers an integrated stack that robotics companies can build upon — similar to how cloud platforms provide managed services that reduce customer operational complexity.

For robotics developers, Halos provides:
- Standardized safety primitives for perception and planning systems
- Hardware参考 designs optimized for real-time safety validation
- Software frameworks for implementing fail-safe operational boundaries

This full-stack approach addresses a practical challenge: robotics companies developing safety systems from scratch face significant engineering costs and regulatory hurdles. By providing proven safety building blocks, Nvidia reduces time-to-market for safe robotic deployments.

---

## Industry Adoption

Several robotics companies have already adopted Halos for Robotics, including Agility Robotics — maker of the Digit humanoid robot — which uses the platform to enable safe human-robot collaboration in warehouse settings. The integration allows Digit to navigate dynamic environments while maintaining safety boundaries around workers.

As physical AI transitions from research prototypes to commercial deployments, safety frameworks like Halos are likely to become standard infrastructure — much like how operating systems and networking stacks became essential for cloud computing.
