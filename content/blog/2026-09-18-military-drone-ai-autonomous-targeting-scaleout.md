# Small AI Models Enable Drones to Autonomously Identify and Attack Battlefield Targets

A NATO-backed startup called Scaleout has developed an AI system that allows small military drones to autonomously identify and attack battlefield targets without requiring continuous communication with a human operator. The system uses decentralized AI-driven learning, where models are trained locally and then distributed to drone fleets.

---

## How the System Works

Scaleout's approach centers on running compact AI models directly on individual drones. These models are trained on mission-specific data before deployment, and can make target identification decisions on-device. The decentralized architecture means the drones do not require a continuous data link to a command center — each unit can operate independently within its programmed parameters.

The company describes the system as designed for "offensive and defensive" missions, with the AI models tailored to specific operational contexts before deployment.

---

## The Autonomy Question

Autonomous weapon systems remain one of the most contentious topics in AI policy. Critics argue that delegating life-and-death decisions to machines removes human judgment from the loop in ways that violate the laws of armed conflict. Proponents argue that small, highly localized models can actually reduce collateral damage by making targeting more precise.

Scaleout says its system requires human authorization for weapons release, but critics note that the line between "identification" and "attack" can blur in practice.
