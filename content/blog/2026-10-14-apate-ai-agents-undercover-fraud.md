# Apate: Autonomous AI Agents Go Undercover to Battle scammers

---

Most companies use AI agents to ship products faster or to speed up workflows. Apate.ai takes a different approach — it sends them undercover to get scammed. Founded in 2024 based on research from Macquarie University in Sydney, the Australian startup deploys thousands of AI agents that masquerade as innocent victims, answering scam calls, responding to shady messages and emails, following phishing links, and joining social media groups to bait fraudsters.

The agents pretend to be people of all stripes, sporting different ages, genders, and accents, and speak various languages as they try to keep scammers engaged long enough to learn something useful. The company says its fleet of agents has so far wasted **17 years of scammers' time** across **2.5 million conversations**.

## From Academic Research to Anti-Fraud Intelligence

Apate's agents gather intelligence that would otherwise be learned only after a victim has been scammed: bank accounts, crypto wallets, phone numbers, malicious websites, the brands they're impersonating, and other details about the scams they're running. This information is then fed to Apate's customers — banks, telecom companies, government agencies, and utilities — who use it to inform their anti-fraud measures.

The company is one of the finalists at this year's **Startup Battlefield 200 at TechCrunch Disrupt**, pitching a panel of VC judges at the event held at Moscone West in San Francisco through Thursday, October 15.

## The AI Scam Landscape

It's a curious evolution of scam-baiting, but it's not so surprising given how much AI has empowered scammers and hackers. The FBI received more than **22,000 complaints of AI-related crimes in 2025**, resulting in about **$893 million in losses**. More than $632 million of that came from investment scams, in which fraudsters use AI to run conversations and deepfake the voices and appearances of celebrities, executives, and trusted figures.

For a long time, people have made a hobby of wasting scammers' time — so-called scam baiting — but Apate automates and scales this at a level no human could match. The startup's approach represents a new frontier in using AI defensively against AI-powered threats.
