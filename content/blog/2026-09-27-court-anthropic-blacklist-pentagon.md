# Court rules Pentagon can blacklist Anthropic for refusing to enable Claude features

Source: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/court-rules-trump-can-blacklist-anthropic-for-refusing-to-enable-claude-features/)

---

A federal court has ruled that the Pentagon can proceed with blacklisting Anthropic from government contracts based on the company's refusal to enable certain Claude features for military use. The ruling represents a significant escalation in the ongoing tension between AI safety priorities and government operational requirements.

The court found that Anthropic's refusal to modify Claude to meet specific feature requirements could justify excluding the company from defense contracts. The judges noted that "overly constrained AI models" could cause military operations to fail, suggesting that the government's interest in capable AI systems outweighed Anthropic's safety concerns in this context.

Anthropic has maintained that certain capability restrictions in Claude are essential safety features designed to prevent misuse. The company has argued that deliberately weakening those restrictions, even for government customers, could set dangerous precedents and undermine the model's safety architecture.

The ruling is separate from an earlier district court decision that found the blacklist potentially unconstitutional as a First Amendment retaliation measure. The current appellate ruling addresses statutory authority under a different legal framework, specifically 41 U.S.C. 4713, which governs procurement-related exclusions.

The case highlights the growing legal confrontation between AI safety priorities and government operational demands. As AI systems become more capable and more integrated into defense and intelligence workflows, similar conflicts are likely to multiply. Anthropic has not indicated whether it plans to appeal further.
