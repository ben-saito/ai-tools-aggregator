# Full-Color Infrared Imaging System Enables Natural Night Vision

Researchers at the Beijing Institute of Technology have developed a device that renders infrared radiation in full color, representing a significant advance over conventional night vision systems that display only shades of green. The system, led by Xin Tang and Ge Mu, combines mercury telluride colloidal quantum dots that absorb infrared light with a dual-layer OLED that converts absorbed energy into visible color.

Conventional night vision goggles use IR photodetectors wired to visible-light LEDs, translating infrared into brightness levels but not color. The new approach translates different infrared wavelengths into distinct parts of the visual spectrum, giving viewers something closer to natural daytime vision.

The technology relies on a fundamental limitation of human vision: the molecular shape change in retinal pigments that initiates nerve impulses requires roughly 1.6 electron volts of energy. Infrared photons with wavelengths longer than 700 nanometers lack sufficient energy to trigger this process, placing over half of the Sun's radiant energy outside human visual reach.

By stacking mercury telluride colloidal quantum dots with a dual-layer OLED and the right internal wiring, incoming infrared radiation is converted into ordinary full-color images. The research represents a departure from the decades-old green-tinted night vision paradigm used by military and security services worldwide.
