# Lyft Settles Driver Misclassification Lawsuit for $272.5M

Lyft has agreed to a landmark $272.5 million settlement with the state of California, resolving allegations that the ride-hailing company illegally misclassified its drivers as independent contractors rather than employees.

The case dates back to May 2020, when then-Attorney General Xavier Becerra — now the Democratic candidate for governor — sued both Uber and Lyft. Thursday's settlement affects Lyft only, while the case against Uber continues.

---

## A Landmark Win for Workers

"We are proud to announce this landmark win for workers, the largest misclassification settlement in California's history," Attorney General Rob Bonta said in a statement. "Los Angeles and our statewide partners will not allow businesses to exploit their workers and evade their obligations under the law."

The settlement marks the culmination of a legal battle that has shaped the gig economy's employment classification debate for years. Ever since rideshare companies began in the early 2010s, they have faced scrutiny for underpaying and mistreating drivers.

---

## Proposition 22 and Its Aftermath

In November 2020, California voters approved Proposition 22, a ballot measure organized primarily by Uber and Lyft. The law created a new category of "app-based drivers" and exempted gig companies from providing employee benefits like minimum wage guarantees, health insurance, and unemployment insurance.

Despite the passage of Proposition 22, the state has continued pursuing the misclassification case, arguing that the law does not shield companies from all labor protections.

---

## Industry Implications

The settlement signals that even with Proposition 22 in place, California regulators will continue to challenge how gig economy companies classify their workers. Uber and Lyft have built their business models on avoiding the costs associated with full-time employees, including healthcare, overtime pay, and workers' compensation.

For drivers, the settlement may provide additional leverage in ongoing debates about earnings guarantees and benefit requirements. The gig economy remains under pressure as labor advocates push for stronger worker protections.

*Lyft's settlement was announced on October 2, 2026.*
