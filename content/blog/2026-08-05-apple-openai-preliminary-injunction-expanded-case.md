# Apple Escalates Trade Secrets Case Against OpenAI: Preliminary Injunction Sought, 11 More Ex-Employees Implicated

Apple has escalated its legal battle with OpenAI, seeking a preliminary injunction to halt the development of any AI device or product based on Apple's proprietary technology, while simultaneously expanding the scope of the case to include 11 additional former employees who may have misappropriated confidential information.

---

## Escalation: Preliminary Injunction and Expanded Investigation

In a new court filing, Apple requested expedited discovery from the accused OpenAI employees — senior systems engineer Chang Liu and Chief Hardware Officer Tang Yew Tan — as well as OpenAI, its foundation, and io, the device startup co-founded by Apple's former lead designer Jony Ive.

Apple's investigation has widened significantly since the original complaint. The filing reveals that 11 other former Apple employees beyond Liu and Tan may have been witnesses to or participants in the alleged trade secrets theft. Among the new evidence cited:

- Another former Apple employee reportedly met with Liu and Yu-Ting Peng (another accused former Apple employee now at OpenAI) before Peng's interview at OpenAI, discussing Apple proprietary information relating to unannounced products.
- A separate former Apple employee took screenshots of confidential Apple documents relating to an unannounced Apple product before an interview at OpenAI.
- After Apple filed its original complaint, multiple former Apple employees now working at OpenAI allegedly reacted by deleting or concealing evidence.

The preliminary injunction, if granted, would prevent OpenAI from moving forward with developing products based on the allegedly stolen technology — potentially affecting not only software but the io hardware device being developed in partnership with Jony Ive.

---

## Apple's Broader Legal Strategy

This filing marks a significant escalation in what was already a high-profile intellectual property dispute. Apple's legal team is taking an aggressive approach, not only pursuing damages but seeking to stop the alleged misuse of trade secrets at a product-development level.

OpenAI has denied the allegations, stating it does not possess and does not want Apple's trade secrets, and has accused Apple of making vague accusations and attempting to change the narrative around the dispute.

The case is notable for its intersection of talent mobility, trade secret law, and the competitive dynamics between major technology companies racing to develop AI hardware and devices.
