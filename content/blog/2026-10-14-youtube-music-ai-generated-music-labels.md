# YouTube Music Rolls Out AI-Generated Music Labels for Transparency

---

YouTube Music announced Wednesday the rollout of a new labeling system aimed at improving transparency around songs created with the help of generative AI. Now listeners will be able to easily identify tracks created using AI, including those involved in composition and vocals. The label will appear on the player page. Clicking on this label provides additional information about the specific role of AI in the track's creation. Notably, these labels will also extend to the main YouTube app.

YouTube said it collaborated with various music partners and deployed its own detection tools to identify AI-generated music. According to its help page, partners using YouTube can declare their work's use of generative AI through one of three designations: Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, or No Gen AI. "Fully Gen AI" applies to tracks that are entirely generated by AI, while "Partly Gen AI" refers to works where artists may have incorporated AI-generated components but added their own live elements. Tracks edited using pitch-correction tools such as Auto-Tune fall under the "No Gen AI" category.

The move comes as AI-generated music rapidly expands across streaming services, and many platforms are adopting their own approaches. For instance, Apple Music has plans to introduce "Made With AI" labels as part of its AI Transparency Tags later this year. Spotify also has AI labels, as well as a music spam filter. Deezer has taken one of the stricter stances, reporting in July that it was receiving nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks daily. The platform labels detected AI-generated music and excludes it from algorithmic recommendations and editorial playlists, while also withholding royalties from fraudulent streams. The company is also working on an option to let users filter out AI-generated music. TIDAL also labels recordings it identifies as entirely AI-generated, allows listeners to disable them, and excludes these recordings from royalty attribution.

YouTube, by contrast, is focusing on disclosure rather than blanket restrictions. Its new labels are intended to inform listeners about AI's role in music creation, but AI involvement alone doesn't make a track ineligible for monetization. YouTube even experiments with AI-powered music creation, including its tool Dream Track, which lets select creators generate soundtracks featuring AI-generated versions of participating artists' voices.

---

The streaming music industry is entering a new phase of AI transparency as major platforms race to disclose AI involvement in music creation. YouTube Music's labeling system, rolling out this week, represents one of the most comprehensive approaches to AI disclosure in the industry, offering users detailed information about how AI was used in producing the tracks they listen to.

The three-tier classification system — Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, and No Gen AI — provides nuanced differentiation that addresses the spectrum of AI involvement in modern music production. This approach acknowledges that AI渗透 in music creation is not binary: from AI-generated vocals to AI-assisted mixing, the technology appears in many forms.

The broader industry trend reflects the challenge platforms face in managing the flood of AI-generated content. Deezer's data — nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks daily — illustrates the scale of the issue. As generative AI tools become more accessible, distinguishing human-created from AI-generated music has become a pressing concern for rights holders, artists, and listeners alike.

YouTube's strategy of disclosure over restriction marks a notable approach. While Deezer and TIDAL actively exclude AI-generated content from recommendations and royalty calculations, YouTube is opting to inform rather than filter. This positions AI involvement as a labeling issue rather than a content policy issue, potentially setting a precedent for how the industry handles the intersection of AI and creative work.
