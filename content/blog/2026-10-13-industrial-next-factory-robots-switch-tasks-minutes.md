# Industrial Next wants factory robots to switch tasks in minutes, not weeks

---

In most factories, automation is fixed: robots and machines are programmed for one specific task and product. Changing a line means reprogramming and recalibrating everything from scratch — a process that typically takes weeks and costs millions in lost production time.

Industrial Next, a robotics startup founded by former Tesla and Amazon robotics engineers, is building adaptive manufacturing systems that can switch between tasks in under 30 minutes. The key innovation is a combination of vision-based scene understanding and a library of pre-trained manipulation primitives that can be composed on the fly.

The system's robots use foundation models trained on millions of hours of manufacturing video to understand object poses, tolerances, and process flows. When a new product comes down the line, the robot can identify the relevant fixtures, pick up components in the correct orientation, and execute assembly steps without explicit reprogramming.

This represents a shift from "hard automation" — where robots repeat one precise motion — to "soft automation" that handles variation and changeover. For factories producing short runs of customized products, this flexibility could be the difference between automation being economically viable and sticking with manual labor.

Industrial Next's primary target is electronics assembly, where product cycles are shortening and SKU counts are exploding. The company is piloting its system with several major EMS (electronics manufacturing services) providers and expects commercial deployment in 2027.
