# Federal judge rules Trump administration illegally blacklisted Anthropic

A federal judge ruled that the Trump administration illegally retaliated against AI company Anthropic by designating it a supply-chain risk to national security. The ruling marks Anthropic's first court victory in its fight against the government's blacklist.

---

## Court ruling

Judge Rita Lin, a Biden appointee in the US District Court for the Northern District of California, granted key portions of Anthropic's motion for summary judgment in a ruling issued on August 27, 2026.

"The undisputed record shows that the challenged actions constituted unlawful retaliation in violation of the First Amendment," Lin wrote.

The ruling found that the administration blacklisted Anthropic after the company refused to let its Claude AI models be used for autonomous warfare and mass surveillance.

---

## Background of the conflict

Anthropic sued the Trump administration in March 2026, arguing that the government had branded it a "potential adversary and saboteur of the US" for expressing views on AI safety and refusing to support lethal autonomous warfare.

Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had ordered all federal agencies to permanently stop using Anthropic's products and banned all defense contractors from doing business with the company.

"The Department of War is undisputedly free to select the AI vendor of its choice, but the evidence demonstrates that the broad measures imposed on Anthropic were illegal and baseless," Lin wrote.

---

## Adminstration's justification deemed "slim"

The Trump administration provided only a "slim" justification to the court, Lin noted, stating that federal defendants "have now backed away from the thrust of their risk assessment, which relied on speculation rather than evidence."

The White House responded to Anthropic's lawsuit by stating that "President Trump will never allow a radical left, woke company to jeopardize our national security by dictating how the greatest and most powerful democracy in the world should use AI."

The Trump administration had won an earlier ruling from Trump-appointed judges in the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, which denied Anthropic's emergency motion for a stay. However, Anthropic also pursued parallel litigation in California, where Lin issued a preliminary injunction on March 26 prohibiting the government from enforcing the blacklist.

---

## Broader implications

The preliminary injunction ruling stated that nothing in relevant US law "supports the Orwellian notion that an American company may be branded a potential adversary and saboteur of the US for expressing its views on AI safety."

This case could set a precedent for how the government can regulate AI companies based on their political or ethical positions, with First Amendment implications for the entire tech industry.
