# Meta AI Agent Muse Outpaces ChatGPT in Early Mobile Launch Metrics

---

## Appfigures Data Shows Muse Outperforming ChatGPT's First 12 Days

Meta's AI agent Muse has achieved stronger early mobile metrics than ChatGPT did during its initial launch period, according to new estimates from Appfigures published September 21, 2026. The data shows Muse reaching 2.8 million global installs in its first 12 days, compared to 1.3 million for ChatGPT over the same timeframe when ChatGPT launched on iOS in May 2023.

To enable a direct comparison between the two apps, Appfigures restricted the analysis to iOS data from the United States and Canada during the first 12 days after each launch. Under these parameters, Muse recorded 1.8 million downloads against ChatGPT's 1.3 million. Muse also surpassed ChatGPT in daily active users: 642,000 versus 231,000 when measuring all US mobile platforms, and 359,000 versus ChatGPT's iOS-only figure when the comparison was narrowed to iOS alone.

Muse launched simultaneously on iOS and Android, while ChatGPT debuted exclusively on iOS. Appfigures noted that the comparison is complicated by these different launch strategies, but even when controlling for platform availability, Muse's early daily active user numbers exceeded ChatGPT's from the same point in that product's rollout.

---

## Cross-Promotion as a Growth Lever

Meta has applied a familiar playbook to Muse's launch. The company leveraged cross-promotion across its largest properties — Instagram and Facebook — to drive installations, a strategy it previously deployed to scale Threads to over 500 million users. Muse also climbed from its launch position of No. 2 overall on the US App Store to No. 1, according to Business Insider.

The comparison with ChatGPT carries significance beyond mobile metrics. ChatGPT's mobile debut was widely seen as a pivotal moment for consumer AI adoption, demonstrating that an AI chatbot could achieve mainstream mobile relevance. Meta's internal metrics suggest Muse may be on a similar or faster trajectory, which would reinforce the case for AI agents as a distinct consumer product category rather than a feature layered onto existing apps.

---

## Agentic Commerce and Platform Policy

The strong launch numbers coincide with a notable friction point: Amazon.com has blocked Muse from operating as a purchasing agent on its platform. Starting September 21, 2026, users attempting to complete transactions through Muse received an error message citing violation of Amazon's Conditions of Use regarding unauthorized AI agents.

Amazon's own foundation models and inference platform create a competitive dynamic that gives the company little incentive to accommodate a rival's agent. There are also operational concerns: an AI agent that produces incorrect orders would generate customer service overhead for Amazon, creating a risk-reward calculation that favors exclusion.

The block illustrates an emerging tension in the AI agent ecosystem. As agents grow more capable and are embedded into consumer workflows, the platforms they attempt to interact with face decisions about whether to welcome, restrict, or charge for agent access. Amazon's choice to block Muse represents a platform policy stance that other e-commerce and service sites may face similar decisions around.

---

## Market Position and Implications

Muse's early traction matters for the broader AI agent market because it demonstrates that a major consumer platform can drive agent adoption at scale through existing distribution advantages. Whether that adoption translates to sustained engagement and commercial value remains an open question. Appfigures provides third-party estimates rather than Meta's internal figures, so the precision of the comparison is approximate.

What the data does suggest is that Meta's combination of cross-platform availability, aggressive cross-promotion, and integration with its social graph gives Muse a distribution edge that ChatGPT lacked at launch. If that edge translates into durable user habits, it would represent a meaningful shift in how AI agent winners are determined — not purely on model capability, but on platform distribution and product integration.
