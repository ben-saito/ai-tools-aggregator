# Manus Seeks $4B Valuation in New $500M Fundraise as It Resumes Independent Operations

Chinese AI startup Manus, which earlier this year had to break off a merger with Meta, is in discussions to raise $500 million at a $4 billion valuation after resuming operations as an independent company, The Wall Street Journal reported on September 18, citing anonymous sources.

---

## From Meta Merger to Independent Operations

Manus had been in talks for a merger with Meta, the American social media giant, but the deal was blocked by Beijing. Chinese regulators cited potential violations of export controls and foreign investment rules as intensifying worries over losing AI talent and researchers to the West culminated in blocking the transaction.

The company has since been untangling itself from Meta, and early investors and backers have reportedly helped Manus buy back its shares at a valuation of approximately $2 billion. The founding team will continue to lead the company as it resumes independent operations.

---

## $500M Fundraise and IPO Considerations

Potential investors in the new round include IDG Capital, Boyu Capital, battery maker Contemporary Amperex Technology, and existing backers Tencent, HSG, and Zhenfund, the Journal reported. Manus is also said to be considering a restructuring exercise to prepare for a public listing in Hong Kong.

Manus makes AI products and agents similar to offerings from OpenAI, Lovable, and Replit. Its platform includes a chatbot, vibe-coding tools for building apps and websites, design and presentation generation, video creation, and a browser assistant.

---

## Market Context

The fundraise comes as AI agent startups face intensifying competition for consumer market share. The broader AI agent space has seen rapid development, with players including OpenAI, Instinct, and others rushing to launch new features. Instinct, one of the newest entrants frequently discussed in tech circles, is said to be raising funds at a $10 billion valuation.
