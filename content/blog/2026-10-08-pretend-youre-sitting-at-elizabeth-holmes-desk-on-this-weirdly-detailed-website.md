# Pretend You're Sitting at Elizabeth Holmes' Desk on This Weirdly Detailed Website

---

A new website lets you browse over a thousand emails, slides, texts, and documents from the United States v. Elizabeth Holmes trial. Extend engineer Bo Lau built the site to simulate what it might have been like to rifle through the Theranos founder's desk.

The interactive recreation offers an unusual window into one of the most notorious fraud cases in Silicon Valley history. Visitors can explore reconstructed communications and artifacts from the landmark trial that convicted Holmes of defrauding investors in her blood-testing startup.

---

## How the Site Works

The website presents a digital simulation of Holmes' desk environment, organizing trial documents in a format that mirrors how they might have appeared during courtroom proceedings. Each piece of evidence is labeled and categorized, allowing visitors to follow the flow of information that prosecutors argued showed intentional deception.

The project highlights how digital preservation of legal proceedings can create new opportunities for public understanding of complex cases. Rather than paging through thousands of PDF filings, users can navigate a spatial interface that contextualizes documents by their physical arrangement.

---

## AI Tools Behind the Recreation

According to the TechCrunch report, the site reportedly leveraged AI tools including Claude (Anthropic), OpenAI, and other machine learning technologies to help organize and present the vast corpus of trial materials. This represents a practical application of generative AI in legal document management—using AI to sort, categorize, and make searchable a large collection of unstructured text.

The case continues to serve as a touchstone for discussions about startup culture, due diligence, and the responsibilities of founders in the health technology sector.
