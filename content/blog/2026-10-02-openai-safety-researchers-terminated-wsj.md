# OpenAI Parts Ways With Three Safety Researchers After Internal Investigation

OpenAI has terminated three safety researchers following an internal investigation that found they mishandled sensitive company information, according to a Wall Street Journal report. The departures represent the latest in a series of staffing changes at the frontier AI laboratory as it navigates internal tensions around safety priorities.

---

## Policy Violations Around Sensitive Information Access

"We have parted ways with three individuals for violating our policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information," an OpenAI spokesperson said in a statement to the Wall Street Journal. "Our investigation concluded that these violations did not rise to the level requiring external notification."

The company did not disclose the names of the departing researchers, the organizations with which they were affiliated, or the specific nature of the sensitive information involved. OpenAI did not immediately respond to TechCrunch's request for additional comment.

The incident raises questions about internal access controls and information handling practices at a company that has positioned safety as a core organizational priority while simultaneously pursuing aggressive commercial deployment timelines.

---

## Context of Safety Team Restructuring

The terminations occur amid broader restructuring of OpenAI's safety-focused teams. The company has faced public scrutiny over its approach to responsible AI development, including recent controversies around model deployment decisions and the departure of other safety-oriented personnel.

OpenAI has maintained that it takes information security seriously and that its policies are designed to protect both proprietary information and sensitive data related to AI capabilities. The company has not disclosed whether the investigation was conducted by internal teams or external parties.

---

## Industry Implications

The departure of safety researchers from a frontier lab following information handling investigations carries implications beyond the individual case. Research communities that collaborate with AI laboratories depend on trust that sensitive information will be handled appropriately, and incidents involving information policy violations can affect collaborative relationships.

The timing of the announcement, coming shortly after OpenAI's series of product announcements including the Decisions API and ChatGPT shopping features, suggests the company is managing multiple internal priorities simultaneously as it prepares for anticipated public market debut.
