# Private School Defends Itself in AI-Generated Nudes Scandal as Victims Allege "Catastrophic Institutional Failure"

A Pennsylvania private school is fighting back against lawsuits alleging it failed to protect students after boys used AI to generate nude images of at least 59 classmates. Lancaster Country Day School (LCDS) argues it properly handled a tip from the state Attorney General and that gaps in current law shielded it from obligations to report.

---

## The School's Defense

In a motion to dismiss this week, LCDS — a private K-12 school with fewer than 600 students — argued it was "false to say the school never reported the harm to law enforcement." The tip it received came from the Pennsylvania Office of the Attorney General, which is itself a law enforcement agency, the filing noted.

The school also argued it was "false to say the school knew that girls were being targeted" because the tip did not mention specific victims. LCDS said it "looked into the tip as best as their educator training allowed," but the tip only identified one bad actor and was too vague to identify potential victims. The school says it never saw the images and that all activity connected to the AI nudes happened off-campus.

Under Pennsylvania law, schools are only required to report tips on actual child abuse. The school argues AI-generated images do not yet qualify under the current legal framework.

---

## The Victims' Allegations

Girls suing the school allege at least 59 were traumatized by what they call "an extended, catastrophic institutional failure." They claim they "were forced to go to high school while 'deepfake' nude images of them were circulating within the school community."

According to the complaint, Lindsay Deibler-Wallace, assistant head of Upper School, took no protective action after telling parents that "boys will be boys." The school allegedly discouraged girls from going to police, withheld information from parents, and obstructed investigations — including refusing to provide a yearbook photo to detectives seeking to identify suspects.

The boys accused of creating the images have since pled guilty.

---

## A Legal Gray Zone

The case highlights a gap in laws written before AI-generated imagery existed. Schools face unclear obligations when confronted with AI CSAM that originates off-campus. The outcome of LCDS's motion to dismiss could set precedent for how educational institutions handle AI-facilitated abuse.

---

## Why This Matters for AI Policy

This case is not primarily about AI technology — it is about the legal and institutional frameworks that have not caught up with AI capabilities. As AI tools make it trivially easy to generate non-consensual intimate imagery, the question of who bears responsibility — platform developers, schools, or lawmakers — remains largely unanswered.
