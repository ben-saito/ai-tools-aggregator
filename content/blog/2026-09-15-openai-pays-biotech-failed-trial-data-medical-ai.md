# OpenAI Is Paying Biotech Companies for Failed Trial Data to Train Medical AI

OpenAI has begun acquiring detailed regulatory filings, manufacturing strategies, and safety data from bankrupt biotech companies, paying significant sums to access what was previously considered worthless: the detailed documentation of failed drug development programs. The move represents a strategic bet that the path to powerful medical AI lies not in generating new data, but in unlocking the vast troves of underutilized data that already exist.

---

## The Strategy: Mining Failure for Insight

Drug development is notoriously expensive and failure-heavy. For every drug that reaches FDA approval, hundreds of candidates fail in clinical trials. While these failures are commercially sensitive, they contain enormous scientific value: detailed records of what was tried, why it failed, and what the safety profiles looked like.

Ruxandra Teslo, a clinical trial policy analyst, first proposed the approach in 2025, arguing that bankruptcy proceedings represented an untapped source of high-quality medical data. By bidding on biotech company assets at bankruptcy auctions, OpenAI can acquire detailed regulatory filings that would otherwise remain locked in corporate archives or destroyed.

The data includes manufacturing protocols, safety monitoring reports, and regulatory correspondence that reveals how these companies approached specific biological mechanisms. For training a medical AI system, this represents a rich dataset of real-world biological experimentation, including failure modes that are never published in academic literature.

---

## Why This Data Matters for AI Training

Large language models trained purely on published scientific literature develop a distorted view of biology. Published papers represent a highly selected subset of scientific work — only the successful hypotheses make it into journals. The much larger universe of failed hypotheses, abandoned approaches, and negative results is never documented in accessible formats.

For AI systems that aim to assist in drug discovery, this selection bias is a significant limitation. Understanding why certain approaches failed is often as valuable as understanding what succeeded. A model that has seen hundreds of failed compound development programs has learned the contours of biological possibility in ways that a model trained only on published papers cannot.

OpenAI's acquisition of this data suggests the company is building toward AI systems capable of genuine scientific reasoning about biological mechanisms, not just pattern matching on published literature.

---

## Competitive Landscape

The approach puts OpenAI in direct competition with other AI healthcare initiatives, including Google's DeepMind, which has focused on protein structure prediction, and Anthropic, which has explored AI-assisted scientific reasoning. Microsoft, through its healthcare AI division, has also signaled interest in medical applications of large language models.

The key differentiator for OpenAI may be the volume and variety of regulatory data it can acquire. Biotech bankruptcy rates fluctuate with the broader funding environment, and the current cycle has produced a significant number of companies with detailed regulatory archives.

---

## Challenges and Limitations

Accessing this data comes with challenges. Much of the acquired documentation contains proprietary manufacturing processes that biotech companies were required to file with regulatory agencies. While OpenAI's acquisition of the assets gives it legal access to these documents, the data must still be carefully processed to remove personally identifiable information and trade secrets before it can be used in training.

There are also questions about how representative failed trial data is of broader biological truth. Failed trials fail for many reasons — poor study design, manufacturing issues, or simple bad luck — and an AI trained heavily on failures without proper weighting could develop an overly pessimistic view of promising research directions.

OpenAI has not disclosed the specific terms of its data acquisition agreements or the total investment in the data licensing program.
