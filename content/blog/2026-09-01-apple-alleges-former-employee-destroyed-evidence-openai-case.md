# Apple Alleges Former Employee Destroyed Evidence in Data Theft Case Linked to OpenAI

Apple has filed what it describes as "shocking evidence" in its lawsuit against a former employee accused of stealing company trade secrets for OpenAI's benefit. The filing includes allegations that the employee, Chang Liu, destroyed evidence after learning he was under investigation — a claim that could significantly escalate the legal stakes of the case.

---

## Evidence Destruction Allegations

According to court documents, Apple investigators gained access to Liu's old work laptop after his legal counsel voluntarily handed it over for investigation in August 2026. Apple claims the device shows evidence of data wiping consistent with intentional destruction of potentially incriminating files. The company argues this behavior demonstrates consciousness of guilt and should weigh heavily in any penalty determination.

Liu, who joined Apple as a machine learning engineer before moving to OpenAI, is alleged to have transferred proprietary Apple AI research to external systems. Apple is seeking damages and injunctive relief to prevent further use of the allegedly stolen information.

---

## Trade Secret Law and AI Talent Mobility

The case highlights the ongoing tension between AI companies and former employees who change employers. Non-compete agreements and trade secret protections are central to how AI labs guard their research advantages, but enforcement has proven difficult as the industry competes aggressively for experienced researchers.

Legal experts note that evidence destruction allegations can complicate employment mobility cases. Courts have historically been willing to consider adverse inference from destroyed evidence, potentially strengthening Apple's position even if direct proof of theft is incomplete.

---

## Broader Industry Implications

The lawsuit arrives amid heightened scrutiny of AI talent movement between major labs. Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon have all pursued aggressive hiring strategies in AI research, while employees increasingly view job changes as career advancement opportunities. The outcome of this case could set precedent for how trade secret claims related to AI research are handled in future disputes.

OpenAI has not commented publicly on the specific allegations, though the company has stated that it maintains robust policies regarding intellectual property from prior employers.
