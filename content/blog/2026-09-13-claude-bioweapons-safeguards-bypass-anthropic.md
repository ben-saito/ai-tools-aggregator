# Claude Users Found Ways Around Safeguards for Bioweapons Research

Anthropic has published a report detailing how some users attempted to circumvent Claude's safety measures to obtain information potentially useful for creating biological weapons, highlighting the ongoing challenge of securing advanced AI systems.

---

## Bypassing Safeguards for Dual-Use Research

The startup gave five examples of times actors "circumvented controls" and made other efforts to "obfuscate" the purpose of their research to dodge safeguards. The cases involved some users in nations Anthropic doesn't support, who spent weeks planning experiments involving avian influenza and other potentially dangerous biological research.

"We hope that by sharing these examples, we spark a conversation within the AI industry and with governments about emerging biological risks and how best to counter them," Anthropic said in its report.

## The Jacob Coxon Resignation

A maelstrom over AI safety kicked off earlier this week when Jacob Coxon, an Anthropic safety researcher, resigned from the company, saying employees "earnestly believe it [AI] could kill us all by the end of the decade." The timing of Anthropic's report appears designed to reinforce the urgency of safety research.

## Dual-Use Dilemma

Anthropic emphasized it could not be sure that the scientists in its examples intended to cause harm. The same information needed to create biological weapons could also be used to develop a vaccine — a fundamental challenge in biosecurity.

Some dangerous biology looks much like legitimate research, complicating AI safeguards. The line between a virologist studying pathogen evolution and someone seeking to engineer a pandemic is often invisible to automated systems.

## Industry Response

Concerns over the technology began to escalate earlier this year with the release of advanced models such as Anthropic's Mythos. OpenAI also caused alarm in July when it said its models had autonomous capabilities that raised concerns.

There is an expanding consensus among AI executives and biosecurity researchers that the use of the technology in biology needs to be secured and regulated as models become more advanced. But even if AI can be designed to refuse dangerous biology requests, potential obstacles remain — including the ability to make weapons in practice and the resources needed to do so.

## Anthropic's Response

Anthropic said it had banned the accounts mentioned in the report, but it did not disclose the names of the research institutions or the nations where the incidents took place. The company continues to refine its safety measures, though the report acknowledges that determined actors can sometimes find gaps.
