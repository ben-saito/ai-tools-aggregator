# Paul Christiano, AI Alignment Researcher, Joins OpenAI Board

Paul Christiano, one of the most influential researchers in the field of AI alignment and an outspoken voice on the risks of advanced AI systems, has joined the OpenAI Foundation as a member of its board of directors.

The appointment, reported by TechCrunch on September 9, marks a notable convergence: Christiano, who has spent years arguing that current AI development trajectories pose serious risks to human civilization, is now formally part of the governance structure at one of the companies most associated with those trajectories.

---

## Christiano's Background

Christiano is best known in the AI research community for his work on AI alignment — the field dedicated to ensuring that increasingly capable AI systems continue to behave in ways that align with human interests. His research at the Machine Learning Institute and subsequently at OpenAI (where he worked from 2017 to 2022) has shaped how the field thinks about scalable alignment, AI safety evaluations, and the long-term trajectory of AI development.

Christiano is also known for his candid public statements about the risks of advanced AI. In a widely circulated 2023 essay, he argued that current AI development was progressing faster than the field's ability to ensure safety, and that there was a meaningful probability of catastrophic outcomes within the decade.

His work on "functional" alignment — the idea that AI systems should be trained to behave as if they were aligned, rather than relying on interpretability to verify alignment — has been both influential and controversial within the AI safety community.

---

## Why This Appointment Matters

The appointment of an AI safety researcher to the board of a frontier AI lab is not unprecedented, but it is relatively rare. Most AI safety researchers have maintained arm's-length relationships with the companies whose products they study, partly to preserve intellectual independence and partly due to concerns about cooption.

Christiano's move is significant precisely because of the tension it embodies. An AI safety researcher joining the board of OpenAI could provide the company with deeper technical insight into alignment challenges — but it also raises questions about whether such proximity compromises the independence of safety research.

OpenAI has faced increasing scrutiny over its approach to safety as its models have grown more capable. The company's decision to publish increasingly detailed capability evaluations while simultaneously restricting access to its most powerful systems has drawn criticism from both sides of the safety debate: those who want faster development and those who want more caution.

---

## The Broader Context

The appointment comes at a moment of heightened attention to AI governance. Congress is actively debating several AI-related bills, the EU AI Act's provisions are entering into force, and the 2026 midterm elections are putting AI policy front and center in political campaigns.

Christiano's board seat gives OpenAI a direct line to one of the most technically sophisticated thinkers on AI safety — someone who has been among the most willing to publicly articulate what could go wrong. Whether that translates into meaningful governance changes at the company remains to be seen.

---

## Christiano's Own Words

In a statement accompanying the announcement, Christiano said he was joining the board because he believed OpenAI was at a critical juncture where governance decisions would have outsized impact on the long-term trajectory of AI development. He emphasized that his role would be focused on improving the company's technical safety practices and helping to develop more rigorous evaluation frameworks.

He also acknowledged the inherent tension of the position. "I think it's fair to be skeptical about whether this will actually make things better," he said in the statement. "Companies and governments have a long history of saying the right things about safety while doing the opposite. I'll try to be honest about what I think is actually happening."
