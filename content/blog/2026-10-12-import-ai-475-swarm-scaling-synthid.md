## Swarm Scaling: When AI Agents Work in Parallel

Toby Ord's analysis of AI swarms reveals a key insight: **swarms are useful when you're in a hurry**. A 4-agent swarm needs roughly twice the total tokens to match single-agent performance, but achieves the same task in half the wall clock time through parallelization.

The **"stepping on toes" parameter** describes how swarm scaling faces diminishing returns — coordinating more agents creates overhead, much like managing larger human teams. Scaling agents 10x yields only 3-5x performance improvement, not 10x.

Critically, swarm scaling may increase the likelihood of an **RSI-driven intelligence explosion** — Ord notes "I'd hoped that the value of λ for AI agents would be lower, making an intelligence explosion less likely, but that appears to not be the case."

## Americans Don't Trust AI Self-Governance

New polling from the Center for Shared AI Prosperity (CSAIP) reveals that **61% of Americans** believe current voluntary AI self-policing agreements are "not enough." Notably, 53% of Trump voters agree.

Additional findings: 54% of voters want government to "set and enforce rules for AI" — 61% of Harris voters and 48% of Trump voters.

The mood reflects growing public demand for binding AI regulation beyond industry self-commitments.

## SynthID Bio: Watermarking AI-Generated Biology

Google DeepMind's **SynthID Bio** extends watermarking to synthetic biology outputs. The system modifies amino acid sequences and adjusts 3D atomic coordinates in AI-designed proteins while maintaining functional properties.

In wet-lab testing across VEGF-A, SARS-CoV-2 spike protein RBD, and PD-L1, watermarked designs matched unwatermarked versions in hit rate and binding affinity. This enables detection of AI-generated biology without disrupting function.

## DeepMind's AI Science Economy Vision

DeepMind researchers argue we'll need a new **science economy** to manage AI-accelerated scientific discovery. As AI scientists proliferate, coordinating knowledge creation and validation becomes as important as the discoveries themselves.

The vision: AI systems that not only discover but also **curate, validate, and build on** each other's findings — a self-sustaining scientific ecosystem.
