# CEO Arrested for Allegedly Smuggling $300M in Nvidia Chips to China

The US Department of Justice has arrested Greg Lui, CEO of Earthmade Computer, for allegedly orchestrating a scheme to smuggle more than $300 million in Nvidia AI chips — including A100 and H100 GPUs — into China through shell companies and falsified shipping documentation.

The indictment, announced October 2, 2026, marks the latest escalation in the US government's ongoing effort to stem the flow of advanced AI hardware to China in violation of export controls.

---

## The Scheme

According to the DOJ, Lui used freight-forwarding firms in Malaysia and Singapore to illegally divert chip shipments. The defendants submitted false paperwork claiming the Nvidia GPUs were destined for Malaysian companies, when prosecutors say they knew the final destination was China.

One shipment described in the indictment involved 70 servers containing export-restricted GPUs worth approximately $7.6 million, routed through Malaysia with fraudulent documentation. Another batch of 92 servers was shipped from the US to Singapore, then to Malaysia, then Hong Kong — and ultimately to a Chinese AI company based in Hangzhou, described by The Wall Street Journal as China's AI hub.

In one particularly brazen move, Lui allegedly used a fake buyer's identity — including documents purchased from another individual — to mask a shipment of 100 servers worth over $22 million in Nvidia H100 GPUs.

---

## $176 Million in Revenue from Smuggling

Court documents allege that in 2024 alone, Earthmade Computer received more than $176 million from the smuggling operation. Bank records from Lui's accounts at Bank of America and JP Morgan provided investigators with evidence of the money trail.

The DOJ is seeking seizure of all funds gained through the alleged scheme. Lui faces three counts including conspiracy to violate the Export Control Reform Act, export smuggling, and money laundering.

---

## Nvidia's Ongoing Chip Smuggling Problem

The arrest is the latest in a series of cases involving Nvidia chip smuggling. In August 2026, a Nvidia senior manager was indicted for allegedly coordinating with Supermicro to circumvent export controls on AI servers destined for China.

Nvidia has been required to design specialized chips — including the H100 — for the Chinese market that comply with export restrictions. But the persistent smuggling of US-origin chips suggests that export controls face significant enforcement challenges even for designed-down variants.

Advanced AI chips remain central to US-China technology competition, and the scale of this latest smuggling case — $300 million in hardware — underscores the commercial incentives driving circumventors.
