# Meta Open-Sources Muse Gadgets SDK — Build Your Own AI Hardware

Meta has released an open-source toolkit called Muse Gadgets that lets developers create custom hardware devices integrating with its Muse AI assistant. The release includes firmware, a Linux SDK, and reference project designs, marking a notable expansion of Meta's strategy to move Muse beyond standalone chatbot interactions into physical hardware.

---

## From Chatbot to Hardware Platform

Muse, Meta's AI assistant built on its Llama model family, has until now operated primarily as a software product accessed through apps and web interfaces. Muse Gadgets changes that equation by providing the technical specifications for attaching Muse to sensors, displays, and home automation systems.

The SDK supports low-cost development boards including Raspberry Pi and ESP32, allowing hobbyists and professionals alike to connect Muse to custom hardware. Example projects in the documentation include an e-ink display showing Muse responses and an HDMI dongle that brings Muse to any television.

Meta's Nat Friedman, head of product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, noted on X that the company built a prototype called Muse Home Link — a USB-C device that connects Muse to home networks and smart devices. Meta produced 5,000 units and distributed them free to Muse subscribers, though Friedman indicated they were claimed quickly after his announcement received nearly 30,000 views.

---

## Business and Enterprise Push

The Muse Gadgets release fits a broader strategy Meta outlined this week at TechCrunch Disrupt. Alongside the hardware SDK, the company introduced Muse for Small Business — a free tier connecting Muse to Shopify, Dropbox, and Slack — and launched a new Meta Enterprise Platform division targeting corporate customers.

This dual-track approach of open hardware tools for developers while building paid enterprise relationships mirrors Meta's playbook from other platform expansions. The question is whether third-party hardware innovation will materialize at meaningful scale, or whether Muse Gadgets remains a developer curiosity.
