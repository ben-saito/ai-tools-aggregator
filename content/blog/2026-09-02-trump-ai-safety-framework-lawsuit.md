# Nonprofit Sues Trump Administration to Reveal Secret AI Model Review Framework

A nonprofit organization called Protect Democracy filed a lawsuit on September 2, 2026 seeking to force the Trump administration to disclose its framework for reviewing AI models before public release. The organization alleges that "almost no details" about the review process have been released to the public or Congress, and that the framework has only been shared with a select group of AI companies.

---

## The Secret Framework

In August, the White House announced completion of a voluntary framework for reviewing AI models before public release. The framework emerged after the government flagged Anthropic's Mythos 5 model as too dangerous to release earlier this summer, prompting rapid expansion of industry collaborations through the Center for AI Safety.

Protect Democracy's lawsuit claims the framework has been shared only with "a select set of AI companies," excluding AI safety groups, independent researchers, and smaller AI firms. The organization alleges that decisions about which AI models are approved may be the most important policy question of this White House.

---

## Concerns About Accountability

The nonprofit alleges the framework could enable corruption, with officials from the Office of the National Cyber Director, the Office of Science and Technology Policy, the Treasury Department, and the Commerce Department potentially favoring rapid deployments for AI firms that Trump has financial ties to.

Protect Democracy is seeking an order requiring officials to produce all information by September 30, including the framework's text, terms of participation, identity of participants, and the process and criteria by which AI models are assessed.

---

## Connection to CISA and GOLD EAGLE

The lawsuit has implications for the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act (CISA) of 2015, which provides liability protections for information sharing between government and industry. Those protections, which the administration says sustain the GOLD EAGLE program for sharing vulnerability information, are set to expire.

Protect Democracy says Congress cannot meaningfully oversee programs whose terms it has not been shown, and that courts cannot review actions they cannot identify. The group wants all documents produced through FOIA requests posted publicly if the lawsuit succeeds.
