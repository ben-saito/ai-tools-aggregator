# Amazon Destroys Rare Books to Train AI - Airtag Tracked Shipment to Vegas Facility

---

## How It Came to Light

In August 2026, IT outlet 404 Media revealed that Amazon has been purchasing and destroying rare books en masse for AI model training. A bookseller agreed to plant an AirTag in a rare book included in a bulk order. The AirTag was tracked to an Amazon AI training facility in Las Vegas, where a team called VGT3 has been systematically tearing books from their spines and scanning pages.

The facility's door displayed a T-rex logo prepared to "devour a book" - an image 404 Media published alongside their report.

---

## Training Data Shortage

Amazon's book digitization team, operating under the name VGT3, has been shredding rare texts to create training data. Workers posted in online forums that earlier in 2026, Amazon had nearly run out of books to scan. The shortage became so severe that the facility faced potential shutdown. Amazon subsequently widened its procurement of rare books through various channels.

Unlike competitors Anthropic and xAI, which have publicly stated they do not train on rare or antique books, Amazon has declined to specifically address its training data composition, offering only a generic statement: "Amazon purchases books through commercial channels to help develop and improve the products and services our customers use."

---

## Reactions

Book lovers have called the destructive scanning "horrifying." Rare books are particularly valuable for AI training because they contain unique text difficult to find online. This makes them a potential competitive advantage - a reason to keep the practice secret rather than disclose it.

---

## Assessment

From a business standpoint, keeping training data proprietary makes sense for differentiation. However, destroying rare books represents an irreversible risk to cultural heritage. Copyright-expired publications could serve as an alternative source for AI training data.
