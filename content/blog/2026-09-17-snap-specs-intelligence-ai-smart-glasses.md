# Snap Unveils Specs Intelligence: A New AI-Powered OS for Its $2,200 Smart Glasses

Snap is making another push for its $2,200 Spectacles smart glasses, unveiling a suite of new features at an event in Los Angeles this week. The centerpiece is **Specs Intelligence**, described by CEO Evan Spiegel as an "AI native operating system" designed to anticipate user needs and connect the dots across Snap's AR camera effects, AI chatbots, and services.

---

## From AR Selfies to Enterprise AI Tool

Since the debut of Spectacles earlier this year, Snap has struggled to justify the $2,200 price tag and bulkiness of the hardware to mainstream consumers. The company is now repositioning the device as a professional tool, launching **Specs for Enterprise** with partnerships across major IT management and security providers.

During the event, Spiegel demonstrated how IT departments could deploy and manage Spectacles across teams using mobile device management systems. "We're building out mobile device management, security controls, and ways to deploy and manage stacks across teams with the world's most trusted providers," Spiegel said, emphasizing enterprise adoption as a key growth vector.

---

## Entertainment and AI Integration

On the consumer side, Spectacles can now connect with HBO Max and Spotify, providing streaming and music integration that smart glasses have historically lacked. The new Specs Intelligence system represents Snap's most ambitious AI integration yet, attempting to create an ecosystem where the glasses serve as an ambient computing interface rather than a simple AR display.

The AI system draws on Snap's strengths in AR lenses and visual recognition, but extends beyond pure visual inputs to encompass a broader personal assistant functionality. The exact technical specifications and the underlying AI model powering Specs Intelligence remain undisclosed.

---

## Strategic Pivot or Desperation?

Industry analysts are divided on whether the new features represent a genuine strategic pivot or an attempt to salvage a product that has yet to find product-market fit. The $2,200 price point continues to be a significant barrier for mass consumer adoption, and the enterprise market for AI-connected smart glasses remains nascent.

What is clear is that Snap is committing to the hardware business for the long haul, betting that ambient AI interfaces will eventually replace smartphones as the primary computing platform. Whether that vision materializes in time to save Spectacles from becoming another Google Glass remains to be seen.
