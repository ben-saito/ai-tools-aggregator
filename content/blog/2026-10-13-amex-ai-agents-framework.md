# Amex Introduces Framework to Prevent AI Agents from Making Unauthorized Purchases

---

Personal AI agents are increasingly making transactions on their users' behalf, but given the nondeterministic nature of large language models, AI bots are prone to doing what they're not supposed to.

To get ahead of this problem, American Express is introducing a new framework designed to capture and authenticate a customer's purchase intent before an AI agent completes the transaction.

The idea is straightforward: before an AI agent executes a purchase, it must present the transaction details to the card network and receive authorization based on the cardholder's known preferences and spending patterns.

## The AI Agent Economy Problem

As AI agents proliferate — assistants that can book travel, order supplies, negotiate contracts, and execute transactions — the payment infrastructure wasn't designed for machine-initiated purchases. Traditional 3D Secure and fraud detection systems assume human decision-making patterns and response times.

Amex's framework creates a standards-based way for AI agents to declare their intent, providing merchants and networks with enough context to distinguish legitimate agent activity from compromised or misbehaving bots.

## Enterprise Implications

Enterprises deploying AI agents for procurement, travel booking, and supply chain management need auditable transaction authorization that fits within existing financial controls. Amex's approach provides a path to compliant AI agent deployment in enterprise contexts.

American Express says it's working with major card issuers and merchants to adopt the framework as an industry standard.
