Building a Safer Path to Autonomous Industrial AI

Industrial AI is entering a new phase. After decades of predictive analytics and specialized applications, advances in foundation models, physical AI, and agentic AI are making it possible to automate entire industrial workflows.

The shift from narrow AI to autonomous AI systems in industrial settings brings new challenges around **safety, governance, and human oversight**. Organizations deploying fully autonomous AI in manufacturing, logistics, and energy need frameworks that can handle failure modes unprecedented in traditional software.

AVEVA's chief technologist Arti Garg outlines several critical considerations for safe industrial AI deployment:

**Data governance for autonomous systems**: Training data quality becomes existential when AI decisions affect physical processes. Organizations need rigorous data lineage, drift detection, and continuous validation pipelines.

**Human oversight mechanisms**: Even as AI takes on more autonomous roles, human operators need meaningful intervention points. The challenge is designing oversight that is neither too hands-off nor so intrusive that it negates automation benefits.

**Failure mode analysis**: Unlike traditional software bugs, AI failures in physical systems can be catastrophic. Redundant safety systems, graceful degradation, and clear emergency protocols are essential.

The transition to autonomous industrial AI is not purely technical—it requires organizational change, new skill sets, and updated regulatory frameworks. Companies that invest in safety infrastructure now will be better positioned as the industry matures.

## The Foundation Model Opportunity

Foundation models trained on industrial data offer promising capabilities for fault prediction, process optimization, and predictive maintenance. However, their deployment requires careful validation against domain-specific safety requirements that differ substantially from consumer AI applications.
