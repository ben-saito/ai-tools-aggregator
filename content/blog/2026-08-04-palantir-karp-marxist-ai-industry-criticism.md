# Palantir CEO Alex Karp Calls AI Industry "Marxist" After Record $1.1B Profit Quarter

Palantir CEO Alex Karp has once again criticized AI frontier labs, this time calling the broader AI industry "Marxist" in unusually strong language following Palantir's record-breaking quarter. The company reported $1.9 billion in revenue and $1.1 billion in profit for Q2 2026 — more profit in a single quarter than the company generated in total revenue just one year prior.

---

## Karp's Unusual Critique: "Marxist Overtones"

In Palantir's quarterly shareholder letter, Karp wrote: "There are Marxist overtones and undertones to our business. Others, including many of those building large language models, intend, knowingly or otherwise, to capture the means of production of their purported partners."

The CEO, who holds a PhD in social theory, drew a sharp distinction between Palantir's approach — which emphasizes data sovereignty and model-agnostic AI — and that of frontier labs that seek to accumulate customer data and intellectual property within their own systems.

---

## "Token Self Pleasurings" and the "Colonization" Critique

On the quarterly conference call with Wall Street analysts, Karp elaborated on his critique, using colorful language to characterize what he sees as the extractionary nature of many AI business models. He described customers as "paying for the right for them to migrate your IP, your know-how, your expertise to their model, so that they can build a competitive business that doesn't require your business or people."

He argued that AI labs are "doing it for what they believe are moral reasons. They are superior to you. They deserve to colonize your enterprise."

---

## Palantir's Contrasting Approach: Data Sovereignty

Karp contrasted Palantir's model-agnostic software, which serves governments and enterprises, with the proprietary approaches of frontier AI labs. Palantir allows organizations to control their data and their AI "exhaust" — prompts, orchestration, and context — rather than allowing that information to flow to external model providers.

This stance has found resonance in the defense and government sectors, where Palantir has seen significant growth. The company's emphasis on keeping AI infrastructure within customer environments appeals to organizations wary of sending sensitive data to external AI providers.

---

## The Numbers: 93% Revenue Growth

Despite his criticism of the AI industry, Karp acknowledged that the broader AI boom has been good for Palantir. The company reported $1.9 billion in revenue for Q2 2026, up 93% year-over-year, along with $1.1 billion in profit. The company has now generated more profit in a single quarter than it did in total revenue just one year prior, a testament to the accelerating demand for enterprise AI infrastructure that keeps data under customer control.
