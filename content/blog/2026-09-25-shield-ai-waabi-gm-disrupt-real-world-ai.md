# Shield AI, Waabi, and General Motors Discuss Building AI Where Failure Is Not an Option

Executives from Shield AI, Waabi, and General Motors appeared at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 to discuss building AI systems for high-stakes environments where failures carry serious consequences, TechCrunch reported September 24, 2026.

---

## Shield AI: Autonomous Drones in Combat

Shield AI builds AI pilot systems for military aircraft. The company's technology enables drones to operate in GPS-denied and jammed environments where human operators cannot maintain control. The focus is on reliability — the AI must function correctly the first time and every time.

---

## Waabi: Autonomous Trucks at Scale

Waabi is developing AI for commercial trucking, targeting the freight industry's driver shortage. Unlike passenger vehicles, trucks operate on fixed routes for extended periods, but they present enormous physics and reaction-time constraints. Waabi's approach combines simulation with on-road validation.

---

## General Motors: AI in Vehicles

GM's participation reflects the automaker's investment in AI-assisted driving and autonomous vehicle technology. The company has deployed Super Cruise and is working toward full self-driving capability, balancing consumer convenience with safety regulatory requirements.

---

## The Reliability Imperative

The common thread across all three companies is that their AI systems operate in domains where a mistake can be fatal. This creates design constraints that consumer AI applications don't face: exhaustive validation, fail-safe behaviors, and graceful degradation under unexpected inputs.

---

## Regulatory Landscape

High-stakes AI deployments face heightened regulatory scrutiny. The discussion at Disrupt likely addressed how to demonstrate safety to regulators while maintaining competitive development timelines.
