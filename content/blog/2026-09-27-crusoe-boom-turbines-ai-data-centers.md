# Crusoe Abandons $1.25B Plan for Boom Supersonic Turbines at AI Data Centers

Crusoe, one of the largest builders of AI data centers in the United States, has abandoned its $1.25 billion plan to purchase 29 Superpower turbines from Boom Supersonic for deployment at its AI data centers. The cancellation comes after months of partnership discussions and represents a notable shift in how AI infrastructure companies are approaching power generation for compute-heavy facilities.

---

## From Bitcoin Mining to AI Data Centers

Founded in 2018 as a bitcoin mining operation that ran on excess natural gas from oil fields, Crusoe has transformed into a major player in AI infrastructure. The company now operates some of the largest AI data centers in the country, including a massive campus in Abilene, Texas that provides computing power to OpenAI. Crusoe is also building a 900 megawatt data center in Abilene for Microsoft.

The company positions itself around building "AI factories from the power up," emphasizing flexible energy solutions that can adapt to each site's evolving needs.

---

## Boom Supersonic's Pivot to Power Generation

Boom Supersonic, best known for developing the Overture supersonic passenger jet, launched a new business line last year to sell stationary power plants based on a derivative of the jet engine it was developing. The Superpower turbine shares approximately 80% of its parts with the airborne engine, representing a significant technology transfer from aviation to power generation.

Under the now-cancelled deal, Crusoe was to be the first customer, agreeing to purchase 29 of Boom's 42-megawatt Superpower turbines for $1.25 billion, with first deliveries scheduled to begin in 2027.

---

## The Breakup

Boom Supersonic CEO Blake Scholl announced the termination of the partnership in a post on X, congratulating Crusoe founders Cully Cavness and Chase Lochmarter on their company's recent funding raise before noting that the companies were no longer moving forward with the turbine launch partnership. Scholl emphasized that other customers were in Boom's pipeline, targeting approximately 250 megawatts of Superpower deliveries next year and 1 gigawatt in 2028.

A line Scholl initially included but later removed read: "The TL/DR is that turbines are no longer part of Crusoe's near term primary power mix at Abilene/etc., so a launch partnership just didn't make sense."

Crusoe spokesperson Andrew Schmitt confirmed that the company's energy plans had evolved, stating that Crusoe "stays flexible, choosing the energy solutions that are right for each site as its needs evolve."

---

## Grid Power for the Abilene Facility

Crusoe's initial 1.2 gigawatt data center in Abilene — built for Oracle and OpenAI — is powered by the electrical grid, with a gas-turbine power plant serving only as backup capacity. The shift away from Boom's turbines suggests that AI data center operators are increasingly defaulting to direct grid connections and established utility infrastructure rather than novel power generation approaches.
