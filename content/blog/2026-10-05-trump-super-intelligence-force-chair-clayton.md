# Trump Unveils Super Intelligence Force, Taps Clayton as Chair

The Trump administration has formally established the Super Intelligence Force, a new federal task force charged with coordinating the government's response to advanced AI risks and opportunities. The announcement represents the latest evolution in the administration's evolving stance on AI safety, following an executive order last week that officially rebranded artificial intelligence as "super intelligence" in official communications.

---

## A New Task Force Takes Shape

The Super Intelligence Force is tasked with coordinating the efforts of the Federal Government to ensure America continues to lead the world in super intelligence, which administration officials have described as larger than previous technological shifts. The Wall Street Journal reports that Clayton will chair the task force, effectively making him the de facto AI safety czar for the administration.

The task force has been given 120 days to produce a report assessing the risks and opportunities presented by advanced AI systems. The charter reportedly commits the force to developing plans for responding to "SI-enabled threats to our society, while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation."

---

## From Rebranding to Implementation

The task force announcement follows an executive order signed last week that formally adopted the term "super intelligence" to describe advanced AI systems, replacing "artificial intelligence" in official government communications. That executive order was accompanied by a gathering of major tech executives including Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Elon Musk, and Anthropic's Dario Amodei, all of whom signed what the President called a "morally binding" voluntary safety pledge.

The distinction between "AI" and "super intelligence" remains undefined in technical terms, with the executive order focusing on aspirational criteria rather than technical benchmarks. Critics have noted that the rebranding could complicate international regulatory coordination, given that no agreed-upon technical definition exists for what constitutes "super intelligence" versus conventional AI.

---

## Industry Reception

The voluntary nature of the CEO safety pledge has prompted skepticism from observers who note that competitive pressures in the AI industry make non-binding commitments difficult to enforce. The task force's 120-day reporting timeline suggests concrete policy proposals are still forthcoming, though the specifics of what "responding to SI-enabled threats" means in practice remain unclear.

The formation of the Super Intelligence Force marks a notable organizational step, even as the substantive scope of its authority and the details of its mandate continue to take shape.
