# Palo Alto Networks Pays $500M for AI Security Startup Console

Palo Alto Networks has acquired Console, an AI-powered security operations startup backed by Thrive Capital, for approximately $500 million, according to sources familiar with the deal. The acquisition marks one of the largest security infrastructure deals of 2026 and positions Palo Alto Networks to strengthen its Cortex platform with Console's agentic threat detection capabilities.

Console, founded in 2024 by Andrei Serban following his previous exit of code-security platform Fuzzbuzz to Rippling, had raised $29 million across two funding rounds including a $6.2 million seed led by SV Angel and Abstract Ventures. Prior to the acquisition, Console was valued at $157 million according to PitchBook, delivering a swift return for early investors.

The company competed primarily with Serval, another ServiceNow challenger that achieved unicorn status after raising a $75 million Series B led by Sequoia last December. Following the Console acquisition, industry watchers believe Serval becomes the de facto startup leader in AI IT service automation.

Palo Alto Networks will integrate Console into its Cortex platform, which uses AI to automatically detect and neutralize threats across enterprise environments. Console's agentic functionality allows security teams to automate complex incident response workflows that previously required manual intervention. The deal represents Palo Alto Networks' seventh acquisition of a venture-backed company in 2026, following earlier purchases including Greylock and Lux Capital-backed startups.

---

## Background on Console and Fuzzbuzz

The Console acquisition is notable because founder Andrei Serban had previously built Fuzzbuzz, a code security platform that was acquired by Rippling. Fuzzbuzz focused on automated security testing for software codebases, a market that intersects with both DevOps workflows and AI-assisted code generation tools.

After the Fuzzbuzz exit, Serban founded Console in 2024 to address a gap in enterprise security operations: the inability of traditional Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) tools to handle the scale and complexity of AI-generated alerts across cloud-native infrastructure. Console's platform was designed from the ground up for agentic AI systems that can autonomously investigate and respond to threats.

Customers of Console included Ramp, Flock Safety, and other growth-stage technology companies that needed security operations tooling capable of handling high-volume, automated workflows.

---

## Market Context

The acquisition reflects continued consolidation in the AI security operations market, where traditional security vendors are racing to acquire AI-native startups to compete with pure-play security operations platforms. Palo Alto Networks' aggressive acquisition strategy in 2026 has been focused on building out its Cortex platform to handle the shift toward autonomous security agents that can operate without human oversight in real-time.

The $500 million price tag represents roughly 3.2x Console's last valuation of $157 million, a premium that reflects the strategic value of Console's agentic security operations technology as enterprises deploy AI agents across critical infrastructure.

---

## Serval's Position

With Console absorbed into Palo Alto Networks, Serval — backed by Sequoia Capital — emerges as the primary independent competitor in the AI IT service automation space. Serval achieved a $1 billion valuation after its Series B in December 2025, making it one of the most valuable private companies in the security operations sector.
