# Congress's Favorite AI Tool? ChatGPT Dominates Paid AI Use on Capitol Hill

House spending records reveal that OpenAI's ChatGPT has become the dominant paid AI tool among congressional offices, with staff relying on the chatbot to draft memos, summarize legislation, and assist with constituent communications. The disclosure places ChatGPT at the center of a broader shift in how the U.S. legislative branch adopts AI tools — raising questions about oversight, data handling, and the speed at which government agencies are integrating generative AI systems.

---

## ChatGPT's Footprint in Congressional Offices

The spending records, reviewed by TechCrunch, show a clear preference for ChatGPT over competing enterprise AI products. Congressional offices have been using the chatbot for a range of tasks that span both administrative and policy-related functions. Drafting internal memoranda and summarizing legislative documents are among the most common use cases, reflecting the high volume of written material that passes through a typical lawmaker's office.

Constituent communication represents another significant use category. Staff members handling incoming mail and email have begun using ChatGPT to draft form responses and compile constituent concerns into readable summaries — a workflow that mirrors the private sector's adoption of AI for customer-facing text tasks.

The pattern of adoption tracks closely with what has been observed in the executive branch and among federal contractors, where ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot have led enterprise AI deployments. However, the legislative branch has moved with notable speed despite the absence of comprehensive federal AI governance frameworks.

---

## Data Handling and Compliance Questions

The congressional adoption of ChatGPT raises data handling questions that have yet to be fully resolved at the federal level. Congressional offices are not subject to the same Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program (FedRAMP) requirements as executive branch agencies, meaning the evaluation and approval process for AI tools in the legislature operates on a different track.

OpenAI's enterprise agreements with government customers include data processing terms that prohibit the use of customer inputs for model training — a provision that addresses one category of concern. However, questions persist about how ChatGPT interactions are logged, whether transcripts are retained, and how offices ensure that sensitive legislative information does not enter AI systems that retain or process it beyond the immediate interaction.

The Senate and House each maintain their own internal IT governance structures, and both have issued guidance on AI use, but no unified congressional policy on generative AI exists as of this writing. Individual offices have largely made their own determinations about acceptable AI tools and use cases.

---

## Broader Context: AI in Government

The congressional embrace of ChatGPT occurs against a backdrop of accelerating AI adoption across the federal government. Federal agencies have moved to integrate AI into functions ranging from healthcare administration to tax processing, often faster than regulatory frameworks can adapt. The Government Accountability Office (GAO) has published multiple reports flagging the need for stronger AI governance, but legislative action on AI-specific rules has moved slowly.

ChatGPT's penetration into congressional offices also reflects the broader reality that consumer-grade AI tools have outpaced the development of purpose-built government AI platforms. Congressional staff, like their counterparts in the private sector, have turned to widely available tools rather than waiting for government-specific alternatives.

For technology developers, the congressional adoption pattern signals continued momentum for enterprise AI products with strong text-processing capabilities. It also underscores the policy gap: as AI tools become entrenched in core government functions, the demand for coherent governance frameworks that address procurement, data handling, and accountability is likely to intensify.
