# Meta Settles Child Safety Claims for $18B; Florida Rejects Deal

Meta has agreed to a landmark settlement with nearly all U.S. states, resolving claims that the company designed its products to foster compulsive use by children and failed to warn users of addiction and mental health risks. The deal is valued at up to $18 billion, though Florida rejected the settlement as insufficient, calling it "peanuts."

---

## Settlement Terms: Time Limits and Notification Controls

Under the settlement, Meta agreed to impose on users under 18 a default two-hour daily time limit that teens can only disable with parental permission. The agreement also includes a default block between midnight and 6 a.m. for minors and a school mode that mutes notifications from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.

Meta, which already uses ID checks and face analysis to verify user ages, will implement these restrictions across its platform, including Instagram and Facebook.

---

## Florida's Opposition

Florida Attorney General Ashley Moody rejected the settlement, arguing that $18 billion does not adequately compensate for the harm caused to children. Florida is pursuing separate legal action against Meta, with Moody calling the national settlement "peanuts" compared to the damage inflicted on Florida families.

---

## The Broader Regulatory Landscape

The settlement reflects growing regulatory pressure on social media companies regarding their impact on young users. The U.S. Surgeon General has called for warning labels on social media platforms, and several states are considering legislation to restrict minors' access to certain features.
