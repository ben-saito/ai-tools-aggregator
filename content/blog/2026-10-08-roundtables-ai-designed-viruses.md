# MIT Tech Review Roundtables: AI-Designed Viruses and the Future of Biosecurity

---

MIT Technology Review's subscriber-only roundtable series features a conversation with Samuel King, a Stanford University PhD student who used a generative AI model to propose genetic blueprints for microscopic viruses — work that raises fundamental questions about biosecurity and the future of AI in biology.

## AI in Genetic Design

In 2025, King used a generative AI model to propose genetic blueprints for microscopic viruses. While not an example of AI-generated life itself, the work represents a significant step toward AI-assisted biological design and raises important questions about access to such capabilities.

## Biosecurity Implications

The ability to design genetic sequences computationally, even for benign research purposes, opens concerning possibilities if the same techniques are applied to more dangerous pathogens. Unlike physical weapons, genetic information can potentially be replicated and distributed digitally, making containment difficult.

## Innovators Under 35

King was named one of MIT Technology Review's Innovators Under 35 for this work, recognizing both its technical innovation and its broader implications. The roundtable discusses not just the science but also the ethical frameworks needed as AI becomes increasingly capable in biological domains.

## What's Next

The full conversation explores questions about what safeguards might be appropriate, how the research community should handle potentially dangerous dual-use findings, and what role regulators should play without stifling beneficial research.
