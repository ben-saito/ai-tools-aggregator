# Microsoft President Says UK Is Best Placed to Drive AI Regulation

---

Microsoft president Brad Smith is calling for urgent regulation of AI, and his argument is that consumer products like milk and washing machines have more oversight than the companies building artificial intelligence.

On a recent BBC podcast, Smith called AI both a "weapon and a tool" that is putting the world's cybersecurity at risk and said the "moment has arrived" to institute global safety rules.

Smith also said the U.K., and not the U.S. or European Union, is best placed to lead on global AI safety regulation.

## Why the UK?

Smith's argument rests on several factors: the UK's existing expertise in AI safety research through institutions like the Alan Turing Institute, the UK government's stated commitment to being a leader in AI safety rather than just a passive regulator, and the practical reality that effective AI governance requires international coordination that the EU and U.S. have been slower to achieve.

The UK's AI Safety Institute has been conducting evaluations of frontier AI models and sharing findings internationally. Microsoft appears to view this as a more workable model for global coordination than the EU's more prescriptive regulatory approach.

## Microsoft's Regulatory Play

Microsoft has a complex position in the AI regulation debate: it benefits from regulatory clarity that helps enterprise customers feel confident deploying AI, but heavy-handed rules could also raise barriers to entry for competitors.

Smith's call for the UK to lead reflects a strategic preference for a regulatory approach that balances safety with innovation — one that Microsoft is well-positioned to influence given its significant UK investment and presence.
