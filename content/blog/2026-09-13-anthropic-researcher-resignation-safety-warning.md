# Anthropic researcher resignation sparks debate over AI safety timeline

An Anthropic researcher who resigned this week has publicly warned that the company is "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives," raising fresh questions about the pace of advanced AI development within the leading safety-focused lab.

The researcher, identified as Jacob Coxon, posted their concerns on X (formerly Twitter), citing worries about Anthropic's trajectory toward self-improving AI systems. The resignation comes at a particularly sensitive time, coinciding with CEO Dario Amodei's public calls for the industry to slow down.

---

## Background: Competing pressures on AI labs

Anthropic has long positioned itself as a safety-first AI company, with its Claude family of models emphasizing constitutional AI and harmlessness training. However, the departure of a researcher openly criticizing the company's safety direction highlights the tension between commercial pressure to advance capabilities and the stated mission of building safe AI.

The company's CEO Dario Amodei has recently published essays outlining strategies to pace the AI frontier, including proposals for industry coordination and third-party evaluators. Yet critics argue that these measures may be insufficient if development continues at current velocity.

---

## The resignation and its timing

The resignation's timing is notable: it follows Amodei's public statements about slowing AI development and coincides with a broader industry debate over AI safety timelines. Coxon's warnings about self-improving superintelligence represent a more alarmist position than Anthropic's official communications have historically taken.

Industry observers note that the incident reflects deeper divisions within the AI safety community about how to balance capability advancement with risk mitigation.
