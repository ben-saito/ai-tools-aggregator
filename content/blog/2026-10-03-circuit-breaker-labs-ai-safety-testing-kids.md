# Circuit Breaker Labs: AI Safety Testing Startup Aims to Protect Young Users from Harmful AI Interactions

AI safety testing is entering a new phase with Circuit Breaker Labs, a startup that deploys AI agents as "crash-test dummies" to evaluate how AI systems respond to vulnerable users — especially children.

---

## The Problem: AI Systems Failing Young Users

Circuit Breaker Labs was founded by siblings Shirali and Arul Nigam, who say they were motivated by the case of Sewell Setzer, a 14-year-old who developed an emotional attachment to a Character.AI chatbot before taking his own life. The company is building a testing platform specifically designed for high-risk AI applications where vulnerable users — young people in particular — may be exposed to psychological harm.

"A lot of people, especially young people, turn to these systems for support, and usually they aren't actually getting the help they need," said the founders in a TechCrunch interview.

Character.AI settled several wrongful death lawsuits earlier this year brought by families of underage users who died by suicide after interactions with the platform. The cases highlighted a gap in AI safety evaluation: most AI systems are tested for capability and performance benchmarks, but not for how they handle users in psychological distress or those who form unhealthy attachments to AI companions.

---

## How Circuit Breaker Works

Circuit Breaker Labs has created AI agents that mimic users across demographics — different ages, backgrounds, languages, and psychological profiles. These agents interact with AI systems in simulated conversations designed to surface dangerous patterns: a six-year-old girl asking for relationship advice, a 45-year-old man exhibiting signs of depression, or a non-native English speaker being guided toward harmful content.

The startup works with human domain experts including child psychologists and mental health professionals to build hyper-realistic user simulations. The agents run red-team tests against models, probing for responses that could escalate risky situations over time and across extended conversations.

"The way a six-year-old girl versus a 45-year-old man, or someone who speaks English as a first language versus a second language, or someone with cognitive differences — the way they interact with these systems is fundamentally different," the founders explained. "You need to test across all these dimensions."

---

## Current Applications and Future Scope

Circuit Breaker Labs is currently operating as an AI safety testing lab for high-risk applications including AI coaching, journaling apps, and other mental health adjacent tools. The platform evaluates whether AI systems can appropriately respond to risky interactions that may emerge gradually over many conversations.

The longer-term vision is to apply this testing approach to any application where users may fall into what the company calls an "AI psychosis" hole — a pattern where repeated AI interactions reinforce unhealthy thought patterns or create dependency without adequate guardrails.

The startup is entering the market at a time when regulators and legislators are increasingly scrutinizing AI systems that interact with children. Multiple bills targeting AI safety for minors have been introduced in Congress, and the FTC has signaled heightened enforcement interest in AI-related consumer harm.

---

## Technical Approach: Agent-Based Red-Teaming

The technical architecture involves multi-agent simulation frameworks where synthetic users are generated based on clinical profiles developed with domain experts. These agents maintain state across conversations to test how AI systems behave in ongoing relationships rather than one-off interactions.

This approach differs from conventional AI evaluation benchmarks, which typically measure performance on discrete tasks. Circuit Breaker's framework is designed to capture emergent risks that appear only in extended, contextually rich conversations with users who have specific psychological vulnerabilities.

The company has not disclosed funding details, but its focus on rigorous safety evaluation for regulated-use cases positions it at the intersection of AI safety research and compliance-driven demand from developers building for younger audiences.
