# Crusoe Abandons $1.25B Plan to Use Boom Supersonic Turbines at AI Data Centers

---

## Boom Supersonic CEO Confirms End of Partnership; Crusoe Cites Shift in Power Strategy

Crusoe, the data center developer that has become one of the largest builders of AI infrastructure in the United States, has abandoned its $1.25 billion plan to use Boom Supersonic's Superpower turbines at its AI data centers. The reversal was confirmed by Boom Supersonic CEO Blake Scholl in a post on X on September 25, 2026, who described the change as a strategic shift in Crusoe's primary power mix.

Under the original agreement, Crusoe had committed to purchasing 29 of Boom's 42-megawatt Superpower turbines, with first deliveries scheduled to begin in 2027. The deal was valued at $1.25 billion and would have marked Boom's first major entry into the stationary power generation market. Boom's Superpower turbine shares approximately 80% of its parts with the engine being developed for the company's Overture supersonic passenger jet.

"We build AI factories from the power up, and we're bringing new campuses online across the country, powered by innovative energy sources," said Crusoe spokesperson Andrew Schmitt in a statement. "As our portfolio grows, we stay flexible, choosing the energy solutions that are right for each site as its needs evolve."

Scholl's announcement came after congratulating Crusoe founders Cully Cavness and Chase Lochmiller on the company's recent $3.9 billion funding round, which pushed Crusoe's valuation to $30.9 billion. "The TL/DR is that turbines are no longer part of Crusoe's near term primary power mix at Abilene/etc., so a launch partnership just didn't make sense," Scholl wrote. He noted that Boom will be delivering approximately 250 megawatts of Superpower turbines to other sites next year, with a target of 1 gigawatt in 2028.

---

## From Crypto Mining to AI Infrastructure Powerhouse

Founded in 2018 as a bitcoin mining operation that ran on excess natural gas from oil fields, Crusoe has undergone a dramatic transformation into a major AI data center builder. The company now counts Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI among its customers, and has constructed massive campuses including one in Abilene, Texas that supplies computing power directly to OpenAI.

The company's rapid growth has been fueled by billions in funding. In addition to the recent $3.9 billion round, Crusoe previously raised $3 billion at a $30 billion valuation, and signed a landmark $13 billion, five-year cloud contract. The company has attracted investment from Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital, Valor Equity Partners, Founders Fund, GIC, Nvidia, Qatar Investment Authority, Radical Ventures, and TPG.

---

## Implications for AI Infrastructure Energy Strategy

The abandonment of the Boom turbine plan highlights the evolving energy strategies being adopted by AI data center operators. As the industry scales to meet explosive demand for AI computing power, operators are carefully evaluating which power technologies best match their operational requirements and expansion timelines.

Boom Supersonic continues to advance its Superpower turbine technology for deployment at other sites, demonstrating continued market interest in the company's approach to stationary power generation despite losing its launch customer. The incident underscores the dynamic nature of the AI infrastructure buildout, where strategic partnerships and technology choices are subject to rapid revision as the sector matures.
