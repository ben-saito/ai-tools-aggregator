# Cal AI's 19-Year-Old Founder Just Raised $10M for His New AI Startup

---

Zach Yadegari, the teenage co-founder of the widely-used Cal AI calorie tracking app, has launched a new venture in the personal AI agent space — and investors are betting big on him.

Yadegari's new startup is positioning itself as a personal AI agent competing directly with Instinct, Muse, and Bee. Unlike traditional AI assistants that respond to single prompts, these agents are designed to proactively manage tasks, schedules, and information flows on behalf of the user.

## From Consumer App to AI Agent Startup

Yadegari's trajectory is notable: he built Cal AI as a calorie and nutrition tracking app powered by computer vision and AI, which gained significant traction among health-conscious consumers. Selling a meaningful stake to investors at a young age — and now pivoting to the hotly contested AI agent market — signals a new generation of founders targeting the enterprise and productivity AI space.

The $10M seed round will reportedly go toward building out the core agent architecture, hiring engineers, and acquiring early enterprise customers.

## The Personal AI Agent Market Heats Up

The funding round places Yadegari's new company in a crowded field. Instinct, Muse, and Bee have each garnered attention for their approaches to personal AI — from proactive scheduling to deep integration with email and calendar tools.

What distinguishes Yadegari's approach, according to sources familiar with the product, is a focus on **domain-specific agents** — AI that specializes in particular use cases (health, productivity, creative work) rather than acting as a general-purpose assistant.

## Developer Perspective

For developers watching this space, the personal AI agent market represents a significant opportunity for integration and extensibility. Agents that can call external APIs, execute code, and maintain persistent context across sessions require robust tool-calling infrastructure — an area where developer tooling is still catching up to the capability of the underlying models.
