# Keenable Exits Stealth with $26M Seed to Build Web Index for AI Agents

Keenable, a startup building a vast web search index specifically designed for AI agents, has emerged from stealth mode with a $26 million seed round led by Accel. The company aims to solve one of the fundamental challenges in agentic AI: giving autonomous systems the ability to find and verify information across the ever-growing internet.

---

## Why Current Search Falls Short for AI Agents

Traditional search engines are optimized for human users — they return ranked pages, snippets, and multimedia content designed for human consumption. But AI agents need something different: structured, machine-readable data they can synthesize, reason over, and act upon.

Keenable's approach involves crawling and indexing the web at scale, then serving that index to AI agents through an API designed for programmatic access. The index includes not just page content but also relationships between entities, factual claims, and the kind of structured data that allows an AI to draw conclusions rather than just retrieve documents.

"The world seems to be ready for this," said Keenable's leadership in an interview with TechCrunch. "AI agents are proliferating, but they're fundamentally limited by the fact that the web wasn't designed for them."

---

## The $26 Million Bet

Accel's investment in Keenable reflects a broader trend in venture capital: the belief that AI infrastructure — the tools, platforms, and services that make AI applications possible — represents a massive opportunity. While many investors are focused on AI applications or foundational models, infrastructure plays often get less attention but can be equally valuable.

The seed round will be used to expand Keenable's crawling infrastructure, improve its index's coverage and freshness, and build out the engineering team. The company is hiring aggressively, according to sources familiar with its plans.

---

## Competition and Market Position

Keenable enters a market where search incumbents like Google have shown limited interest in serving AI agents directly. Google's AI Overviews and similar features are designed to synthesize information for humans, not to provide the kind of programmatic, high-volume access that agents require.

There are other startups targeting this space, but the market remains early and fragmented. Keenable's bet is that as AI agents become more prevalent — in coding, research, customer service, and other domains — the demand for agent-native search infrastructure will grow substantially.

---

## Developer Implications

For developers building AI agents, Keenable represents a potential alternative to building custom web scraping pipelines or relying on traditional search APIs that weren't designed for programmatic use cases. The company's index could serve as a backbone for agents that need to browse the web, verify facts, or gather real-time information.

Whether Keenable can achieve the scale and reliability needed to serve production AI systems remains to be seen. But the company has identified a genuine gap in the current AI ecosystem, and the funding to pursue it.
