Major tech companies including Amazon and Microsoft are moving away from using nondisclosure agreements when negotiating data center deals with local governments, a shift that could reshape how communities evaluate and respond to AI infrastructure proposals.

The change reflects growing pressure on the AI industry to address concerns about data center expansion. Critics have argued that confidentiality agreements prevent public scrutiny of deals that can include substantial tax incentives and affect community resources.

Amazon recently committed to dropping NDAs following Microsoft's earlier decision to do the same. The commitments come as part of a broader effort by the industry to preempt stricter regulations and improve community relations amid mounting opposition to data center development in several US regions.

The TechCrunch analysis notes that data center negotiations have historically been conducted behind closed doors, with communities often learning about deals only after agreements were finalized. The new approach represents a transparency experiment for an industry that has faced criticism for its opacity.

## Key Highlights

- **Companies:** Amazon, Microsoft
- **Policy Change:** Ending NDA usage in data center negotiations
- **Goal:** Improve community relations and preempt regulation
- **Context:** Growing opposition to AI infrastructure expansion

## Technical Perspective

Data centers are the physical backbone of AI operations, housing the servers and networking equipment required to train and deploy AI models at scale. The expansion of AI capabilities has accelerated data center construction, drawing attention to energy consumption and environmental impacts.

The move toward transparent negotiations reflects the industry's recognition that social license is becoming a critical factor in AI infrastructure deployment. Companies that fail to address community concerns face increasing risk of regulatory pushback and project delays.
