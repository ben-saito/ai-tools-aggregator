# XDOF in Talks for Series B at $1.2B Valuation Just Three Months After Exiting Stealth

---

## Overview

Robot data infrastructure startup XDOF is in discussions for a Series B funding round that could value the company at $1.2 billion, according to a TechCrunch report. The company, co-founded by UC Berkeley researchers Philipp Wu (CEO) and Fred Shentu (CTO) in 2024, has raised $70 million in Series A funding as recently as June 2026, with participation from Thrive Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, Lux, and Spark Capital.

---

## The Robot Data Problem

XDOF was born from a research challenge: while large language models trained on internet-scale data drove the LLM boom, physical robots lack an equivalent data foundation. As a PhD student, CEO Philipp Wu studied how robots learn from large datasets and encountered a major obstacle -- the absence of large-scale training data for physical tasks.

To solve this, Wu and Shentu developed GELLO, a low-cost teleoperation system that lets human operators control robotic arms remotely to generate training data. This research became foundational for XDOF and led to an influential paper in robotics.

---

## Business Model: Scale AI for Physical Robotics

Investors describe XDOF as "the Scale AI or Mercor for physical robotics" -- referring to the data-labeling giants that powered the LLM era. XDOF aims to build data pipelines, collection tools, and annotation systems that frontier AI labs and robotics companies cannot easily build themselves, essentially functioning as an outsourced data supply chain for the robotics industry.

The company is partnering with UC Berkeley's AI Research (BAIR) lab to release what it claims will be the largest collection of high-quality robot training data, called ABC. To capture this data, XDOF combines remote robot teleoperation with human collectors wearing sensors to record everyday tasks like folding clothes and flattening boxes.

---

## Rapid Growth and Customer Base

XDOF previously told TechCrunch it is already working with 20 customers, including several frontier AI labs. The company plans to hire and train data collection teams worldwide, including teleoperators who remotely steer robots and egocentric operators who wear body sensors to capture movement data.

The rapid progression from Series A to Series B discussions underscores the intense investor appetite for robotics and physical AI infrastructure, as the industry seeks to replicate the data infrastructure success that powered the LLM revolution.
