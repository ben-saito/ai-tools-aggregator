# HackerRank Launches Chakra: An AI Interviewer That Evaluates Real-World Coding Skills

HackerRank has made Chakra, its AI-powered interview platform, generally available after six months in beta. The system has already conducted more than 500,000 interviews, with Snowflake, Snorkel, and Capgemini among the early enterprise customers. The launch positions AI not just as a screening tool but as an active evaluator of how candidates approach real work.

---

## From Code Tests to Job Simulations

Traditional technical interviews have long centered on artificial scenarios: candidates solve isolated algorithm problems under time pressure, demonstrating algorithmic thinking rather than the messy, context-dependent work of actual software development. HackerRank built its original business on exactly this model.

Chakra represents a deliberate break from it. Rather than presenting a candidate with a standalone coding puzzle, Chakra gives them a task involving a real-world code repository. The candidate works through the problem using the same tools, documentation patterns, and debugging approaches they would use on the job. The system evaluates not just whether they arrive at the correct answer, but how they navigate ambiguity, use available resources, and build toward a solution.

"The previous modality of evaluation was evaluating the output," said HackerRank co-founder and CEO Vivek Ravisankar. "Now, because of AI, anybody can produce an artifact." The shift reflects a broader recognition that in an era where AI can generate plausible code, the differentiator is not what you produce but how you work.

---

## Suspicious Activity Down, Not Up

One counterintuitive finding from Chakra's beta period: when candidates had access to AI assistance during the interview, the rate of suspicious activity flags actually dropped by 70 to 80 percent. This runs against the expectation that giving candidates AI tools would increase cheating.

HackerRank's interpretation is that the ability to use AI in a controlled way creates a more comfortable testing environment. Candidates who might otherwise feel pressure to cheat are instead able to demonstrate their process through collaboration with AI tools they would actually use on the job.

The platform also reframes what it means to "pass" an interview. Rather than a binary correct-or-incorrect judgment, Chakra generates a multidimensional assessment of how a candidate approaches problems, adapts to feedback, and structures their thinking.

---

## The Business Bet

Chakra represents a significant pivot for HackerRank's economic model. The company's original business was selling standardized coding assessments to HR and engineering teams — a transactional relationship based on test scores. Chakra opens a more ongoing consulting-style engagement, where the interview process itself becomes a product that reflects the hiring company's actual engineering culture and workflow.

Enterprise customers like Snowflake and Capgemini are early signals that the model resonates in the corporate hiring market. Whether smaller companies and startups will adopt the higher-touch format remains an open question as the product moves beyond its initial rollout phase.
