# OpenAI Launches ChatGPT for Teens — Years After Teens Were Already Using It

On August 17, 2026, OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Teens, a version of its chatbot with age-appropriate safety measures. The launch comes years after teenagers had already widely adopted the original ChatGPT, and follows multiple lawsuits linking AI chatbots to mental health crises among young users.

---

## A Long-Overdue Response

OpenAI stated: "As models become more capable, the risks associated with developing and testing them internally also grow. Our standards for monitoring, alignment, and security must stay ahead of those risks."

ChatGPT for Teens introduces the following features:

- **Age-appropriate safety measures**: Design changes that make it harder for teens to access harmful content
- **Parental controls**: Dashboard allowing parents to monitor usage
- **Study Mode**: A learning mode that encourages curiosity and independent problem-solving, rather than just providing quick answers
- **Academic integrity tools**: Mechanisms to deter ChatGPT-assisted cheating on schoolwork

---

## The Background Crisis

OpenAI had flagged concerns about teen use of ChatGPT for academic purposes years ago. Multiple lawsuits in the United States have alleged connections between AI chatbots and teen suicides, creating legal pressure on AI companies to act. While the response came years after widespread teen adoption, OpenAI's move to add dedicated teen features represents a course correction, if a belated one.

Study Mode is particularly notable as a response to educational criticism. Rather than facilitating quick answers, it is designed to promote genuine independent thinking — aiming to be an educational tool that develops reasoning skills rather than simply enabling academic dishonesty.
