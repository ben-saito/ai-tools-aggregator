# RAND US Superintelligence Strategy: "Freedom of Action" Approach for Uncertain AI Future

---

## Background: Unclear Path to Superintelligence

RAND Corporation has published a lengthy report in 2026 on what the United States should do to "secure geopolitical advantage on an uncertain path to superintelligence." The core argument: because the timeline and form of eventual superintelligence remain unknown, the optimal strategy is to maintain maximum optionality through a "Freedom of Action" approach.

The key insight: it is unclear exactly what is optimal, so the US should spend money now to preserve options rather than commit prematurely to any single path.

---

## Details of the "Freedom of Action" Strategy

The strategy recommended by RAND includes several concrete elements:

- **Voluntary Commitments**: Mechanisms for AI companies to make autonomous safety commitments
- **Phased Deployment**: Risk management through staged AI system rollouts
- **Independent Evaluation**: Third-party evaluation systems for AI capabilities
- **Incident Reporting**: Reporting obligations for AI-related incidents and anomalies
- **Domestic Capacity**: Building domestic capability to absorb and recover from AI incidents

These elements are positioned as preparatory measures before the full-scale societal impact of advanced AI systems becomes apparent.

---

## Coordination with Industry and Regulatory Gaps

The report recommends promoting voluntary commitments and phased deployment through "informal coordination" with industry. However, critics argue that non-binding voluntary commitments alone may be insufficient as effective regulation for large-scale AI labs.

The safety of superintelligence sits at the intersection of national strategic considerations and technical risk management, requiring unprecedented cooperation between government and industry.
