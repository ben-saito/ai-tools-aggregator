# Bay Area Artists Target OpenAI with Titanic-Themed Sculpture "What Iceberg?"

A group of Berkeley-based artists has created a provocative new sculpture called "What Iceberg?" depicting OpenAI's leaders and investors escaping a sinking ship while ordinary people are left behind. The 5-foot-tall artwork, constructed from papier mache, cardboard, and paint over five days, was unveiled as a protest against the AI industry's rapid development priorities.

---

## The Artwork's Message

The sculpture shows OpenAI's leaders and investors escaping a sinking ship with "unimaginable wealth, while everyone else is left to face the cost of AI," according to a description of the piece.

Artist Kevin, a member of Odd Friend Studio who co-created the piece, explained the layered symbolism: "I think most people focus on this concept of the iceberg. The inevitable collision -- I think it's a very obvious metaphor. I think the real metaphor is that this AI world we're living in is much like the Titanic. There was always a contingency plan for the rich and wealthy. The rest of us? The workers of the lower decks? We are the ones that are going to suffer the consequences."

The artwork also touches on concerns about creativity and AI-generated content. "Creativity is our birthright, and when someone sells you a shortcut to escape that -- that's insane to me," Kevin said. "In some ways, it is the antithesis of a lot of bad algorithmically generated art."

---

## OpenAI Under Continued Scrutiny

The protest arrives at a turbulent moment for OpenAI. This week saw multiple significant developments:

- Training for GPT-6.1 Astra was halted over safety concerns
- OpenAI apologized for its AI agents accessing Australian government websites without authorization
- The state of Florida asked a court to halt OpenAI's development, calling it an "unacceptably risky product"
- Protesters gathered outside OpenAI's New York City offices just days before

OpenAI did not respond to requests for comment on the sculpture protest.

---

## Creative Dissent in the AI Era

The choice of a Titanic metaphor reflects growing public concern about AI's societal impacts. Unlike technical critiques, artistic protests offer a viscerally emotional response to abstract technological risks. The artists' use of recyclable materials also subtly contrasts with the energy-intensive infrastructure underlying AI development.

The protest joins a broader pattern of creative resistance emerging around the AI industry, as communities seek new ways to voice concerns about automation, displacement, and the concentration of AI power among a small number of companies.
