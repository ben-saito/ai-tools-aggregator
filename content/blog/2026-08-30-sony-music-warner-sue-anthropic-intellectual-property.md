# Sony Music and Warner Sue Anthropic Over Alleged Copyright Infringement

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell, alongside other music publishers, have filed a lawsuit against AI company Anthropic, accusing the lab of conducting a "brazen campaign" of illegally scraping and downloading copyrighted works to train its AI model Claude.

The lawsuit was filed late Friday in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. The publishers allege that Anthropic and its co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann engaged in "blatant theft" by using thousands of copyrighted works without permission.

---

## A Pattern of Legal Battles

This lawsuit represents the latest in a series of intellectual property disputes involving Anthropic. The same legal team behind this case also represents Concord Music Group and Universal Music Group in a separate lawsuit filed in January 2026. Additionally, these lawyers led the Bartz v. Anthropic case, in which a group of authors accused Anthropic of using copyrighted works to train products like Claude.

In the landmark Bartz case, a judge ruled that while it was legal to use copyrighted works for training, Anthropic still owed compensation. The company was ordered to pay $1.5 billion in damages.

---

## Industry-Wide Implications

The music publishers' lawsuit highlights growing tensions between creative industries and AI companies over training data. Major record labels and publishers have increasingly turned to litigation as a means of addressing what they characterize as unauthorized use of their catalogs.

Anthropic has not yet publicly responded to the lawsuit. The company previously argued in the Bartz case that AI training on copyrighted material constitutes fair use under U.S. law.
