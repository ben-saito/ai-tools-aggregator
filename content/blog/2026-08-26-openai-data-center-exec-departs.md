# OpenAI Loses Head of Data Centers as Executive Departures Continue

Chris Malone, OpenAI's former head of data centers, departed the company last week according to a Wall Street Journal report. Malone, who previously spent nearly five years at Meta and more than a decade at Google, joined OpenAI not long after the launch of the Stargate Project—a $500 billion data center initiative backed by the Trump administration to develop large-scale AI infrastructure.

---

## Infrastructure Reshuffling

Before Malone's departure, OpenAI had already reorganized its infrastructure team, shifting reporting lines away from President Greg Brockman and placing Vice President Sachin Katti in charge of the group. The reorganization preceded a broader series of high-profile departures from OpenAI that have included the COO, CRO, and other senior executives.

The reason for Malone's specific departure remains unclear. In a competitive hiring market for AI infrastructure talent, data center strategy has become one of the most closely watched roles at major AI labs—companies are competing aggressively for executives with experience building and operating large-scale compute facilities.

---

## What Stargate Means for OpenAI's Infrastructure

The Stargate Project represents one of the largest infrastructure commitments in AI history. The initiative aims to build data centers capable of supporting the next generation of AI models, which require unprecedented amounts of power and cooling capacity. Managing such a project requires specialized expertise that spans hardware procurement, real estate, utility negotiations, and AI-specific facility design.

Malone's departure comes at a critical time as OpenAI scales its compute infrastructure to support GPT-5 and future model generations. The loss of institutional knowledge around data center operations could create challenges for execution, though Katti's promotion suggests OpenAI has an internal succession plan.

---

## Departures Pile Up

Malone's exit adds to a growing list of senior departures from OpenAI over the past several months. The company's rapid growth from research lab to commercial enterprise has created organizational stress that some executives have apparently decided to exit rather than navigate. OpenAI has been recruiting externally to fill gaps, most recently hiring Dali Rajic as CRO.
