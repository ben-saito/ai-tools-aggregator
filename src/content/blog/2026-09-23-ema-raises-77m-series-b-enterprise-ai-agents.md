# Ema Raises $77M Series B to Build AI Agent Teams for Enterprise Workflows

Ema, a startup that uses teams of AI agents to automate corporate processes across HR, IT, and finance, has raised $77 million in a new funding round as it aims to take on more of the work traditionally handled by enterprise software incumbents. The Series B round was led by Bengaluru-based venture firm Creaegis, with existing investors Accel, Section 32, and Prosus increasing their stakes. The financing brings the startup's total funding to $140 million to date.

---

## AI Agents Eating Into Enterprise Software

Ema's approach centers on deploying coordinated teams of AI agents — rather than single large language models — to handle complex, multi-step business workflows. Where traditional enterprise software requires rigid configuration and manual oversight, Ema's agents can reason across process steps, handle exceptions, and complete end-to-end tasks without constant human intervention, according to the company's product descriptions.

The startup has accumulated more than 50 enterprise customers, including major technology companies such as Google and Microsoft. That customer base represents a meaningful foothold in a market where AI-native automation is rapidly displacing legacy robotic process automation (RPA) tools and traditional workflow software.

The enterprise software market has historically been dominated by large vendors with deep integrations into back-office systems. Ema's multi-agent architecture is designed to work across disparate systems — pulling data from HR platforms, IT service desks, and financial databases — without requiring the months-long implementation cycles that have slowed enterprise software adoption in the past.

---

## Why Investors Are Backing Agentic AI Platforms

The funding reflects continued investor appetite for platforms that position AI as a direct replacement for knowledge work rather than an assistant to it. Ema's model allows AI agents to divide complex tasks among themselves — one agent handling document retrieval, another processing approval logic, a third updating records — and coordinate outputs without human routing.

Creaegis, the Bengaluru-based lead investor, has been active in backing AI infrastructure plays across India and global markets. The firm's decision to lead Ema's Series B signals confidence that the startup's agent coordination approach can scale beyond early adopter accounts into broader enterprise deployments.

Accel, Section 32, and Prosus all participated in the round, a consortium that brings both capital and enterprise sales credibility. Existing investors increasing their stakes is generally viewed as a positive signal — it suggests that early-stage metrics are tracking toward the milestones set at the Series A.

---

## The Competitive Landscape

Ema enters an increasingly crowded field of AI agent startups targeting enterprise automation. Competitors include both well-funded pure-plays and large enterprise software vendors building agent capabilities into existing platforms. The key differentiator for Ema is its emphasis on agent teams rather than single-model deployments — an architecture that the company argues is better suited to the complexity of real enterprise workflows.

Traditional RPA vendors and enterprise software incumbents have begun incorporating LLM-based automation into their platforms, but the integration often remains shallow — surface-level chat interfaces layered over unchanged backend systems. Ema's architecture is more deeply integrated, with agents capable of operating across multiple enterprise systems simultaneously.

---

## Outlook

The $77M injection will support Ema's expansion into additional enterprise workflows and geographic markets. With 50 enterprise customers already on board and total funding of $140M, the startup has the runway to pursue larger enterprise contracts while continuing to build out its agent coordination stack.
